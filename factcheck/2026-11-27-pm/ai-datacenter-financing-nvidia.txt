⁠Nvidia has made clear⁠
 it won’t backstop an AI data center until it’s nearly ready to run, and that’s landing just as neoclouds report construction delays and more expensive debt. The message to developers is that land, power, and a finished building matter more than a pitch deck. 
⁠Anita Verma-Lallian⁠
 has been selling exactly that. Her company, Arizona Land Consulting, assembles data center sites for developers, hyperscalers, AI labs, and neoclouds, giving her a view into who’s buying, what makes a site viable, and where projects are running into trouble. She also partners with investor 
⁠Chamath Palihapitiya,⁠
 who has been predicting an industry shakeout. 
On this episode of TechCrunch’s 
⁠Equity⁠
 podcast, Rebecca Bellan talks with Verma-Lallian about who bears the risk when Nvidia gets pickier, why speculative projects could be the first to fall apart, and what a 2027 shakeout might look like. 
Listen to the full episode to hear more about: 
What “land, power, shell” means, and why having all three could give some developers a major advantage. 
How community opposition and state-level restrictions are making some markets harder to build in than others. 
Utilities getting major customers like TSMC to help fund the infrastructure needed to deliver more power. 
Who gets stuck with the bill when a data center project stalls, from developers to lenders to customers. 
Why AI labs and hyperscalers might increasingly build their own data centers instead of relying on neoclouds. 
Who could benefit from a 2027 shakeout, including the companies best positioned to acquire distressed projects.

S&P considers Nvidia credit downgrade over governance and ‘key man’ risks
S&P Global Ratings, one of the three major credit rating agencies, is considering downgrading Nvidia’s rating, citing concerns about the company’s management and governance practices.
Despite Nvidia’s strong financial performance, the ouster and reinstatement of CEO Jensen Huang has raised concerns about “weak executive oversight and lack of succession planning” and “key man risk,” meaning heavy reliance on Huang for its future success, S&P said in a 
statement
. 
The agency expects to finish its review within 90 days and could cut Nvidia’s rating from AA to AA-, the fourth-highest on its scale.
Although Nvidia is certain to keep its investment-grade rating, a downgrade could raise its borrowing costs. Because Nvidia backstops neoclouds and other infrastructure companies that lack strong credit on their own, a lower rating could make their debt costlier or force them to post more collateral.
In August, Nvidia announced plans to arrange 
$500 billion
 in financing for AI infrastructure with six financial institutions, including Goldman Sachs, Apollo, and Blackstone. Under the original terms, Nvidia would guarantee 25% of the loans. But even before Huang’s ouster and return, lenders had been demanding that Nvidia guarantee more, Bloomberg 
reported
.
A downgrade would likely prompt lenders to demand even larger guarantees.
