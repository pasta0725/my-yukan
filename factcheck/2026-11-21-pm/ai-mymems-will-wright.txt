The computer gaming world hasn’t heard much from Will Wright (
SimCity
, 
The Sims
) since 
the underwhelming release of 
Spore
 in 2008
 and Wright’s 
subsequent departure from Maxis owner EA
. Now, well over 15 years later, Wright is starting to promote his next digital simulation, which will use generative AI technology to craft a game-like experience from players’ memories.
MyMems
, which is slated to release next year, differentiates itself from Wright’s previous projects by “turn[ing] the simulation inward,” as Wright’s Gallium Studios and partner Artificial Agency 
put it in a press release this morning
. The game will mine memories provided by the player to create digital people that can then form teams to solve puzzles in the game world.
“If my Grandmother ended up in a Grand Slam match against Serena Williams, who would win?” Wright says by way of example in the release. “If I had to build a bridge across a short chasm, which three people from my life would I choose to help me?”
In 
an accompanying announcement video
, Wright talks about how the “plausible behavior” in a game like 
The Sims
 still didn’t effectively capture the nuances of people he encounters in real life. “It was nowhere near them understanding my claustrophobia or why my sister and I fought so much… We’re actually taking parts of your life, your memories, your experiences, people you know, and turning that into a game that’s very personal.”
      Will Wright introduces 
MyMems
 to the world.
Wright said 
MyMems
 will be able to extract generalized themes from your memories into self-contained areas that Wright compared to the various parts in a theme park: Sports, vacations, injuries, memories, and deaths could all be the basis for their own zones to play in. Players will also have the option to create worlds based on shared memories with family, friends, and co-workers, for example.
They’re artificial 
and
 intelligent?
Since this is 2026, it shouldn’t be surprising that MyMems is making heavy use of generative AI (with a “proprietary behavior engine” on top) to transform your typed-up memories into simulated locales and characters. In the announcement video, Wright praises the “entirely open-ended” AI systems Gallium is using “to extract from the stories and whatnot based upon the memories you put into the game.”
But Wright’s focus on mining memories for simulation content predates the current generative AI boom. What has now become 
MyMems
 started off as 
Proxi
, a mobile simulation that was 
first announced in 2018
 as “a game of self-discovery, a game where we actually uncover the hidden you.” But after years of pre-production work (as outlined in 
an excellent Vulture piece earlier this year
), Wright’s Gallium Studios finally ran out of funding in late 2024, leading to mass layoffs for the small team and an uncertain future for the project.
Now the idea has been revived thanks in large part to a partnership with 
AI middleware firm Artificial Agency
. Until now, that company has mainly been focused on agentic systems that can create “virtual players” to playtest a game or serve as a partner for human players.
Now, Artificial Agency’s marketing says the company’s AI tool will be used to create “a personal behavior model and a knowledge graph” from players’ memories, drawing some unsettling comparisons to big tech surveillance.
“Meta knows what you click,” the company writes. “Netflix knows what you watch. Nobody knows what you remember. MemDNA learns from your memories locally and only with your permission.”
Wright said the partnership with Artificial Agency will let Gallium benefit from “off-the-shelf” AI solutions without having to “become an AI research company” in its own right. He also said the collaboration will help limit costs by providing a cheaper option than the “really expensive” frontier models that might be overkill for a project like this.
“Right now AI has opened a vast new design area and I’m enjoying, you know, exploring that as a designer,” Wright said.

Will Wright’s next game uses AI to map your memories
MyMems, from the creator of SimCity and The Sims, is a new social simulator powered by AI.
MyMems, from the creator of SimCity and The Sims, is a new social simulator powered by AI.
by
Jay Peters
Nov 18, 2026, 2:00 PM UTC
Link
Share
Gift
Image: Gallium Studios
Jay Peters
is a senior reporter covering technology, gaming, and more. He joined The Verge in 2019 after nearly two years at Techmeme.
Will Wright, the creator of legendary franchises like 
SimCity
 and 
The Sims
, is working on a new game — and it’s built around AI. The new title, focused on your memories, is called 
MyMems
. Like Wright’s other titles, it sounds extremely ambitious, giving players a new way to explore worlds they create on their own. “We want to take your autobiography and turn it into a toy world,” he tells 
The Verge
.
The 
MyMems
 website
 describes the mobile game as a “social simulation” with worlds “built from your memories and the people who shaped you.” You can add “memories” to your game, like speaking a short story to your phone, and those memories are generated into pieces you can place on the game’s map. Your worlds can also include AI-powered avatars, which can represent people you know in your life. Ideally, as you play, the planet starts to become a “map of your psyche,” kind of like 
Inside Out
, Wright says. Worlds can be private, but you can also share them with others.
Here’s another way to think of it, from Virginia McArthur, executive producer and GM at 
MyMems
 developer Gallium Studios: “It’s a cozy world builder with a really deep social simulation underneath.”
Image: Gallium Studios
I’ll admit that I don’t totally get it, and it seems like a tricky idea to grasp without playing it. Still, as heady of a concept as it might be — turning your memories into a game world — Wright and team still want it to be fun. “This isn’t meant to be your legacy diary necessarily,” says Anthony Maitz, a product manager at Gallium Studios. “It’s meant to just be something you kind of play around with.” However, Maitz notes that what you’re working toward is a “proxy” of you — a word that seems to be the inspiration of the 
game’s earlier title, 
Proxi: Yesterday’s You Tomorrow
. The game will use the “Behavior Engine” from a company called 
Artificial Agency
, which makes AI tech for video games, 
as a “foundational technology.”
Like other industries, gaming is starting to reckon with generative AI. Early AI virtual worlds created with models from a startup 
backed by a Pixar cofounder
 and 
Google’s Project Genie
 weren’t experiences you’d actually want to spend dedicated time with, and indie developers I spoke to in March were 
largely opposed to the technology
. But AI technology has taken some big leaps this year with companies like 
Google
, 
Meta
, and 
Roblox
 now letting users make games with AI right from their phone, and retro gaming becoming 
overrun with AI-powered ports
 of classic titles.
Take-Two Interactive CEO Strauss Zelnick said earlier this year that generative AI has “zero part in what Rockstar Games is building” 
with 
GTA VI
. But even as a recent industry survey indicated that more game developers are starting to use generative AI 
as part of their job
, workers are pushing back 
against generative AI use
, and the technology is at the center of fierce debates 
over indie game awards
.
Many players are rejecting AI outright, and I wondered if the Gallium Studios team was worried about opposition toward 
MyMems
’ use of AI. Wright says he has three boys at home and that they are all “pretty negative” on AI. He attributes negativity over AI to be toward the meaning of art and authorship and credit, a concern he calls “well-founded.” But he also calls AI an “amazing tool.”
Image: Gallium Studios
“We’re not making slop,” says McArthur. But Maitz notes that while the team has “been concerned” about a negative response to the generative AI use, they’ve learned that people don’t mind when they use AI in a “
Scribblenauts
-y style” for things like having the game make a high school. The team also found that people got “addicted” to a test app with a character creator, including Maitz’s own daughter, who he says “hates” AI. “By making it so that the player is authoring it over and over, bit by bit, it doesn’t really feel like you cheated,” Maitz says.
“We’re not making slop.”
MyMems
, which is set to launch in 2027, will join a growing field of other social simulations, like Krafton’s 
Inzoi
 and Nintendo’s 
smash hit
Tomodachi Life: Living the Dream
. But none quite have the pull of 
The Sims
, which remains one of EA’s most important franchises. With 
The Sims
, Wright says that many people tried making their real families in the game.
“It was interesting how people really were naturally trying to map this game, which was like a little toy, onto their real life,” he says. With 
MyMems
, that’s what the team is trying to do the “follow-through” on — what if they made the whole game focused on you?
Imagine opening a family scrapbook and the way people and things jump out, Wright explains. “As you go through more and more pages, pretty soon you have this whole little circus of people and events and things from your life.” It’s chaos, he says. “I want something that feels kind of like that.”

[San Francisco and Edmonton, November 18, 2026]
 Gallium Studios, the new game studio founded by legendary game designer Will Wright, and Artificial Agency today announced that Gallium has selected Artificial Agency’s Behavior Engine as a foundational technology for Wright’s new Social Simulation experience. Wright, creator of hit franchises including SimCity, The Sims, and Spore, representing combined lifetime sales exceeding $5B, and his team are defining a new type of simulation that uses agentic behavior to bring players’ personal memories and relationships to life through experiences in an open-ended, evolving world. The collaboration provides the foundational agentic intelligence needed to bring Wright’s vision to life at scale, while letting the team focus on gameplay and the connections between family and friends. The game, MyMems, is currently in development, and Gallium is targeting a live community release in 2027.
“Here are some questions that come to mind as I build my world in MyMems,” said Will Wright, Founder and CEO of Gallium. “If my Grandmother ended up in a Grand Slam match against Serena Williams, who would win? If I had to build a bridge across a short chasm, which 3 people from my life would I choose to help me? By mining your memories and experiences, we begin to bring the people you have known to life inside our game world. Your “mems” become a team that you can draft smaller groups from to solve challenges in your game world. Memories from your life can also be combined into thematic structures that both create and help solve new challenges. Players are always trying to see some connection between their entertainment experiences and their real life, whether that’s in music, movies, or games. Games are the one medium in which we can actually make that happen to a really meaningful degree. I want to design a game where the player’s actual life becomes the scaffolding for the game world and game experience.”
Where SimCity lets players model communities, and The Sims lets them explore relationships, MyMems turns the simulation inward. Players will turn real memories into a playable world with monuments and characters inspired by friends, family, and other people from their lives. Those characters will interact with players, one another, and meaningful objects and places, creating an unbounded, deeply personalized social simulation that evolves with every memory and interaction. Bringing such an ambitious vision to life requires a system that can support the infinite variety of people, memories and relationships in each player’s world without individually scripting every character or encounter. Artificial Agency’s Behavior Engine gives Gallium the means to realize that vision, combining open-ended character behavior with the care, creative control and craftsmanship of a hand-built game.
“Will has spent his career redefining what simulation games can be, and his latest vision is his most groundbreaking yet – nothing like this exists in the world,” said Brian Tanner, CEO and co-founder of Artificial Agency. “Agentic behavior gives game creators a new tool to build worlds that can surprise even the people who designed them. Gallium is taking that creative freedom in an extraordinary direction, giving players the ability to turn their own lives into experiences no developer could have scripted.”
Artificial Agency’s Behavior Engine also addresses the economics of bringing agentic behavior to a global player base. The company combines small, optimized, game-specific models with a reflex architecture that separates immediate reactions from decisions requiring deeper model reasoning. This keeps characters responsive while reducing latency and inference costs, making dynamic, personalized gameplay commercially viable at scale. For a creator whose games have reached hundreds of millions of people across multiple generations, accessibility matters. Artificial Agency’s approach gives Gallium the foundation to bring Wright’s most personalized simulation yet to a broad global audience.

About Artificial Agency
Artificial Agency is an AI company pioneering agentic behavior for gaming. Its Behavior Engine trains game-specific models that enable characters and game systems to react, coordinate, and adapt to player actions and changes in game state, while reducing latency and inference costs to make agentic gameplay practical to build and ship at scale. With AI talent from Google DeepMind, gaming veterans from top AAA studios, and backing from Radical Ventures and Toyota Ventures, Artificial Agency is redefining the boundaries of gaming and completely reimagining the player experience.
About Gallium
Gallium Studios, led by Will Wright, creator of The Sims and SimCity, is building MyMems, a social simulation driven by your memories. MyMems turns the people, places and moments that shaped you into a playable world to explore, build and share. Players start in their Guide’s world, using merge mechanics and their memories to solve puzzles and unlock a world of their own. Gallium’s proprietary Personal Behavior Model, memDNA, works with Artificial Agency’s behavioral AI technology to shape how characters behave and how each world responds. The result gives people new reasons to reconnect through play.
