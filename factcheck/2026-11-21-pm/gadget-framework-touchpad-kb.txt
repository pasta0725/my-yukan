===== https://www.pcworld.com/article/3287093/frameworks-all-in-one-keyboard-wants-to-be-the-go-to-pick-for-makers.html
(status 200)
Framework is one of my favorite PC makers thanks to its repair-friendly ethos and surprisingly open communication with customers. And I am, for my sins, PCWorld’s resident keyboard nut. So the moment I heard Framework was making a keyboard, my ears perked up like a dog that’s heard the can opener. 
Only to have them droop back down again. The Framework Keyboard is not mechanical, as I first assumed. But that doesn’t mean it’s 
bad
, it’s just aiming for a different market. Specifically, the kind of market that needs a keyboard and mouse (touchpad) all in one device. The Framework Keyboard is a lot like the Logitech K400 and similar designs, ideal for PCs in odd places where a mouse won’t fit, like a home theater PC. Or indeed, 
the Steam Machine
. 
Framework sent me a pre-production unit ahead of the planned Q1 release. The look is basic bordering on utilitarian, with slate-gray plastic, no backlighting, and only a device-switching button and a power toggle on top of the regular keyboard keys. The only splash of distinction comes from flipping the keyboard over, where you’ll see a smoke-transparent bottom housing that shows off its guts. That’s very cool, even if I would have preferred a splash of orange or purple. The lack of keyboard feet for an adjustable typing angle is significantly less cool, but I suppose this keyboard is intended to be used in a lap most of the time. 
Michael Crider/Foundry
And…yeah, that’s about it as far as the use case goes. It’s a standard wireless design with multi-device Bluetooth and dongle-based wireless. It has all the standard keys you’d find on a laptop board, plus a function row. It has no separate mouse buttons, the multi-point touchpad “clicks” instead. It’s a little wide because of the form factor…um, it has a USB-C port for charging or data? Yeah, I’m struggling to say much more about the physical design, because it’s 
just 
a keyboard with a touchpad. 
But if you thought Framework wouldn’t have something up its sleeve for its first input accessory design, you’ve got another thing coming. Like the company’s laptops and desktop, you can take this thing apart easily with a Torx screwdriver. Six little black screws are all that stand between you and the inside. From there you can unplug a ribbon cable, and it’s easy to see how the separate components like the battery, charging and control board, main keys, and touchpad can be replaced with a little more screwdriver work. 
Michael Crider/Foundry
That’s about all you can do with this repair-friendly approach, as there’s no real upgrades for these parts. But Framework does intend to sell these components individually, along with releasing Open CAD files, firmware, and schematics for all necessary parts. The idea is that the Framework Keyboard could become the de facto slim, laptop-style board for creators. 
This keyboard also works with the 
ZMK firmware system
, a familiar interface for mechanical keyboard nerds. That makes it much easier to program than most regular keyboards, if they support that feature at all. 
Michael Crider/Foundry
Oh, one last cool thing: Framework sent me a “cradle” for the dongle, which fits into a standard Framework laptop or desktop modular USB-C port. With this extra adapter, the dongle sits flush with the case. Very cool, and something only Framework could do. It’s a separate purchase, and I haven’t been told how much yet. 
Here’s the downer: Framework plans to sell this board for $100 USD when it releases in early 2027. That’s not unreasonable for a repairable design with these components, especially considering its open nature. But it is about double what you’d pay for a similarly equipped combination board and touchpad, even from a major brand like Logitech. And though the keyboard Framework sent me is still a pre-production unit, its rather basic and mushy typing doesn’t make up the difference. Since it already has a steel backing, I can’t see this improving too much in a couple of months.
Michael Crider/Foundry
If all you really need is a keyboard and touchpad for a PC or other gadget in a tricky spot, I don’t see a reason to pay double. The 3D printing folks might 
love 
it, and they already love Framework…but I think even they have cheaper options if they want these parts, not least of which are the parts that Framework already sells. I’d be a lot more enthusiastic about it if the price were knocked down 20 bucks or so. 
Framework’s Keyboard is up for pre-order now, with a refundable $50 deposit. It costs $100 in the U.S., $139 in Canada, €119 in the European Union, and £99 in the UK. Availability is “Q1 2027,” so you’ll have some time to work out if it’s worth the price for you or not.

===== https://www.notebookcheck.net/Framework-releases-new-Logitech-K400-rival-with-improved-trackpad.1452795.0.html
(status 200)
Framework has finally started taking orders for its first wireless keyboard with an integrated trackpad. To recap, the company unveiled the Wireless Touchpad Keyboard 
in April
. Presented alongside the 
Framework Laptop 13 Pro
, the Wireless Touchpad Keyboard did not have a launch date or set pricing in April.
Fast forward to November, and Framework has now opened pre-orders globally. Currently, the company has priced the Wireless Touchpad Keyboard at $99, or £99 in the UK and €119 in the Eurozone. However, one must put down roughly half of that as a deposit, which reserves a spot in Framework's production batch.
It is also worth noting that the company is only committing to shipping batches 1-4 during 'Q1'. Hence, it is unclear at this stage whether the Wireless Touchpad Keyboard will start reaching people in early January or in late March. Also, Framework has not clarified what happens to shipment windows when it sells through its fourth pre-order batch.
Setting that aside, the Wireless Touchpad Keyboard targets the Logitech K400 Plus 
(curr. $29 on Amazon)
 with wired USB Type-C, 2.4 GHz and triple-Bluetooth channel connectivity. Moreover, the keyboard combines the nRF54 wireless microcontroller with the keyboard and touchpad from the Framework Laptop 12. Also, Framework promises to publish its open-source and ZMK-based firmware to GitHub soon. Please see the 
company's website
 for more details about the Wireless Touchpad Keyboard.

Framework Wireless Touchpad Keyboard To Sell For $99 USD, Begins Shipping In Q1
Written by 
Michael Larabel
 in 
Hardware
 on 17 November 2026 at 11:40 AM EST. 
26 Comments
Earlier this year Framework Computer announced the Framework Wireless Touchpad Keyboard as a new product. Today the device is open for pre-order but will not begin shipping until Q1'2027.
The keyboard supports three Bluetooth channels as well as a wired USB-C connection or USB-A dongle. Framework talks up this keyboard as being "ultra efficient" with a "year+ battery life when used sporadically." Completing the device is open-source firmware and there is also open-source CAD files for the Framework Wireless Touchpad Keyboard.
This new Framework product opened up for pre-orders today for $99 USD, a $50 fully refundable deposit is required. The first batches will begin shipping in Q1'2027 across all regions.
Those wishing to learn more about the Framework Wireless Touchpad Keyboard can do so via the 
Framework Blog
.
