石川温の「スマホ業界 Watch」
有機ELで薄型化した新「iPad mini」を試す、iPhone Duo併用での本音
石川 温
2026年11月12日 00:27
 　2026年11月6日に発売されたばかりのiPad miniを数時間、試用する機会を得た。これまでマイナーアップデートばかりであったiPad miniであるが、今回はハードウェアを一新し、完成度がさらに高まった。携帯性の良さ、パワフルな使い勝手と申し分のない一台に仕上がっている。 
 　今回、発売されたiPad miniは新たに有機ELディスプレイを搭載。圧倒的に美しく、特に黒の表現が本当に引き締まっている。小さい画面ながらも、写真や動画の表示に圧倒される。 
 　256GBと512GBモデルにおいてはNano-textureディスプレイ抑える特殊加工）を選択可能だ。実際にNano-textureディスプレイを試用すると、画面の反射がなく、動画や文字が本当に見やすい。オプションの選択肢がなくても何ら不満はないのだが、いざ実物を体験してしまうと、どうしてもNano-textureディスプレイを選びたくなってくる。 
 　画面サイズは8.5インチと従来モデルの8.3インチよりも大きくなった。 
 　従来モデルが195.4×134.8×6.3mm、なのに対して、新しいiPad miniは195.3×132.6×5.4mmと、わずかながらコンパクトになっている。 
 　重さにおいては前モデルが297g（セルラーモデル）で、新しいのは263g（セルラーモデル）だ。有機ELディスプレイにより、ハッキリと「薄く、軽く」なっているのがわかる。 
 　これだけで、既存のiPad miniユーザーも所有欲がくすぐられるのだ。 
 　チップはiPhone 18 Proなどと同じA20 Proチップを搭載。ビデオ会議など複数のアプリを頻繁に切り替えて使っていっても、何らストレスなくサクサクと使えてしまう。 
 　これまでのiPad miniでは外部ディスプレイに対してはミラーリングしかできなかったが、iPadOS 27.2では拡張ディスプレイモードにも対応するため、本格的に仕事でも活用できるだろう。 
 　また、内側のカメラも、従来モデルでは横置きにした際、左端にいってしまったが、新しいiPad miniでは横置きにした際、画面の上部に配置されるよう位置が変更となっている。ビデオ会議でも目線が自然になったのも、地味ながら便利な改善点と言えるだろう。 
 　音質面でも、スピーカーの穴がなくなり、画面そのものを振動させて音を鳴らす「Acoustic Display」に対応した。。画面全体が共振するため、音圧もかなり上がった印象だ。Acoustic Displayにより、本体側面にあったスピーカーの穴がなくなり、デザイン的にもシンプルでスッキリとした印象になった。 
 　また、穴がなくなったせいか、水回りでも安心して使えるレベルであるIP67等級の防水・防塵性能に対応。日本だと風呂に浸かりながらiPad miniで動画や書籍、マンガを読むという使い方で人気が出そうだが、お湯や湯気が充満し、さらには石けんなどがある風呂場という環境での使用は「表向き」オススメはできないと言っておきたい。 
7.6型の折りたたみ「iPhone Duo」ユーザーが感じたリアルな葛藤
 　コンパクトでいままで以上にパワフル、画面表示や音質も大幅に強化されたiPad miniは間違いなく「買い」のデバイスといえる。 
 　個人的にも購入欲がフツフツと湧いているのだが、悩ましいのが買ったばかりのiPhone Duoとの組み合わせだ。 
 　正直言って、開いて7.6インチのiPhone Duoと、8.5インチのiPad miniでは、動画や書籍、ウェブの視聴体験はかなり近い。この2台を同時に持ち歩くというのは、使用感においてかなり重複しており、iPhone Duoが1台あれば充分といえる。 
 　ただ、そもそもiPhone DuoとiPad miniは別物だ。 
 　従来のiPhoneを使いつつ、iPad miniも携帯し、ちょっとした時間に動画を見る、あるいはデスクで仕事をこなすという点においては最強のパートナーだろう。 
飛行機内でも活躍する絶妙サイズ、携帯性はバツグン
 　身の回りでiPad miniをよく見かける場面といえば、飛行機の中だ。 
 　キャビンアテンダントさんがiPad miniでマニュアルを確認したり、食事の注文をメモしたり、業務日報を書いている（ような）姿を頻繁に確認できる。よく見ると、腰にiPad miniを収納できるベルトのようなものを巻いており、さっと取り出して、使っていたりする。 
 　機内をせわしく行き来し、荷物を棚に持ち上げたり、料理を配膳したりするキャビンアテンダントさんにとってiPad miniはちょうどいい大きさのデバイスになっているようだ（ちなみに、世界中、どこでも使えるSIMカードが入っているらしい）。 
 　画面が美しく、音が良く、携帯性に優れたiPad miniは（iPhone Duoユーザーを除く）幅広い人にお勧めできる1台といえそうだ。

The iPad mini 8 has many great features for reading. It’s slimmer and lighter, making it easier to hold for long reading sessions. The new OLED panel helps text and color images pop, and the nano-texture display option gives the panel a more paper-like feel.
Even if the iPad mini 8 is more feature-rich, the Kindle Colorsoft is still a fantastic e-reader thanks to its light and sturdy build, large color display, and smooth integration with the Kindle ecosystem. And even the more expensive Signature Edition is still hundreds of dollars less than an iPad mini 8.

iPad mini 8 vs. Kindle Colorsoft: Price
(Image credit: Tom's Guide)
The iPad mini 8 starts at $699 for the 128GB model with a glossy screen. You can upgrade to a model with the glare-reducing nano-texture display, but that requires upgrading to 256GB, which adds $200 to the overall cost.
Conversely, the Amazon Kindle Signature Edition costs $319 with 32GB of storage and no ad support. The standard Kindle Colorsoft with 16GB of storage and without the aluminum back housing costs $289.
The price difference between the iPad mini 8 and Kindle Colorsoft Signature Edition is substantial. Between the two, the Kindle is the more affordable option, though both are relatively expensive compared to their previous models.

iPad mini 8 vs. Kindle Colorsoft: Design
(Image credit: Tom's Guide)
The new iPad mini 8 and Kindle Colorsoft have been redesigned to be thinner and lighter than their respective predecessors. As a result, they're easy to read for extended periods.
Of the two, the iPad mini 8 is larger and heavier, measuring 7.69 x 5.22 x 0.21 inches and weighing 8.9 ounces. The Kindle Colorsoft Signature Edition is still pretty sizeable at 6.9 x 5.0 x 0.3 inches and 8.1 ounces, but it’s decidedly smaller in comparison.
The iPad mini 8 is one single piece of machined aluminum, whereas the only aluminum part of the Kindle Colorsoft is its back housing. Still, both feel sturdy and smooth in hand. Both devices are also waterproof, with the iPad mini 8 rated IP67 and the Colorsoft rated IPX8. Colorwise, Apple’s miniature tablet comes in Midnight and Starlight, while the Kindle Colorsoft comes in Fig and Graphite.
The iPad mini 8 has the more premium design overall, but both are elegant slabs in their own right.
iPad mini 8 vs. Kindle Colorsoft: Display
(Image credit: Tom's Guide)
Here’s where things diverge sharply. Since we’re discussing a digital ink display against an OLED panel, which is admittedly not a one-to-one comparison, I’ll instead focus on the actual reading experience each provides to make things fairer.
The iPad mini 8’s display is a big improvement over the past, thanks to its larger 8.5-inch display, thinner bezels, and higher resolution of 2316 x 1512 pixels at 326 pixels per inch (ppi). Its OLED panel also delivers excellent contrast and vivid colors. Novels, comics, and everything else look fantastic.
(Image credit: Tom's Guide)
Apple sent us a review unit with a nano-texture display, which makes the screen more comparable to a Kindle for reading by reducing glare. Plus, the texture itself gives it a more paper-like feel. While it stings that you have to pay $200 more for the 256GB model to get the nano-texture option, it’s a great feature.
The Kindle Colorsoft has the same 7-inch 300ppi color display as the previous model, but Amazon has employed a new reverse-stack display construction to make the screen-to-bezel transition more seamless.
While black-and-white content looks brighter and bolder on a Kindle Paperwhite due to this reader’s color layer, colors are bolder than before. Whether it’s books or comics, everything looks great. However, color reading drops the resolution to 150ppi.
Though the iPad mini 8 might give you more eye strain since it can get significantly brighter, it still has the more vibrant display of the two. However, if you want a more paper-like experience, the Colorosoft beats the iPad.
iPad mini 8 vs. Kindle Colorsoft: Battery life
(Image credit: Tom's Guide)
Battery life is the one area where the Kindle Colorsoft clearly beats the iPad mini 8. Apple rates the iPad mini 8 as having up to 10 hours of battery life while surfing the web on Wi-Fi. Meanwhile, Amazon says the Colorsoft can last up to eight weeks on a single charge.
I’ve used the iPad mini 8 for nearly two weeks and have charged it about once every other day. Conversely, after almost three weeks, I still haven't charged the Kindle Colorsoft since I unboxed it. At the time of writing, it is at 60% battery life!
Kindles have always boasted long battery life since they are dedicated e-readers, unlike an iPad, which is a more demanding computing device. It’s no surprise that the Colorsoft can last much longer than the iPad mini 8.
iPad mini 8 vs. Kindle Colorsoft: Features
(Image credit: Tom's Guide)
This is another area where things don’t match one-to-one. You can read on both, but that’s about it on the Colorsoft, while the iPad mini 8 is basically a computer that can do so much more. To keep things fair though, I’ll focus on the respective Kindle ecosystem experiences the devices offer.
Naturally, the Kindle Colorsoft integrates seamlessly with the Kindle Store and Goodreads. I can easily purchase new books right on the Kindle and also upload my reading status to Goodreads.
(Image credit: Tom's Guide)
UI-wise, the Home page shows all the books you’re currently reading or have read, a list of recommended books, and top Kindle Unlimited recommendations based on your reading history. You can also change the font, theme, and more within a book’s dedicated settings.
The iPadOS version of the Kindle app has all these features, except that the UI is arranged differently to fit an iPad’s screen size. One downside is that you can’t buy books directly in the Kindle app. To do that, you’ll have to open a web browser and log in to Amazon. That said, the Kindle app still syncs your reading status across Kindles and other devices.
One of Colorsoft’s major advantages over the iPad mini 8 is its truly distraction-free reading experience. You’ll never see pop-up notifications on a Kindle, whereas you’ll need to manually disable notifications on an iPad. It’s also much easier to wander off to another app on the iPad because everything is so easily accessible. On a Kindle, you can only read or browse for books.
iPad mini 8 vs. Kindle Colorsoft: Bottom line
Both the iPad mini 8 and Kindle Colorsoft are fantastic for reading. You can’t go wrong with either for that, but depending on what you’re looking for, one might suit you better than the other.
If you want an enhanced version of the classic Kindle experience, then the Kindle Colorsoft is the device to get. Its new design is superb, and colors look better than ever. Yes, it’s now the second-priciest Kindle after the Kindle Scribe, but there’s no denying Amazon delivered a more refined color e-reader.
Conversely, the iPad mini 8 impresses thanks to its premium design and mesmerizing OLED panel. Both black-and-white text and color images look absolutely stunning on its display. The lighter design also makes it more Kindle-like to hold. And if you want to do more than read, the iPad mini delivers strong performance for work, light gaming, and streaming videos.

The first reviews of 
Apple
‘s eighth-generation OLED iPad mini arrived on Tuesday, with technology critics praising its A20 Pro processor, thinner water-resistant frame and brighter screen. Reviewers across major publications questioned its $699 starting price and 
Apple
‘s decision to keep the 8.5-inch display locked at a 60Hz refresh rate.

A thinner chassis and Acoustic Display
The new iPad mini brings the most extensive physical overhaul to Apple’s compact tablet line in its 14-year history, according to 
MacRumors
. The aluminium chassis measures 5.4mm thick, down from 6.3mm on the 2024 model. It weighs 0.56 pounds (255 grams) in the Wi-Fi configuration and 0.58 pounds with cellular connectivity, dropping from 0.65 pounds on the prior edition.
Apple achieved that profile partly by removing external speaker grilles. Audio now vibrates directly through the front glass panel using a system Apple calls an Acoustic Display, which pairs display-driven woofers with tweeters set inside the frame. 
Gizmodo reported
 that while deep bass frequencies produce noticeable vibrations against the user’s palms, the setup delivers full stereo sound and avoids muffled audio when holding the device in landscape orientation. 
Engadget
 reported a stronger bass presence than the previous model, which sounded tinny by comparison.
Removing the speaker holes allowed Apple to secure an IP67 rating for dust and water resistance, marking a first for any iPad model. The tablet can withstand immersion in up to one metre of freshwater for 30 minutes. 
The Verge
 called the device “the Field iPad”, noting that the added protection makes it suitable for use in kitchens, bathrooms and industrial environments. Apple also relocated the 12-megapixel Center Stage camera to the landscape edge, though biometric authentication remains limited to a Touch ID power button rather than Face ID.
A20 Pro performance and battery tests
The tablet runs Apple’s 2nm A20 Pro processor, the same chip family used in the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone Duo. Apple splits the silicon into two tiers based on storage capacity. The 128GB and 256GB configurations feature a binned five-core CPU, a seven-core graphics processor and a 16-core Neural Engine. Buyers who spend at least $1,000 on the 512GB model receive a six-core CPU and a dual 16-core Neural Engine for on-device machine learning tasks.
MacRumors reported that all configurations ship with 12GB of RAM, matching the base M5 iPad Pro. In Geekbench 7 testing by Gizmodo, the A20 Pro lagged the M4 chip in the 11-inch iPad Air by only 15 percent in multi-threaded workloads, while posting faster single-core scores. The mini also beat the M4 iPad Air by 15 percent in Geekbench 7’s Metal GPU test and by roughly 25 percent in 3DMark Steel Nomad Lite.

Reviewers tested demanding gaming titles including Resident Evil 4, Control and Assassin’s Creed Mirage, reporting steady frame rates and fast load times. Gizmodo found that the tablet throttled performance when tested inside Apple’s Smart Folio case, though speeds remained stable without the cover.
Battery testing showed mixed results depending on workload. The Verge and MacRumors logged between nine and ten hours of battery life during video streaming and web browsing. Engadget found that intensive 3D gaming reduced run times to between five and six hours. The tablet also introduces fast-charging support, recharging from empty to 50 percent in 30 minutes when paired with an adapter rated at 35W or higher. For wireless data, Apple integrated its N1 networking chip for Wi-Fi 7 and Bluetooth 6, alongside an optional C2 modem that exceeded 500Mbps on 5G networks in Engadget’s testing.
Display quality and the 60Hz limit
The headline feature of the hardware update is an 8.5-inch Super Retina XDR OLED screen with a 2,000,000:1 contrast ratio, replacing the 8.3-inch LCD panel from the seventh-generation model. Bezels are 24 percent narrower, allowing for a slightly larger display without increasing the tablet’s exterior footprint. Standard dynamic range brightness reaches 600 nits, up from 500 nits on the previous model, with minimum brightness dropping to 1 nit for nighttime reading.
Reviewers noted a discrepancy in peak HDR brightness across Apple’s technical figures. Gizmodo cited an Apple rating of 1,000 nits for HDR highlights, while Engadget and 
9to5Mac
 reported that peak HDR brightness reaches 1,200 nits. The screen uses a single-layer OLED stack rather than the tandem OLED technology found on the M5 iPad Pro.
The omission of a 120Hz ProMotion refresh rate drew criticism across every review. Apple defended the 60Hz limit by stating that OLED’s faster pixel response times reduce motion blur during navigation. Both The Verge and 9to5Mac disputed that reasoning. Reviewers observed that while system animations feel marginally crisper than on the older LCD, fast scrolling and Apple Pencil input continue to show visible judder when compared side by side with ProMotion displays.
Buyers of the 256GB and 512GB tiers can pay an extra $100 for an etched nano-texture matte glass finish. The treatment dispersed direct reflections under bright lamps and outdoor light. However, reviewers noted that the matte coating introduces a slight shimmer on white backgrounds and softens peak contrast compared to the standard glossy panel.
Pricing and tablet lineup trade-offs
Software usability highlighted the limitations of the form factor. iPadOS 27 supports external monitors at 4K resolution and runs creative software smoothly, including Canva’s free 
Affinity suite
 app and Adobe Lightroom. Multitasking proved more difficult, with side-by-side windows feeling cramped on the 8.5-inch display and system controls occasionally triggering accidental Spotlight searches.
Pricing remains the main barrier for reviewers. The eighth-generation iPad mini starts at $699 for 128GB of storage, which is $100 more than the post-price-increase cost of the A17 Pro model and $200 higher than its original 2024 launch price. A 512GB cellular configuration with nano-texture glass reaches $1,249.
That entry price places the compact tablet only $50 below the 11-inch M4 iPad Air, which starts at $749 with 128GB of storage, while the entry-level iPad costs $449 and the M5 iPad Pro starts at $1,199. David Phelan of Forbes Innovation noted that while the new small-screen tablet has strong hardware advantages, it remains pricey. Apple begins retail sales for the device on Friday, 6 November, offering starlight and midnight finishes.
