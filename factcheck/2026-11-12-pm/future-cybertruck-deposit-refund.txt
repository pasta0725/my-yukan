Tesla CEO Elon Musk is defending the Cybertruck and the company’s decision to 
stop producing
 the polarizing steel-clad truck, claiming that the order rate far exceeds its production.
Tesla will shut down production of the Cybertruck in about eight months Musk 
said in an X post
 on Monday. He also said the company will try to make as many of the vehicles as possible before then, though Tesla will “have to return deposits for a lot of people.”
Musk argued the Cybertruck was not unprofitable, and that Tesla was ending production because the “opportunity cost of the complexity of Cybertruck is vastly outweighed by the value of incremental Cybercab production.”
In other words, Musk is arguing that Tesla can make more money by using the factory space and resources dedicated to the Cybertruck for building Cybercab robotaxis. Musk said last week that Tesla plans to use the factory space to build more two-seater Cybercabs.
The first Cybertrucks were delivered to customers in late 2023, when it seemed like it could be a breakthrough hit for the company. Reservations for the vehicle had ballooned to 1 million, according to 
Musk
, who also predicted that the company would sell around 250,000 of the trucks annually.
But those reservations never translated into actual sales. Tesla doesn’t break out sales of the Cybertrucks, but industry experts estimate a total of about 71,000 Cybertrucks have been sold since the first deliveries began in November 2023.
Many of those sales came from inside Musk’s ecosystem of other businesses. For instance, SpaceX (now known as SpaceXSI), which he also leads and is the largest shareholder of, purchased 
$131 million worth of Cybertrucks
 from Tesla in 2025, according to the company’s S-1 registration statement ahead of its IPO. Another Musk company, xAI, also bought $430 million worth of Tesla Megapacks for its data centers, before it merged with SpaceX.
But sales of the Cybertruck, which lagged for months, could see a final bump now that it’s going out of production. Still, even a scarcity boost is unlikely to push the truck’s demand to the lofty projections from several years ago.

===== https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/tesla-will-stop-making-the-cybertruck-in-2027/
(status 200)
Tesla will stop making the Cybertruck next year, according to CEO Elon Musk, who 
said
 on X that the company will use the factory space to build more two-seater Cybercab robotaxis. 
The decision to wind down the futuristic, steel-clad EV comes after just a few short years of underwhelming sales. When Musk handed over the first few Cybertrucks in late 2023, he predicted Tesla would sell around 250,000 per year. It was supposed to be such a cornerstone of Tesla’s future that, before construction began, the company talked about its Texas factory as the “Cybertruck factory.” But Tesla has instead sold just tens of thousands — total — over the last three years.
Tesla is killing off the Cybertruck at a moment when it is trying to become an AI- and robotics-focused company. A big part of that is its robotaxi ambitions. 
The company has spent the last two months putting Cybercabs on the streets in its hometown of Austin, Texas. With no major incidents to date, the company has said it plans to expand the Cybercabs operations to other cities, starting with Dallas later this month. (Tesla also operates robotaxi versions of its Model Y SUVs in Austin, Houston, and Dallas, as well as three cities in Florida.)
This is the third product Tesla has discontinued in order to fuel those greater ambitions. Earlier this year, the company decided to end production of the Model S sedan and Model X SUV and instead use the factory space dedicated to those vehicles to make Optimus humanoid robots.
First announced in 2019, the Cybertruck was a product that Musk had long obsessed over.
“Probably my personal favorite for the next product is the pickup truck, and we are going to just do an amazing pickup truck,” he said on one of Tesla’s earnings calls that year.
Musk promised the Cybertruck would be radical, inspired by the designs of cyberpunk sci-fi stories like “Blade Runner.” While that certainly bore out in the design, the company overpromised on the basics of the Cybertruck from the jump. During the now-infamous reveal event in 2019, when head designer Franz von Holzhausen cracked the Cybertruck’s windows with steel balls, Tesla said the EV was supposed to start at under $40,000 and have up to 500 miles of range. It achieved neither of those figures.
Instead, the truck was delayed for two years, in part because of the COVID-19 pandemic. When it finally did hit the market in late 2023, Tesla handed over the first few Cybertrucks to customers in a 
short, somewhat odd for Tesla event
 (by the company’s own standards, at least), which saw a poorly-lit Musk standing in a truck bed promising the truck was “the finest in apocalyptic equipment” fit for “late civilization vibes.” The base price had inflated to around $60,000, and the top-end range had shrunk by over 100 miles.
The truck has struggled on the market ever since. The polarizing design never seemed to sit well with buyers, especially at a higher-than-expected price. Then, during its first full year of sales in 2024, Musk became a major financial backer of Donald Trump, which turned the instantly recognizable Cybertruck into a target for protesters. That continued into 2025 as Musk led the so-called Department of Government Efficiency, which fired thousands of federal workers.

“We will try to make as many as possible before shutting down the line in about 8 months, but will have to return deposits for a lot of people,” Musk wrote.
He did not say how many customers would get refunds, or how much money was involved.
The post follows Musk’s 
announcement on Friday
 that Tesla will start winding down Cybertruck production at its Texas factory around the middle of 2027. The move will free up space to build more Cybercabs, Tesla’s robotaxi.
Musk has also said Tesla will add an extra production shift to fill outstanding Cybertruck orders before the line is taken apart, Reuters reported.
The factory near Austin is the only known Tesla plant that builds the pickup. It is not clear whether the truck will be made anywhere else.
The Cybercab is a two-seat car with no steering wheel or pedals, and the company began building it in Texas earlier this year and started offering paid rides in Austin in September.

Sales have fallen far short of that. US sales fell 48% to 20,237 last year, down from 38,965 in 2024, according to Kelley Blue Book. Sales in the first nine months of this year fell a further 28%, the Associated Press reported, citing the same data.
In response to a user on X, Musk called the Cybertruck the “best vehicle Tesla has ever made” and the wind-down the “end of an era”.
The Cybercab plays a central role in Musk’s plan to turn Tesla into an AI and robotics company. Tesla lists an installed capacity of more than 125,000 Cybercabs a year at the Texas plant.
