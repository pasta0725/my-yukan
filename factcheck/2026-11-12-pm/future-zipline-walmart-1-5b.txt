Zipline
 has raised $1.5 billion and ramped up its partnership with Walmart, a pair of deals that have raised the autonomous drone delivery and logistics startup’s profile and valuation.
Zipline and Walmart reached a new multi-year agreement to add 300 drone delivery sites across the United States. The two companies have had a drone delivery agreement since 2021, but this deal is a significant jump from the 21 active drone delivery sites they have together today.
On the fundraising side, Zipline’s $1.5 billion raise was led by Paradigm, WCM Investment Management, and Layer Global, with participation from Valor Equity Partners, Fidelity Management & Research Company, Uber, Tiger Global, J.P. Morgan, ARK Invest, and Salesforce Ventures. Zipline is now valued at $21.5 billion.
The company, which has developed a drone delivery ecosystem that includes aircraft, launch and landing systems, and logistics software,  said it plans to use the funds to expand its U.S. manufacturing facilities and hire more workers to build out a nationwide drone delivery network. 
Founded in 2014, Zipline got its start in Africa, where it used its autonomous drones to deliver blood in Rwanda, and later expanded to Ghana and Nigeria. The California-based company began its first operations in the U.S. in 2020, ferrying medical equipment and supplies to Novant Health in North Carolina. The following year, it struck a deal with Walmart.
But it was in 2022 that Zipline’s business really started to take off in the United States. Zipline received its 
Part 135 air carrier certification
 from the Federal Aviation Administration that year, a critical regulatory hurdle that authorized the company to complete long-range commercial drone deliveries. It has locked in a number of partnerships since then, including with 
Uber earlier this year
.  
Zipline now uses its drones to deliver food, retail, agricultural, and health products in five African countries, several cities in the United States, and Japan. Zipline’s home delivery service uses Zipline’s Platform 2 drones, which are designed to carry up to eight pounds and travel to customers within a 10-mile radius. Its larger Platform 1 drones are used for long-range deliveries for enterprise, business, and government that can cover 120 miles round trip. 
The company said Thursday that its share of delivery revenue grew from 10.8% to 34.9% between March and October of this year. This latest funding aims to push that metric even higher.

That fandom carried over to Cybertruck reservations, which skyrocketed to 1 million, 
Musk

And now the Cybertruck is headed to the scrap pile. Tesla will stop making the Cybertruck next year, Musk said Friday.
Was the vehicle too edgy? Maybe, but this was always going to be a niche market. I believe more practical reasons dampened sales. Musk promised the Cybertruck would have a 500-mile range and a price tag under $40,000. It achieved neither, senior reporter Sean O’Kane

 Zipline Raises $1.5 Billion to Build Nationwide Drone Delivery Network
Zipline announces a $1.5 billion raise to build a nationwide U.S. drone delivery network, expand manufacturing and ramp hiring.
South San Francisco — November 5, 2026
 — Zipline today announced a $1.5 billion raise at a $21.5 billion post-money valuation, led by Paradigm, WCM Investment Management, and Layer Global with participation from Valor Equity Partners, Fidelity Management & Research Company, Uber, Tiger Global, J.P. Morgan, ARK Invest, and Salesforce Ventures.
Zipline will use the capital to expand its U.S. manufacturing facilities and ramp hiring as it builds a nationwide drone delivery network. Demand for delivery has never been higher, but ground delivery is constrained by traffic, aging infrastructure, and rising costs. Roads can’t keep up. The future of delivery is in the sky.
Walmart and Zipline are now working together to launch 300 additional sites as part of a new national multi-year agreement with potential to grow further. Uber is targeting one million deliveries a day with Zipline by 2029. Enterprise leaders and small businesses alike are investing in drone delivery to unlock growth.
Hundreds of businesses are now growing their revenue through Zipline orders. Dozens of new businesses are joining Zipline’s network weekly. Existing partners are expanding their relationships with Zipline to include hundreds or thousands of new locations. 
A cohort of Zipline's longest-tenured restaurants shared their results to date: between March and October, Zipline’s share of their delivery revenue grew from 10.8% to 34.9%. Zipline orders were incremental and did not reduce demand from other platforms. The restaurants saw a 26% increase in delivery GMV, with 80% of the increase coming from Zipline orders.
Drone delivery is now a routine convenience in Zipline’s metros, with more than one million deliveries made within the Dallas-Ft. Worth metroplex alone this year. Customers choose Zipline because it's fast, convenient, and quiet. In October, the median Zipline order was delivered 19 minutes after checkout, compared to 30+ minutes for traditional delivery. Restaurants with quick prep times are even faster: the median bakery order arrived 10 minutes after checkout. 
By using the sky for automated deliveries, Zipline expands the number of customers businesses can reach. Today, only 5% of U.S. restaurant orders are for delivery. In practice, those orders are constrained to customers within a couple miles of the store, as cost and travel times become less attractive for longer ranges. Zipline delivers without causing traffic, helping businesses serve customers. Companies like Wonder are seizing this opportunity by building new storefronts and kitchens that are optimized specifically for Zipline.
“Zipline is now one of the largest autonomous robotics companies in the world -- operating with scale, safety, and a magical user experience for consumers and merchants,” said Matt Huang, Co-Founder and Managing Partner of Paradigm. “It's no wonder that some of the largest merchants in the world are partnering with Zipline to use their delivery network.”
"Zipline has built a team and culture that has been developing cutting-edge drone delivery capabilities for over 10 years, and the market is now recognizing how transformational its technology is. Within e-commerce and food delivery, Zipline represents a generational improvement in user experience and delight,” said Alan Tu, Portfolio Manager at WCM Investment Management
“Zipline has already brought the future of delivery to the communities it serves. Now, the opportunity is to reach millions more,” said Anton Levy, Founder and Managing Partner of Layer Global. “With partners like Uber and Walmart and a repeatable model for entering new markets, Zipline is well positioned to make drone delivery a mainstream part of everyday life.”
As Zipline scales, it keeps improving its service for customers and communities. The company recently rolled out 
sound-optimized dynamic flying
, an industry-first acoustics upgrade that makes flights quieter, faster, and more energy efficient. It also introduced its next-generation aircraft, designed to fly higher and quieter while carrying heavier orders over longer distances.
Ten years after its first flights, Zipline is the world’s largest and most experienced autonomous delivery service, with 150 million commercial autonomous miles flown without a safety incident. Zipline’s long-range medical deliveries help save more than 14,000 lives each year across multiple continents. In Côte d’Ivoire, Zipline is now building a nationwide network expected to serve more than 2,200 health facilities and 12 million people.
Drone delivery will give the earth back to humans, while saving time, saving money, and saving lives. Zipline is building a nationwide network to make that future real.

Nov 5 (Reuters) - Zipline said on Thursday it has raised $1.5 billion in its latest funding round, valuing the US drone delivery startup at $21.5 billion post-money, nearly tripling its valuation in about 10 months.
              The funding comes as competition in drone delivery intensifies, with companies expanding their delivery networks, although regulatory approvals and community concerns over noise and safety remain barriers to broader adoption.
              The round was led by Paradigm, WCM Investment Management, and Layer Global and drew participation from Uber, Tiger Global and J.P. Morgan, among others.
              The company plans to use the funding to expand its US manufacturing capacity, hire more workers and develop a nationwide drone delivery network.
              Chief Executive Keller Cliffton told Reuters in an interview that Zipline had to develop much of its technology and manufacturing operation itself because there was no established drone-delivery supply chain to draw from.
              "None of our customers care about drones. They care about teleportation, and so that meant that we had to do everything necessary on the back end to build this."
              Cliffton said regulatory restrictions initially kept Zipline from launching commercially in its home market, prompting the company to begin operations overseas.?
              "For the first 10 years from when we built the company, operating beyond visual line of sight was illegal in the US," he added. "It's the reason that we launched outside the US for the first eight years of our commercial operations."
              Zipline, which operates across four continents, was valued at $7.6 billion when it raised $600 million earlier this year.
              In August, the company partnered with Uber to bring autonomous drone delivery to Uber Eats customers, with the ride-hailing giant also taking a strategic stake at an undisclosed valuation.
              Amazon, meanwhile, also plans to expand its drone-delivery service from 11 locations to about 500 US locales by year-end.
