AWSで「ChatGPT Enterprise」を購入可能に　AWSアカウントで一括管理するメリットは
OpenAIは「ChatGPT Enterprise」をAWS Marketplaceで提供開始した。企業は既存のAWSアカウントや契約枠でChatGPT Enterpriseを購入・管理できる。企業のAI調達はどう変わるのか。

Googleで優先するソースとして追加
　OpenAIは2026年11月9日、「ChatGPT Enterprise」をAWSのソフトウェア流通サービス「AWS Marketplace」で提供開始した。企業は既存のAWSの購買枠や請求システムを利用して同製品の契約や管理ができるようになる。
ChatGPT Enterpriseの支出をAWS利用枠に充当可能
　企業が新たなAIツールを全社導入する際、新規ベンダーとの契約手続きや購買審査、請求管理の追加が情報システム部門や購買部門の負担になりやすい。
　こうした課題に対応するのが、AWS MarketplaceでのChatGPT Enterpriseの提供だ。AWSを利用している企業は、既存のAWSアカウントを通じて同製品の検索、購入、管理を完結できる。対象となる支出をAWSの既存の利用額コミットメントに充当できるため、予算消化や支出管理を一本化できる。
ログイン または 会員登録するとすべてご覧いただけます。
会員登録・ログインして続きを読む
　OpenAIによると「ChatGPT」は毎週12億人以上に利用されている。ChatGPT Enterpriseは法人向けプランで、ChatGPTの他に「ChatGPT Work」「Codex」「dots」といった機能群を含む。AWS Marketplace経由で契約した場合でも、OpenAIから直接購入する場合と同じAIモデルや機能を利用でき、新機能も同時にリリースされる。
　これらのOpenAI製品は、AWSの生成AIサービス「Amazon Bedrock」で利用可能なOpenAIモデルを補完する位置付けだ。システム開発部門はAmazon Bedrock上のOpenAIモデルを使って独自のAIアプリケーションやAIエージェントを構築でき、業務部門は日々の業務で「ChatGPT Enterprise」を使うといった使い分けができる。企業は自社のワークロードやガバナンス要件に合わせて導入形態を選択できる。
　AWSのマット・ヤンチシン（Matt Yanchyshyn）氏（AWS Marketplaceおよびパートナーサービス担当バイスプレジデント）は「企業は調達手続きを複雑化することなくAIを導入したいと考えている」と指摘した。AWSアカウントを通じた購入やソフトウェア支出の一元管理によって、評価から導入までの期間を短縮できると説明した。
　OpenAIのクリス・グルース（Chris Grusz）氏（クラウドパートナーシップ担当マネージングディレクター）は、AWSとOpenAIの協業によって、顧客は信頼しているクラウドプロバイダー経由でChatGPTを導入できるようになったと評価した。「AWSと共に導入手続きを簡素化して、より多くの組織が大規模なAI活用の可能性を引き出せるよう支援する」とコメントしている。
　ユーザー企業であるSophosのCTO（最高技術責任者）ジョン・ピーターソン（John Peterson）氏は「単一の窓口で購入でき、支出を既存のAWS利用額コミットメントに充当できるようになったことで、企業のAI導入を遅らせがちな障壁が取り除かれる」と述べた。調達に費やしていた時間を顧客価値の創出に振り向けられるとしている。

===== https://ppc.land/aws-customers-can-now-buy-chatgpt-enterprise-against-cloud-commitments/
(status 200)
OpenAI today made ChatGPT Enterprise available through AWS Marketplace, allowing companies to buy the workplace version of ChatGPT through their existing Amazon Web Services accounts and to count eligible spending toward the cloud commitments they have already signed with Amazon. The listing is sold by OpenAI, billed by AWS and carries no public price. Buyers must request a private offer.
In Short
OpenAI's business version of ChatGPT can now be bought through Amazon's cloud store, with the charge landing on a company's AWS bill. That matters for organisations that have promised to spend a fixed sum with AWS, because eligible ChatGPT Enterprise spending can now count toward that promise. There is no published price, every deal is a private quote, and Anthropic's Claude Enterprise is already sold the same way on the same marketplace.
What was announced
The announcement on OpenAI's site, tagged under ChatGPT and Partnerships, is short: about a minute and twenty seconds in its audio version. Its central claim is that the AWS listing gives organisations "a simpler way to discover, buy, and manage ChatGPT Enterprise using their existing AWS purchasing and billing relationship and to apply eligible spend toward their AWS commitments."
According to OpenAI, customers buying through AWS "get the same models, features, and functionality available directly from OpenAI, with new features released at the same time." The company also said eligible customers can access two vertical versions of the product, ChatGPT for Healthcare and ChatGPT for Financial Services, through the same marketplace. Eligibility is confirmed by an OpenAI or AWS account team. Neither the post nor the listing sets out what the criteria are.
The post opens its case with scale. More than 1.2 billion people use ChatGPT each week, according to OpenAI. The figure is not new to this announcement: OpenAI used the same number on October 21, when it 
extended ChatGPT advertising to 26 more countries, bringing the total to 89
, and the enterprise post links back to that advertising announcement as its source. In February, 
OpenAI put ChatGPT at 900 million weekly users and 9 million paying business users
. No updated count of business users or Enterprise seats appears in today's material.
The only quotation in the announcement comes from Amazon's side. Matt Yanchyshyn, Vice President, AWS Marketplace and Partner Services, said: "Enterprises want to put AI to work across their teams without adding new layers of procurement. With ChatGPT Enterprise in AWS Marketplace, customers can buy it through their AWS account, govern it alongside the rest of their software spend, and move from evaluation to rollout faster. Working with OpenAI, we're bringing one of the world's most widely used AI products to AWS customers."
Four products in one listing
The AWS Marketplace page describes ChatGPT Enterprise on AWS as bringing "together Chat, ChatGPT Work, Codex, and dots in one enterprise offering." It is listed under three categories - Software Development, Research and Generative AI - and delivered as 
SaaS
, meaning the software runs as a hosted service rather than inside the customer's own AWS environment. The page also carries AWS's "Deployed on AWS" label, set to yes.
Chat
 is the conversational product most people know. The listing describes it as a place to "explore ideas, ask questions, write, and work through problems in conversation."
ChatGPT Work
 is the newest of the four. According to the listing, it takes on "research, analysis, and projects that span multiple steps," drawing on connected apps and files to produce "documents, presentations, spreadsheets, and reports," while users review progress, steer the work and approve important actions. OpenAI introduced it on July 9, when 
it began to sunset its standalone Atlas browser and folded those functions into the new ChatGPT Work agent
. Enterprise customers were in the first rollout group, alongside Pro and Edu, and the Codex desktop app was merged into the same unified ChatGPT desktop application.
Codex
 is OpenAI's coding agent. The listing says it can "understand codebases, develop features, fix bugs, run tests, and review changes." Codex had 1.6 million weekly users in February, according to the same company update that produced the 900 million ChatGPT figure. By April 28, OpenAI was saying more than 4 million people used Codex every week, 
according to its post on bringing its models to AWS
.
Dots
 is the least familiar name. The listing describes dots as agents that are always on, learn a user's preferences and follow through on ongoing responsibilities. They can, according to the page, "track updates, keep documents and analyses current, and move projects forward between conversations," returning results and decisions that need a person's attention. The listing gives no limits on how many dots an organisation can run, what they cost, or how their activity is metered.
How the money moves
The financial mechanism is the substance of the announcement. The listing's highlights say purchasing and billing are managed through AWS Marketplace, with spending counted toward the customer's AWS EDP or PPA commitment. Further down, the wording is more careful: eligible consumption counts toward those commitments "subject to" the customer's agreement with AWS.
Neither abbreviation is spelled out on the page. EDP refers to AWS's Enterprise Discount Program and PPA to a Private Pricing Agreement - contracts under which a company commits to a minimum level of AWS spending over several years in exchange for discounts. Large organisations sign these with the 
hyperscaler
 and then face a practical problem: the commitment has to be consumed. Marketplace purchases that count toward the commitment allow software bought from third parties to fill that quota. How much of any given commitment can be spent this way is set contract by contract, which is what "subject to" signals.
Price is the gap. Under "Pricing," the listing shows only "Custom pricing options" and states that pricing depends on a buyer's requirements and eligibility. A private offer has to be requested to receive a quote. No per-seat price, minimum seat count, contract length or usage rate is published. The page also displays AWS's standard description of SaaS delivery, under which buyers "pay recurring monthly usage fees through" their AWS bill. That same generic text says AWS "handles deployment and infrastructure management," whereas the product description says buyers keep "the product experience managed by OpenAI." AWS's content disclaimer on the page states that vendors are responsible for their product descriptions and that AWS does not warrant them.
How usage is measured inside ChatGPT Enterprise matters for how those monthly fees add up. On June 18, OpenAI introduced 
usage analytics and updated spend controls for enterprises
, bringing ChatGPT and Codex credit usage into a single admin view and letting administrators set default limits for a workspace, specific limits for groups and individual overrides, according to OpenAI. Today's materials do not explain how credit consumption inside ChatGPT Enterprise translates into the charges that appear on an AWS invoice.
Neither company disclosed what share of each sale AWS retains. The structure is not new, though. Anthropic's IPO filing gave an outside view of how cloud marketplaces handle the money: 
the marketplaces bill end customers, collect payment and pass the proceeds on net of platform and other fees
, with Anthropic booking the marketplace fees as an expense rather than deducting them from revenue.
Two other features on the page belong to AWS Marketplace rather than to OpenAI. A financing banner says the marketplace accepts line-of-credit payments through the PNC Vendor Finance program, available to select AWS customers in the US, excluding Nevada, North Carolina, North Dakota, Tennessee and Vermont. And the page offers AWS's "agent mode," an AI assistant that can evaluate the listing or help a buyer draft requirements. At the time of the announcement, the listing showed no customer ratings or reviews.
Data, processors and administrative control
The listing makes four specific security and privacy statements. Business data is encrypted at rest and in transit. It is not used to train OpenAI's models by default. Administrators can manage user access, permissions and connected tools. And users decide what agents can reach and which actions require their approval.
On data protection roles, the listing casts OpenAI as the 
data processor
, the party that handles personal data on behalf of the customer, which remains the controller. "OpenAI is the data processor for ChatGPT Enterprise and engages subprocessors, including AWS, to support the service," the listing states. A subprocessor is a further company the processor engages. Naming AWS as a subprocessor, together with the "Deployed on AWS" label, indicates that AWS infrastructure is involved in running the service for these customers. The listing does not say whether all ChatGPT Enterprise data for AWS-billed customers stays on AWS, nor in which regions it is processed.
The administrative controls echo earlier changes. When OpenAI added a batch of 12 third-party connectors to ChatGPT Enterprise on November 24, 2025, 
connectors for enterprise workspaces were off by default and required an administrator to enable them
. The AWS listing's language on connected tools and agent permissions follows the same model, with administrators deciding what the agents can touch.
Healthcare is where the two AI listings on the page read differently. The ChatGPT Enterprise listing makes no statement about 
HIPAA
, the US health privacy law; OpenAI's post points healthcare buyers instead to ChatGPT for Healthcare, for eligible customers. Anthropic's Claude Enterprise, shown on the same page as a similar product, describes itself as HIPAA-eligible "with a BAA available," a reference to the business associate agreement US healthcare organisations sign with vendors that handle patient data.
Bedrock for developers, Marketplace for staff
OpenAI positions the listing as the second half of a two-part arrangement with Amazon. "These OpenAI products complement the OpenAI models available in Amazon Bedrock," the post says. "Developers can build AI applications and agents with OpenAI models in Amazon Bedrock, while teams across the business use ChatGPT Enterprise in their daily work."
The Bedrock half has been built over six months. On April 28, OpenAI launched its models, Codex and Amazon Bedrock Managed Agents on AWS in limited preview. On June 1, 
AWS made GPT-5.5, GPT-5.4 and Codex generally available on Bedrock
, with pricing that matches OpenAI's first-party rates, according to AWS. On August 11, OpenAI made its Daybreak cybersecurity models available through Bedrock for enrolled customers. And when Amazon reported its third-quarter results on October 29, 
its release named GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6 Sol, GPT-6 Luna and GPT-6.1 Sol among the OpenAI models on Bedrock
, along with Bedrock Managed Agents "powered by OpenAI." The same results showed AWS revenue of $45.98 billion for the quarter, up 39.3%.
The arrangement rests on changes to OpenAI's terms with Microsoft. The 
October 2025 restructuring of the Microsoft-OpenAI partnership kept Azure API exclusivity but allowed OpenAI's non-API products to be served on any cloud provider
, a category ChatGPT Enterprise falls into. The partners rewrote the arrangement again in April: 
the amendment announced on April 27, 2026 lets OpenAI serve all of its products to customers on any cloud
, while Azure remains the primary cloud partner and the place OpenAI products ship first, unless Microsoft cannot or chooses not to support the capability. OpenAI's post today compares the AWS version only with buying directly from OpenAI and does not address timing relative to Azure.
Microsoft remains the larger financial counterparty. In its September quarter, Microsoft 
recorded $10.0 billion of revenue from commercial arrangements with OpenAI and held an approximate 24% interest on an as-converted basis
.
Same shelf as Claude
The listing's "Similar products" panel makes the competitive picture explicit. It shows three products: Perplexity Enterprise Pro, Perplexity's API platform, and Anthropic's Claude Enterprise. The Claude Enterprise description says it gives "every employee access to Claude Chat, Claude Code, and Cowork," lists Claude Security and Claude Design as recently added capabilities, and adds that for AWS customers, spend draws down directly from an EDP or PPA commitment. OpenAI's offer, on that comparison, arrives with the same commitment mechanism its main rival already uses.
For Anthropic, the marketplaces are not a side channel. Its amended IPO filing put sales through the cloud marketplaces of Amazon, Google and Microsoft at 
about 11% of revenue in 2023, 32% in 2024, 47% in 2025 and 44% in the nine months to September 30, 2026
. For the first half of 2026, 
the share was 48%
. OpenAI has not disclosed what share of its business revenue comes through cloud marketplaces, and today's announcement does not say whether ChatGPT Enterprise is also listed on the marketplaces of Microsoft or Google.
AWS's position is unusual. It now sells the workplace products of both leading model developers through the same storefront, while Amazon is also one of Anthropic's investors and compute suppliers. Each sale through the marketplace generates a fee for AWS regardless of which model wins the seat.
Why it matters for marketing teams
For marketing departments, agencies and publishers, the announcement is less about new capability than about who buys the tool and from which budget. ChatGPT Enterprise purchased through AWS sits on the same invoice as cloud hosting and is governed, in Yanchyshyn's words, "alongside the rest of their software spend." That ties the purchase to whoever manages the AWS relationship, rather than to the department that uses the seats.
That shift intersects with a known problem. Telemetry from LayerX, covered in May, found that 
nearly half of enterprise AI use runs through personal accounts
, the pattern usually described as 
shadow AI
. AWS's pitch today is about removing layers of procurement. Neither OpenAI nor AWS offered evidence on whether marketplace availability changes adoption or moves usage off personal accounts.
There is also an advertising angle, even though the product itself carries no ads. When OpenAI expanded ChatGPT advertising in October, 
ads were shown only to users on the Free and Go tiers
. Enterprise workspaces bought through AWS fall outside that audience. Yet the 1.2 billion weekly user figure OpenAI uses to sell enterprise seats is the same figure it used to announce its advertising expansion. The two businesses draw on the same headline reach while serving separate sets of users.
The products in the bundle also map onto marketing work. OpenAI's own usage data, published in October, found that 
decision-making and problem-solving accounted for 21.6% of US work-related ChatGPT requests
. ChatGPT Work produces documents, presentations, spreadsheets and reports, Codex reaches the engineering teams that build tracking, feeds and internal tools, and dots are pitched at recurring monitoring work that continues between conversations. Whether those three products perform at enterprise scale is not something a marketplace listing can show, and the page currently carries no customer reviews.
What the announcement does not say
Several basic facts are missing from both documents. There is no price or pricing model, no disclosure of AWS's fee, and no information on which countries or AWS regions are eligible. Eligibility criteria for ChatGPT Enterprise, ChatGPT for Healthcare and ChatGPT for Financial Services are left to account teams. There is no customer count for ChatGPT Enterprise, no figure for how many AWS customers are expected to buy this way, and no explanation of how credit-based usage controls interact with monthly AWS billing.
The data residency picture is partial. The listing names AWS as a subprocessor and marks the product as deployed on AWS, but does not say whether data for AWS-billed customers is processed differently from data for customers who buy directly from OpenAI. And the post's promise of features "released at the same time" covers the comparison with OpenAI's direct sales, not with Azure, where the amended Microsoft terms still give OpenAI products a first-to-ship position by default.

ChatGPT Enterprise can now be bought on an AWS bill
OpenAI listed ChatGPT Enterprise in AWS Marketplace, so companies with Amazon cloud contracts can pay for it through the account they already hold.
AI Insiders Staff
EDITED BY  
ALESSANDRO BENIGNI
PUBLISHED  
 NOV 11, 2026 
1 MIN READ
Follow on Google
16 HR AGO
OpenAI has put ChatGPT Enterprise on AWS Marketplace, which means a company can buy it through its existing Amazon cloud account instead of signing a separate contract with OpenAI. In its announcement, OpenAI says eligible spend can count toward a customer’s AWS commitments, the minimum amounts big buyers have already agreed to pay Amazon.
For a procurement team, the change is in the paperwork. The product stays the same: OpenAI says buyers on AWS receive identical models and features to those who go direct, with new releases landing together. Eligible customers can also add its healthcare and financial-services editions through the listing.
The listing sits beside OpenAI’s models in Amazon Bedrock, which developers use to build their own applications and agents. Marketplace is the route for staff who just want ChatGPT at work. OpenAI does not say how much of the spend will count toward commitments, and it tells buyers to ask their account team at either company.
Companies with unspent AWS commitments now have a way to move part of that budget into ChatGPT seats, if the account team agrees the purchase qualifies.
Announced by OpenAI on its website; the source page carries no publication date.
