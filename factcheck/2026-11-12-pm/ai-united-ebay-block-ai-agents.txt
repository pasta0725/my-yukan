United says it 
won’t let personal AI assistants book tickets
 on the airline, citing both privacy and regulatory concerns.
The company says it hasn’t yet seen many people try to use AI agents to book their flights, 
The Wall Street Journal
 reports
, in line with observations from other businesses like 
Doordash
. But United is concerned that agents could expose customers’ information or make purchases they didn’t intend, and potentially skirt federal rules about the kind of information airlines need to share with customers — like having them attest that their bags don’t have explosives.

While agents can still show users United flights, they won’t be able to actually complete the transaction on their behalf. The decision, and others like it, could limit the potential usefulness of newer personal AI assistants like 
Meta’s Muse
 and 
OpenAI’s Dots
. “To be honest, I don’t want to create another middleman,” United CEO Scott Kirby said at an event on Tuesday, according to 
the Journal
.
Across sectors, businesses are grappling with whether to let AI agents transact on their sites, and United is not alone in blocking access for unauthorized agents. eBay 
appeared to block agents
 from browsing the site, and 
several major banks
 have also placed restrictions on them. United suggests it could reconsider its decision based on future advancements. “As third-party AI agents advance, we see the opportunities they present. However, at the same time, allowing them to interact directly with a United account or booking could involve sensitive actions, and we want to make sure we have established protections for travelers first.”

eBay appears to have blocked AI agents, including Meta’s Muse, from browsing on the platform — the latest speed bump in the AI agent race.
A reader tells 
The Verge
 that for several weeks he’s had his Muse agent browse and curate a daily digest of watches for sale on platforms like eBay, including a link to listings, price, shipping details, and relevant notes about condition or style. This weekend, though, Muse was unable to pull any links or details from eBay, with Muse saying it was blocked from the site. 
The Verge
 was able to re-create the experience using other search requests, and in some cases we found that Muse instead directed users to outside sites that mirrored eBay listings.
Shopping is frequently invoked as one area where AI agents can augment or improve the human-driven experience. Technology companies tout the use of agents to book travel, find deals, and even purchase goods and services on behalf of humans. But AI agents are regularly getting 
blocked from accessing retailers’ sites
, calling into question how useful they can really be.
eBay previously 
updated
 its user agreement to ban the use of tools like “buy-for-me agents” and “LLM-driven bots” that place orders without human review. Until recently, though, agents like Muse were able to browse the site and pull listings for users.
AI is becoming embedded throughout the platform for the discovery, personalization, and selling process, eBay spokesperson Sybille St. Arromand told 
The Verge
 in an email. St. Arromand declined to directly answer whether eBay recently imposed new limits on AI agents.
“As it pertains to third-parties, we take a selective approach to how they access and interact with our marketplace. We have restricted agent access in certain cases, including unauthorized scraping and training on our data,” St. Arromand said. “We have ongoing discussions with potential partners and are evaluating ways third-party agents could help new customers discover eBay’s unmatched inventory, and our focus is on preserving the trusted end-to-end experience that buyers and sellers expect from eBay.”
Meta declined to comment.
Retailers and service providers who’ve enthusiastically added AI features to their platforms have shown varying levels of comfort with third-party agents accessing their sites. On one end of the spectrum is Amazon, which is blocking external agents entirely from browsing or shopping. Some retailers like Target have restrictions in place that allow agents to successfully browse in some instances but get blocked in others. Shopify, meanwhile, has opened its catalog of a billion listings so that shoppers 
can set up their agents to purchase items on their behalf
.
Related
Websites are closing their doors to AI agents
AI agents are ready to shop. Some retailers are wary
Throwing agents into the e-commerce mix has the potential to upend how people shop online, partly because retail sites are largely assuming they’re selling to humans, with human limitations, and not swarms of bots. For example, 
The Verge
 discovered in October that someone could use Muse to effectively hold product listings hostage on The RealReal, a secondhand designer consignment site, by having an agent continually add the product to the user’s cart. The RealReal 
updated its bot system
 after 
The Verge
’s reporting.
But as retailers tweak their sites in real time to handle bots, it remains unclear how widespread agentic commerce is. In fact, there are signs that most people are not using AI this way. Uber 
said
 it fulfilled only four rides via third-party agents in Q3 and only around 500 Uber Eats orders during the week of Halloween. DoorDash similarly 
said
 it fulfilled only “several hundred” orders placed via third-party agents. It reported 993 million total orders last quarter.

United Restricts Access for Third-Party AI Agents
November 11, 2026
Protecting our customers’ data and accounts is a priority for United.
That’s why we designed our industry-leading, award-winning United app to securely empower customers to manage their own travel experience – everything from booking a trip and choosing seats to rescheduling a flight when the weather is bad and so much more.  
On the other hand, sharing account credentials with personal, third-party, AI assistants can expose sensitive information, and actions taken by these tools may result in unintended purchases, account changes, or use of MileagePlus miles. Plus, these automated AI actions have the potential to violate FAA and DOT regulations - as an example, flight check-in requires human acknowledgment that bags do not contain banned substances like explosives.
As a result, United is taking steps to restrict access - directly within our digital platforms and indirectly via travel agencies and aggregators - to prohibit use by unauthorized, third-party personal AI agents or assistants. This work includes updates to our booking, ticketing and distribution policies.
Corporate leaders in the banking and retail industries have taken similar actions regarding AI agents and we encourage others to consider measures that preserve the data and security of their customers and strengthen safety.
We will also update our 
Digital Trust Center
 so customers can better understand what these new restrictions mean for them, how to protect their accounts, and where to get help if an agent takes an unintended action.
As personal AI assistants evolve, United will continue to evaluate how these tools interact with our services, with a focus on protecting our customers.

===== https://www.cbsnews.com/news/united-airlines-bans-ai-agents-booking-flights/
(status 200)
United Airlines said on Wednesday that it is banning artificial intelligence agents from making flight reservations on behalf of travelers, citing 
security risks
 and the possibility that such actions could violate federal rules.
Many consumers are turning to AI agents such as 
Meta's Muse 
and 
OpenAI's dots 
to complete tasks ranging from researching products and tracking down deals to making purchases.
To make an airline booking, an AI agent requires access to a customer's personal information, including credentials like payment information. United has a problem with that, saying that "sharing account credentials with personal, third-party, AI assistants can expose sensitive information, and actions taken by these tools may result in unintended purchases, account changes, or use of MileagePlus miles."
The carrier also said that using an AI agent to, for example, check in for a flight could violate federal regulations, including rules requiring travelers to verify in person that their luggage does not contain banned substances.
"As a result, United is taking steps to restrict access — directly within our digital platforms and indirectly via travel agencies and aggregators — to prohibit use by unauthorized, third-party personal AI agents or assistants," said the airline, which is updating its booking and ticketing policies to reflect the new rule.
United's new policy suggests the company wants to retain control over the customer booking process, said Henry Harteveldt, an aviation analyst at Atmosphere Research Group. 
"United is saying they don't want to add yet another, and potentially even more powerful, intermediary than they already face in terms of Google search, price-comparison metasearch sites and online travel agencies," he told CBS News. "Flight searches cost airlines money, and undoubtedly United wants to avoid the expense associated with the surge of AI bot flight searches."
Other airlines are taking a different tack to the emergence of AI agents. For example, Southwest has made its flight inventory viewable to ChatGPT users, who can access the airline's website through the AI app. 
United fares will continue to be bookable through flight aggregators such as Kayak, Hopper and Google Flights, Harteveldt said, adding that "consumers shouldn't find it any more difficult to find good fares on the airline."
"But by blocking the AI bots, United won't make it any easier to find its fares, either," he said.
