Copilotを「仕事の新しいOS」に　MicrosoftがAI・セキュリティ強化
浅井 淳志
2026年11月18日 15:00
Copilotによるローカル診断機能
 Microsoftは17日(米国時間)、開発者向けイベント「Microsoft Ignite 2026」において、「Copilot」の機能拡充やWindowsのセキュリティ強化、AIエージェント向けの新たな実行環境などを発表した。 
 サティア・ナデラCEOは同イベントの基調講演において、Copilotを「仕事の新しいOS(The new OS for work)」と位置付け、AIとの対話やエージェントへの業務の委任、アプリ開発、自律的なタスクの実行などをCopilotに統合する方針を示した。 
CopilotがPC内のファイル操作や診断に対応
 Copilotでは、「Copilot+ PC」向けに、PC内のファイルやアプリなどを参照する「ローカルコンテキスト」、PCを操作する「ローカルアクション」、端末上でAIを動作させる「ローカルモデル」の3機能を導入する。 
 ローカルコンテキストでは、ユーザーの許可に基づき、開いているファイルや使用中のアプリ、PCの状態などをCopilotが参照できるようになる。ローカルアクションでは、自然言語によるファイルの検索・整理・移動・コピー・名前変更などに対応する。 
 また、PCを読み取り専用で検査し、Bluetoothの接続不良やネットワークの速度低下などの原因を調べるローカル診断機能も提供する。AIについても、処理内容に応じてローカルとクラウドを使い分ける仕組みを導入する。 
Windowsのセキュリティ強化　障害発生時や特権昇格に伴うリスクを軽減
 セキュリティ分野では、「Windows Endpoint Security Platform」と「Windows Administrator Protection(管理者保護)」の一般提供を開始した。Windows Endpoint Security Platformは、従来カーネルモードで動作していたセキュリティ保護機能をユーザーモードに移行できるようにするもので、障害発生時にデバイス全体に影響を与えるリスクを軽減する。 
 Windows Administrator Protectionでは、管理者権限を常時与えるのではなく、必要なタスクの実行時に限ってWindows Helloによる本人確認を行ない、一時的に権限を付与することで、特権昇格に伴うリスクからデバイスを保護する。 
 また、セキュアブートや仮想化ベースのセキュリティ、メモリ整合性などのセキュリティ状態をまとめて確認できる「Windows Baseline Protection」もプレビュー提供されている。 
AIエージェントの実行環境に新機能
 AIエージェント向けには、安全な実行環境を提供する「Microsoft Execution Containers(MXC)」に新たな封じ込め機能を追加した。MXCは、エージェントごとに隔離された実行環境を用意し、アクセスできるファイルやネットワークを制限する技術。 
 今回、通信対象やアクセス先などの制限を強化した改良版のプロセスコンテナをプレビュー提供するほか、高リスクのワークロード向けにハードウェアによる隔離に対応した小型仮想マシンの開発も進めていることを発表した。 
 また、AIエージェント向けのクラウドPC環境「Windows 365 for Agents」も拡充。「Copilot Credits」に対応するほか、2027年初頭には「Microsoft Foundry」でのプレビュー提供を予定している。 
PCからAzureまでをつなぐAIインフラ構想
 同社は、PC、クラウドPC、データセンターを1つのAIインフラとして統合する方針も示した。クラウドPCの「Windows 365」には、新たなストリーミング技術「Direct Stream」を発表した。 
 接続の安定性や速度、セキュリティなどを強化する技術で、クラウドPCをローカルPCのように使えるようにするという。また、2027年初頭には、NVIDIAのGPUを搭載したクラウドPCのプレビュー提供を予定している。 
 サーバー分野では、Microsoft Foundryをパブリックプレビュー中の「Windows Server 2028」に拡張するとともに、AMDやNVIDIAのAI向けハードウェアへの対応も進める。

A trusted, secure, and more resilient foundation
Critical work happens on Windows, and staying protected while threats evolve has never been more important. That starts with a trusted, secure, and more resilient Windows foundation. We are making investments to harden the platform, make it easier to protect and manage devices, and keep them current.
Historically, some security protections ran in the Windows kernel, where one failure could affect the entire device. In 2024, we started working with our security partners on a more resilient approach. Today, the Windows endpoint security platform is

Application developers can use baseline protection to verify that the devices running their applications meet their security requirements. The baseline will continue to evolve as we raise the bar in security. 
We are proud to be collaborating with partners from across the ecosystem to see how Windows baseline protection helps meet their needs.
We’re also making it easier to protect your devices from the risks that come with elevated privilege. Your Copilot+ PC will soon organise files and diagnose Windows issues locally

For most of us, using a PC still means doing a lot of small jobs ourselves. Finding a document buried in Downloads, moving files into the right folders or figuring out why Bluetooth has stopped working can eat into the day. Microsoft wants Copilot to take on some of that work. At Ignite 2026, the company announced new local capabilities coming to Copilot+ PCs, including the ability to understand what you are working on, carry out simple actions in Windows and help diagnose common PC problems. The important bit is that some of this will happen directly on the device, rather than relying on the cloud for every request. Here’s everything you need to know about the latest development.
Copilot will understand what you're doing on your PC
One of the biggest changes Microsoft is working on is giving Copilot more context about what you are doing on your computer. Think about it. If you have several documents open, are working on a presentation or have a particular application running, you usually have to explain all of that when asking an AI chatbot for help. Microsoft wants to reduce that effort. With local context coming to Copilot+ PCs, Copilot will be able to understand which files you have open, which applications you're using and the current state of your PC. This will work with your permission, so you remain in control of what information Copilot can access.
Also read: 
Microsoft is building dedicated PCs for AI agents, what does it mean?
For instance, imagine you're working on a presentation and need help finding a particular document or understanding something in an open file. Instead of explaining what you're working on and manually sharing the relevant information, Copilot could use the context available on your PC to provide more useful assistance.
You can ask Copilot to organise your files
Microsoft is also bringing local actions to Copilot, which means it won't just answer questions but will be able to perform certain tasks directly in Windows. One example is file management. Microsoft says users will be able to ask Copilot in plain language to find, organise, move, copy or rename files. So, if your Downloads folder is full of documents, screenshots and random files you've forgotten about, you could simply ask Copilot to help organise them.
You could also ask it to find files related to a particular project and bring them together. Instead of manually searching through folders, opening documents and moving them one by one, Copilot will handle the task through a simple instruction. This is a useful addition because file management is one of those everyday PC activities that can get unnecessarily tedious. You don't always remember where you saved something, especially when you're juggling multiple projects.
Again, Microsoft hasn't suggested that Copilot will take over complete control of your files. The company says users will remain in control while Copilot carries out these actions. We'll have to see how well it handles more complicated instructions when the features roll out.
Copilot could also help you figure out what's wrong with your PC
Another interesting addition is local PC diagnostics. If you've ever struggled with a slow internet connection, Bluetooth audio that refuses to work or a PC that suddenly starts behaving strangely, you'll know how frustrating troubleshooting can be. Usually, you end up searching online, going through Windows settings or contacting customer support. Microsoft wants Copilot to make this process simpler.
With local PC diagnostics, you will be able to ask Copilot about a problem, and it can run a read-only scan of your system to look for possible causes. It can then explain what's going wrong in simpler terms, rather than making you deal with technical information that might not mean much to you. For example, if your Bluetooth headphones aren't connecting properly or your network connection is unusually slow, you could ask Copilot to investigate. It will examine relevant system information and help identify the possible cause.
There's an important distinction here, though. Microsoft says Copilot will run a read-only scan and explain the problem. It hasn't promised that the assistant will automatically repair every issue it finds. You may still have to follow the suggested steps yourself.
Not everything will need the cloud
There's another part of Microsoft's announcement that deserves attention. Copilot will increasingly use AI models running directly on your PC, rather than sending every request to the cloud. Microsoft calls this Hybrid Intelligence. The idea is to use the computing power available on your device for tasks that can be handled locally, while turning to cloud-based models when a request needs more computing power. This could make certain AI features more responsive and reduce how much businesses spend on cloud-based AI services. It also means that your PC's hardware will play a bigger role in determining what Copilot can do.
Microsoft hasn't said that every Copilot feature will work offline, so don't expect to disconnect your PC from the internet and have the entire assistant available. Instead, the company wants Windows to decide where a particular AI task should run. All three local capabilities, including context, actions and models, are coming soon to Copilot+ PCs. Microsoft hasn't announced a specific public rollout date yet.
Is this what using a Windows PC will look like?
Microsoft's announcement is appealing because it focuses on things people actually do on their computers every day. You don't necessarily need an AI assistant to write an essay or generate an image. Sometimes, you just want it to find a missing file, tidy up a folder or help you understand why something isn't working. That's where these additions could make a real difference. Copilot is moving beyond answering questions and getting closer to handling everyday PC tasks. There are still questions around how reliably it will perform these actions and how much control users will have over them. But if Microsoft gets the basics right, using a Windows PC could become a little less about managing files and settings, and a little more about getting your actual work done.

With the new features, Copilot can run AI models on the PC and send work to the cloud when needed. Microsoft describes Hybrid Intelligence as designed for a broad range of Windows hardware, but it’s tying these Copilot features to Copilot+ PCs.
A separate developer preview is already available in the GitHub Copilot app, GitHub Copilot CLI, and Visual Studio Code. It runs on RTX Spark PCs, including Surface Laptop Ultra-class machines, as well as computers powered by AMD Ryzen AI Max and Ryzen AI Max 400.
In his Ignite analysis, 
Paul Thurrott
 sees the local Copilot features as the biggest user-facing change but argues agents in Windows 11 are still a long way off.
