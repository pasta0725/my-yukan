Suno now lets you make AI videos for your AI music
The new Visuals feature generates images and 15-second videos that automatically sync with Suno songs.﻿

Suno has introduced image and video generation in its latest effort to expand beyond being an AI music platform. The new feature, 
called “Visuals,”
 allows users to turn Suno songs into AI-generated video clips that automatically lipsync to lyrics, or make images via prompt descriptions that can be used as thumbnails and album artwork.
Visuals is available on web, iOS, and Android. The exact layout differs across Suno’s desktop and mobile interfaces, but users will find 
a new Visuals tab under Create mode
 on each version. Web users can also access Visuals by selecting the three dot menu on a song in their library. From there, users can select which section of the track to use, up to a maximum of 15 seconds, and select a reference image to build the generated video clip.
There are three options available for the starting image reference: selecting one of the original character presets provided by Suno, uploading your own photo or artwork, or generating the image directly on Suno using descriptive prompts. The image generation capabilities also allow users to select specific aspect ratios, optimizing results for landscape or portrait modes and online platforms.
For video generation, users can select whether the reference image is used as the start or end frame, making it easier to edit the 15-second clips together. There’s a toggle to automatically lipsync vocals to the generated video, and tools for adjusting aspect ratio and clip length. Visuals also includes a dedicated video editing timeline for assembling clips into longer videos, and adding customizable song lyric text.
The H3 Move AI model is being used to power Visuals, based on H3 from MiniMax. Suno says the AI has been “optimized for our community including adding additional protections and safeguards,” which include a “suite of new text and visual filters” designed to block people from using or generating the likeness of specific artists, public figures, copyrighted works and IP. Visuals also uses its own “Visuals credits” to generate videos and images, which are distinct from the song credits used to make AI music, meaning Suno users will have additional expenses to pay in order to use this new feature.
The Visuals launch follows Suno introducing 
a Speech feature in October
 for generating spoken words and voiceovers. Adding video capabilities is another attempt to diversify the platform after Suno’s music generator 
attracted a
number of
high-profile lawsuits
, though it seems some users would rather the company focus on improving those 
song-generating capabilities
. In the YouTube video for the Visuals launch, one user says “FIX AUDIO BEFORE ADDING CRAP NOBODY ASKED FOR” — a sentiment that other commenters are echoing.

Original Creation, By Design
Earlier this year we shared our 
principles
 for building the future of music responsibly. Our goal is to build the world’s best platform for creative entertainment, in ways that expand possibilities for artists, rightsholders and the larger music ecosystem. From the beginning, we deliberately designed Suno to enable original creation, with

 to keep our tools from being used in ways that don’t conform to our principles.
We’ve built Visuals the same way, with additional safeguards to protect artists from unauthorized use of their likenesses or works, on top of the ones we already have in place for music.
We’ve adopted a suite of new text and visual filters intended to block uploads and prompts that try to use or create visual representations of specific artists, public figures, copyrighted works and IP. We then use these same filters and additional third-party tools to check generated videos or images for violations of our Community Guidelines, which prohibit a comprehensive range of harmful content. We will use our labeling tools to apply embedded watermarks to Visuals before they can be shared off-platform.
We are pairing these protections with in-platform user education. The first time a Suno user tries to upload a video or image to use with Visuals, we remind them the tool is for original creation, and to confirm they have the rights to any file they are trying to use.
If we detect misuse of our products or it is reported to us, our Trust and Safety team investigates and takes action, which could include removing the content, restricting the account or – in the most severe cases – terminating it completely.
We’re always working to strengthen our guardrails and will continue to add new safeguards that protect artists and our users as our technology evolves.
