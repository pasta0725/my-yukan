Google is extending how long it keeps temporary Gemini chats and conversations made with chat history turned off to around two months, saying the change aims to improve safety and security as AI systems become more capable.
The 
change
, which will roll out globally in the Gemini app over the coming weeks, marks a significant increase from the previous 72-hour retention period mentioned in Google’s Gemini Apps Privacy Hub, according to an 
archived version
 from November 12.
OpenAI, by comparison, says it may 
keep temporary chats in ChatGPT
 for up to 30 days, whereas Anthropic 
retains incognito chats
 in Claude for 30 days by default. However, companies may keep some data longer for legal or safety reasons, or to investigate policy violations.
Google did not answer questions about what prompted the change and whether it would affect how conversations are accessed or reviewed, instead pointing to a 
blog post
 that says chats are kept longer to respond to users, process feedback, and protect its services and users. Its updated Gemini Apps Privacy Hub also cites potential system failures as a reason for retaining chats and says temporary conversations are not used to train AI models.
The longer retention period comes as Google introduces separate settings for Gemini’s chat history and AI training. Until now, both were tied to a single “Keep Activity” setting. Users will now be able to keep their conversations in their chat history without using them to train Google’s generative AI models.
Google says both new settings will be turned off by default for users who previously disabled Keep Activity. For those who had Keep Activity turned on, their chat history will remain enabled. They can, though, decide separately whether Google can use their conversations to train AI models.

It can be hard to use Google products—or even the Internet itself—without coming into contact with Gemini AI. That is, of course, 
by design
. The more you use Gemini, the more data Google collects to train and improve its generative AI chatbot. The options to limit training on your data have been scant, but Google is finally 
adding more controls
 to the Gemini app.
The change currently rolling out to Gemini will separate your chat history from training, which allows you to retain basic AI functionality without handing Google quite so much control over your data. Prior to this change, the only way to opt out of contributing your chat contents (including attachments) for training was to turn off your history. That made it impossible to go back and pick up a chat down the road.
As the new settings roll out, users will see separate toggles for model training and chat history. The history setting will continue to support an adjustable limit, but you will no longer have to change it to stop Google from training on your Gemini usage. Meanwhile, AI training will be an opt-out situation. You’ll need to find the toggle to block Google from using your chats for AI training. It’s under Activity in the Gemini settings. You can also access the menu from your Google account under Data and Privacy > My Activity > Other activity > Gemini Apps Activity.
Importantly, this only applies to future interactions with Gemini. If you have previously fed any data into Google’s AI, that’s already part of the training data. That can include your inputs, Gemini’s responses, uploaded files, tool calls, your location, and data from connected apps. 
Google says
 it may retain this data for up to three years to improve its models. The company has previously noted it attempts to filter personal information out of the Gemini activity history, but that isn’t a guarantee.

Regardless of your chosen activity settings, you can always manually remove anything you don’t want saved on the same page with the history and training toggles. However, reviewed chats that are already part of the training data won’t be retroactively deleted. Opting out of training doesn’t mean Google won’t have your data, either. Google may use this data in 
other
 ways for up to three years—it just won’t add it to AI-training data sets.
If you want to use Gemini and not retain any data, your options are actually a bit more limited now. Currently, if you disable history, Google still retains that data for 72 hours (and 24 hours for the user). Once the updated page is live, this duration will change to two months. So you can access that data for longer, but so can Google. The company says this is to “respond to you, process your feedback, and protect Google, its users, and the public.” Essentially, Google will be more able to identify nefarious AI usage even if someone disables history.
Even with the longer chat retention, the ability to control AI training is still a welcome privacy improvement. Google says the change is rolling out globally, but it may take a few weeks to show up for everyone.

What changes in the Gemini app?
Until now, the old Keep Activity setting was a single switch that was doing two jobs. First, it decided how long Gemini saved your chats. Second, whether Google could use them to train its 
generative AI models
. 
Recommended Videos
It wasn’t just prompts or responses; it also included photos, files, audio, location info, and data collected from connected Workspace apps. 
Those who asked for their history had to agree to let Google train its AI models on their data with no other option. That compensation is ending now. The new “History” section now controls how long Google saves your chats. 
Then there’s the second setting that decides whether your chat content, text and attachments included, can train those models. Basically, you can keep history enabled (chats are deleted after 18 months by default) and skip training. 
So you can keep your history and skip training
.
If Keep Activity is already off, both new settings start off as well.

What’s the catch if I turn History off?
There’s a big one. If you turn History off, Google now keeps your chats for around two months instead of up to 72 hours. You see? It’s 60 days versus three days. During that window, Google uses the information to personalize the responses and process feedback.
There’s another wrinkle, too. Even with History off, sending feedback on a response makes Google collect the last 24 hours of your chats, along with any attachments. 
The rollout is global over the coming weeks. Google is already emailing users. In my experience, menus rarely match the email. I’d suggest reopening your Gemini activity page when yours lands and setting History and training separately.
