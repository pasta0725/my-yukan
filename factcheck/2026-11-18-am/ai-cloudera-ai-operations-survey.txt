AIの本格運用に潜むトークン消費増とガバナンス負担　企業の約9割が再設計を迫られる実態
AIの業務導入が進む一方、本格展開のフェーズで運用体制の抜本的な見直しを迫られる企業が相次いでいる。トークン消費による想定外のコスト膨張や、各国・地域の法規制、データ主権への対応など、ITリーダーが直面する新たな制約とは何か。

　Clouderaは2026年11月4日、世界の企業におけるAI活用の実態をまとめた調査レポート「エンタープライズAIの現状 2026：AIの本格展開に向けた新たなルール（The State of Enterprise AI 2026: The New Rules of Scale）」を公開した。回答者の89％がトークン消費量の増加を理由にAIの用途を制限・再設計しており、98％が障害やハルシネーション、予期しない動作を受けて導入や展開を縮小、または見直したことが分かった。

　同レポートは、企業がAIの大規模活用を維持するためのコスト管理や運用上のコントロール、柔軟性の確保にどのように取り組んでいるかを分析した。企業が直面する課題として、コストの増加やガバナンス、データの保管場所、データ主権、国・地域ごとの法規制が挙がった。84％はデータ保存場所やデータ主権、法規制に関する条件がAIワークロードの展開先に影響すると答えた。
　ガバナンスとコンプライアンスをAIの本格展開を妨げる要因に挙げた回答者は75％に達した。55％は関連コストが想定を上回る速度で上昇していると回答した。コストの増加や変動拡大を理由にAIワークロードを別の環境に移した企業は45％に上った。58％はAIワークロードの環境移行に中程度か大規模な再設計を要すると回答しており、実行環境を変える選択にはコストと技術的な負担が伴う実態が示された。
　複数の環境でAIワークロードを運用する主な理由としては、「レジリエンス」（42％）が最も多く、次いで「データ主権やデータの保存場所に関する要件」（38％）、「コスト削減」（38％）、「データへの近接性」（37％）が続いた。単一の環境に固定せず、要件に応じて処理場所を選べる体制への関心が表れている。
　AI活用の取り組みを初期段階からやり直せる場合に優先する施策としては、49％が「初期段階からのガバナンス強化」を挙げた。このほか、「データ品質への早期投資」（46％）、「インフラのモダナイゼーションの前倒し」（45％）、「従業員トレーニングへの投資拡大」（45％）がほぼ同率で並んだ。
　ClouderaのApplied AI担当最高技術責任者（CTO）であるブライアン・マーティン（Brian Martin）氏は、現在有効なAI運用方法が将来もコストや運用の面で合理的とは限らないと指摘した。コストやモデル、規制の変化に応じて適切な実行環境も変わるとして、企業にはデータ管理やガバナンス、支出の統制を保ちつつ、環境変化に柔軟に対応する戦略が必要だと述べた。
　同社の山賀裕二氏（社長執行役員）は、日本企業もコスト変動や規制要件の変化に対応してAIを安定運用する必要があると述べた。コストや性能、データ主権を踏まえた実行環境の柔軟な選択が欠かせないとの認識を示し、持続的なAI活用に必要な環境構築を支援する方針を説明した。
　ClouderaはデータをAI実行環境に移動させるのではなく、ガバナンスが適用されたデータのある場所でコンピューティング処理を実施する。異なる環境でも統一的なガバナンスと管理を維持してAIのライフサイクル全体を統制できる仕組みだという。企業はコストやパフォーマンス、データ主権、ビジネス上の要件に応じてワークロードの実行環境を選択できる。同社はAI活用の持続性を支える環境整備を今後も支援するとしている。

Nearly nine-in-ten (89%) organizations have scaled back or been forced to redesign AI plans due to token costs this year, according to research from Cloudera.
Findings from the firm’s 
State of Enterprise AI 2026
 report show enterprises are still struggling with AI-related spending, despite a notable decrease in token costs over the last three months.
Brian Martin, CTO of Applied AI at Cloudera, said the findings highlight a “new reality for enterprise AI”, adding that “what works today may not make economic or operational sense tomorrow”.
“Costs change, models evolve, regulations shift, and the right place to run a workload changes with them,” he commented.

Cost volatility spooks IT leaders
The study specifically highlighted cost volatility as a key challenge for enterprises, with nearly half (45%) having moved AI workloads as a result.
According to Cloudera, this shows that “AI’s price tag is starting to shape its path through the enterprise” – and that could spell further bad news in terms of ROI and adoption rates.

Last year’s report, for example, found cost was the leading barrier to AI implementation, and that trend has intensified across 2026 amidst surging token costs.
Figures from the 
LLM Token Expenditure Index
 show prices hit over $2 per million tokens in June this year. As of 25 October, the index shows token prices stand at $0.95 per million tokens.
That price decrease will offer little solace for IT leaders, however, many of whom have been battling costs throughout the first six months of the year. Cloudera said the cost of the technology has placed huge pressure on enterprises to “demonstrate returns and manage the ongoing cost of AI at scale”.
More than half (55%) of respondents said their AI investment increased, yet despite greater spending that is yet to translate into “clear returns”. Indeed, 58% of respondents the value generated by AI initiatives is roughly equal to what they cost.
Fewer than one-quarter (23%) said AI generates more value than it costs.
From a business impact perspective, the situation is roughly the same, according to Cloudera, with IT leaders reporting mixed returns on the technology.
“More than a quarter (27%) report little or no measurable impact from their AI initiatives, while 41% have encountered higher-than-expected costs,” the report states.
Cloudera added that for those investing heavily in the technology, its findings “raise questions about how quickly that spending will translate into meaningful returns”.
A year of AI costs
The study from Cloudera is the latest in a string of reports questioning the costs associated with AI over the last year - and there have been a number of high profile incidents involving lavish overspending.
Uber, for example, revealed it 
blew through its entire annual AI budget in just four months
, prompting the ride hailing firm to introduce spending caps for staff.
As 
ITPro 
reported in late June
, Accenture also ordered staff to stop using AI for basic tasks such as converting PDFs to slides amidst rising costs.
It’s worth noting that a key factor behind these incidents lay in the tokenmaxxing trend, rather than general costs associated with AI. This is a practice of maximizing AI use within the enterprise by incentivizing staff to use the technology.
Some companies introduced leader boards to track usage rates, while others used it as a means to measure productivity.
In response to growing costs, some enterprises have begun reviving time-tested FinOps practices, as 
ITPro
 reported in late July
, enabling them to better track costs and cut down on spending.
Cloudera’s report also highlighted a sharpened focus on this front. Nearly all enterprises believe they now have “some visibility” into AI spending, which marks a move in the right direction.
“Ninety percent say they can measure end-to-end AI costs at least somewhat accurately,” the report notes. Cloudera added that there’s still room for improvement here, however.
“Only 27% say they can do so very accurately, suggesting there is a considerable gap between having a general understanding of AI costs and a precise picture of where that spending goes.”

===== https://techstrong.ai/articles/survey-cost-concerns-top-list-of-issues-driving-shifts-in-ai-strategy/

TL;DR — Key Takeaways
AI costs are forcing changes:
 89% of respondents have limited or redesigned AI use cases because of rising token consumption.
Governance remains a scaling barrier:
 75% say governance and compliance constrain AI expansion, while 84% say residency, sovereignty or jurisdiction-specific requirements influence workload placement.
Financial returns remain uneven:
 Only 23% say AI generates more value than it costs; 58% report roughly equal value and costs, and 19% say costs exceed value.
A 
global survey
 of 1,500 IT leaders and decision-makers published today finds that 89% of respondents have limited or redesigned use cases involving artificial intelligence (AI) because of cost issues stemming from rising token consumption.
Conducted by Wakefield Research on behalf of Cloudera, the survey also finds that every respondent reports their organization has experienced higher or more volatile AI costs, with 99% moving to reduce them by, for example, moving a workload to another IT environment (45%) or reducing processing frequency, model size, context size, or output volume (40%). Just under a quarter (24%) have paused or canceled an AI use case altogether.
Additionally, nearly all respondents (98%) said their organization has pulled back on AI deployments due to failure, hallucinations, or unexpected behavior, with 68% of respondents needing to pull back workloads sometimes or frequently.
The survey also finds that data residency, sovereignty, or jurisdiction-specific requirements are influencing where AI workloads are deployed for a full 84% of respondents. More than half of respondents (58%) also noted that moving workloads requires moderate or major redesigns.
Three quarters of respondents (75%) also noted that governance and compliance issues are limiting their ability to scale AI deployments, with 55% reporting that governance costs are increasing faster than expected.
Overall, a total of 58% said the value their AI initiatives generate is approximately equal to what they cost, while just 23% say AI currently generates more value than it costs. Another 19% said costs outweigh the value generated. More than a quarter (27%) report little or no measurable impact from their AI initiatives, while 41% have encountered higher costs than expected.
A full 90% said they can measure end-to-end AI costs at least somewhat accurately, but only 27% say they can do so with a high degree of accuracy, the survey finds. Only 19% continuously monitor their costs.
Collectively, these issues suggest that the average enterprise is still wrestling with many AI fundamentals, says Jeff Healey, senior vice president of product marketing for Cloudera. Those issues include everything from optimizing costs and improving scalability to addressing latency, security, compliance and governance requirements, he adds. For example, not every workload requires access to the latest frontier AI model when an open weight model deployed in an on-premises IT environment might prove to be much more cost-effective, he adds. Managing token costs has become an arbitrage exercise, notes Healey.
In other cases, organizations need to determine if using AI makes sense in the first place, he adds. “You don’t need to use AI for everything,” says Healey.
The degree to which each of these issues is a challenge naturally varies. In fact, more than three-quarters (76%) say it is easy to apply consistent data governance, security and access policies across their AI environments. Just over half (54%) believe their organization has struck an effective balance between the speed of AI adoption and appropriate governance.
Nevertheless, the survey finds that nearly half of respondents (49%) would strengthen governance from the beginning, while 46% would have invested in data quality earlier. A similar percentage (45%) would have invested in both modernizing infrastructure and employee training sooner.
In general, survey respondents cite resilience (42%), sovereignty and residency requirements (38%), cost reduction (38%), and proximity to data (37%) as the top reasons for running AI workloads in multiple IT environments.
Despite all these issues and concerns, however, investments in AI continue to be made. Nearly two-thirds (64%) of respondents say their organizations spend at least $500,000 annually on AI initiatives, while 28% spend $1 million or more. In fact, 43% report greater-than-expected productivity.
Nearly two thirds (63%) also said AI is an important input in organizational decision-making, with just over a quarter (27%) noting that AI regularly shapes important decisions or serves as a primary driver. More than three quarters (78%) require human approval for at least some AI-driven decisions, but the circumstances vary. More than half (52%) trigger approval when a confidence, risk or value threshold is crossed, while 34% require it for legally regulated or designated high-impact decisions and 30% for decisions affecting customers, employees or regulated outcomes.
It’s apparent that while there are still plenty of AI challenges ahead the benefits still outweigh the sum total of the costs that organizations are moving to rapidly contain.

This is according to a report from Cloudera, which is based on survey fielded by Wakefield Research in September 2026, covering 1,500 respondents,
Results show that 84% say data residency, sovereignty, or jurisdiction-specific requirements influence where AI workloads are deployed, underscoring the need for greater flexibility and control as enterprises scale AI.
The study also found that AI in production requires adjustment, as 98% have pulled back an AI deployment due to failure, hallucinations, or unexpected behavior.
Also, governance is constraining scale, as 75% cite governance and compliance as a constraint on scaling AI, while 55% report faster-than-expected growth in governance and compliance costs. 
A similar trend is observed in the Asia-Pacific region (APAC), where 73% cite governance as a scaling constraint and 56% report higher-than-expected compliance costs.
Further, AI costs are reshaping strategy as 45% have moved AI workloads in response to higher or more volatile costs. In APAC, 26% have gone further, pausing or canceling an AI use case in response to cost volatility.
In addition, sovereignty is shaping workload placement as 84% of global respondents and 79% across APAC say residency or sovereignty requirements influence where AI runs, while 58% globally say moving workloads requires moderate or major redesign.
“Scale is exposing a new reality for enterprise AI: what works today may not make economic or operational sense tomorrow,” said Brian Martin, CTO of Applied AI at Cloudera. 
“Costs change, models evolve, regulations shift, and the right place to run a workload changes with them,” said Martin. “Leaders need an AI strategy built for that reality, one that gives them the freedom to adapt without giving up control of their data, governance, or spend.”
Across APAC, rising AI investments have yet to translate into stronger business returns for many organizations. 
While 58% of respondents in the region report increased AI investment over the past year, only 28% state  that AI currently generates more value than it costs,  and 21% indicate that costs outweigh the value delivered.
“Enterprises across Asia Pacific face growing challenges in delivering measurable business value from their AI investments while navigating rising costs and increasingly complex governance and regulatory requirements,” said Remus Lim, Cloudera SVP for APAC and Japan. 
“To relieve these pressures, organizations need to move beyond simply expanding AI deployments to focus on maximizing their existing data and infrastructure,” said Lim. “By bringing AI directly to data wherever it resides, enterprises can minimize unnecessary data movement, optimize workload placement based on business needs, and maintain consistent governance across environments.”
The findings also reveal a growing focus on the foundations required for long-term AI success. If given the opportunity to restart their AI journey, 49% of respondents say they would strengthen governance from the beginning, 46% would invest in data quality earlier, and 45% would modernize infrastructure sooner. 
Beyond these technical priorities, organizations also recognize the importance of building internal capabilities, with another 45% saying they would invest more in employee training. This need is also evident across Asia Pacific, where 47% of respondents identify skills and expertise as a constraint on scaling AI.
This need for adaptability is making flexibility across environments increasingly important. Organizations cite resilience (42%), sovereignty and residency requirements (38%), cost reduction (38%), and proximity to data (37%) among the leading reasons for operating AI workloads across multiple environments.

The AI Scale Trap: Why 89% of Enterprises Are Forced to Redesign AI Use Cases Amid Rising Costs
Cloudera’s 2026 State of Enterprise AI report finds rising costs, governance demands, and sovereignty requirements are forcing enterprises to rethink how and where they run AI
SAN JOSE, Calif. — October 26, 2026 — 
Cloudera
, the only data and AI platform company that brings AI to data anywhere, today released 
The State of Enterprise AI 2026: The New Rules of Scale
, a new global report examining how enterprises are navigating the cost, control, and flexibility required to sustain AI at scale.
Based on a survey commissioned by Cloudera and fielded by Wakefield Research of 1,500 global IT leaders and decision-makers in September 2026, the report finds that the rules of enterprise AI are changing as rising costs, governance demands, and sovereignty requirements reshape how and where organizations run AI. Nearly nine in ten (89%) respondents have already limited or redesigned AI use cases because of rising token consumption. And 84% say data residency, sovereignty, or jurisdiction-specific requirements influence where AI workloads are deployed, underscoring the need for greater flexibility and control as enterprises scale AI.
Among the report’s key findings:
AI in production requires adjustment:
 98% have pulled back an AI deployment due to failure, hallucinations, or unexpected behavior. 
Governance is constraining scale:
 75% cite governance and compliance as a constraint on scaling AI, while 55% report faster-than-expected growth in governance and compliance costs.
AI costs are reshaping strategy:
 45% have moved AI workloads in response to higher or more volatile costs. 
Sovereignty is shaping workload placement:
 84% say residency or sovereignty requirements influence where AI runs, and 58% say moving workloads requires moderate or major redesign.
“Scale is exposing a new reality for enterprise AI: what works today may not make economic or operational sense tomorrow,” said Brian Martin, CTO of Applied AI at Cloudera. “Costs change, models evolve, regulations shift, and the right place to run a workload changes with them. Leaders need an AI strategy built for that reality, one that gives them the freedom to adapt without giving up control of their data, governance, or spend.”
The findings also reveal a growing focus on the foundations required for long-term AI success. If given the opportunity to restart their AI journey, 49% of respondents say they would strengthen governance from the beginning, 46% would invest in data quality earlier, and 45% would modernize infrastructure sooner. Another 45% would invest more in employee training. 
This need for adaptability is making flexibility across environments increasingly important. Organizations cite resilience (42%), sovereignty and residency requirements (38%), cost reduction (38%), and proximity to data (37%) among the leading reasons for operating AI workloads across multiple environments.
