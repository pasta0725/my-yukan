"The Collection Launches This December": Gentle Monster Dates Samsung's Android XR Glasses Before Google Does
By 
Jordan Kuo
Staff Writer, VR.org
Share
Platform launches usually run on the platform holder's calendar, and the first 
Android XR
 glasses keep running on the eyewear brands' calendars instead. Gentle Monster, the Seoul label whose frames carry one of the two versions of Samsung's audio glasses, 
posted a teaser to X
 on Friday that put a month on a product Google and Samsung have declined to date since they revealed it in July. The post is a trailer for a short film, and the plainest line in its caption is the news: "The collection launches this December."
Advertisement
Joaquin Phoenix in Gentle Monster's Intelligent Eyewear campaign, shot in the same hotel as Jonathan Glazer's short. Image: Gentle Monster
The brand named the month, then its storefront named the day
The caption around that line is film-trade copy rather than product copy, which is part of why it took a day to register as a date. It says the short stars Joaquin Phoenix, that Jonathan Glazer directed it, that the piece is "a cinematic journey between reality and dream," and that the "Full film releasing mid-November." Readers are asked to register for a collection preview event in New York rather than to buy anything. 
Gentle Monster's US homepage
 is more specific than its social account: it carries an 
Intelligent Eyewear
 banner reading "Pre-Order November 12" and two buttons beneath it, one to subscribe and one to reserve a New York preview.
The Korea Herald
 filled in the rest on Sunday, reporting that US preorders will run through Gentle Monster's own website ahead of a December release, and that the New York preview opens on November 13 and runs through November 23. No price appears in the report. A South Korean release date is still unconfirmed, which is a strange sentence to write about a Seoul brand selling Samsung hardware.
Thursday's open question has an answer
When Warby Parker's third-quarter release landed with a preorder date buried in a co-CEO's quote, 
VR.org noted
 that the sentence covered Warby Parker's collection and nobody else's, and listed as open the question of whether Gentle Monster would open orders on the same day. Its storefront now answers that, and the answer is yes. Two eyewear companies on two continents have independently published November 12 for a product whose software vendor has published nothing, which is roughly the inverse of how a Galaxy or a Pixel reaches a retail calendar.
A still from the Glazer short, the image on Gentle Monster's US homepage beside the November 12 preorder banner. Image: Gentle Monster
The list of things nobody will confirm is still long
Engadget's read
 on Friday was that Google's partners cannot keep a lid on its plans, and the specifics support it: Google has confirmed neither the preorder date nor the launch month, and its consumer page still said "this fall" when VR.org checked it on November 2. Samsung's July Unpacked release remains the only first-party sheet anyone can cite, and it lists a Snapdragon AR1 Gen1 platform, a camera, and battery life of up to nine hours with seven more charges from the case. ChosunBiz reported on October 30 that Samsung had settled on mid-December for South Korea and the US, a report 
VR.org covered
 alongside Google's Play changes and one that Samsung has never acknowledged. Gentle Monster saying December does not confirm ChosunBiz, since a brand can hit December without hitting the middle of it, but it is the first time the month has come from a company that will actually take the money. Price remains nobody's number, in any currency, from any of the four companies involved.
Advertisement
A launch campaign built on the one feature that works without a display
The film is worth a developer's attention for what it chooses to dramatize. In the Korea Herald's description Phoenix plays a salesman who meets mysterious figures in an unfamiliar hotel, and he uses the glasses to understand their language and talk back. Gentle Monster frames the story as a statement about AI-assisted communication rather than a demonstration of features, which is the sort of thing a fashion house says when it does not want to be held to a spec, but the choice of scenario is still a choice. These glasses have no display. Everything the wearer gets arrives through speakers in the temples, so translation and spoken answers are the whole surface a film can show, and Google has been building toward exactly that demo since it put a 
listening area control
 into Translate for glasses in August. A campaign that leans on understanding a stranger's language is a campaign that has read its own feature list honestly.
Five weeks, scheduled by a lookbook
For anyone shipping against this hardware the practical change is small but real, because a December stated by a seller is firmer than a December reported by an industry paper, and the gap between November 12 and a holiday delivery is now bracketed at roughly five weeks on both brands' word. The distribution path has not moved. Augmented experiences reach these glasses through the ordinary phone release track on Google Play, a projected activity is what earns the Differentiated for Glasses badge, and the Jetpack Projected library behind that badge was still at an alpha version in late October, so a listing that earns the badge in December rests on an API surface that can rename things under it. Google's 
packaging guide
 also warns that a device which has not launched may be missing from Play Console's device catalog until closer to launch, which means the usual pre-release hygiene of excluding an untested device is not necessarily available.
Google has shipped consumer hardware through partners before, and the Nexus years set the pattern of a partner building the device while Google owned the software story. What is different here is who controls the announcements. A phone maker schedules around a developer keynote and a carrier launch, while an eyewear label schedules around campaign films, preview spaces and a holiday window, and the beats of this launch have landed in that second rhythm: a teaser on Friday, a full film in mid-November, a preview room in New York for eleven days, and orders opening in between. Developers reading those beats for a ship date are reading a marketing calendar, not a release calendar, and the two have never been the same document.
November 12 should put a price on these, and it should say which countries are in the first wave and whether a Gentle Monster order and a Warby Parker order land in the same week. Until Samsung or Google says so in writing, December is a promise from two opticians, and it is still the most specific thing anyone selling these glasses has said.
