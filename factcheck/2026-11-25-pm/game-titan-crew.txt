Titan Crew
』を発表した。早期アクセスとして近日登場予定。日本語にも対応する。

　本作は、最大4人で高さ27メートルの巨大な二足歩行機械“MULE”を操縦し、見捨てられた谷を探索する協力ゲーム。乗員はマシンの操縦だけでなく、発電機や冷却、油圧、燃料、脚部のバランス調整といった操作・機器管理のほか、機械の修理など、それぞれの持ち場を担当しながらさまざまなミッションに挑む。ソロプレイにも対応。
　MULEには操縦席や食堂、作業場、パワールーム、脚部接続室などが用意されている。操縦士が進行方向や歩調を管理する一方、ほかの乗員は発電機や圧力を監視。機械に異常が発生した場合は、工具を使って修理する必要がある。なお、機内マニュアルは112ページにも上る大ボリュームとなっているようだ。
　ゲームの舞台は、22年前の洪水によって工場が停止し、避難済みと宣言されたヴェール盆地。プレイヤーは無線で連絡してくる住民アニェスや、機内記録装置に残された過去の乗員の報告を手掛かりに、地域の状況を把握していく。
　探索では、後部のウインチで橋脚を支えたり、吊り上げアームで最大8トンのモジュールを運んだりと、MULEの機能をフル活用。水位や地形に応じて進行ルートを選び、橋の修復などを通じて谷の復興を目指す。戦闘要素はないが、乗員の空腹や渇き・疲労といった要素に加え、昼夜の変化や天候、水位の変動も存在するため、環境への対応は求められる。
　早期アクセス版では、ストーリーミッション5つ、サイドクエスト3つ、発見要素ふたつを収録した第1幕をプレイ可能。正式版では全5幕、全24ミッションを収録予定で、湿地や湖、稜線などヴェール盆地の各地を巡る物語が描かれる。また新たな依頼や、MULEの道具なども登場予定。

What the developers have to say:
Why Early Access?
					“Titan Crew is a co-op game where one machine is shared between several players. What works with two, three or four people only shows with real crews. Act I is complete and plays from start to finish; we want to hear it played before building the next four acts on it: what drags or is unclear, and what you would like to do with the MULE that it cannot do yet.”
Approximately how long will this game be in Early Access?
					“About six months before the full version. Updates will add the following acts, each with its region of the basin and its missions.”
How is the full version planned to differ from the Early Access version?
					“The full game has five acts and twenty-four missions across the whole Veyre basin. It adds the marshes, the lake and the ridge, more people with their requests, new tools and new loads for the MULE; at the end, the crew picks one of three great works. It tells the whole story of the old crew and of the Day 87 flood.”
What is the current state of the Early Access version?
					“Act I plays in full, alone or with up to four players: five story missions, three side quests and two discoveries, in the basin's first region. The MULE and all its systems are there, with the onboard manual, the crew's survival, day, night and weather, in French and English. We estimate act I and its side quests at four to six hours depending on the crew; your playthroughs will tell us whether that holds. Act I saves will carry over into updates; if an update ever had to break them, it would say so beforehand.”
Will the game be priced differently during and after Early Access?
					“Yes: the price will rise with the content, when version 1.0 comes out. Anyone who buys during Early Access will not pay anything more.”
How are you planning on involving the Community in your development process?
					“Through the Steam forums and discussions: each update will say what it owes to players' feedback. Feedback on act I will decide what changes before act II: how readable the machine is and how long the trips are, among other things.”

About This Game
The MULE is a twenty-seven-meter building standing on two legs. It has been waiting for its crew at the depot for ten years.
One machine, four stations
The MULE carries the pilot station, the mess with its bunks and coffee machine, the workshop, the engine room and the leg junction. The pilot holds the heading and the pace while someone else watches the generators and the pressure; when a bearing makes a noise they don't like, someone climbs down the leg, mallet in hand, to listen to it. With four, everyone has a station. Alone, you run from one end of the machine to the other.
Everything reads on the machine
The MULE's state shows on its needles and lamps, and many faults are heard before they are seen: a whistling bearing, a pump sucking gravel. You cold-start it one group at a time, the onboard manual open at the right page, and the workshop has what you need to bleed, grease or swap a cartridge.
A valley left "until further notice"
Twenty-two years ago, a flood stopped the Veyre basin's factories, and the Office declared it evacuated. Those who stayed live with whatever still holds, like the village set up in the wagons of a train that never left. Agnès talks to you over the radio from the depot, and the onboard recorder keeps the old crew's reports.
The winch and the arm
The rear winch holds up the pier of a leaning bridge, and the lifting arm raises an eight-ton module. To reopen the bridge, the MULE pushes a spare span across the gorge. You choose your route on the navigation table, over the bridge or through the ford depending on the water. A mistake kills no one: if a foot slips in the mud and the machine goes down, it is up to the crew to raise it.
What Early Access contains
All of act I: five story missions, three side quests and two discoveries, an estimated four to six hours depending on the crew.
The basin's first region: the depot, the Semelles ford, the Contrepoids bridge, the locks, the village on rails, the Relief relay station and the Antennes refuge.
The whole MULE: generators, cooling, hydraulics, fuel, legs and balance, winch, lifting arm, and its 112-page onboard manual.
The crew's hunger, thirst and fatigue, day and night, changing weather, high and low water.
One to four players in co-op, invites through Steam; keyboard and mouse, or Xbox and PlayStation controllers.
The game in French and English, voices included.
What the full game aims for
Five acts and twenty-four missions across the whole Veyre basin, from the marshes to the lake and the ridge, up to the Relief system's control center. Not everyone wants the same thing from the land, and at the end the crew picks one of three great works that change the valley for good. This plan will evolve with your feedback.
No combat
Nobody fights in Titan Crew: the danger comes from the machine and the water.						
AI Generated Content Disclosure
The developers describe how their game uses AI Generated Content like this:
					Titan Crew is made by one person. It is a project I have carried since I was a kid, and alone I could never have brought it this far without artificial intelligence: I used it as the team I did not have. The radio voices, the music and the soundscapes were generated with AI from voices and atmospheres designed for the game; most of the sound effects, from the machine to the footsteps, come from real recordings. The texts and the English translation were written with an AI assistant, then reviewed. Some store page visuals were composed with AI from in-game images, and the assistant helped me write the tools that build the machine and the valley. The game generates nothing with AI while you play. Going forward, my goal is to stop using AI to create the game's content.
