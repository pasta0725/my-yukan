The 
European Space Agency
 awarded 
OHB
 a 102 million euro ($112 million) contract to serve as the prime contractor for the ClearSpace-1 (CS-1) active debris removal mission. In addition, OHB signed an agreement with startup 
ClearSpace
 to provide key navigation and capture solutions for the mission. 
The company ClearSpace has been working with ESA on the CS-1 mission since 2020. The goal of the mission is to demonstrate active debris removal with an object in orbit not designed to be captured. 
The mission originally planned to target a VESPA payload adapter left in orbit from a 2013 Vega launch, but that object was 
hit by debris in 2023
. That incident led ESA and ClearSpace to shift the mission to target the PROBA-1 spacecraft instead. The mission is planned to launch in 2030.
OHB will serve as prime contractor and lead a team of contractors including ClearSpace, Deimos Engenharia, and OHB subsidiary OHB Sweden. The partners advanced the program on Wednesday during Space Tech Expo Europe in Bremen, Germany. 
ClearSpace explained the mission aims to demonstrate capabilities including autonomous rendezvous, close-proximity operations and capture, which are all “fundamental building blocks for satellite inspection, servicing, life extension and responsible end-of-life operations.”
“ClearSpace-1 began with a bold ambition: to demonstrate that we can take responsibility for what we leave behind in space,” ClearSpace CEO and co-founder Luc Piguet said. “For ClearSpace, our focus remains on delivering the critical rendezvous and capture capabilities at the heart of ClearSpace-1.”

===== https://roboticsandautomationnews.com/2026/11/18/clearspace-moves-closer-to-launch-of-european-space-debris-removal-mission/107251/

ClearSpace
 has signed a new agreement with 
OHB System AG
 to provide critical navigation and capture technologies for the 
European Space Agency
’s (ESA) ClearSpace-1 mission, marking another step towards the launch of one of Europe’s first active space debris removal missions.
The agreement follows the formalisation of the next phase of the mission between ESA and prime contractor OHB, which leads the European industrial consortium responsible for developing and delivering ClearSpace-1.
Under the agreement, ClearSpace will continue developing the technologies needed to approach, navigate around and capture objects in orbit. 
The mission is intended to demonstrate that satellites not originally designed for retrieval can be safely captured and removed from space.
Demonstrating robotic capture in orbit
ClearSpace-1 will target ESA’s Proba-1 satellite, using autonomous rendezvous, close-proximity navigation and robotic capture technologies to demonstrate the removal of an unprepared object from Earth orbit.
The mission is part of ESA’s wider efforts to address the growing problem of space debris, which poses risks to operational satellites and other spacecraft.
ClearSpace was originally selected as prime contractor for the mission in 2020. Although the industrial structure has since changed, the company retains responsibility for developing key rendezvous and capture capabilities.
The technologies could also support applications beyond debris removal, including satellite inspection, maintenance, life extension and end-of-life disposal.
Luc Piguet, CEO and co-founder of ClearSpace, said: “ClearSpace-1 began with a bold ambition: to demonstrate that we can take responsibility for what we leave behind in space. Today’s milestone reflects years of engineering, determination and collaboration.
“We are grateful to ESA and its Member States for their continued confidence, and to OHB and our European industrial partners for bringing together the expertise needed to advance this mission.
“For ClearSpace, our focus remains on delivering the critical rendezvous and capture capabilities at the heart of ClearSpace-1.
“We are equally grateful to Omega, whose belief in our pioneering ambition since 2022 has remained constant throughout the mission’s evolution. Partnerships built on this kind of trust and shared determination are what make meaningful progress possible.”
Incidentally, Omega was the 
first watch worn on the Moon
 in July of 1969.
Photos taken during Space Tech Expo Europe in Bremen, on 17 November 2026. © ClearSpace
Expanding in-orbit servicing activities
ClearSpace is also developing its capabilities through other European space programmes.
The company has been selected as prime contractor for the UK’s first nationally commissioned active debris removal mission, which aims to capture and remove two defunct satellites registered in the United Kingdom.
It is also working with ESA on Prelude, an in-orbit inspection and technology demonstration mission, and Phoenix, a programme focused on extending the operational life of satellites in geostationary orbit.
Together, these projects form part of ClearSpace’s strategy to develop commercial in-orbit servicing capabilities, including satellite inspection, maintenance and disposal.
Swiss watchmaker Omega, which has supported ClearSpace since 2022, is continuing its partnership with the company as ClearSpace-1 progresses towards flight. Omega is not a contracting party to the ESA-OHB mission agreement.
Founded in Switzerland and headquartered in Luxembourg, ClearSpace operates across Luxembourg, Switzerland, the United Kingdom, Germany and France.
The company is developing robotic technologies intended to support a more sustainable space economy by enabling spacecraft to be inspected, serviced and eventually removed from orbit.

Cleaning up space: OHB awarded EUR 102 million contract by ESA for Europe’s first active space debris removal mission
			Bremen, November 17, 2026		
Low Earth Orbit (LEO) is becoming increasingly congested as the amount of space debris continues to grow. Thousands of defunct satellites and millions of debris fragments orbit Earth, increasing the risk of collisions. With the ClearSpace-1 mission (CS-1), Europe aims to demonstrate for the first time how a satellite can be captured and safely removed from orbit in a controlled manner. For this pioneering mission, the European Space Agency (ESA) has selected OHB System AG as prime contractor. The contract, valued at more than €102 million, was signed today at Space Tech Expo Bremen.
“We are delighted by ESA’s confidence in us and by the award of the ClearSpace-1 contract,” says Chiara Pedersoli, Chief Executive Officer of OHB System AG. “This mission represents an important milestone for OHB and for the European space sector. Together with our partners, we will take on a challenge that has never been accomplished in this form in space: the autonomous capture and controlled removal of a defunct satellite. In doing so, we are making an important contribution to the safety of future space missions.”
“ESA’s Space Safety program is committed to enabling and demonstrating market-oriented technologies for a circular economy in space. ClearSpace-1 paves the way to remove large non-functional objects from Earth orbit. Making active debris removal a viable option is crucial to enabling future policies and regulations that support sustainability in space,” said Rolf Densing, Director of Operations at ESA.
He added: “With ClearSpace-1, built under OHB System’s leadership, we will demonstrate for the first time how a spacecraft can approach, capture and safely bring down an unprepared and uncooperative satellite for re-entry into the atmosphere, while cleaning up one of our own satellites in the process.”
As part of the mission, an industrial consortium led by OHB will develop a servicing spacecraft that will autonomously rendezvous with ESA’s PROBA-1 technology satellite after the end of its operational life. Using optical and radio-frequency sensors, the spacecraft will determine the target’s attitude, rotation and relative motion, safely approach it, and capture the satellite using a specially developed capture mechanism. The two spacecraft will then be guided to a controlled atmospheric re-entry.
Enhancing space safety
ClearSpace-1 is among Europe’s most ambitious initiatives in the fields of in-orbit servicing and active debris removal. As PROBA-1 was not designed to support rendezvous and capture operations and cannot actively cooperate with the servicing spacecraft, the approach, navigation and capture sequence must be performed largely autonomously. The mission will therefore demonstrate key technologies that are expected to play an important role in ensuring the safe use of Earth’s orbital environment in the future.
The mission is based on a comprehensive redesign of the original ClearSpace-1 concept. After the initially selected target object, a VESPA payload adapter, was damaged in 2023 following a collision with space debris, the mission was refocused on ESA’s PROBA-1 satellite. The pre-development phase was successfully completed in March 2025. The mission launch is currently planned for 2030.
European teamwork at its best
As prime contractor, OHB System AG is leading a highly specialized European industrial team. Key partners include the in-orbit servicing company ClearSpace, which has conducted the extensive preparatory work underpinning the mission, Deimos Engenharia S.A. and OHB subsidiary OHB Sweden. Together, the companies are combining their expertise in mission design, autonomous navigation, rendezvous and capture technologies, and spacecraft development to deliver Europe’s first active space debris removal mission. They signed a letter of intent for their collaboration at Space Tech Expo.

OHB To Lead ESA Space Debris Removal Mission
Share
Robert Wall
November 17, 2026
ESA plans to deorbit PROBA-1 by testing a new debris removal system.
Credit: ESA
BREMEN, Germany—OHB has secured a €102 million ($112 million) European Space Agency (ESA) contract for a space debris removal demo.
The mission, known as ClearSpace-1, would represent Europe’s first effort to show it can capture and dispose of debris left in low Earth orbit, OHB said. ESA aims to fly the mission in 2030.
As part of the exercise, ESA will aim to deorbit its PROBA-1 technology spacecraft after it has served out its life.
The target will be uncooperative, meaning it will not aid in its removal, and will not have been prepared for the mission.
“ClearSpace-1 paves the way to remove large nonfunctional objects from Earth orbit,” Rolf Densing, ESA's director of operations, said in a Nov. 17 statement. “Making active debris removal a viable option is crucial to enabling future policies and regulations that support sustainability in space,” he added.
ESA has been a driver of efforts to deal with
 the growing problem of space debris
, pushing companies and governments to adopt processes that avoid creating such objects that can be a hazard to other spacecraft.
The ClearSpace-1 mission will involve a spacecraft that can autonomously rendezvous with PROBA-1, using optical and radio-frequency sensors to help determine how the target is moving. The satellite then will use a specifically designed capture mechanism to snag PROBA-1, and both spacecraft will perform a controlled reentry to burn up in the atmosphere.

The European Space Agency has awarded German satellite builder OHB a €102 million contract to build ClearSpace-1, a spacecraft designed to capture and remove the agency’s Proba-1 satellite after it is retired from service.
Signed on 17 November at Space Tech Expo Europe in Bremen, Germany, the contract marks the start of the mission’s implementation phase. It also serves to cement the mission’s design and industrial structure, both of which have undergone substantial changes over the last six years.
In December 2020, ESA awarded an initial €86 million contract for the ClearSpace-1 mission to a consortium led by Swiss startup ClearSpace. At the time, the mission was expected to be launched in 2025 and remove a Vega payload adapter left in orbit following a launch in 2023. However, in August 2023, the adapter was struck by space debris, forcing a redesign and the selection of Proba-1 as the new target.
In April 2024, ESA announced that 
OHB would take over leadership of the restructured project
, with ClearSpace retaining responsibility for close-proximity and capture operations. The latest contract formalises OHB’s role as the project’s prime contractor for the implementation phase.
The restructuring also appears to have prompted a change in how the mission will be launched. In May 2023, ClearSpace signed a contract with Arianespace to launch the ClearSpace-1 mission aboard a Vega C rocket in the second half of 2026. However, a 
technical paper presented at the 9th European Conference on Space Debris in April 2025
 revealed that the mission would instead be launched aboard a dedicated microlauncher flight.
While the microlauncher in question has yet to be announced, an 
OHB presentation delivered at ESA’s Clean Space Days event in the Netherlands in July 2026
 may have provided insight into the matter. A mission overview slide features an illustration that appears to depict the RFA ONE rocket under the heading “Launch Service.”
The selection of the RFA ONE rocket for the mission would be notable given that OHB holds a majority stake in Rocket Factory Augsburg, the German company developing it. According to OHB’s latest financial report, the group holds a 56.8% stake in the rocket developer. However, neither ESA nor OHB has publicly confirmed RFA ONE as the mission’s launch vehicle.
According to ESA’s 18 November news release, the ClearSpace-1 mission is expected to be launched in 2030. While several months have passed since it was presented, OHB’s July 2026 presentation narrows that window to the second quarter of 2030.
