AI需要や産業向け需要の回復で出荷面積増
　SEMIは2026年10月21日（米国時間）、2026年第3四半期（2026年7〜9月）のシリコンウエハー世界出荷面積が37億5600万平方インチだったと発表した。前期比で5.1％増、前年同期比で13.4％増になる。
シリコンウエハー世界出荷面積の四半期ごとの推移［クリックで拡大］出所：SEMI
　SEMIによると、旺盛なAI関連需要と産業向け需要の回復が成長をけん引していて、AI関連では先端ロジックやメモリだけでなく、パワーデバイスやフォトニクスにも需要が広がっているという。ウエハーボンディングや先端パッケージング用途も、300mmポリッシュトウエハーの需要を下支えする。
2029年には170億3400平方インチに
　同年11月4日（米国時間）には2026年通期のシリコンウエハーの世界出荷面積が、前年度から11.8％増の144億2000万平方インチに達する見通しだと発表した。
　AI関連需要によって最先端ロジック向けのエピタキシャルウエハー、メモリ用途向けポリッシュトウエハーの需要が拡大。メモリでは広帯域メモリ（HBM）だけでなくサーバ向けDRAM、エンタープライズ向けNANDフラッシュにも需要が広がっているほか、成熟ノードラインの稼働率回復や、在庫調整の進展も出荷量増加に寄与した。
　SEMIでは、こうしたAI需要が引き続きシリコンウエハー需要の成長を支え、2027〜2029年にかけて過去最高の出荷量を更新し、2029年には170億3400万平方インチに達すると予想する。
2027〜2029年にかけて過去最高を更新見込み［クリックで拡大］出所：SEMI

2026年2Qのウエハー出荷面積は7.4％増　AI需要がけん引
SEMIによると、2026年第2四半期（4〜6月）の世界シリコンウエハー出荷面積は35億7300万平方インチとなった。前年同期比で7.4％の増加となり、前四半期に比べると9.1％増えた。
2026年Q1のウエハー出荷面積、前年同期比13.1％増
SEMIによれば、2026年第1四半期（1〜3月：1Q）の世界シリコンウエハー出荷面積は32億7500万平方インチになった。前年同期に比べ13.1％の増加だ。2025年第4四半期（10〜12月）に比べると季節変動もあって4.7％の減少となった。
シリコンウエハー世界市場、成長軌道は先端とレガシーで二極化
SEMIは、SEMI Silicon Manufacturers Group（SMG）による分析結果を基に2025年のシリコンウエハー世界市場を発表した。シリコンウエハーの出荷量（面積）は前年比で5.8％増の129億7300万平方インチとなったが、販売額は同1.2％減の114億米ドルだった。先端半導体とレガシー半導体でその動向は大きく分かれた。
300mm SiCウエハーでパワー半導体、Infineonが実物を初公開
Infineon Technologiesは、ドイツ・ミュンヘンで開催中のエレクトロニクス展示会「electronica 2026」（2026年11月10〜13日）において、パワーデバイスを形成した300mmのSiCウエハーの実物を初公開した。EETimes Japanは会場で同社のパワー＆センサーシステムズ事業部プレジデントを務めるAdam White氏に話を聞いた。
レゾナックが300mm SiC単結晶基板を開発
レゾナックは2026年9月15日、直径300mmの炭化ケイ素（SiC）単結晶の成長と基板加工に成功したと発表した。SiC基板では6インチから8インチへの移行が本格化する中、既に中国や米国のメーカーらがその先の300mm品について、相次いで開発成果を示していた。

Evertiq
Worldwide silicon wafer shipments rose 13.4% year on year in the third quarter, to 3,756 million square inches (MSI). Compared with the second quarter, shipments were up 5.1%.

Bonding and advanced packaging applications also continued to support demand for 300 mm polished wafers.

Worldwide silicon wafer shipments rose to 3,756 million square inches (MSI) in the third quarter of 2026, up 13.4% from a year earlier and 5.1% from the previous quarter, the SEMI Silicon Manufacturers Group (SMG) reported on November 4. The more useful number is the distance to the full-year forecast. Shipments for the first three quarters total 10,604 MSI, against the 14,420 MSI full-year forecast SEMI published on October 21. The fourth quarter therefore needs 3,816 MSI — just 1.6% above the third quarter — to reach it.

Year-on-year growth ran at 13.1% in the first quarter, 7.4% in the second and 13.4% in the third. The second-quarter narrowing says more about the comparison base — the second quarter of 2025 was that year's strongest quarter — than about demand. In absolute terms, shipments have risen for two straight quarters after the seasonal first-quarter dip, and the third-quarter figure is the highest in this series. Because the statistics are compiled by area rather than by wafer count, a rising share of 300mm wafers makes the area series the better read on actual material consumption.
Shipments have grown for two straight quarters after the seasonal Q1 2026 dip. VLSI Korea chart
What pushed it up — the AI demand base is broadening
Masatsugu Miura, chairman of the SMG and a sales and marketing general manager at SUMCO, cited strong AI-related demand and improving industrial demand. What changed in the explanation is the scope of the AI term. Demand that sat in advanced logic and memory now reaches power devices and photonics, while industrial demand strengthened on robotics, AI server power supplies and general-purpose industrial semiconductors. Bonding and advanced packaging applications also kept supporting demand for 300mm polished wafers. Set against the first-quarter release, where the then chairman linked weaker smartphone and PC shipments to tighter memory supply under HBM allocation, the demand center has widened somewhat from AI alone toward industry as well.
The arithmetic to the full-year forecast — 1.6% in Q4
SEMI's annual forecast puts 2026 shipments at 14,420 MSI, up 11.8% on the year, on a path of successive record highs from 2027 through 2029 that reaches 17,034 MSI at the end of the period. The stated drivers are AI demand supporting advanced epitaxial wafers for leading-edge logic and polished wafers for memory, memory demand extending beyond HBM to server DRAM and enterprise NAND, recovering mature-node utilization and easing customer inventory adjustments.
The 3,816 MSI needed in the fourth quarter is 11.0% more than the fourth quarter of 2025 shipped. Against the 3.7% sequential gain the fourth quarter of 2025 actually posted, the required 1.6% sequential rise is not a stretch on seasonality alone. Two caveats belong with it. First, SEMI states that the forecast is developed from input gathered from SMG members and does not represent an official forecast of the SMG or of any individual company. Second, the forecast's scope is polished and epitaxial wafers shipped to end users, excluding non-polished and reclaimed wafers; the quarterly release does not separately state whether its scope is identical.

More area does not automatically mean more revenue
Shipment area and wafer-maker revenue do not move in lockstep. The proof is 2025: annual shipments rose 5.8% to 12,973 MSI while wafer revenue slipped 1.2% to USD 11.4 billion. Volume becomes revenue only after price, product mix and long-term agreement terms have had their say.
This quarter's company numbers show the same gap. Siltronic's third-quarter sales rose 18.3% year on year to EUR 355.4 million, yet the company still booked an operating loss under the depreciation load of its new Singapore fab. GlobalWafers posted third-quarter revenue of NT$14,871 million, up 2.6% year on year but down 2.26% sequentially, with a 20.0% gross margin and EPS of NT$3.13. The company attributed the sequential decline to a fire on an 8-inch line at its Italian facility, while shipments at its Texas site set records for four consecutive months and both 12-inch SOI output in Missouri and revenue at its Niigata site in Japan hit quarterly records. In a quarter when worldwide area grew 5.1% sequentially, an individual maker's revenue can still move the other way on a plant incident and product mix.
What it means, and what to check next
Two judgments carry this release. First, wafer shipments are the furthest-upstream volume gauge in chip production, and growth here confirms that fab runs and material intake are actually rising. Second, with the growth drivers spreading from advanced AI processes into power devices, photonics and industrial uses, this advance is less exposed to a single application rolling over.
The checkpoints are four. First, whether fourth-quarter shipments clear 3,816 MSI and the full-year total beats the forecast; on last year's pattern, the confirmed annual figure arrives with the annual release the following February. Second, whether volume converts into price and profit in wafer makers' fourth-quarter results, against markers such as Siltronic's full-year sales and EBITDA margin guidance. Third, how fast new 300mm lines, of the kind GlobalWafers is ramping, turn customer qualifications into reported monthly revenue. Fourth, whether the broadened demand mix into industrial and power applications is still there in the first-quarter 2027 release. Area alone is not enough; whether maker revenue and margins follow is what decides how durable this cycle is.

On November 4, SEMI's Silicon Manufacturers Group (SMG) announced that global silicon wafer shipments in the third quarter of 2026 reached 3,756 million square inches (MSI). That was up 
13.4%
 from a year earlier and 
5.1%
 from the previous quarter. But the most notable aspect of the figure is how it relates to SEMI's full-year 2026 forecast of 14,420 MSI, issued on October 21. Based on results through the third quarter, fourth-quarter shipments need to grow only 
1.6%
 quarter on quarter to hit that forecast. Given recent shipment trends, there is a good chance that full-year results will exceed it.
AD
Q3 shipments reached 3,756 MSI, with year-on-year growth accelerating
According to SEMI, third-quarter shipments rose 
13.4%
 from 3,313 MSI in the same period a year earlier and 
5.1%
 from 3,573 MSI in the second quarter. Because the second quarter's year-on-year increase was 
7.4%
, the growth rate widened by about 6 percentage points.
The acceleration cannot be explained by last year's low base alone. Shipments in Q3 2025 fell 
0.4%
 from 3,327 MSI in Q2, so the weak comparison lifted the year-on-year growth rate by only about 0.4 percentage points. The acceleration mainly reflects the 
5.1%
 quarter-on-quarter increase in Q3 2026 shipments.
Masatsugu Miura, chairman of the SMG and head of SUMCO's sales division, cited strong AI-related demand and a recovery in industrial demand as drivers of the increase. He said AI demand is spreading beyond advanced logic chips and memory to power devices and photonics. In industrial applications, demand is improving for robotics, power supplies for AI servers and general-purpose industrial semiconductors, while the spread of wafer bonding and advanced packaging technologies is also supporting demand for 
300mm
 polished wafers.
Hitting the 14,420 MSI full-year forecast requires only 1.6% growth in Q4
In its annual forecast released on October 21, SEMI projected that global silicon wafer shipments in 2026 would rise 
11.8%
 year on year to 14,420 MSI. It also forecast that shipments would set a new record every year from 2027 through 2029, reaching 17,034 MSI in 2029. With the Q3 results now published, we can calculate concretely how much needs to ship to meet the 2026 forecast.
Shipments in 2026 were 3,275 MSI in Q1, 3,573 MSI in Q2 and 3,756 MSI in Q3, for a January–September total of 10,604 MSI. To reach the full-year forecast of 14,420 MSI, the fourth quarter needs to ship 3,816 MSI. That is an increase of 
just 1.6%
 from Q3's 3,756 MSI.
The Q3 quarter-on-quarter increase was 
5.1%
, and Q2 rose 
9.1%
, including a rebound from the seasonal dip in Q1. Compared with those figures, the 
1.6%
 growth needed to meet the full-year forecast is low. Even if Q4 shipments merely matched Q3 at 3,756 MSI, annual shipments would come to 14,360 MSI, only 60 MSI, or about 
0.4%
, short of the forecast.
Applying past seasonal patterns suggests even more upside. Shipments in Q4 2025 were 3,437 MSI, up 
3.7%
 from 3,313 MSI in Q3. If 2026 posts a similar gain, Q4 would reach about 3,900 MSI and the full year about 14,500 MSI. This is, of course, only a simple extrapolation of last year's seasonal pattern, but it suggests the 14,420 MSI forecast has room to be exceeded.
One caveat concerns the scope of the shipment figures. SEMI's full-year forecast covers the area of polished and epitaxial wafers shipped by wafer makers to end customers, and excludes non-polished and reclaimed wafers. The quarterly announcement, however, does not state its scope, so we cannot confirm that it matches the full-year forecast exactly. If the two use different criteria, the required Q4 shipment of 3,816 MSI should be treated only as a guide.
In addition, SEMI describes its 2026 full-year forecast as an 
11.8%
 increase from the previous year, but calculating the increase from 2025's actual annual shipments of 12,973 MSI to 14,420 MSI gives about 
11.2%
, which does not match the published growth rate. The base figure may have been revised, but SEMI does not state the base value it used. For that reason, this article bases its analysis not on the 
11.8%
 growth rate but on the absolute forecast of 14,420 MSI and the published quarterly results.
AD
Why the forecast may not be exceeded, and what to watch next
The full-year forecast is not necessarily conservative. The strongest counterargument is that SEMI may already have factored in slower growth in Q4. The 3,816 MSI needed to meet the forecast is 
11.0%
 higher than Q4 2025's 3,437 MSI. That is below Q3's 
13.4%
 growth and could be read as a reasonable forecast that anticipates slowing growth, given that Q4 2025 was relatively high.
Also, higher wafer shipment area does not necessarily mean revenue or end demand for semiconductors will grow at the same pace. Global silicon wafer shipments in 2025 rose 
5.8%
 from the previous year to 12,973 MSI, but revenue fell 
1.2%
 to $11.4 billion. SEMI attributed the revenue decline to a delayed recovery in demand for conventional applications and a pricing environment that has not improved. Growth in shipment area is an important indicator of a market recovery, but it does not directly guarantee improved earnings for wafer makers.
The effect of inventory adjustments cannot be ignored either. In its October full-year forecast, SEMI cited easing customer inventory adjustments as a tailwind for the shipment recovery, and future shipments may depend on changes in inventory levels. In the Q1 2026 statistics announcement, Ginji Yada, then SMG chairman, said "the recovery is not uniform" and noted that tight memory supply could dampen smartphone and PC shipments. Moreover, this full-year forecast aggregates views from industry participants and, according to SEMI, is not an official forecast of the SMG or of individual member companies.
Still, we maintain the view that full-year results could exceed the forecast. SEMI's forecast is based on information from wafer makers that are SMG members, so Q3 shipment trends may already have been reflected when it was published on October 21. Even so, the 
11.0%
 year-on-year growth needed in Q4 is almost the same as the 
11.2%
 year-on-year increase for the January–September cumulative total. The forecast does not assume that the 
13.4%
 growth recorded in Q3 will continue, and simply maintaining the previous quarter's shipment level would narrow the gap to the forecast to 60 MSI.
Future statistics will show whether this view is correct. SEMI released full-year 2025 shipment statistics on February 10, 2026, and annual results including Q4 2026 are expected around February 2027. Assuming past quarterly figures are not revised, if Q4 shipments fall below 3,816 MSI, the full year will miss 14,420 MSI and the upside view will prove wrong. Conversely, if they exceed 3,816 MSI, the forecast will be surpassed, and if they reach around 3,900 MSI, annual shipments would hit about 14,500 MSI. Beyond Q4 actual shipments, it will also be necessary to check for revisions to past quarterly data and the consistency of the reporting scope.
