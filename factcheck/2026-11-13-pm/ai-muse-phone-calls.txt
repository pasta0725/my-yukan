Previously, only a select number of Muse early adopters were able to try this feature after asking the AI for access. Now, anyone who pays for Muse can make this type of request. Currently, Muse subscription plans 
start at $20 per month
 for 500 million Muse tokens per week. Heavier AI users can opt to pay $100 per month for 3 billion tokens.
Muse is not the only AI agent able to place calls to businesses; the 
startup Instinct has been offering a similar option
, as has 
Google’s Gemini
. However, while the AI tools are now certainly capable of handling the task, the reception on the other end of the line can be mixed. Many businesses and receptionists 
hang up on calls from AI agents
 as soon as they identify that it’s not a human on the phone. But with AI now better able to mimic a real human voice, being able to tell the difference between a customer and an AI working on the customer’s behalf will get tougher.
Despite the challenges, this feature is one that could appeal to consumers who have grown frustrated with the state of customer service over the years. For too long, customers have been forced to navigate complex phone trees, and then sit on hold waiting for a human representative to pick up. If they’re lucky, that person can actually help them, and not transfer them to another department. Now, those consumers could potentially use their AI, with its infinite patience, for this tedious task instead.
While calls remain a paid-only feature for the time being, the recently added option to make a Muse email address will soon roll out to free users, 
Wang said
 this week.

Meta's Muse just got another ability that could make the AI agent feel a little bit more like an actual personal assistant. Muse is now able to 
place phone calls
 on your behalf, if you have a paid subscription.
With the change, Muse is able to call businesses to help with tasks like making reservations, scheduling appointments and other "calls you don't want to make," Meta's Chief AI officer Alexandr Wang said in 
an update
. The feature is available to those who have paid for a separate Muse subscription, which comes with additional tokens and some extra features.
Some users who have had access to the feature already have reported that Muse has been able to handle phone-related tasks with relative ease. Moltbook creator and Meta Superintelligence employee Matt Schlicht shared a recreation of a phone conversation his agent had with a Volvo dealership last month, noting that it was able to wait on hold in order to get the info he needed.

Interestingly, in the call shared by Schlicht, Muse doesn't explicitly identify itself as an AI agent though it notes it's calling his behalf and that it's transcribing the call. That may be enough for some straightforward tasks, but there's always a chance these types of calls could go off the rails or the person on the other end hangs up when they realize they're speaking with an AI.
Meta has been steadily churning out new features for Muse, which has already drawn more than 
10 million users
, according to the company. People can also now assign a 
dedicated email address
 to their agent in order to loop it into important emails without giving wholesale access to their inbox.
The company isn't the first to experiment with phone-calling abilities for AI. Google has introduced a number of similar features over the years, including "Call For Me" 
for Pixel phones
 and Instinct recently introduced a 
"Concierge" feature
 for its agent that's meant to handle more complex calls.

Meta has added outbound calls to its Muse AI agent for paid subscribers in the United States, letting the assistant book appointments and request information from businesses.
The calling feature began as a beta in September, with a public beta test opening by mid-September. It extends Muse’s existing tasks, which include sending emails, shopping and booking travel.
In an earlier limited test, human contractors helped when Muse struggled with a call. That setup raised privacy concerns because a person might hear a call that users expected software to handle. Meta rolled back the approach and indicated it would pursue calls handled by AI alone, with privacy disclosures.
The feature follows Meta’s Nov. 5 U.S. launch of 
real-time Muse voice chats and animated avatars
.
