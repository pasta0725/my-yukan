In a sudden and closely watched milestone, Tesla Robotaxi has edged past Waymo on the Texas Department of Motor Vehicles automated vehicle registry. As of November 10, Tesla lists 1,302 registered vehicles under Tesla Robotaxi, LLC, compared with Waymo’s 1,297.
The flip is narrow, but real, according to the State’s books. Tesla’s Texas fleet, following an expansion on November 10, 
now consists of 729 Model Ys and 573 purpose-built Cybercab vehicles
. Waymo’s roster is dominated by the Jaguar I-PACE and a growing number of Zeekr-based Ojai minivans.

Those figures come from the same public TxDMV automated vehicle listings that independent trackers have monitored since a Texas law required operators to disclose fleet sizes.
The scale of Tesla’s climb is pretty interesting and shows how much the company has grown its autonomous ride-hailing fleet over the past months. When the registry first became public in late May 2026, Tesla listed just 42 vehicles on it. Meanwhile, Waymo had several hundred vehicles registered to it.
Tesla CEO Elon Musk shades Waymo: ‘Never really had a chance’
Since then, Tesla has multiplied its authorized Texas fleet more than thirty times, driven by repeated 
Model Y additions and a steady build of Cybercabs
. Waymo has also expanded, but at a slower pace than Tesla.
It is also important to note that registrations are not necessarily equal to operational fleet. The TxDMV list shows the number of vehicles each company is permitted to run, but it does not report how many are actively carrying riders, which cities they serve, or trip volume. Both companies stage vehicles and run mixed fleets that can include units in testing or temporarily offline.
A lead on the registry therefore signals capacity and regulatory positioning more than immediate ride volume.

Tesla has use the growing authorized fleet to expand its unsupervised service beyond the original Austin geofence area, as now it is 
flowing into cities like San Antonio and Dallas
. The company has highlighted plans for a broader Cybercab deployment, eventually into new states and territories.
Waymo is operating across multiple Texas markets and in other areas of the United States.
Right now, the five-vehicle margin is thin and could reverse with the next large registration from either side. For the moment, however, the public Texas record shows Tesla holding the larger authorized Robotaxi fleet in the state.

The move arrives days after

SpaceX Starship is going for a catch that could change spaceflight forever
SpaceX will try to catch both Starship stages today, a first for any orbital rocket.
Published
7 hours ago
on
November 13, 2026
By
Gene
SpaceX is set to launch Starship Flight 15 from Starbase on Friday afternoon, and if both stages make it back to the tower, the mission will go down as the first time an orbital rocket has ever been fully recovered. The 75 minute launch window opens at 4:45 p.m. CT from Pad 2, a day after SpaceX rolled Ship 42 out to the pad and 
shared photos of the move on X
.
The first catch comes quickly. About six and a half minutes after liftoff, Booster 22 is scheduled to fly back to Starbase and settle into the launch tower’s chopstick arms, the first catch attempt for SpaceX’s V3 Super Heavy. SpaceX has caught the booster three times before most recently in March 2025.
Ship 42 moves to the Starbase pad ahead of launch https://t.co/pMUFTHneJQ
— SpaceX (@SpaceX) 
November 13, 2026
The second catch is the one that has never been done. Ship 42 will climb to an orbit about 275 kilometers up, deploy 26 Starlink V3 satellites and complete about 14 orbits. Three of those satellites carry cameras to inspect the ship’s heat shield before it commits to coming home. If its reentry, landing burn and tower approach systems pass their checks, the ship will fire its engines to leave orbit, fall through the atmosphere in its belly flop position, flip vertical and drop into the tower about 22 and a half hours after launch, on Saturday afternoon. SpaceX gave a preview of that maneuver 
in an animation during its third quarter earnings webcast
.
Elon Musk described a successful double catch on that call as “the first time that an orbital launch vehicle has been fully recovered” and a “profound technology milestone,” putting the odds of catching the ship at “more likely than not.” SpaceX has called it a potential “breakthrough moment in making Starship a reliable, rapidly reusable rocket,”
Advertisement
-
Reuse is where the economics change. Falcon 9 lands its boosters routinely, but its upper stage is thrown away on every flight. Starship is designed so both stages can be refueled and flown again, which is key to cutting launch costs. That matters for Starlink, where each V3 satellite is expected to carry roughly 10 times the download capacity of the V2 Mini satellites flying today, and for Starmind, SpaceX’s planned constellation of AI compute satellites.
It matters for NASA too. Starship is the lunar lander for the Artemis program, and reaching the Moon requires refilling a ship in orbit using several tanker flights. That plan only works if Starships can fly again and again. Musk has said he expects Starship to reach a weekly launch cadence in the first half of 2027.
Investors are paying attention. Morgan Stanley’s Adam Jonas wrote this week that a catch of both stages would likely be “a material positive” for SpaceX stock.
The last Starship flight in September reached orbit for the first time but ended after about three hours with a splashdown in the Pacific, short of its planned 10 hours, after some engines shut down. Since then, Booster 22 has completed a full 33 engine static fire and Ship 42 a six engine firing, as 
Teslarati previously reported
. As of Friday morning, SpaceX was still waiting on the FAA to approve a license change covering the ship’s return over land.

Tesla Passes Waymo in Texas Robotaxi Registrations, 1,302 to 1,297
Tesla's count on the state's driverless vehicle register edged ahead of Waymo's late on Nov. 10, though the list shows vehicles allowed to operate, not vehicles carrying riders.
Sam Okonkwo, Robotaxi · Nov. 11, 2026 · 3 min
Photo: Steve Jurvetson / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 4.0)
EVwire
AUSTIN, Texas - Tesla now has more driverless vehicles on the books in Texas than Waymo. The state's automated vehicle register lists 1,302 vehicles under Tesla Robotaxi LLC and 1,297 under Waymo LLC, a lead of five, according to counts of the 
Texas Department of Motor Vehicles register
 published by 
EVwire
 and 
TeslaNorth
.
The lead changed hands just before midnight on Nov. 10, Texas time, when Tesla added a batch of 20 Model Y vehicles to its authorization and went from 1,282 to 1,302, EVwire reported. Until then Waymo had been 15 vehicles ahead. On Nov. 1, TeslaNorth counted 1,204 vehicles for Tesla and 1,259 for Waymo.
The climb has been steep. Tesla had 42 vehicles registered in late May, when Waymo had 619, according to EVwire's records of the register. Waymo has roughly doubled its Texas fleet since then, often adding vehicles in large batches, while Tesla's count has grown more than thirtyfold.
The register itself lists only vehicle identification numbers. Decoding those numbers through the federal vehicle database, EVwire counted 729 Model Ys and 573 purpose-built Cybercabs in Tesla's Texas fleet, and 874 Jaguar I-PACEs and 423 Ojai vehicles in Waymo's. Tesla's Cybercab count has held at 573 since Oct. 27, while it has added 222 Model Ys over the same period.
The figures have limits. A vehicle on the register is one a company is allowed to operate without a driver, not one that is carrying riders. The state does not publish how many vehicles are in service at any moment, which cities they serve or how many trips they complete, so the count does not show which company runs the larger active ride-hailing fleet. Under the state's program, companies must hold an authorization before operating driverless vehicles commercially and keep their own lists current by adding and removing individual vehicles, 
TeslaNorth noted
.
Tesla and Waymo together account for about three-quarters of the 3,424 vehicles that 17 companies have registered in Texas, according to 
EVwire's tracker
. Avride is a distant third with 344 vehicles, and the rest of the list is made up largely of autonomous truck operators, led by Aurora with 91.
Tesla now runs unsupervised Robotaxi service in five Texas cities: Austin, Dallas, Houston, Fort Worth and San Antonio, where it opened this week. The company said on its third-quarter earnings call that Cybercab rides would begin in Dallas this month and that it aims to run Robotaxi around the clock by the end of the year.
