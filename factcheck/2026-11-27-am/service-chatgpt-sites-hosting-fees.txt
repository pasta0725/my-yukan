　米OpenAIは2027年2月1日から、ChatGPTで作成したウェブサイトの運営に、月間の利用枠を超えた分だけ
追加料金
を課す予定だ。対象は、サイト作成機能「Sites」を利用するChatGPT Plus、Pro、Businessの契約者。データベース付きのサイトは、データベースが空でアクセスが一切なくても利用枠を消費する。
     提供：Algi Febri Sugita/SOPA Images/LightRocket/Getty Images
  ※クリックすると拡大画像が見られます
　「
Sites
」は、ChatGPTやCodexに作りたいものを伝えるだけで、ウェブサイトやアプリを作成し、URLで共有できる機能だ。サイトの作成や編集はCodexの利用量に計上される。一方、サイトの運営にかかる利用量は、ページの読み込みやデータの保存などに応じて別途計算される。
データベース付きならアクセスゼロでも1サイト月約25クレジット
　サイトの運営には「クレジット」と呼ばれる単位を用いる。毎月の利用枠はPlusが125クレジット、Proが375クレジット、Businessが契約1席あたり125クレジット。複数のサイトを運営する場合は、全サイトでこの利用枠を共有する。Enterpriseの料金体系は契約によって異なる。
    ChatGPT Sitesで作れる社内情報サイトの例（画像：OpenAI）
    ※クリックすると拡大画像が見られます
　データの保存機能を使わないサイトには、サイトごとの基本分のクレジット消費はない。一方、データベース付きのサイトは、データの有無にかかわらず1サイトあたり月約25クレジットを消費する。例えば、Plusの契約者が空のデータベース付きサイトを6つ運営すると、アクセスが一切なくても月に約150クレジットを消費し、利用枠を約25クレジット超える計算になる。
　ページや画像などの読み込みには、10万リクエストあたり約15クレジットを消費する。1回のページ閲覧で通常30〜50件のリクエストが発生するという。データベースに約1.07GBのデータを1カ月間保存すると、基本分とは別に約150クレジットを消費する。独自ドメインの利用にも1件あたり月約25クレジットがかかる。
　サイトの運営は2027年1月31日まで無料で、同年1月1日からはアプリでクレジットの消費量を確認できるようになる予定だ。利用枠を超えた場合の日本円での料金や、登録済みのクレジットカードに自動で請求されるかどうかは、公式の料金案内では確認できない。

===== https://maccus.fr/en/chatgpt-sites-hosting-charges/
(status 200)
OpenAI plans to start charging subscribers for hosting the websites and small apps they publish with ChatGPT Sites, a website builder inside the chatbot. Hosting stays free through January 31, 2027, according to the company’s documentation as of November 26, 2026. Starting February 1, OpenAI intends to charge customers on its paid Plus, Pro and Business plans for hosting above a monthly allowance. That allowance is counted in credits, the unit OpenAI uses to bill usage beyond what a plan includes.
In OpenAI’s own example, a site that keeps 1 GiB (one gibibyte, about 1.07 gigabytes) of saved records for a month uses about 175 credits. That is 50 credits more than the Plus plan’s monthly allowance of 125. OpenAI publishes no single price for a credit, so its public pages do not say what those 50 extra credits would cost in dollars.
The schedule has also slipped by a month, and no entry in OpenAI’s release notes, its public log of product changes, mentions the move.
A website builder inside ChatGPT
OpenAI released Sites as a public beta, an early test version, on July 9, 2026. Its release note described a way to turn “your work or ideas into an interactive website or lightweight app without leaving ChatGPT.” Its examples included dashboards, project trackers, internal portals and reports.
Customers on Business and Enterprise, OpenAI’s plans for companies, already had access to Sites at that point. Starting July 9, they could publish sites to anyone through a public web address.
The beta then rolled out to more plans, including Pro and Edu (education), with Plus users “following over the coming days.” Sites is not offered on the Free plan or on Go, OpenAI’s entry-level paid plan.
Europe was partly left out at launch. The July 9 note said public publishing and the expanded beta “are not available in the EEA, Switzerland, or the United Kingdom at launch.” The EEA, or European Economic Area, includes the European Union. As of November 26, no later entry in OpenAI’s release notes says that has changed.
Other OpenAI products are reaching Europe in stages. Dots, the always-on agents in ChatGPT that keep working between conversations, are one case: the company’s 
dots agents are reaching Europe through business accounts first
.
Free hosting through January 31
Two OpenAI pages give the schedule in identical words: the Sites “Usage and cost” documentation and the FAQ on OpenAI’s pricing page. “We plan to show your Sites hosting usage in the app starting January 1, 2027,” both pages say. “Hosting is free through January 31, 2027.” OpenAI plans to start charging for use above the monthly allowance on February 1, 2027, while Enterprise billing “follows your organization’s agreement.”
OpenAI defines hosting as the traffic a site receives and the storage it uses. Building and editing a site is counted separately, as usage of Codex, OpenAI’s AI agent for writing code. Starting in December, Codex usage on the Plus, Pro and Business plans 
shares one token-based limit with ChatGPT itself
. Tokens are the small units of information that ChatGPT reads and writes.
OpenAI’s help center article on Sites does not mention the 2027 dates. It says “Sites hosting is currently included with ChatGPT Plus and Pro,” and that Enterprise hosting charges “are currently waived.” The article was last updated in early November.
One allowance for all of a user’s sites
On the Plus, Pro and Business plans, all of a user’s sites share one monthly hosting allowance. OpenAI sets it at 125 credits on Plus, 375 credits on Pro and 125 credits per seat, or paid user, on Business. “You can create as many sites as you want,” the documentation says.
Credits per site, per request and per gigabyte
OpenAI lists its hosting rates in “approximate” credits. A site without storage has no monthly base fee. A site with storage costs 25 credits a month, even when its database is empty.
Traffic costs 15 credits per 100,000 requests. A request is any page, image or other resource a browser loads. OpenAI says one page visit typically generates 30 to 50 requests. By that measure, 100,000 requests come to roughly 2,000 to 3,300 page visits.
Saved database records, such as form responses, cost 150 credits per GiB per month. Uploaded files, such as images or PDFs, cost 3.75 credits per GiB per month. A custom domain, meaning the site’s own web address, adds 25 credits a month. Registering the domain costs extra.
In OpenAI’s worked example, a site that stores 1 GiB of records for the full month uses about 175 credits. That is the 25-credit base fee plus 150 credits for storage.
Monthly hosting credits for four example sites, against the Plus and Pro allowances. Data: OpenAI, Sites usage and cost documentation, accessed November 26, 2026. Examples calculated from the rate card.
At OpenAI’s listed rates, a site without storage starts at 0 credits before traffic. A site with an empty database, a custom domain and 100,000 requests a month would use about 65 credits, inside the Plus allowance with 60 credits to spare. The same site holding 1 GiB of records would use about 215 credits: 90 over the Plus allowance and 160 under the Pro allowance.
No single public price per credit
OpenAI does not publish one public price for a credit on ChatGPT plans. Its help article on credits for personal plans, such as Plus and Pro, says that purchase amounts, prices and payment methods “can vary by account, region, and plan.” Subscribers see the price at checkout, in their own account.
Plus and Pro users who reach their usage limit can buy extra credits without upgrading their plan. The help article lists Codex, the ChatGPT Work agent for longer tasks and ChatGPT for Word, Excel and PowerPoint among the features credits can pay for. Sites hosting is not on that list.
OpenAI’s own pages said December 31
The earlier dates survive in copies of OpenAI’s pages kept by the Internet Archive, a nonprofit digital library. The copy of the Sites documentation captured on November 14, 2026, read: “Hosting is free through December 31, 2026. Starting January 1, 2027, Plus, Pro, and Business customers will pay for hosting usage above their monthly allowance.”
The same copy said users would be able to see each site’s traffic and storage usage starting December 1, 2026. It described the rates as “proposed to take effect on January 1, 2027.” In that version, traffic cost 16 credits per 100,000 requests, compared with 15 today.
A copy of the FAQ on OpenAI’s pricing page, captured on November 24, still said “Hosting is free through December 31, 2026.” The next day, ASCII.jp, a Japanese tech news site, reported that charging would start on February 1.
Both dates have moved one month later: free hosting now ends January 31 instead of December 31, and the in-app usage display starts January 1 instead of December 1. OpenAI’s current pages carry no note about the change. As of November 26, it appears in none of the ChatGPT, ChatGPT Business or ChatGPT Enterprise and Edu release notes, and no OpenAI blog post about it could be found.
Questions the documentation leaves open
The schedule is now worded as an intention. The November 14 copy said customers “will pay.” The current pages use “We plan to” for both the January 1 usage display and the February 1 start of charging. Every figure on the rate card is still labeled approximate.
Neither the Sites usage and cost page nor the help center says what happens to a site once its owner’s allowance runs out and no credits are bought. They do not say whether such a site would be paused, blocked or billed. They also do not say whether hosting above the allowance on Plus and Pro would be paid from the same credit balance that pays for extra Codex use.
What it means
For a simple site that stores no data, the change may cost nothing, because it pays only for traffic. At 15 credits per 100,000 requests, the Plus allowance of 125 credits covers about 833,000 requests a month, or roughly 16,700 to 27,800 page visits, shared across all of the account’s sites.
Storage drives the bill. Every site with storage pays a base fee of 25 credits a month, even with an empty database. Five such sites would use the whole Plus allowance before a single visit.
Forms that save responses, directories and small internal tools all keep records. One GiB of those records costs 150 credits a month, more than the entire Plus allowance of 125.
OpenAI’s own pages set out the dates, allowances and rates. They do not give the price of a credit in dollars or euros.
Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI assistant, is taking a different route. Since November 24, it has been rolling out a beta option that 
installs the tools Claude builds as Mac and Windows apps
, so they run on the user’s own computer. With ChatGPT Sites, OpenAI instead hosts the sites people build and counts that hosting against a monthly credit allowance.
The next date to watch is January 1, when OpenAI plans to start showing hosting usage in the app. Before charging begins on February 1, its pages have yet to give the price of a credit in each region or the rule for a site that exceeds its allowance. Whether February 1 holds is the other open point, since the first date moved without a release note.

OpenAI will start charging for ChatGPT Sites hosting on 1 February 2027, its own documentation now says, giving site owners one more free month than the page promised days ago. Hosting stays free through 31 January, and usage above a monthly allowance will then draw on credits.
What the meter charges
Every Plus, Pro and Business customer gets a monthly hosting allowance shared across all their sites: 125 credits on Plus, 375 on Pro and 125 per seat on Business. Enterprise workspaces draw on their contracted credit pool instead, while dollar-commitment customers pay separate hosting and Codex rates from one commitment.
The rates themselves are approximate credits, and the database is where the money goes. A site with storage costs 25 credits a month even when its database is empty, traffic runs 15 credits per 100,000 requests, and each gigabyte of saved records adds 150 credits a month.
OpenAI’s own example makes the squeeze plain: a site holding one gigabyte of records for a month uses about 175 credits, the 25-credit base fee plus 150 for storage. That is 50 credits more than a whole Plus allowance, and just under half of Pro’s 375.
Static sites escape the meter almost entirely. A site with no storage pays nothing to stay online, and its only charge is traffic, at roughly 15 credits per 100,000 requests. One page visit typically generates 30 to 50 requests, so a Plus allowance stretches to tens of thousands of visits.
The date moved
The February start is itself new. Write-ups that quoted OpenAI’s page on 24 November gave free hosting through 31 December and billing from 1 January; the page today gives 31 January and 1 February. OpenAI has said nothing about the change, and the new dates appear only in the documentation.
Usage will become visible before billing begins. OpenAI plans to show hosting usage inside the app from 1 January, a month before the first charges land. Site owners can also run the numbers now in the estimator on the usage page, which weighs estimated use against the plan allowance.
Why It Matters
Sites turned ChatGPT into a host as well as a builder, and OpenAI said more than five million sites were created in its first three months. Until now they have all run free, so February is the first moment owners learn what their pages actually cost to keep online.
The sting is in the database, not the traffic. A form that saves responses, or a tracker that stores rows, can push a single site past the whole Plus allowance before a visitor arrives. Owners who treat Sites as a free host for data-heavy pages will feel the meter first.
Our read
OpenAI is quietly turning a free beta into a metered product, and the one-month delay reads as a soft launch rather than a rethink. The allowance is generous for static pages and tight for anything that stores data, which pushes builders toward file storage and away from the database.
The missing piece is dollars. OpenAI publishes no price for a credit, so nobody can say what 50 extra credits cost until the first bills arrive. That gap matters more than the rates themselves, and it is the number to watch when February comes.

A request loads a page, image, or other resource. One page visit typically generates 30–50 requests, depending on the site.
A site with an empty database uses about 25 credits per month before traffic. If it stores 1 GiB of records for the full month, the base fee and database storage together use about 175 credits. One GiB is about 1.07 GB.
Private connections on Enterprise
A Site–tunnel connection costs approximately 125 credits for 30 days, prorated daily. Each Site–tunnel pair counts once per UTC day, even if the Site is unpublished or sends no traffic. A tunnel with no Sites attached during the day counts as zero connections.
Three Sites that share one tunnel count as three connections, even if they attach at different times that day.
One Site attached to two tunnels counts as two connections, including when it switches from one tunnel to the other.
Disconnecting and reconnecting the same Site to the same tunnel on the same UTC day counts as one connection.
