Walmart will now let Meta’s Muse shop on your behalf, allowing the AI agent to find and purchase Black Friday deals via Walmart’s Sparky AI assistant. The retail giant is rolling out a live implementation of the

AI agents are ready to shop. Some retailers are wary
Muse
 can make purchases through Walmart’s “Agent Profiles,” which provide a dedicated space for agent activity within your Walmart customer account. These profiles also allow you to set spending limits, approve purchases, and check the purchase history of everything bought by Muse. While other online retailers like 
Amazon
 and 
eBay
 have blocked AI agents from browsing their platforms, Walmart says that support for Meta’s Muse is “just the beginning,” and that additional Walmart shopping experiences for other AI agents and apps are “on the way.”
Here are Walmart’s instructions for shopping its website via Muse:
1. Tell Muse what you need.
 For example, ask it to find a great TV deal or show you which Walmart Black Friday deals are available for delivery. Muse can help you explore products and build your basket.
2. Set your shopping rules.
 Muse will take you to Walmart to sign in, then bring you back to Muse. The first time you add an item to your cart, you’ll set up your Agent Profile on Walmart within your Walmart account, including a spending limit you’re comfortable with.
3. Check out.
 Complete your purchase within the conversation using a card saved in your Walmart account, with the same Walmart prices you’d find on Walmart.com. And if something isn’t quite right, Walmart’s return policy still applies.

 Walmart informed The Verge after publication that the Agent Gateway launch has been delayed until November 24th.
Update, November 23rd:
 Walmart contacted The Verge again to say the update is no longer being delayed, and will roll out today on November 23rd.

Your next Black Friday haul might be picked out by software that never has to fight for a parking spot. 
Walmart
 is now letting 
Meta
’s Muse shop on its website on behalf of users, finding and buying holiday deals without a human clicking through every page.
The move turns Walmart’s Agent Gateway, announced last month, into a working system just as the busiest shopping stretch of the year arrives. It also marks a clear split from 
Amazon
, which has taken a much more guarded approach to the same AI agent.
How the Agent Gateway works
Walmart first unveiled the Agent Gateway and a companion feature called Agent Profiles on October 21, 2026. The live rollout means AI agents, starting with Muse, can search for products, assemble shopping carts, and complete purchases on Walmart.com and Sam’s Club.
Under the hood, the system relies on the Model Context Protocol, or MCP. Think of it as a dedicated service entrance for robots, instead of making them squeeze through the front door built for people.
Without something like MCP, an AI agent typically has to operate a web browser the way a person would. It reads pages, clicks buttons, and hopes the layout doesn’t change mid-checkout. MCP lets the agent talk directly to Walmart’s backend systems, which sidesteps a lot of that fragility.
Walmart says the gateway helps agents surface relevant products without handing over a shopper’s purchase history. According to the company, agents will not get access to extensive customer purchase records.

Agent Profiles put guardrails on the bots
Agent Profiles are Walmart’s answer to the obvious question: what stops an AI from going on a spending spree? The feature gives users a way to manage how their AI agents interact with Walmart.
Shoppers can set spending limits and monitor transactions the agent makes. Access to payment information is also restricted, keeping sensitive details out of the agent’s reach.

Walmart is also stressing that prices will be the same whether a human or an agent is doing the shopping. That pledge addresses a quiet worry in AI commerce: that bots might see different deals than people, or that retailers might use agents to steer buyers toward pricier items.
Muse’s fast rise and the Amazon snub
Meta launched Muse on September 8, 2026. Within weeks, the AI shopping agent had picked up more than 10 million users across the US and Canada.
Amazon chose to restrict Muse’s access after the agent launched, limiting what it could do on Amazon’s platform.
Muse adjusted accordingly. It now defaults to searching retailers such as Walmart and Target, skipping Amazon unless a user specifically asks for it.
Why Black Friday is the real test
Black Friday sales run from November 23 to November 29, 2026. That window gives Walmart a high-pressure environment to see whether agent-driven shopping holds up at scale.
The rollout reflects a broader trend of retailers leaning on AI to engage customers during peak shopping periods. Walmart’s version stands out because it doesn’t just add a chatbot to its site. It opens a structured path for outside AI agents to transact directly.
What this means for retail and shoppers
The bigger story is a philosophical fork in how major retailers treat AI agents. Walmart is building a formal on-ramp with privacy limits, spending controls, and price parity. Amazon, at least for now, is keeping Muse at arm’s length.
With Muse now defaulting to rivals, Amazon risks missing traffic from a user base that already exceeds 10 million people.
For shoppers, the practical upside is convenience with guardrails. Spending caps and transaction monitoring reduce the odds of an AI making an expensive mistake, while limits on purchase history access ease some privacy concerns.

The Verge reported
 on November 23, 2026, that 
Walmart’s Agent Gateway
 is live with support for Meta’s Muse, allowing the AI agent to find and purchase Walmart products, including Black Friday deals, through customer-controlled Agent Profiles.
Walmart’s 
Agent Gateway page
 also presents Muse shopping as currently available. The setup involves telling Muse what to find, connecting a Walmart account, creating an Agent Profile and choosing shopping controls before checkout.
How a Muse Purchase Works
A shopper can ask 
Muse
 for a television deal or for Walmart Black Friday deals available for delivery, then explore products and build a basket in the conversation. The customer signs in through a Walmart handoff and returns to Muse. Adding the first item to the cart triggers Agent Profile setup, including a spending limit chosen by the customer.
Checkout stays inside the Muse conversation and uses a card already saved in the Walmart account. The customer pays the same prices shown on Walmart.com, and Walmart’s return policy continues to apply.
Customer Controls and Checkout Rules
An Agent Profile keeps the agent’s activity in a dedicated space within the Walmart account, separate from the customer’s other purchases. Walmart’s product page says customers can set spending limits, choose which purchases need approval, and review and track what the agent buys.
Under Walmart’s 
Agentic Access Terms
, only the customer may designate the payment method an agent can use. The agent cannot add or change payment methods and never receives raw stored payment-card data. Outside the Gateway, Walmart requires human approval before every agent checkout; within it, autonomous checkout can proceed when the agent stays within customer-configured guardrails, although Walmart may require additional approval.
The practical effect is that Muse can complete an authorized purchase without receiving the customer’s card details or gaining unrestricted authority over the account. The customer can delegate the transaction while retaining the payment and approval boundaries.
Gateway Design and Beta Limits
Walmart introduced Agent Profiles, the Gateway and agent terms in an 
October 21, 2026 announcement
, with profiles expected in the following month and an initial Gateway beta for companies and developers. It said browser-based agents can be sluggish and prone to mistakes on websites designed around human navigation and sign-ins. The Gateway uses an MCP or similar connection protocol instead, allowing an external agent to shop with 
Sparky, Walmart’s own agent
. Walmart also said the connection can help identify a customer’s usual products without disclosing broader personal information or purchase history to the agent.
The current rollout still has explicit limits. Walmart describes the overall Gateway as an early beta with invitation-only access that may be suspended at any time, and says some features may change or remain unavailable to some participants. The live Muse integration operates within those restrictions.
Walmart said support for Muse was the beginning, with shopping experiences for other AI agents and apps on the way. The Muse implementation gives Walmart a working model for those additional integrations, with the retailer controlling the connection and transaction rules and customers retaining control over spending and approval.
