Casio’s GM-5600HT26-1JR wraps Tomoyasu Hotei’s grey Guitarhythm pattern around a metal-bezel square, and Japan is getting a second shot at buying it this month. The second preorder window runs November 25 through November 30 at Hotei’s own stores, first come and first served.

It shuts early if the allocation runs out, and round one buyers need not apply this time. It’s the fourth Casio and Hotei collaboration, after three earlier anniversary watches. The occasion is Hotei’s 45th year.
Hotei has influenced Japan’s rock scene for decades, and this is the fourth time Casio has built a G-Shock with him. The pattern is his own Guitarhythm maze.
Hotei’s stores ran the 
first preorder round
 from October 14 to October 19, and those watches start shipping from late November onward to their buyers. The second round is the last early window before Casio’s own sale starts in December. Fans who buy through Hotei’s stores also get a pair of double-name picks the Casio channels don’t include.
Round two runs on stricter rules
One watch per person carries over from round one, unchanged. The new part is a lockout that reaches backward into the first round and shuts its buyers out of this one.
Anyone who completed an order in the first round can’t buy in the second at either store, whatever their payment status, and second-round buyers can’t place a second order either at any point. The lockout is the headline. Hotei says the rule spreads a capped allocation across as many buyers as possible, first come and first served.
Hotei caps the allocation, and warns the sale can end without notice once the planned number is reached, even inside the five-day window at either store. First-round buyers checking a shipping status may see a second-round label on the page, a system quirk Hotei flags in the same notice, so read it as a display bug, nothing more.
Orders run online only, through the beat crazy Store for fan club members and the Hotei.com Store, and tour venues take no preorders at all during the window. Second-round watches start shipping in early December, rolling out in order as orders clear, a few days behind the first round’s late November start. Once an order is complete it can’t be cancelled, and anyone ordering the watch alongside other items waits for a single combined shipment, everything in one box.
The pattern does the talking
Across the face and the resin band runs the grey Guitarhythm pattern, printed big enough that the maze-like geometry reads clearly from across a room, no squinting. Grey on black, end to end.
Casio engraved the Guitarhythm mark into the caseback, printed 45TH on the band keeper, and finished the buckle and buttons in black IP over the steel. The packaging gets the same treatment, the pattern wrapped over the whole box instead of a plain lid. A red LED backlight is the single hit of color on the watch, and it flashes in sync with the alarm, the hourly signal and the countdown timer behind the patterned face.
Bonus picks carry both names and ship only with Hotei Store orders. The special box wears the pattern on every face, so the packaging becomes part of the edition rather than a plain container for the watch inside.
Standard GM-5600 hardware underneath
Underneath the graphics sits the regular metal-bezel GM-5600. Nothing exotic hides inside, and nothing needs to.
Casio’s case measures 49.6 by 43.2 by 12.9 millimeters and the watch weighs 80 grams, with a resin case under the stainless steel bezel and a band fitting wrists from 145 to 215 millimeters. Water resistance runs to 20 bar, and the glass is plain mineral, not sapphire, over the display, and the caseback is steel.
Inside sits the standard digital toolkit: a 1/100-second stopwatch, a 24-hour countdown timer with auto-repeat, multi-function alarms, an hourly signal and a full auto calendar that never needs setting. Accuracy runs plus or minus 15 seconds a month, and Casio rates the battery at about five years of use. Every function here matches the standard GM-5600 line, part for part, with nothing held back.
Its red LED backlight carries an afterglow setting, and the flash alert blinks that light in time with the alarms, the hourly signal and the timer in the dark. Five years of battery life means the watch asks nothing of its owner between now and the next anniversary edition.
