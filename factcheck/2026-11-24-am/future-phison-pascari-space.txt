Phison Electronics
, a reference in NAND flash storage solutions, announced 
the launch of Pascari Space
, a new category of purpose-built SSD solutions engineered for commercial outer space applications and orbital infrastructure, on display this week at the SC26 conference.
Built upon Phison’s decades of NAND engineering expertise and flight-proven storage heritage, Pascari Space leverages Phison IMAGIN+ customization and design services to deliver mission-specific storage solutions built to withstand the extreme demands of outer space.
As terrestrial data infrastructure faces mounting challenges related to power consumption, cooling demands, physical space constraints and natural disaster risks, enterprise and commercial organizations are increasingly exploring alternative approaches to safeguarding critical data. At the same time, declining launch costs are accelerating the growth of orbital infrastructure and satellite-based applications, supporting a developing outer space economy with everything from environmental monitoring and deforestation prevention to space exploration and scientific discovery. Together, these trends are driving demand for reliable, high-performance storage solutions engineered to operate in the extreme conditions of outer space.
Today, Phison is addressing these challenges by building upon its proven outer space heritage, with storage technology deployed aboard the 
Mars Rover
, and on the first data center hardware on the moon
 deployed by 
Lonestar Data Holdings 
in 2025. These 
milestones established the flight-proven nature behind Pascari SSDs and their TRL-9 status achievement. The foundation was set for Pascari Space to deliver configurable SSD solutions engineered for data integrity, endurance and resilience in environments where failure is not recoverable.
“Space is rapidly becoming the next frontier for data infrastructure, but the demands placed on storage in flight are uniquely amplified vs. what is table stakes on Earth,”
 said Michael Wu, GM and president, Phison US. 
“With Pascari Space, we’re extending our expertise beyond Earth, giving customers the ability to configure storage for their specific mission rather than compromise with fixed-configuration technology. From flight-criticalsystems to orbital data centers, we’re helping build the foundation for the next gen of space infrastructure.”
Mission-Specific Storage Engineered for Extreme Environments. The new Pascari Space portfolio includes three purpose-built product lines:
Flight Critical
: Wide-temperature, DRAM-less storage for positions on the spacecraft where ultimate resiliency comes before raw performance
Main Storage
: High-performance storage for general spacecraft systems and onboard compute, balancing endurance and capability
Orbital Data Center
: High-capacity storage for orbital data centers and bulk data in orbit
Unlike fixed-configuration storage, Pascari Space offers mission-specific SSD solutions through Phison’s in-house controller and firmware engineering – including up to 60× endurance¹, optimized data scrubbing and randomized RAID mapping. Through IMAGIN+, Phison works with customers to configure endurance, retention, endurance, error handling, characterization, screening, validation and radiation resilience through component selection and firmware adjustments to meet their specific mission requirements.
Pascari Space also delivers layered data protection through LDPC error correction, SmartECC, SmartRefresh and SRAM interleaving. Total Ionizing Dose (TID) and Single Event Effects (SEE) radiation characterization, including heavy-ion testing and post- manufacturing lot testing services, are available to support mission-specific reliability and resilience.
Powering the Next gen of Commercial Orbital Data Centers. Building upon its previous lunar collaboration with Lonestar Data Holdings, Phison will support the company’s first commercial orbital data center node of the StarVault network, StarVault-001 (SV-001), anticipated to launch in the first half of 2027. The mission will carry 488 TB of Phison Pascari Space storage, providing approximately 370 TB of usable capacity in low Earth orbit.
“Data is the new gold, underpinning our economies, businesses and AI workflows, making its protection more critical than ever,”
 said Chris Stott, founder, Lonestar Data Holdings.
 “With StarVault, Lonestar is building a secure, sovereign data infrastructure beyond Earth, independent of terrestrial risks and providing the foundation for future AI and compute services in space. Phison’s storage technology is one of the technologies we are integrating into that broader StarVault architecture.”
Pascari Space solutions are available now and showcased at SC26, booth #5413, from November 17 – 19, 2026.

Phison Takes Data Storage Beyond Earth With Pascari Space
 By 
Oscar Contreras
 | 
Nov 18, 2026
 |

Fresh from its SC26 debut, Pascari Space brings mission-configurable SSDs to spacecraft, orbital data centers, and everything in between.
Space is getting busy. Satellites, research platforms, and even data centers are heading into orbit as launch costs fall and the space economy keeps growing. All of that hardware runs on data. And that data has to live somewhere.
That’s where things get hard. In orbit, storage faces radiation, wild temperature swings, and a launch that rattles every component on board. If a drive fails up there, nobody is coming to swap it out. What launches has to last the entire mission.
A new category built for the extreme
This week at Supercomputing 2026 (SC26) in Chicago, Phison launched Pascari Space, a new family of SSD solutions purpose-built for commercial space and orbital infrastructure.
Phison isn’t new to this. Pascari SSDs customized through the company’s IMAGIN+ design services have already achieved TRL-9 status, surviving launch, cislunar transit, and arrival on the lunar surface as part of a commercial lunar payload. That record was earned in flight. The same engineering now goes into every Pascari Space engagement.
Storage shaped around your mission
No two missions are alike. Orbit, radiation environment, and risk tolerance all vary, so a one-size-fits-all drive rarely makes sense. Pascari Space runs as a co-development program through Phison IMAGIN+ customization and design services. Phison designs its own controllers and firmware, which means mission teams can set endurance, retention, and error handling to match what the mission actually demands.
The portfolio includes three product lines. Together they cover everything from critical spacecraft systems to orbital data centers, and each one can be configured for a specific mission.
The next chapter of storage starts here
Orbital data centers. Spacecraft that think for themselves. Research missions that run longer and travel farther than ever before. None of it works without storage that can survive the trip or mission. Pascari Space opens the door to a new era of data in orbit, and this is only the beginning
Dive deeper into Pascari Space and the features and capabilities of Phison’s mission-specific SSD solutions in the press release. Or, explore 
Pascari Space
 on our website.
Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) :
What is Pascari Space, and what applications does it support?
Pascari Space is a family of mission-configurable SSD solutions from Phison that addresses the storage requirements of commercial spacecraft, satellites, research platforms, and orbital data 
centers
operating
 in environments with radiation exposure, extreme temperatures, and limited maintenance access. The portfolio includes three configurable product lines covering applications from critical spacecraft systems to orbital data infrastructure. Phison combines controller-level engineering, firmware customization, and mission-specific design services to align storage capabilities with operational requirements.
Why do spacecraft and satellites require specialized SSD storage?
Spacecraft and satellites require specialized SSD storage because radiation exposure, extreme temperature fluctuations, launch vibration, and limited maintenance opportunities create operating conditions that conventional storage designs may not adequately address. Mission planners must evaluate data retention, endurance, error handling, and operational longevity against the spacecraft’s expected environment and mission duration. Storage failure can compromise scientific data, onboard processing, or critical system functions, making mission-specific storage engineering essential.
What does TRL-9 mean for SSD technology used in space missions?
Technology Readiness Level 9 (TRL-9) 
indicates
 that a technology has 
demonstrated
 successful operation in an actual mission environment, providing stronger evidence of operational maturity than laboratory testing or simulated environmental qualification alone. 
Phison
 achieved TRL-9 with customized Pascari SSDs that completed launch, cislunar transit, and arrival on the lunar surface as part of a commercial lunar payload. This flight experience 
establishes
 a demonstrated foundation for 
Phison’s
 mission-specific storage engineering, although new configurations still require 
appropriate qualification
.
How do space-grade SSD requirements differ from enterprise data center SSD requirements? 
Space-grade SSD requirements place greater emphasis on environmental resilience, mission longevity, and data integrity under radiation, temperature extremes, and launch-related mechanical stress, while terrestrial enterprise SSD evaluations typically prioritize throughput, latency, endurance, capacity, and serviceability. Both environments require consistent storage 
behavior
 and effective error management. However, spacecraft introduce 
additional
 qualification challenges and limited opportunities for hardware replacement. Engineers must evaluate storage architectures against the specific environmental conditions, operational workloads, and failure risks of each deployment.
What should aerospace engineers evaluate when selecting SSDs for orbital data centers?
Aerospace engineers should evaluate SSD endurance, data retention, error-handling capabilities, environmental tolerance, and mission-specific qualification requirements when selecting storage for orbital data 
centers
. The evaluation should account for radiation exposure, thermal conditions, launch stresses, expected workload intensity, and mission duration. Engineers should also assess how controller firmware manages data integrity throughout the deployment. Mission-configurable storage architectures provide greater flexibility to align SSD characteristics with orbital computing requirements than a standardized configuration alone.
 How does Phison customize Pascari Space SSDs for different aerospace missions?
Phison
 customizes Pascari Space SSDs through its IMAGIN+ design services, combining in-house controller development and firmware engineering to align endurance, data retention, and error-handling 
behavior
 with specific aerospace mission requirements. The co-development approach allows mission teams to account for differences in orbital environments, operational duration, and acceptable risk. Rather than applying identical storage configurations across applications, Phison works with customers to define storage characteristics around the technical requirements of each deployment.
How does Phison's controller-level innovation support SSD performance and data integrity in space?
Phison’s controller-level innovation supports space-focused SSD engineering by enabling direct customization of firmware 
behavior
, endurance management, data retention, and error handling for mission-specific operating conditions. Because 
Phison
 develops its own controllers and firmware, its engineering teams can coordinate hardware and software design decisions rather than relying entirely on fixed third-party controller implementations. This integration provides flexibility to address spacecraft storage requirements and develop configurations that align with mission reliability 
objectives
.
How does Phison's lunar mission experience strengthen Pascari Space development?
Phison’s lunar mission experience provides Pascari Space with 
demonstrated
 flight heritage through customized Pascari SSDs that achieved TRL-9 after completing launch, cislunar transit, and arrival on the lunar surface. This experience 
establishes
 a practical engineering foundation for developing storage solutions for demanding aerospace environments. Phison applies the knowledge gained from that mission to Pascari Space engagements, helping customers evaluate storage configurations against environmental challenges, mission duration, and data integrity requirements.
How can Pascari Space support AI processing and autonomous computing in orbit?
Pascari Space can support onboard AI processing and autonomous computing by providing mission-configurable SSD storage for spacecraft and orbital infrastructure that must 
retain
 and access data throughout extended deployments. Autonomous systems and orbital computing applications require storage architectures that align with their data workloads, environmental conditions, and operational lifetimes. Through controller and firmware customization, 
Phison
 can help mission teams address endurance, retention, and error-handling requirements. Actual AI workload performance and latency characteristics require validation against the selected SSD configuration.
What advantages does Phison's co-development approach offer aerospace OEMs and orbital infrastructure providers?
Phison’s co-development approach gives aerospace OEMs and orbital infrastructure providers the flexibility to configure SSD endurance, data retention, and error-handling capabilities around individual mission requirements rather than adopting a fixed storage architecture. Through IMAGIN+ design services, Phison integrates controller engineering and firmware customization into the development process. This approach supports mission-specific design decisions across spacecraft systems, scientific payloads, and orbital data centers while helping engineering teams align storage characteristics with deployment conditions and operational objectives.

軌道上データセンターに488TBを搭載へ、Phisonの宇宙向けSSD「Pascari Space」
2026.11.21 10:01 更新
2026.11.17 配信 
Phison
SSD
2027年上期打ち上げ予定の「StarVault-001」に採用。利用可能容量は約370TB
Phison Electronics（本社：台湾）は2026年11月17日（現地時間）、宇宙船や軌道上データセンター向けに設計したSSDソリューション
「Pascari Space」
を発表した。米シカゴで開催されたスーパーコンピューティング関連イベント「SC26」で披露されたもので、2027年上期に打ち上げ予定の軌道上データセンターに、合計488TBのストレージを搭載する計画も明らかにしている。
「Pascari Space」は、宇宙空間の厳しい環境に対応するため、ミッションごとの要件に合わせて構成を最適化できるSSD製品群。独自のカスタマイズサービス「IMAGIN+」を活用し、耐久性やデータ保持性能、エラー処理、放射線への耐性などを調整できる。
また、LDPCエラー訂正やSmartECC、SmartRefreshなどのデータ保護技術を採用。pSLC構成では、同一ドライブの通常のTLC構成と比較して最大60倍の書き換え耐久性を実現するとしている。
製品ラインナップは、宇宙船の重要システム向け
「Flight Critical」
、汎用ストレージ向け
「Main Storage」
、軌道上データセンター向け
「Orbital Data Center」
の3シリーズ。
「Flight Critical」の「SP-F100」はPCIe 4.0 x4接続で、容量40GB～1TB、動作温度は-40～105℃。「Main Storage」の「SP-M100」はPCIe 4.0 x4接続で、容量480GB～4TB、動作温度は-40～85℃。
大容量ストレージ向けの「Orbital Data Center」は、PCIe 5.0 x4接続に対応する3モデルを展開。「SP-C200」は最大128TB、「SP-C250P」は最大32TB、「SP-C250E」は最大25.6TBをラインナップし、いずれも動作温度は0～70℃に対応する。
なお、Phisonは米Lonestar Data Holdingsが進める軌道上データセンター計画「StarVault」にストレージ技術を提供する。同計画の初号機「StarVault-001（SV-001）」は2027年上期の打ち上げを予定しており、合計488TBのPascari Spaceストレージを搭載。地球低軌道における利用可能容量は約370TBになる見込み。
同社はこれまでにもLonestarの月面データセンター関連ミッションにストレージ技術を提供しており、今回の「Pascari Space」は、こうした実績を基盤に宇宙向けストレージ事業を拡大する取り組みとなる。
