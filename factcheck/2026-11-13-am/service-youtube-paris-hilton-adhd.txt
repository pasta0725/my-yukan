When the word ADHD is mentioned, there’s a specific image that comes to mind for many: a ball of energy at the playground, a classroom disruptor, and…well, a boy.
A successful and highly feminine media personality like Paris Hilton doesn’t fit that mold. Yet, like millions of late-diagnosed women, her life and career have been deeply shaped by the disorder. This week, she’s bringing those hidden struggles to the forefront alongside Haley Baylee, Whitney Cummings, and Lizzo in a new campaign for ADHD Awareness Month.
As a woman with ADHD myself, I was keen to learn more. Here’s what Paris shared with me about reframing her neurodivergence and what needs to change to ensure ADHD is truly understood:
“I want the next generation of girls to grow up feeling understood, supported, and celebrated for who they are instead of believing something is wrong with them”
        - Paris Hilton
You’ve often referred to your ADHD as a superpower. Did it always feel that way?
Paris:
 It was definitely a journey. For so much of my life, I felt like something was wrong with me because I struggled with things that seemed so easy for everyone else. Getting diagnosed didn’t make those challenges disappear, but it helped me understand myself and stop feeling ashamed of how my brain works.
I call ADHD my superpower because I’ve learned to appreciate parts of it, but that doesn’t mean it feels like one every day. Two things can be true!
What are the specific aspects of how your brain works that you’ve come to love and appreciate the most?
Paris:
 I love how creative and curious my mind is. I’m constantly coming up with new ideas, making connections, and imagining what’s possible. And when I’m passionate about something, I can become completely immersed in it. I think those traits have really helped me as an entrepreneur, advocate, and artist because I have so many different passions and love being able to bring them all to life.
What’s the one outdated myth about ADHD in women that you’re most ready to see disappear?
Paris:
 That ADHD always looks like the hyperactive little boy who can’t sit still in class. For so many women and girls, it can look like constantly feeling overwhelmed, struggling to stay organized, or working twice as hard to keep up while nobody realizes how much you’re struggling. So many women I know were high-achieving students who spent years masking their challenges just to fit in.
I want the next generation of girls to grow up feeling understood, supported, and celebrated for who they are instead of believing something is wrong with them or feeling like they have to pretend to be someone else to belong.
As an advocate, how do you balance sharing your own truth while making sure you’re making space for others?
Paris:
 My experience is just one story, and ADHD affects everyone differently. As we continue to grow UNSTOPPABLE, I want to make sure we’re creating space for people with different perspectives and lived experiences, especially those whose stories may not always be heard.
I believe real understanding and meaningful change start when we listen to each other. That’s how we can challenge stigma, identify gaps in support, and work toward solutions that make a difference for more people.
What stood out to you working with Lizzo, Whitney Cummings, and Haley Baylee for this campaign?
Paris:
 What moved me most was how vulnerable and honest everyone was willing to be. I really believe there’s so much strength in vulnerability because it helps us build community and reminds people they’re not alone.
“I believe everyone deserves the opportunity to understand how their brain works and access the individualized support they need.”
        - Paris Hilton
You’ve been so open about the diagnostic journey. How has having access to the right spaces and tools shaped your journey, and what do you think needs to change so that kind of support is available to everyone, regardless of background?
Paris:
 Having access to medication, therapy, and an incredible support system has made such a difference in my life, and I recognize how privileged I am to have those resources. I believe everyone deserves the opportunity to understand how their brain works and access the individualized support they need.
I’ve been a longtime supporter of Understood.org, and I want to use my platform to make credible information and resources more accessible. We still have so much work to do, and I’m actively exploring ways to use my platform and resources to expand support for school-aged children, particularly in underserved communities.

Paris Hilton on ADHD, misinformation, and finally feeling understood
Understood Team
November 09, 2026
Paris Hilton spent much of her life feeling misunderstood and hiding the things that made her different. Her biggest effort yet to help other people with ADHD feel seen is UNSTOPPABLE, a summit that brought more than 200 neurodivergent creators together with experts from 
Understood.org
 and other organizations. We talked with Paris about what she learned, her new PSA, and how creators can share ADHD stories that are honest, accurate, and helpful.
You’ve been open about your ADHD for years. What inspired you to bring creators together for UNSTOPPABLE?
I’ve spent so much of my life feeling misunderstood, and I know how powerful it can be to finally find people who understand the way your mind works. As one of the original influencers, I’ve seen firsthand how creators can shape culture. I wanted to bring this incredible community together to support their own learning and exploration around their own mind. I also wanted to inspire them to continue to use their platform to reduce stigma around ADHD, so the next generation grows up feeling more understood, supported, and celebrated for who they are and their unique strengths. 
There’s a lot of ADHD content on social media, and not all of it is accurate. Why was it important to put creators in the same room as experts? What’s one thing an expert shared that changed how you think about ADHD?
Our personal stories are so powerful, but they’re only one piece of the puzzle. And I think we have a responsibility to make sure the information we’re sharing with millions of people is accurate. Misinformation spreads quicker than anything else online, so I wanted to bring experts to the table that could educate, and also support these creators long after the event was over. 
One message that stuck with me is to not let anyone diminish your story. There is a lot of misinformation suggesting that ADHD is a trend, everyone has it, or it’s overdiagnosed — which is frustrating and harmful. Those of us with ADHD know it affects how we live every day, and without support, it can be debilitating. 
That’s why it was so important for me to share this message at UNSTOPPABLE and point people to credible resources they can use and share. ADHD is real, and so are the challenges and strengths that come with it. We can all use our voices to fight the stigma and the false narratives.
What meaningful feedback have you received from creators who attended UNSTOPPABLE?
The most meaningful part for me was hearing how many people felt like they could finally let their guard down and just be themselves without having to explain or apologize for who they are. So many people came up to me and said they’d “never felt so safe to be themselves.” I think the day was very healing for a lot of people, including myself. So many of us have spent our lives masking parts of ourselves to fit in, and seeing people connect over experiences they thought they were navigating alone was incredibly emotional.
Before the summit, about 66 percent of the creators said they felt proud of how their brain works. After the summit, that number jumped to 94 percent. That’s the magic of feeling seen and understood. You can acknowledge that your brain makes some things difficult while still appreciating the unique way you process and experience the world. 
Together, the creators who attended UNSTOPPABLE have more than 500 million followers. What do you hope they do differently when they talk about ADHD with their audiences?
I hope they walk away knowing that their stories have the power to make someone feel less alone, but also understanding the responsibility that comes with sharing them. That’s why we brought together impactful organizations like 
Understood.org
, 
The Mental Health Coalition
, 
Jake Collective
, and 
The JED Foundation
, so creators could leave with trusted resources and a deeper understanding of ADHD to share with their communities.
After the summit, 93 percent of creators said that their content can make a real difference for people with ADHD, and 91 percent said they feel confident they can spot ADHD misinformation and respond to it. 
You just released a PSA that highlights creators’ own stories of navigating ADHD. Why did you want their voices front and center, and how do you hope people feel when they watch it?
I talk about ADHD being my superpower because I’ve learned to embrace the creativity and imagination that come with it. But that doesn’t mean the challenges I face every day aren’t very real. I wanted to share different experiences because there’s no single story of ADHD. And most importantly, I want the next generation growing up with this diagnosis to know that it doesn’t define what they’re capable of achieving. I wish I’d had someone to look up to when I was younger and struggling, someone who reminded me that even though some things might be harder for me, I was still smart, creative, and capable of doing incredible things.
You also designed a merch collection inspired by your ADHD, with 100 percent of proceeds going to Understood.org. What inspired the designs, and why did you want to give back?
Growing up, I spent so much time feeling like the things that made me different were things I needed to hide, so I wanted to create something fun and empowering that celebrates the way our minds work. I’ve always loved expressing myself through fashion, and I love finding ways to turn the things I’m passionate about into something that makes a difference. Knowing that 100 percent of the proceeds will support Understood.org and help more people access the resources and support they deserve makes this collection so meaningful to me.
On the days you feel “unstoppable,” what’s working for you? 
Feeling “unstoppable” doesn’t mean having everything figured out or never struggling; it means having a greater understanding of what I need and giving myself permission to work differently. I’m really intentional about surrounding myself with people who support me, using tools and routines that help me stay organized, and making time for the creative things that bring me joy and help me recharge.
UNSTOPPABLE is just the beginning. What comes next, for you and for these creators?
I definitely want to host another UNSTOPPABLE! I’m also dreaming up ways to bring the incredible sense of community and connection we created that day to more cities, so more people can experience what it feels like to be truly seen, understood, and celebrated. Ultimately, I want to help create a world where neurodivergent people have the tools, support, and opportunities they deserve, because no one should have to spend their life trying to fit into a world that wasn’t designed for them.
