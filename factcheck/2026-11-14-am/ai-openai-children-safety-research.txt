OpenAI puts $2M into kids AI safety research
StartupHub.ai Staff · Nov 12, 2026 · 2 min read
Share
Credit: OpenAI News
OpenAI
 pledged $2 million for research into AI risks and opportunities for children at the Paris Peace Forum, the company said.
Does that money change what children experience in chatbots? Not directly, but 
OpenAI
 says the funding supports shared measurement work, which is common tooling researchers use to test risks and benefits in comparable ways. The work sits under the Coalition for Children’s Rights and Protection in the Age of AI. The charter is a voluntary text led by France, Spain and Kenya and endorsed by 
Anthropic
, Black Forest Labs, Microsoft and OpenAI, per Captain Compliance reporting.
The next question is whether industry funding skews results. OpenAI says research teams retain control over conclusions and must publish findings, including negative or inconclusive results. That promise matters for product safety teams who need independent benchmarks to justify age defaults and parent controls before deployment.
The practical pressure falls on deployment owners and policy staff. OpenAI points to age-appropriate safeguards by default, which are product settings that limit exposure by age without a parent opting in, plus parent tools, regular risk assessments and accountability. Watch the EU Kids Act talks, where European policymakers will test if voluntary charters turn into enforceable checks.
