Instagram users can now chat with Meta’s AI agent Muse in a DM thread, the company announced on Tuesday. The social media network says users can chat with Muse to get more context about a post, place, or product they come across, or catch up on conversations they may have missed in their other DMs.
The new integration gets rid of the need for users to toggle between Instagram and the Muse app when they want to interact with the AI agent. It also reflects the growing competition among tech companies to make AI assistants a part of users’ everyday digital lives. By embedding Muse directly into Instagram, Meta is making it easier for users to access the AI agent, while also giving Muse access to a platform where users already discover products, places, and content.
Users can share Instagram posts with Muse and ask it to take action, such as adding a coffee shop to their Mexico City itinerary or finding a Black Friday deal on a pair of shoes. 
Meta notes that conversations with Muse on Instagram will appear as a side chat in the main Muse app.
Image Credits:
Meta
Muse in Instagram DMs will begin rolling out on Tuesday for iOS and Android users in the U.S., Canada, Mexico, Japan, and Singapore.
Meta also announced that users can now do more with the posts and reels they’ve bookmarked on Instagram. For example, they can ask Muse to organize their saved content into custom folders, known as Collections. They could also ask Muse to turn a recipe video into a grocery list, turn the travel content from their favorite travel creators into an itinerary for an upcoming trip, or have Muse send them outfit ideas based on fashion reels they’ve collected.
Starting Tuesday, when users save a post on Instagram, they will see a prompt for Muse to organize it for them. This feature is launching in the U.S. and Canada. 
Meta says that users remain in control of their Muse experience on Instagram. Nothing is shared, published, or sent without their approval, and they can ask Muse to forget information or undo actions at any time.

 their Instagram and Muse accounts. And Instagram users in the United States will begin to see prominent pop-ups suggesting they use Muse to organize their saved content in specific collections, like recipe videos or travel clips.

Meta added its 
Muse personal AI agent
 to Instagram direct messages on November 24, 2026, 
announcing through Muse’s verified Instagram account
 that users can send it a DM to “turn your saves into real plans,” with a trip they have been meaning to take as the example.
The addition gives Muse a conversational entry point inside Instagram alongside its other chat surfaces. Meta’s 
Muse product site
 describes chatting through the 
Muse app
 on mobile or desktop, or directly in WhatsApp, by message or voice. It separately lists Instagram among the apps that can be connected to the agent, with users deciding what Muse can see and do.
From Saved Content to Delegated Work
In its 
September 8, 2026 launch post
, Meta said Muse could organize Instagram Saves and create artifacts such as travel guides from them, with Instagram connected as one source of context. The initial standalone rollout was in the US on iOS, Android and the Muse website.
That workflow reflects Muse’s intended difference from a conventional chatbot. Meta describes the agent as remembering conversations, proactively working toward goals and suggesting ideas, rather than only answering isolated questions. The DM entry point connects task initiation with the place where the content was saved. For Meta, the product significance is a shift from Instagram as a source of context to Instagram as both a source and a place to delegate work.
Permissions and Private-Message Access
Meta’s account infrastructure already links some of these services. Its 
connector documentation
 says Facebook, Instagram and Threads connect automatically when the accounts share an Accounts Center. While connected, Muse exchanges information relevant to the tasks assigned to it. Users can manage those accounts through Accounts Center.
Private-message access has a more specific boundary. Meta’s 
security and privacy page
 says Muse does not read private messages unless the user grants access through private-messaging connectors or Muse on Mac. Permissions are set per connector, and users can read, edit or delete what Muse remembers. The new DM surface lets someone message Muse; it does not, by itself, alter that documented rule for reading private messages.
Actions beyond retrieving information carry another set of controls. Meta’s 
approval guide
 distinguishes read actions, such as checking a calendar or reading an email, from write actions, such as sending a message or making a purchase. Users can require approval before every write action and important read actions, or before any action, and inspect a chronological activity log. Some actions can be stopped or undone in chat, but a sent email cannot be reversed.
Connector Data and Deletion Controls
Disconnecting a connector stops future data exchanges, but information previously used for tasks may remain in Muse’s memories and conversation history. The 
data-control guide
 says stored interactions can include text, voice, photos and information obtained through connectors. Deleting material also does not necessarily erase information Muse learned from it.
Users can delete individual messages and side chats, ask Muse to forget a topic or set of interactions, or reset the agent to permanently delete its chat history, files and active tasks. The setting allowing interactions to help train Meta’s AI models is on initially, and turning it off also applies to previous interactions. The result is a more direct route from saved Instagram content to delegated work, with permissions and retention settings determining how far that delegation extends.
