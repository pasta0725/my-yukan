Uber and Nuro have started testing driverless Lucid Gravity SUVs on public streets in the San Francisco Bay Area more than six months after receiving the permit that allowed the companies to pull the human safety driver from behind the wheel. 
The companies will soon begin offering driverless rides to employees in the final lead-up to launching a commercial robotaxi service in the first quarter of 2027.
The milestone comes just 16 months after Uber announced an ambitious plan to launch a 
premium robotaxi service
 using Lucid electric vehicles outfitted with autonomous vehicle technology developed by startup Nuro.
That plan came with hefty investments in both companies and much-needed endorsements of their respective products. Uber initially agreed to invest $300 million in Lucid — a figure that has since ballooned to $500 million. Uber also agreed to invest 
nearly $500 million
 in Nuro, TechCrunch previously reported, citing sources familiar with the deal.
The agreement provided both Lucid and Nuro with boosts beyond the obvious capital injection. Uber agreed to buy at least 20,000 of Lucid Gravity SUVs over the next six years, giving the struggling EV maker a more predictable source of revenue. Nuro, meanwhile, was less than a year removed from announcing a 
risky, but necessary pivot
 away from developing driverless delivery vehicles and toward licensing its autonomous vehicle technology to automakers and mobility companies.
Nuro has spent nearly a year testing the Lucid Gravity robotaxi on public roads in the San Francisco Bay Area with a human safety operator behind the wheel. The California Department of Motor Vehicles, the agency that regulates the testing and deployment of autonomous vehicles in the state, granted Nuro a 
driverless AV permit
 in May 2026.
Uber and Nuro are also testing autonomous vehicles on public roads in Houston with human safety operators behind the wheel. Nuro is supplementing that on-road testing with closed-course testing and simulation to validate the self-driving system before opening the robotaxis to the public. The companies’ combined engineering fleet of autonomous vehicles, which stood at 100 AVs earlier this year, is expanding as Lucid begins manufacturing the first production versions of the robotaxis at its Arizona factory.
The Lucid Gravity robotaxi, 
unveiled in January 2026
, is outfitted with high-resolution cameras, solid-state lidar sensors, and radars that help the self-driving system perceive the real-world environment and operate in it. Uber, which will own and operate the fleet, has focused its efforts on the in-cabin experience, including how riders will interact with the vehicle.

Uber’s high-stakes robotaxi bet is officially ditching its human safety net. Nuro, Lucid, and Uber are starting fully driverless public road testing in the San Francisco Bay Area, with employee-only rides launching shortly after.
First announced in July 2025
, this three-way alliance represents Uber’s aggressive comeback in autonomous ride-hailing, after having 
abandoned its own troubled in-house efforts years ago
. By pulling human safety operators from behind the wheel of Lucid Gravity SUVs, the companies say they are clearing a critical hurdle toward a public launch. That debut is now slated for early 2027, marking a slight slip from the partnership’s original late-2026 target. When it finally goes live, it will pit Uber directly against entrenched rivals like Waymo and Zoox 
on their home turf
.

Lucid has delivered
 more than 100 upgraded Gravity SUVs, up from the 75 engineering vehicles on the road earlier this year.

a roof-mounted LED “halo”
 that flashes a rider’s initials and pickup status.

Uber Technologies Inc. has started testing robotaxis developed by Nuro Inc. and Lucid Group Inc. without a safety driver, a key milestone ahead of a commercial launch next year.
For almost a year, Nuro has been conducting tests in the San Francisco Bay area and Houston using a fleet of Lucid Gravity robotaxis with safety operators behind the wheel. Now, the companies are ready to remove the safety driver as part of the next phase of development, according to a statement Nov. 24. The driverless testing will begin in four California cities — Palo Alto, Mountain View, Los Altos and Sunnyvale — before expanding to additional area municipalities next year.
Employees will also soon get access to the fully driverless rides, and Lucid expects to start manufacturing the first vehicles for the commercial launch fleet in the coming weeks, the companies added. As the service opens up to the public next year, Nuro expects Uber to take over remote assistance and monitoring of the vehicles, Nuro Co-CEO Dave Ferguson said in a July interview.
The development marks a sign of progress for one of Uber’s biggest robotaxi partnerships and investments, which have gained heightened importance after the rideshare giant’s 
relationship with Waymo
 began to unravel. When Uber shared details about the 
Nuro-Lucid partnership
 last year, it said it expected service to start in 2026. But the commercial launch has since been pushed to the first quarter of 2027.
Uber has pursued a partnership approach when it comes to autonomous vehicles. Rather than building its own car, the company sees itself as an aggregator that matches riders to both human drivers and robotaxis. It has struck deals with more than a dozen robotaxi makers that will make their cars available on the Uber app in the coming years, but only a few companies in the U.S. so far have made it out of the testing phase, let alone to the fully driverless stage.
Waymo and Amazon.com Inc.’s Zoox don’t have safety drivers, but Motional and Avride — Uber’s partners in Las Vegas and Texas, respectively — still have have operators behind the wheel who can take over the driving if the vehicle makes mistakes, for example.

Even as Uber, Nuro and Lucid proceed to the driverless testing stage, it will take time for them to catch up with Waymo, the leading provider of driverless rides in the U.S. Notably absent from the list of test markets is San Francisco, where rival Waymo already offers paid trips. Waymo also serves riders throughout the rest of the peninsula, though California regulators recently ordered the Alphabet Inc. unit to 
suspend freeway testing
 and operations due to safety concerns. On Nov. 23, Waymo also temporarily paused service in the area, citing weather conditions.
Like Waymo’s vehicles, Nuro and Lucid’s robotaxis feature cameras and lidar and radar sensors so the car can “see” its surroundings. The Lucid Gravity is roomier than Waymo’s Jaguar model, but the in-car experience — which includes a tablet and dashboard display that shows where the car is going and what it’s detecting on the road — should feel familiar to those who have tried Waymo.

The Lucid-manufactured robotaxis Uber 
revealed
 at CES earlier this year are almost ready to give rides to the public. Uber, Lucid and Nuro, the company that developed the autonomous driving system powering their vehicle, have announced that they've started full driverless testing on the public roads of San Francisco Bay Area. They'll soon also give their employees rides on the autonomous vehicles, as well, ahead of their planned public launch in the first quarter of 2027. 
While the companies presented the vehicle, a modified 
Lucid Gravity
, at CES earlier this year, they announced the global robotaxi program way back in July 2025. In fact, they've been testing with a driver behind the wheel since December last year. They're now moving to the next phase after securing a California DMV Driverless Testing Permit in May, which gives them the permission to test on public roads in the state without a driver behind the wheel. 
Their program's Lucid Gravity vehicles are equipped with high-res cameras, LIDAR sensors and radars, and they're powered by the Nuro Driver end-to-end autonomous driving system. Initially, the tests will be conducted on the roads of Palo Alto, Mountain View, Los Altos and Sunnyvale, before expanding to more Bay Area cities next year. Lucid said its fleet of robotaxis already consists of 100 vehicles, but it's unclear if they'll all be part of the test's initial rollout. 
The companies are planning to launch their robotaxi service in the Bay Area in the first quarter of 2027, with passengers being able to summon the autonomous rides through the Uber aoo. Uber has committed to purchasing 35,000 Lucid-Nuro robotaxis and intends to expand their availability around the world. The ride-hailing company will be designing the vehicle's in-cabin experience and will provide support and rider controls for them when it adds them to its network.
