Most streaming music providers have opted to label AI-generated content on their platforms, and until now, Tidal was one of them. However, the company is about to take a harder stance. Tidal 
announced
 that in the coming weeks, it will fully ban AI music because of the harm it does to human artists.
Tidal debuted its initial AI policy in July 2026, which included labeling and demonetizing all songs created entirely with AI. That wasn’t enough to stem the tide of AI slop, though. Over the past 90 days, Tidal reports that 43 percent of new uploads were completely AI-generated. On November 6, new uploads to Tidal hit a whopping 60 percent AI-generated.
As things stand, Tidal hosts 27 million tracks labeled as AI, all of which were obviously added in the past couple of years. That accounts for 6.3 percent of Tidal’s total catalog. On December 9, Tidal will delete all those songs. The streamer notes this will have essentially no negative impact, because 96 percent of AI-labeled tracks got zero plays in the past 90 days. Throughout its announcement, Tidal is careful to stipulate that it’s only talking about music that has been 
fully
 created by AI. Artists can still use AI tools as long as the final product contains human elements.

Some people don’t particularly mind AI music, but the team at Tidal has come to believe the harm to artists is too great. AI models can pump out random noises faster than humans can create things of real value, which can mislead listeners and siphon royalty payments away from creators. There’s also an issue with AI uploaders who are actively attempting to steal royalty payments. Tidal says it has already removed about 66,000 tracks from its service for impersonating artists, which was against its AI policy even before this update.
Holes in the net
As of December 9, individual uploaders and distributors will no longer be allowed to add AI music to Tidal. However, they could lie, couldn’t they?
Tidal hasn’t specified how it detects AI music, but it will probably rely on watermarking. Almost every AI song on Tidal (94 percent) comes from Suno, which 
added watermarking
 earlier this year. Tidal also cites Google Lyria as a source of AI tunes, which has 
SynthID labels
 embedded in the waveform. 
Deezer claimed
 it has a licensable AI music detector with a false-positive rate of less than 0.01 percent, but it’s unclear if Tidal is using that or something similar.
That will take care of most AI music, but this approach won’t detect everything. There are open models for music generation that you can run locally on a GPU, and some cloud-based providers aren’t bothering to watermark their outputs. Even if Tidal is using a separate detection system, new models may be able to evade it—it’s always going to be a cat-and-mouse game. This will be a problem with 
verifying provenance
 in all types of media in the future. For the time being, at least, Tidal’s policy should clear out most of the AI slop.
The people directing robots to make these songs will have plenty of other options after Tidal blocks AI. Spotify, 
YouTube Music
, Deezer, and Apple Music all allow AI music on their platforms with labeling. Spotify has removed millions of AI tracks from its collection based on user feedback. Other streamers haven’t specified how much slop (if any) they’ve cleared out.

Music streaming service Tidal 
said
 on Tuesday that it will start banning and removing songs generated fully with AI in a bid to stem the flood of submissions to its platform. The company said that over the past 90 days, more than 43% of tracks uploaded to its platform were generated by AI, and that was a major reason driving this decision.
Tidal said it will start removing fully AI-generated music on December 9, and the process will result in more than 29 million songs being deleted from the platform by the end of the year. With the new rules in place, the platform said it will be rejecting roughly 80,000-85,000 tracks per day.
Tidal is also banning distribution services from delivering 100% AI-generated tracks, and will reject submissions if songs’ metadata says they are made fully using AI.
The company said it will still accept songs that were made with AI assistance, but not fully generated.
