SEOPS Expands Waymaker Rideshare Program with Third Falcon 9 Launch with SpaceX
Mission to mid-inclination orbit in late-2028 expands launch capacity as SEOPS moves to meet growing market demand
BREMEN, Germany — November 17, 2026
 — SEOPS today announced it is further expanding its Waymaker rideshare program portfolio of dedicated launch opportunities for spacecraft operators seeking access to mid-inclination orbit. The additional mission will launch aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket in late 2028.
The mission builds on Waymaker’s growing portfolio, which includes two previously announced missions with SpaceX, six launches with Isar Aerospace’s Spectrum vehicle, and Stoke Space Nova missions, announced earlier today. Together, these agreements expand the range of launch options SEOPS can offer customers across providers, vehicles and orbital destinations.
Satellite operators are seeking more opportunities to secure launch dates and plan missions with confidence. SEOPS’ Waymaker program is designed to address that need by providing dedicated launch capacity in advance and managing mission planning, payload integration and execution.
“The capacity crunch is real, and satellite operators need launch options they can plan around,” said Evan Hoyt, president of SEOPS. “That’s why we built the Waymaker program. Customers need both launch opportunities and a clearer view of when additional capacity may become available so they can make informed decisions about their missions and the investments behind them.”
To support that planning, SEOPS is commissioning the Launch Vehicle Readiness Report, developed with support from Analysys Mason. The quarterly report will provide a transparent, evidence-based assessment of launch vehicles’ progress toward commercially repeatable orbital service, helping satellite operators and investors evaluate emerging capacity and inform mission planning and investment decisions. A preview covering 12 to 15 vehicles, including a public readiness matrix, is scheduled to debut at Space Tech Expo Europe in Bremen, November 17–19, 2026.
About SEOPS
SEOPS is redefining access to space by delivering predictable, end-to-end launch solutions. Moving beyond traditional rideshare brokerage, SEOPS secures and manages dedicated launch capacity, overseeing mission planning, payload integration, and execution across low Earth orbit (LEO), geostationary transfer orbit (GTO), cislunar, and beyond. As demand for launch services continues to grow, SEOPS is building the infrastructure that makes access to orbit simpler, more flexible, and more predictable. Visit Seops.space for more information.

Waymaker Program Expands with SpaceX and Stoke Space
Big news for day one of Space Tech Expo! SEOPS is tackling the challenge of securing reliable, predictable rides to orbit – the capacity crunch everyone is talking about. We are aggressively expanding our 
Waymaker
 dedicated rideshare program by adding new capacity with a third Falcon 9 and Stoke Space’s Nova.
What is the Waymaker Program?
Unveiled in May 2026, the Waymaker program represents a major shift in how small and medium satellite operators access low Earth orbit (LEO). Rather than waiting on manifest openings from primary launch providers, SEOPS purchases full launch vehicles years in advance, curates custom rideshare architectures, and manages end-to-end spacecraft integration.
With the industry anticipating tighter launch availability toward the end of the decade—especially as SpaceX transitions its Transporter program—Waymaker offers satellite developers guaranteed schedule certainty, accommodation for non-standard or larger payloads, and dedicated in-orbit safety services.
Scaling Up with SpaceX Falcon 9
Demand for Waymaker capacity surged immediately following its launch. After initially securing a Falcon 9 mission to sun-synchronous orbit (SSO) and quickly adding a second mid-inclination LEO mission for early 2028, we hav continued to expand its SpaceX manifest with a third Falcon 9 launch.
By stacking multiple dedicated Falcon 9 missions, we provide commercial and government customers with regular, predictable launch cadences to high-demand orbital planes, backed by streamlined ground integration facilities designed to cut pre-launch processing times.
Diversifying the Fleet with Stoke Space’s Nova
To build a truly resilient, multi-vehicle launch portfolio, SEOPS has also moved to incorporate next-generation reusable rockets into the Waymaker ecosystem. Securing launch capacity aboard 
Stoke Space’s Nova
 rocket adds a versatile, fully reusable medium-lift option alongside SpaceX’s workhorse Falcon 9.
Stoke Space’s Nova—particularly with its Block 2 architecture designed to deliver up to 15 metric tons to LEO in a fully reusable configuration—is built for rapid turnaround and high flight frequency. Integrating Nova into Waymaker gives SEOPS customers greater flexibility over launch windows, target inclinations, and orbital deployment strategies – something our customers have asked for.
Building a Multi-Vehicle Future
Between its expanding roster of Falcon 9 missions, new partnerships with emerging U.S. launch providers like Stoke Space, and international agreements (such as its multi-launch deal with European provider Isar Aerospace), SEOPS is positioning Waymaker as a vehicle-agnostic gateway to orbit. For satellite operators, the message is clear: getting to space is becoming less about waiting in line for a single rocket, and more about choosing the exact orbit, schedule, and vehicle that fits the mission. The industry has a huge demand for launch capacity right now – and SEOPS is answering the call.

Seops Space Secures Launch Capacity With Stoke Space
Share
Robert Wall
November 17, 2026
Credit: Stoke Space
BREMEN, Germany—Seops Space has secured launch capacity on Stoke Space’s rocket to help underpin its long-term role as a rideshare service provider.
The missions, due to start in 2029, will support Seops’s Waymaker rideshare program. They also represent another bet by the company on a rocket that has yet to fly.
Seops has been working with Stoke for several years ahead of this launch commitment. Seops President Evan Hoyt says the company has built confidence in the Stoke team and so when it came time to place the launch commitment it was “an easy decision.”
Stoke Space has said it aims to fly the Nova Pathfinder rocket early next year from Space Launch Complex 14 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station. More Pathfinder flights are due through 2028. The higher-capacity Nova Block 2 that can place 15 metric tons into low Earth orbit is due to fly in 2029. Seops is ordering the Block 2 version.
Seops’s first dedicated Waymaker mission is due in early 2028, using a SpaceX Falcon 9 for payloads to mid-inclination orbits. Another Falcon 9 mission later that year is due to service customers that need payloads placed in Sun-synchronous orbit.
Seops also said Nov. 17 that it is adding a late 2028 Falcon 9 Waymaker mid-inclination rideshare opportunity.
The company has already committed to using Isar Aerospace’s Spectrum rocket ahead of its first successful flight. It has since topped up the initial deal.
Seops placed the order with Stoke launchers ahead of its rocket's first flight in part because waiting would put it behind the demand curve, Hoyt suggests. Satellite customers, in the face of an acute launch bottleneck, also are willing to commit to a new rocket early, Hoyt says. Seops has already begun talks with potential users of its initial Nova rocket. “The risk tolerance of customers is being reset as well,” he says, pointing to the launcher shortage.
Seops has not yet settled on the orbital target for the first Nova flight, though it is likely to aim to satisfy customers looking for mid-inclination slots.
Hoyt says the company is exploring adding other launchers to its pool of service providers in a bid to provide customers ample choice in terms of launchers and timing.
Seops also is developing a launch cost insurance product to help make it easier for satellite makers to sign on to its flight offering.
