Apple appears to be working on its own AI agent to conduct computer work on behalf of users — including using apps, accessing websites, and coding, among other things.
The news was 
originally reported
 by Bloomberg, which spotted a job listing the iPhone maker recently posted for a senior engineering manager to oversee the creation of an “AI agent that can use a computer.”
“Imagine an AI agent that can use a computer the way a capable person does,” the posting says. “It browses the web, works across apps, runs commands, writes and debugs code, and calls services, and it chooses the right tool for each step. It does all this privately and with the polish people expect from Apple.” 
The posting additionally notes that the agents should be able to move “fluidly between tools” and “carry out multi-step tasks across apps, browsers, command lines, code, and APIs.”
TechCrunch reached out to Apple for more information but did not receive a response. 
Agents capable of computer work have become one of the hottest commodities in the tech industry over the past year. Major labs like Anthropic and OpenAI have launched such services, while other major tech brands — like Meta, which recently launched its Muse agent line — have also moved into the space. It makes sense that Apple — which is trying to 
expand its AI offerings
 in the new John Ternus era — would want to follow suit.
Bloomberg additionally notes that, at the same time that Apple is working on its own digital assistant, the company is making it significantly harder for third-party agents to gain total control over its computers. 
The outlet notes that the version of its upcoming macOS 27.3 will deploy warnings discouraging users from granting full access to third-party agents. In October, Apple 
issued a notification
 to developers stating that the company planned to “introduce additional controls to ensure that users who genuinely wish to grant an app this extraordinary level of access can only do so with very explicit user action.”
Bloomberg writes that while the iPhone maker “has eyed introducing the service as soon as the end of next year,” progress on its in-house agent “remains in the early stages.”

(Bloomberg) -- Apple Inc. is developing its own autonomous artificial intelligence agent that can get things done on behalf of users, looking to enter a fast-growing market already being pursued by OpenAI, Anthropic PBC, Meta Platforms Inc. and others.

The company outlined the plans in job listings added to its website this week. In one, Apple said it's seeking a senior engineering manager for a proactive, agentic AI assistant. The system will write code, browse the web, control apps and navigate devices on a user's behalf, according to the post.
The move follows the breakout success of Meta's Muse, which has captured the attention of consumers in recent months with its ability to make restaurant reservations, book travel and prepare news briefings. There's also been a proliferation of tools that let AI control Mac computers and program software.
Shares of Meta and Anthropic slid in late trading after Bloomberg News reported on Apple's efforts. Meta fell nearly 2%, while Anthropic dropped more than 3%.
Anthropic PBC recently widened user testing of its own agent, called Duet, while OpenAI has expanded the capabilities of Dots. And Alphabet Inc.'s Google overhauled its agent, now called Tasks, earlier this week. Like Elon Musk's Grok Bot, Tasks lets the system make phone calls on a user's behalf — in addition to offering standard AI agent features.
Apple's App Store has been dominated by AI agents, with Muse long occupying the No. 2 spot among free apps, just behind TikTok, on Thursday. OpenAI's ChatGPT and Anthropic's Claude were also both in the top five.
OpenAI, Anthropic and Musk's SpaceXSI are also behind popular developer coding tools Codex, Claude Code and Cursor.
Bloomberg News reported earlier this month that Apple was developing its own AI agent offering, but the new job listings offer fresh evidence of the company's plans. Apple has eyed introducing the service as soon as the end of next year, but development remains in the early stages. An Apple spokesperson didn't respond to a request for comment.
"Imagine an AI agent that can use a computer the way a capable person does," the job listing reads. "It browses the web, works across apps, runs commands, writes and debugs code, and calls services, and it chooses the right tool for each step. It does all this privately and with the polish people expect from Apple."
Story Continues
The listing says the company has a team building the "foundation for that future" and that Apple is looking for the hired manager to "make it real." John Ternus, the tech giant's new chief executive officer, has been pushing software engineering chief Craig Federighi to move faster and take a bolder approach to AI — with an agent being key to that effort.
"People already do their most valuable work by moving fluidly between tools: a browser, a document, a terminal, an editor, a service. We're building AI agents that can do the same," the listing says. The posting adds that Apple is working on agents that can "plan and carry out multistep tasks" across apps, browsers, command lines, code, and application programming interfaces, and they can "coordinate specialized subagents along the way."
The company strongly indicates its platform would be capable of autonomously navigating the interfaces of its operating systems. The listing specifically mentions development of "general-purpose computer-use agents," the technology that allows ChatGPT and Muse to control a Mac. Google Gemini added such functionality earlier on Thursday.
Another central component is privacy, with Apple saying that safety needs to be built into the system's design. "Agents that can run code, use credentials, and act on a user's behalf need strong permission models, sandboxing and transparency."
Sandboxing refers to a process that lets engineers test software without the risk of it breaking out into external networks, the rest of the operating system or the broader internet.
As the company develops its own agent, Apple is also making it more difficult for competing bots to gain broad control over its computers. In the beta version of the forthcoming macOS 27.3 operating system, the company has introduced new warning screens that prompt users to reconsider granting third-party agents permission to control their Macs.
With the change in 27.3, ChatGPT users now need to engage in 14 distinct steps to enable support for third-party computer-use tools, up from four. While Apple has positioned the shift as a move to protect customers, some AI companies and users see it as an attempt to slow down the competition.
Apple has already been sued by the US Justice Department for actions such as blocking third-party access to features and hardware.
While Apple is still far from introducing its own full-fledged AI agent, it has taken steps to integrate its Siri digital assistant more deeply into third-party apps. The company's upcoming iOS 27.3 release lays the groundwork for deeper collaboration between Siri AI and outside applications, allowing the assistant to, say, book a car or restaurant reservation.
Still, the updated system doesn't go as far as a full-fledged agent, which can autonomously carry out complex tasks across multiple apps and services in the background with little additional instruction from the user.
