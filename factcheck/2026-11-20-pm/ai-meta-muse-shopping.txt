Meta said during its third-quarter earnings call on Wednesday that its AI app Muse has been adopted by more than 10 million users, confirming 
estimates released earlier this week
 by market intelligence firm Sensor Tower.
But it is how people are using it and how much they’re spending that has caught CEO Mark Zuckerberg’s attention. Zuckerberg said about 25% of people who set up Muse now use it daily, helping improve retention.
He also shared that over a quarter of Muse’s engaged users who were shopping via the AI agent had an average purchase size of over $200, which was surprising even to industry insiders.
“Plaid told us that they hadn’t seen activity like they’re seeing with their connector on any other service before,” Zuckerberg said, referring to the financial technology company that connects payment apps to customers’ bank accounts. Muse uses Plaid’s connector to enable agentic shopping.
Beyond shopping, Zuckerberg said other popular uses for Muse include personal productivity tasks, such as connecting the AI agent to users’ email or calendars. The exec stressed that this indicates users trusted Muse’s security setup, which includes a feature called “Muse Secure VM” designed to protect their data and privacy. He also mentioned that the forthcoming “Muse Confidential VM” will prevent even Meta from seeing users’ content.
Now that Muse has achieved “product-market fit,” Zuckerberg said Meta’s focus will shift to scaling the AI agent, increasing revenue, and optimizing its compute costs. Remarkably, the tech company is forecasting that Muse will eventually bring in more revenue per user than its “family of apps” users do today — meaning its users on Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and Messenger. Currently, that figure is around “several hundreds of dollars annually,” Zuckerberg said.
Meta has said it ultimately intends to monetize Muse by keeping it free for most users but keeping a percentage of the commerce taking place on the platform. Power users who want access to the most advanced AI models or more tokens will also have to pay.
Zuckerberg also noted that Meta believes Muse will be profitable after it scales.
“On the compute side, we are building out the capacity to serve more than a billion people over the next few years,” he told bankers and analysts on the Q3 earnings call. “In the near term, Muse is very compute-intensive to run, but we expect to improve its cost efficiency by more than 10x in the next year, which will enable it to be profitable once the revenue lines are at scale.” 
Muse will also play a central role in Meta’s smart glasses strategy. Reality Labs, the division responsible for smart glasses, VR, and the metaverse, is still losing billions. However, the 
division’s losses
 are expected to improve from $19.1 billion in 2025 to $15 billion this year.
Beyond scaling and supporting its hardware strategy, Zuckerberg teased the company’s plans for something he called the “Muse network.” He described this as taking Muse from a “single-player game” to one where “your Muses can work with your friends’ and your families’ [Muses].”
“And if you want to buy something, it’s not just navigating to that business’s website; it’s talking to their agent,” he added.

more than 10 million users in Canada and the U.S. have set up Muse agents since they became available on Sept. 8.

Muse can do multistep tasks on behalf of users such as browsing the web, sending emails, and booking travel. One-quarter of Muse adopters use it daily, and the most common use case is shopping, Zuckerberg said, with an average purchase size of over $200. This early shopping activity offers Meta an opportunity to turn its 
billions in AI investments
 into another revenue stream. Rather than just selling ads to businesses hoping to reach potential customers, Meta plans to charge businesses a fee when Muse helps them complete a sale. Using the agent will be free for consumers unless they want to use more tokens or more advanced models. 
Meta’s revenue stream from advertising 
generated $63.1 billion in the third quarter
. Zuckerberg said Meta earns “several hundreds of dollars annually” from its family of apps for each American user, and he expects Muse to generate even more revenue per user as shopping activity grows.
“We expect that Muse will reach an even higher level over time due to the amount of commerce that we already see taking place,” he said.
Meta did not respond to 
Fortune
’s request for comment, and Stripe declined to comment.
A future without shopping sites?
Meta’s ambitions for Muse go beyond helping customers shop online. The tech giant envisions a network of people’s personal AI agents interacting with businesses’ AI agents instead of navigating through a website. 
Meta CFO Susan Li 
said
 the company is working with partners including Shopify and 
Walmart
 to develop a standard called the Personal Agent Protocol to define how consumers’ agents can securely interact with a business’s agents.
For retailers, this could hand over control of the shopping experience to Meta. Instead of discovering and browsing websites themselves, customers could delegate those tasks to Muse. That could help businesses find customers, but it also gives Meta greater influence over which sellers consumers discover and where they ultimately spend their money.
Li acknowledged adoption “will likely be uneven” among businesses wary of giving up their customer relationships. But she said, at some point, when agents demonstrate they can operate safely, “more and more businesses will be excited to participate.”
“We expect the market, ultimately, to evolve away from agents attempting to navigate every digital experience like a human,” Li said.

devoted almost $35 billion to capital expenditures
 in the third quarter, driven by AI servers, data centers, and network infrastructure, and it reported

Zuckerberg said Muse’s computing demands are partly why Meta has limited its launch to the U.S. and Canada. The company wants to eventually serve more than 1 billion Muse users, but reaching that scale requires more compute efficiency.
“In the near term, Muse is very compute-intensive to run, but we expect to improve its cost efficiency by more than 10x in the next year,” Zuckerberg said. 
Meta has traditionally focused on attracting users to new products before finding ways to make money from them. But Zuckerberg told analysts Meta expects to start working on monetizing Muse earlier than it typically does with new products, citing the cost of running the agent and the amount of shopping already taking place. 
“We are going to work on monetization somewhat sooner on this than our normal playbook,” he said. “In doing so, I think we will be able to build a model where we get a percent of the value that we are helping to create for people.”
