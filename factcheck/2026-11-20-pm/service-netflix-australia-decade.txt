A Decade of Contribution to Australia's Economy and Screen Industry
Entertainment
19 November 2026
Global
Australia
Link copied to clipboard
Netflix & Grill brought together screen industry leaders, policymakers, and media to celebrate the people behind Australian stories on Netflix and to mark Netflix’s tenth year in Australia. The event unveiled a closer look at the impact of that first decade, how our investment has flowed through the economy, regional towns across Australia, industry employment, and how far local stories have travelled. 
This production location map
 shows where it all happened.
Reaching Every Corner of Australia
From 2016-2025, our investment and productions in Australia have contributed 
more than A$2.5B
 to Australia's GDP.  That’s $680K to the Australian economy every single day over the last ten years — or in another treasured Aussie currency — enough to buy every Australian around 20 sausage snags.
That investment shows up in the day-to-day of local production. For the shows that have already launched in 2026, including 
Heartbreak High Season 3, My Brilliant Career
 and 
War Machine,
 each worked with an average of 
600 businesses
 across Australia. 
Our productions haven't just stayed in capital cities. As part of our local production, we've shot across 
60 cities and towns in Australia
, transforming regional towns into movie sets and having a huge impact on the locations and people behind the productions. From
My Brilliant Career 
transforming the Barossa Valley and Yallum Park into the 1900s
, to
War Machine transforming a small Alpine town in Victoria into a
US Army team’s fight for survival, many of the productions shot across Australian locations support regional communities, jobs and economies. 
Marcus Warner, President, Bright Chamber of Commerce: 
“A lot of Bright locals lost employment because the ski season ended early. War Machine not only delivered a significant economic boom to the town, but it actually then had a long-lasting effect, with a number of people leaving to go and work in the film and television industry and small businesses set up off the back of it.”
The People Behind the Titles
Behind every title is a mass of talented people. Netflix's total investment has resulted in 
more than 6,000 cast and crew
 employed in Australia, with another 
10,000 extras and day hires
 working across all levels of the creative industry over the last ten years.
We've also invested in the next generation, with 
more than 2,000 people supported
 through our skills and talent development initiatives via our partnerships with AFTRS, NIDA, Screenworks and Bus Stop Films. In fact, since 2021, 
Netflix has invested more than $10 million AUD in building the capacity
 of the Australian screen industry through skills, training and on-the-job training programs.
Sasha
 Parlett, Masters of Screen Production at AFTRs, and a proud Malyangapa and Barkindji woman: 
“I’m so grateful to Netflix for backing tangible pathways for First Nations practitioners, with programs such as the AFTRS & Netflix First Nations Pathway Scholarship. First Nations people have been telling stories for tens of thousands of years, and getting the opportunity to learn how to shape those stories for the big screen completely levels the playing field. Not only does it give our voices, and the work we create, the legitimacy it deserves, but it opens doors to opportunities we wouldn’t otherwise get."
Aussie Stories, Worldwide
Local stories don't stay local for long. In 2025 alone, Australian content on Netflix generated 
more than 1.2B viewing hours worldwide
.
Since the Global Top 10 launched, over 20 Australian titles have featured on it between 2021 and 2025. At home, over 80 Australian titles have appeared in our Australia Top 10 (TV or movies) since it launched in 2021.
Some have gone big. 
Heartbreak High
 Season 1 amassed 42.6M viewing hours during three weeks in the Global Top 10 and reached the Top 10 in more than 43 countries. It also won the 2023 International Emmy® for Best Kids: Live-Action, alongside AACTA and Logie wins. 
Boy Swallows Universe
 spent three consecutive weeks in the Global Top 10 and has generated over 16M views globally since launch.
It's not only originals. Over 600 licensed Australian series and films have been available on Netflix globally between 2016 and 2025. 
In Australia, we’ve seen shows like 
Kath & Kim
, 
Fisk
 and kids show 
The InBESTigators
 gain a genuine second life and global fandom on Netflix. 
Fisk
 broke into our global Top 10 in its second week on the platform, and garnered 11.5 million viewing hours in the second half of 2025. 
The InBESTigators
 also has a huge global following, with 8 million viewing hours in the second half of 2025. 
After premiering on ABC in 2019, 
Love on the Spectrum
 landed on Netflix in 2020 and quickly built an international fandom. On the strength of that success, we commissioned 
Love on the Spectrum US
 in 2022, and is now in its fourth season. For the production company Northern Pictures, it’s been a genuine step-change in scale and visibility - as the most Primetime Emmy-awarded Australian company.
We also invest in films and series while they’re being developed, helping producers and distributors bring more ambitious projects to the screen. For example, most recently Netflix Australia commissioned
The Airport Chaplain
 and
2.6 Seconds
, exporting great Australian stories/ talent to a global audience. 
“It’s quite exciting to have a platform like Netflix for 2.6 Seconds, especially for First Nations stories. I hope it helps continue breaking down the glass ceiling or the assumption that these stories don’t travel because I think what a global sale to Netflix says is that these stories, if they’re made well, if they’re urgent enough, if they demand our attention, they can be seen by the world.” 
Darren Dale, Director of 2.6 Seconds and Managing Director of Blackfella Films
Here for the Long Haul
Amanda Duthie, Director of Content at Netflix ANZ, said: “
What I love most about the last decade of Netflix in Australia is the opportunities it has opened up for the people behind the stories. A small town becomes a film set, and locals find new work. A show made for Australian audiences finds fans on the other side of the world. Every one of those moments starts with a local idea and a local team, and I’m proud we get to help it travel.”
A decade in, our focus is the same as when we started: backing Australian creators, working with local production companies and businesses of all sizes, and getting great Australian stories in front of as many people as possible.
