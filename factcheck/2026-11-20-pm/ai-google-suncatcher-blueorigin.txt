Google、宇宙AIデータセンター構想「Project Suncatcher」でベゾス氏のBlue Originと提携　2029年に衛星打ち上げへ
公開
2026年11月20日 09時25分
[ITmedia]

Googleで優先するソースとして追加
　米Googleは11月19日（現地時間）、衛星軌道上にAIデータセンターを構築する構想「Project Suncatcher」で、ジェフ・ベゾス氏率いる米Blue Originと提携したと発表した。2029年第1四半期を目標に、Blue Originの大型ロケット「New Glenn」で衛星を太陽同期軌道に打ち上げる。Blue Originも同日、Googleと打ち上げサービス契約を結んだと発表した。
（画像：Blue Origin）
　GoogleがProject Suncatcherのために大型ロケットを専用で利用するのは、今回が初めて。各衛星は、データセンターのラック1本分程度の計算能力を持つという。衛星の数は明らかにしていない。
「New Glenn」（画像：Blue Origin）

　GoogleでProject Suncatcherを担当するシニアディレクターのトラビス・ビールズ氏は、Blue Originとの専用打ち上げを、コンピューティングの将来に向けたロードマップ上の重要なマイルストーンと位置付けている。Blue OriginでNew Glennを担当するシニアバイスプレジデントのジョーダン・チャールズ氏は、New Glennは大型ロケットならではの打ち上げ能力と大きな搭載容積により、顧客に柔軟性をもたらすとコメントした。

Blue Origin 
announced Thursday
 that its New Glenn rocket will launch data center satellites for Google in 2029 as part of that company’s Project Suncatcher program.
Google disclosed it was working on scalable orbiting data centers 
about a year ago
 and characterized the project as one of its research “moonshots.” The mission on New Glenn is designed to deploy satellites, each with computing capacity at roughly the scale of a data center rack, into Sun-synchronous orbit where they can receive nearly continuous solar energy.
“Project Suncatcher is a long-term moonshot to scale AI infrastructure in space by tapping into the abundant solar power in orbit,” said Travis Beals, senior director of Project Suncatcher at Google, in the news release. “This dedicated launch with Blue Origin represents a crucial milestone on our roadmap as we expand the boundaries of what’s possible for the future of compute.”
Catching a ride while they still can
The announcement comes as US commercial heavy lift rockets, including New Glenn, are filling up their capacity through the year 2030. The Google mission will fly on the 7×2 variant of the New Glenn rocket, the smaller version of the vehicle that has launched three times to date.
The purpose of the 2029 launch will likely be to test the ability to network solar-powered satellites equipped with AI chips and demonstrate machine-learning compute in space. In that sense, it will be a technology demonstration. Google did not say who it was contracting with to build the satellites, which may weigh something on the order of several tons.
Perhaps the bigger question for Google is not whether it can make this technology work—but whether it is tenable for a company to have ambitions for a large constellation of orbital data centers in the 2030s without its own launch vehicle.
Need for super cheap launch
There remain a lot of questions about the economic viability of orbital data centers, 
but almost everyone agrees
 on one universal truth: Without ultra low-cost, heavy lift capability, there is no way that data centers in space can be economically competitive with terrestrial data centers.
Upon announcing Project Suncatcher a year ago, Google said it was planning for the mid-2030s, when launch costs are projected to drop to as little as $200 per kg. The only rocket likely capable of reaching those costs during the next decade is SpaceX’s Starship vehicle, 
but in recent months it has become clear
 that SpaceX will prioritize its own payloads (including Starmind data centers) on Starship. For Google to reach its target price of $200 per kg, it would likely require a fully optimized Starship V4 (the company is currently flying a V3 Starship) that reaches a lift capacity of approximately 200 metric tons to orbit.
SpaceX has powerful incentives to prioritize its own orbital computing infrastructure, potentially leaving Google dependent on whatever Starship capacity and pricing SpaceX is willing to offer outside customers. So how does Google plan to reach orbit in the 2030s?
Perhaps this explains the new partnership with Blue Origin, which presently covers a single launch but could grow to more. By the 2030s Blue Origin should be flying the larger 9×4 variant of its New Glenn rocket, but this vehicle could face a cost disadvantage against a fully reusable, high-cadence Starship. And Blue Origin is planning its own orbital data center constellation, 
Project Sunrise
. So again, Google could be stuck subsidizing a competitor while paying higher launch costs.
Can anyone else do this?
Perhaps another company, such as Rocket Lab, Relativity Space, or Stoke Space, will have a larger rocket by then.
But developing a super-heavy-lift rocket requires enormous capital. SpaceX has spent more than $15 billion developing Starship, according to its 2026 securities filings, and Blue Origin has likewise invested billions in developing New Glenn. In terms of time, both companies have put more than a decade of work into their large vehicles. For now, all of these other US launch companies are working through the growing pains of developing medium-lift vehicles.
At this point, Google has some not-great options. It can hope that one of the newer US launch companies steps up for commercial satellite operators and decides not to pursue its own constellation. It could buy one of those newer launch companies. Or Google could hire some rocket scientists to start developing powerful rocket engines and begin chasing SpaceX and Blue Origin in launch—both of which have a 25-year head start.

グーグル、「宇宙でAI」大型打ち上げでブルーオリジンと提携
太田 亮三
2026年11月20日 12:39
 Googleは、地球の軌道上で太陽光発電とAIインフラの拡張を行なう計画「プロジェクト・サンキャッチャー」の最初の大型打ち上げについて、Blue Origin(ブルーオリジン)と提携したと発表した。2029年第1四半期を目標に、ブルーオリジンの大型ロケット「New Glenn」(ニューグレン)によって専用で打ち上げられる。 
 Googleの「プロジェクト・サンキャッチャー」は、軌道上の豊富な太陽光発電を利用し、複数の衛星が連携、宇宙空間におけるAIインフラを拡張するという長期的で壮大な計画。すでに試験衛星は打ち上げられており、追加する計画も明らかにされている。 
グーグル、TPUを宇宙へ　放射線・振動・冷却を検証する「Project Suncatcher」
2026年9月25日 12:47
 今回発表された提携はプロジェクト・サンキャッチャーにおける最初の大型衛星打上げという位置づけで、GoogleのAIチップ(TPU)を搭載した、データセンターのラック相当の処理能力という太陽光発電衛星が、太陽同期軌道に導入される計画。Googleは、宇宙空間における機械学習コンピューティングの規模拡大を目指す研究の、重要な節目になるとしている。 
 なお、ブルーオリジンの大型ロケット「ニューグレン」は、4月に実施した3号機の打ち上げで、搭載衛星の軌道投入に失敗、米AST SpaceMobileの低軌道通信衛星7号機の運用が断念されるなどしているほか、6月には4号機向けエンジンの燃焼試験中に大爆発する事故が発生し発射場が損傷、現在は打ち上げ関連施設が復旧作業中となっている。ブルーオリジンの創業者はジェフ・ベゾス氏。
