メモリ24GBという“現実解”　Acerの最新ポータブルゲーミングPC「Predator Atlas 8」で重いゲームはどこまで動く？　軽めのタイトルは何時間プレイできた？

　加速度的な進化を遂げているポータブルゲーミングPCの世界に、Acerから新モデル「Predator Atlas 8」が登場した。
　プロセッサに「
Intel Arc G3 Extreme
」を採用し、8型ディスプレイを搭載したモデルで、兄弟モデルとして7型ディスプレイの「
Predator Atlas 7
」も用意されている。
　今回は日本でも近日中に発売が予定されているPredator Atlas 8で、最新世代のポータブルゲーミングPCの実用性を試してみた。
　なお、Predator Atlas 8はメモリ容量（16GBまたは24GB）とSSD（512GBまたは1TB）により3モデル用意されるが、ここでは最上位モデル（PA08-I51-A94Z）を取り上げる。OSはWindows 11 Homeだ。

AcerのポータブルゲーミングPC「Predator Atlas 8」のキービジュアル
王道スタイルのポータブルゲーミングPC　大画面＆TMRスティックで快適プレイ
　Predator Atlas 8は、中央にディスプレイ、左右にコントローラーを備えている。昨今のポータブルゲーミングPCにおける王道スタイルを踏襲したデザインだ。
　ディスプレイは8型で、解像度は1920×1200ピクセル、最大500ニト、リフレッシュレートは120Hzとなる。パネル表面にはCorning Gorilla Glass with DXCを用いて反射光をカットしており、直射日光の下でも高い視認性を実現しているという。もちろん、10点マルチタッチにも対応する。
見た目は、いたって普通のポータブルゲーミングPCだ
ディスプレイは8型で、解像度は1920×1200ピクセルとなる
ボディー背面。中央にPreadtaorのロゴがあり、電源オン時はロゴが光る
両手で持ったプレイスタイル。表面のコーティングで反射が抑えられている。表面のボタン／スティック／パッドはスタンダードなレイアウトだ。個人差はあると思われるが、両手の親指で届く範囲にあり操作しやすい
　本体サイズは約299（幅）×127.4（奥行き）×28.5〜58.37（厚さ）mmと、Nintendo Switch 2よりもひと回り大きいが、大幅に大きいというほどではない。ただし厚みについては、発熱量の違いとそれに対応する冷却機構の分だけそもそも厚く、グリップ形状も加わるため、かなり厚く感じる。
　重量は公称値が約852g（SSDが512GBの下位モデルは約800g）、実測では866gだった。グリップ形状による持ちやすさもあって重すぎるとは感じないが、長時間プレイではそれなりに疲れるかもしれない。

　コントローラー部分を見てみよう。左右にTMRジョイスティック、左に十字パッド、右にA／B／X／Yボタンを備える。左側の縦方向にはGame Bar／Home Widgetボタンと表示ボタン、右側の縦方向にはPredatorSenseボタンとメニューボタンが並ぶ。
　上部にはバンパー（L／Rボタン）とホール効果式のアナログトリガー（L／R）、背面にはマクロボタン（L／R）とトリガー用の調節スイッチ（L／R）がある。
　ゲーミングでポイントになるのは、まずTMRジョイスティックだ。TMR方式なので、原理的にドリフトが生じない。
　そして背面のトリガー調節スイッチは、スライド式でトリガーのストローク量を調節できるもので、浅く／深くといったフィーリングを好みに合わせられる。

The new series of gaming handhelds featuring 
Intel's Arc G3 Extreme
 chip has caught the attention of many PC gamers thanks to their outstanding performance, and we've already tested and reviewed the 
Acer Predator Atlas 8
and the 
MSI Claw 8 EX AI+
.
Although they each received the same overall 
4.5-star rating 
here at Windows Central, there's some serious nuance that I think potential buyers should know about.
Is it worth spending hundreds more on the MSI Claw 8 EX 
AI
+? Or can you get a satisfying gaming experience on the more affordable Predator Atlas 8? Let's find out.

Grip preference plays a big role in your choice
A look at the Xbox controller grip style on the MSI Claw 8 EX AI+. 
(Image credit: Daniel Rubino)
I'll start by saying that, in our experience, both handhelds are 
super comfortable
. The Claw 8 EX AI+ has a flared 
Xbox-style grip
 setup that I know some love, while the Atlas 8 goes for a more 
fused approach
 à la 
Steam Deck
.
Although the Claw 8 EX AI+ weighs a bit less than the Atlas 8, I never once found Acer's handheld to be too much. Both have smart weight distribution that makes them feel lighter than they really are.
(Image credit: Future)
The Atlas 8's grips don't quite flare out as much, and it reminds me more of the Steam Deck. This should come down to personal preference.
Both handhelds have 
dual fans
 inside, and hot air is routed away from your hands and face, as it should be. Inside, you'll find an 
accessible M.2 SSD

For added security, a 
fingerprint reader

Windows Hello

A look at how the Claw 8 EX AI+'s display hangs down over the bottom edge. 
(Image credit: Daniel Rubino)
One last thing: I've noticed that not everyone loves the 
overhanging display
 as found on MSI's Claw 8 EX AI+. It helps MSI shave off some weight, no doubt, but some will probably go for the boxier, more standard approach as found in the Atlas 8.

There's not much to say about the display, at least in terms of differences. The Atlas 8 and Claw 8 EX AI+ use the same 
8-inch IPS

120Hz variable refresh rate

If I have to choose between an OLED display and Intel's Arc G3 Extreme performance, I'll take the latter every time.
Acer Predator Atlas 8 review
A lack of 
OLED 
is notable across both handhelds, but I don't think it's as big a deal as some assume.
The screens are vivid, bright, and sharp, and I stopped noticing a change from my desktop OLED monitor once I started gaming.
If I dig a bit deeper, I'll say the lack of OLED is a bigger deal in the pricier Claw 8 EX AI+. But I'd take the Arc G3 Extreme's performance any day as compensation.

Acer lets you set trigger pull depth with two toggles on the back of the handheld, something MSI doesn't have.
The Claw 8 EX AI+ is in the same general boat as the Atlas 8, with

I gamed for countless hours on the Acer handheld, and the only thing I didn't love is the minimalist D-Pad. MSI's clicky version with raised directional headings is much more my style.

We put both handhelds through a stress test to see how they handle heat. As you can see from the Claw 8 EX AI+ FLIR images above, nothing gets too hot to handle.
I ultimately discovered the same thing with the Predator Atlas 8. No matter how hard I pushed it, it kept running cool and quiet.
Battery life comes down to power profiles
Plenty of settings are on board the handhelds to help boost battery life. 
(Image credit: Future)
Testing battery life came out a bit skewed, but as I explained in my Atlas 8 review, it's due to how power profiles are configured. Both handhelds have the same 80Wh battery.
The Claw 8 EX AI+'s 
Endurance
 mode drops the handheld to 
15W
, which comes out to about 
3 hours and 23 minutes
 of life in PCMark 10's Gaming rundown test.
The Predator Atlas 8's 
Quiet
 mode only drops to 
25W
. That 
10W difference
 shows up, with the handheld hitting 
2 hours 
in the same rundown.
Intel's Arc G3 Extreme is rather efficient for the performance it delivers. 
(Image credit: Daniel Rubino)
The good news is that you can manually set the Atlas 8 as low as 
10W 
for more extreme endurance situations. This should help bring it in line with the Claw 8 EX AI+.
Bottom line? Battery life is perfectly fine in both handhelds. Demanding games with ray tracing will sap it faster, just like any PC, while lighter indie games will go for hours more.

 and use custom software overlays; both Acer and MSI versions have 
some bugs
.
In the case of the Atlas 8, the PredatorSense overlay sometimes wouldn't appear when I hit the shortcut button. Other times, adjusting one setting would affect something completely unrelated, like RGB lighting turning off.

In Rubino's time with MSI's software, he noticed some games weren't showing up properly in the launcher. Neither software is perfect, and in either case, the cons certainly don't outweigh the pros.
Rubino and I plan to load 
Winhanced
 onto the Atlas 8 and Claw 8 EX AI+, which would probably solve a lot of the issues that might crop up around Windows 11 and custom software.
Acer Predator Atlas 8 vs. MSI Claw 8 EX AI+ — Which should you buy?
Acer's Predator Atlas 8 can save you money and still land you outstanding performance. 
(Image credit: Future)
Your final decision between the Predator Atlas 8 and Claw 8 EX AI+ will weigh heavily on personal preferences. You might like the grip, controls, and overall design more on one than the other, and that's perfectly reasonable.
What I can say is that the display is identical and battery life is practically the same; not surprising with the same 80Wh capacity and the same Intel chip inside.
MSI's Claw 8 EX AI+ managed to edge out the Atlas 8 in performance, and some of that probably lies in it having more RAM. Still, the Atlas 8 runs the most demanding games admirably.
Have deeper pockets? You can't go wrong with MSI's Claw 8 EX AI+. 
(Image credit: Daniel Rubino)

hiked to $1,449.99
, but that $350 difference should still weigh heavily.
Bottom line? 
Stick with MSI's Claw 8 EX AI+
 for the defining gaming handheld experience of 2026, money be damned. But if you're searching for the best value right now, it has to be the Acer Predator Atlas 8.
Affordable pick
Acer
Predator Atlas 8
Acer's Predator Atlas 8 costs $350 less than the Claw 8 EX AI+ despite having an identical Arc G3 Extreme chip and 120Hz display. It's the most value-conscious decision, and although it falls behind in some areas, it still delivers industry-leading performance.

MSI
Claw 8 EX AI+
MSI's Claw 8 EX AI+ represents the very best of gaming handhelds in 2026, but at $350 more than Acer's Predator Atlas 8, you'll really need to be in love with the design and controls to make it count.
