　米オレゴン州のダン・レイフィールド司法長官と米バージニア州のジェイ・ジョーンズ司法長官は11月23日（現地時間）、米SpaceXなどを提訴したと発表した。同社のAI「Grok」が、児童性的虐待コンテンツ（CSAM）や本人の同意のない性的画像を生成・拡散したとしている。両州は連携しつつ別々に、それぞれの巡回裁判所で訴訟を起こした。
バージニア州の訴状冒頭
　GrokはxAIが2023年11月に
リリース
したAIで、Xと統合して提供されている。xAIは2026年2月に
SpaceXに統合
され、現在は
SpaceXSIとなっている
。
　両州によると、Grokは既存の写真1枚から、本人に知られないまま、その人物が裸や半裸の姿、あるいは性的行為をしているリアルな画像や動画を生成できた。実際にそうした画像も生成していたという。両州は次のように主張している。SpaceXと子会社は、AI開発競争で他社と差別化するために業界標準の安全策を無視し、Grokが性的コンテンツを生成できることを宣伝材料にした。Grokを「反抗的な一面」を持つAIコンパニオンとして売り出し、ヌードなどの性的画像を生成できる「Spicy」モードも用意して宣伝した。その一方で、消費者には強固な保護策があると説明しており、その説明は虚偽だったとしている。
Spicyモードについての説明文（6月に公開した新株式発行届出目論見書の訂正事項分より）
　Spicyモードは、xAIが2025年8月に始めた画像・動画生成機能「Grok Imagine」のモードの1つだ。「Normal」「Fun」などのモードと並んで選ぶことができ、オンにすると部分的なヌードを含む性的なコンテンツを生成できると、提供当初から米メディアが報じていた。
　両州は、非営利団体Center for Countering Digital Hate（CCDH）の調査結果を引用した。それによると、2025年12月29日から2026年1月8日までの約10日間に、GrokはX上で約300万枚の性的画像を作成・投稿した。このうち約2万3000枚は児童を描写したとみられる。これは1分当たり約190枚、児童の画像は約41秒に1枚のペースに当たる。CCDHが特定した児童の画像のうち約3割は、特定から1週間以上たっても公開されたままだった。「削除」された画像の多くも、別のURLからアクセスできる状態だったという。
　両州は、問題の行為の差し止めや民事制裁金、不正に得た利益の返還などを求めている。
　ジョーンズ長官は「この訴訟は、Xにアカウントを持ち、データや画像は安全だという同社の偽りの説明に欺かれた数百万人のバージニア州民のために正義を求めるものだ」と述べた。さらに「強力なツールを展開する企業が、消費者の安全を無視してそれをイノベーションと呼ぶことは許されない」とコメントした。レイフィールド長官は「SpaceXはGrokとXプラットフォームに、容認できない危険なコンテンツの作成、ホスト、共有を許した」と非難した。安全策がないことは「バグではなく機能」であり、Spicyモードとして宣伝されていたとも述べた。
　Xで安全対策を担当する公式アカウント「@Safety」は同日、訴訟には触れずに
声明を投稿
した。声明は、未成年者が関わる性的コンテンツや、同意なく作られた実在の人物の露骨な画像は、XにもGrokにも存在してはならないとしている。Grok Imagineでこうした画像を作ろうとした人物は、利用規約に故意に違反し、安全策を積極的に回避しようとしたと主張した。今年初めに悪用が見つかった際には、迅速に保護策を強化したと説明している。現在は、画像の生成前にすべてのプロンプトを審査し、生成後にもすべての出力を確認する分類器を導入しているという。違反したアカウントへの措置や、法執行機関への通報も行っているとした。
　Grokを巡っては、1月に実在の人物の写真を性的に加工する使い方が問題になった。米カリフォルニア州が正式な調査を始めたほか、英国の規制当局Ofcomと情報保護当局ICO、フランスの検察当局も調査に乗り出している。

On Monday, Virginia and Oregon became the first states to sue SpaceX, alleging that Grok continues to sexualize, unchecked, real photos of mostly women and children.
SpaceX acquired xAI
 in February and now must handle the ongoing reckoning over the controversial rollout of Grok’s “spicy mode”—a model feature Musk hyped but was soon found to generate a flood of non-consensual intimate imagery (NCII) and child sex abuse images (CSAM).
Initial safeguards were so lax that Grok sometimes generated 
nude images without being asked
. Although X Safety and xAI (now known as SpaceXSI) were 
silent at first
, Musk’s companies eventually apologized and claimed that additional safeguards were implemented and policies redesigned to block harmful outputs. However, state attorneys general alleged that those statements were “misleading” and that SpaceXSI instead quietly “embraced” sexualized content to seize more AI market share, as Grok continues to generate NCII and CSAM today.
Without intervention, kids in Virginia and Oregon risk a lifetime of distress, as Grok images will likely be impossible to remove online.
“Once created and shared,” Grok-generated CSAM and NCII “can never be fully erased, and it becomes a permanent record of the child’s abuse that may resurface for the rest of the child’s life,” Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones’ complaint said. “Virginia has a profound interest in protecting its children from this harm.”
Millions allegedly harmed
Millions of residents were allegedly harmed, as the attorneys general seek to protect every Grok or X user in their state. Many Grok victims will likely suffer from chronic anxiety, depression, and PTSD, as well as reputational harm, as some viewers may not realize the images are AI-generated, the states alleged. Further—due to Grok’s integration with the social media platform X—users as young as 13 may have been exposed to the AI content and possibly re-traumatized if they, too, are victims of sexual abuse, the states alleged.
“xAI not only knowingly endangered kids and our communities, but they also lied to our faces about non-existent safety measures,” Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield said in a 
press release.
States alleged that xAI could have conducted rigorous red-teaming to block outputs before the new feature launched. But instead, the X Safety team responsible for Grok red-teaming was reduced to two or three team members, states alleged.
Overall, rather than use industry-standard safeguards, Musk’s firms allegedly put profits over women’s and children’s safety, states alleged, while complaining that SpaceXSI won’t even publicly identify “the officers responsible for safety governance.”
“Industry standard requires not only building guardrails but also vigilantly monitoring whether they hold,” states alleged. SpaceXSI is doing neither effectively, states alleged.
SpaceX responds
Asked for comment, SpaceXSI shared an X Safety post on X, which repeated claims that the company has a “zero-tolerance” policy for “any forms of child sexual exploitation.” X Safety said that the company “acted promptly to tighten” Grok protections, which are supposedly “designed to refuse any request to generate explicit images of minors or real people.” That statement also claimed that X Safety continually tests and adjusts protections when bad actors find ways to avoid filters, while relying on “sophisticated classifiers that screen every prompt” to catch harmful outputs “before an image is generated and check every output after.” Although the statement did not mention whether 
xAI sues users 
who break Grok rules to generate CSAM and NCII, it emphasized that xAI reports harmful content generated to police.
“When someone tries to break our safeguards and generate this content, we take appropriate measures, including actioning accounts and notifying law enforcement,” X Safety said. “Attempting to create this material is a serious crime, and the individuals who do it should be prosecuted to the fullest extent of the law. We will continue to work with the authorities via all available means to pursue these bad actors.”
However, state attorneys general alleged that reports from independent researchers and news outlets this year have shown that Grok is still generating NCII and CSAM. They noted that the chatbot “even advises users how to circumvent its own limited moderation measures to generate those images.” In some cases, a Virginia state probe found that Grok complied “with a user request to draft a prompt designed to avoid moderation.” So, even when filters work, the chatbot helps determined users immediately get around them, states alleged.
Nothing xAI did put a dent in the harmful outputs, least of all 
putting the feature behind a paywall
, states alleged, which only ensured maximum profits. A troubled New York City Council speaker, Julie Menin, confronted SpaceX this October with proof that attempts to digitally undress her own photo still worked for free Grok users, states alleged.
Concluding its probe, Virginia found that moderation remains “inconsistent and ineffective,” with Grok sometimes carrying out prompts “that it later blocked.”
In its complaint, Virginia alleged that Grok protections “were weak, ineffective, sparsely enforced, and easily circumvented.” Oregon agreed that rather than act to protect users, SpaceX entities repeatedly made statements “with the intent to deceive consumers” that Grok was safe. Most egregiously to state attorneys general, in January, Elon Musk denied any harm to real people, claiming that Grok is only “supposed [to] allow upper body nudity of imaginary adult humans (not real ones) and only generated nudity of fake people (not real ones).”
“Contrary to Musk’s representation,” Grok “makes the same sexualizing edits to photographs of real, identifiable people,” states warned. “Defendants intentionally released a product that inundated their platforms with CSAM, NCII, and non-consensual sexualized images,” they alleged, and other “contemporaneous statements by Musk make clear that Grok’s ability to create sexualized material was part of xAI’s business strategy.”
Grok CSAM, NCII may trigger steep fines
It’s also troubling to states that the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children (NCMEC) reported to Congress that more than 90 percent of the Grok-generated CSAM that xAI submitted to its CyberTipline was considered a “dead end.”
As NCMEC explained, xAI initially refused to provide NCMEC and police with “basic user information, including location information, that law enforcement needs to act.” To fix the problem, NCMEC had to set up a meeting with xAI and get the reports resubmitted. This, states alleged, confirmed that “the information existed and that xAI had not provided it.” The concerning omission allegedly slowed down police response, just as the average number of Grok CSAM reports jumped by 88 percent from December 2025 to January 2026, then increased by another 66 percent from January to June 2026.
“X users were subjected to the risk that any person, at any time, could use their likeness to create CSAM, NCII, or other non-consensual sexualized images,” states alleged.
Lawmakers have asked state courts for an injunction blocking SpaceX entities from violating consumer protection laws by allegedly operating Grok without industry standard safeguards. If they win, they want SpaceX to pay thousands in civil penalties per violation, as well as disgorge any ill-gotten gains from marketing the chatbot as safe to users.
“Most people who use social media do not want to contribute to the harm caused to victims of CSAM, NCII, and other non-consensual sexualized images by being exposed to those images online,” states alleged.
Although Oregon’s complaint only vaguely estimates the number of users Grok attracted with Musk’s allegedly deceitful marketing strategy as at least “hundreds of thousands,” Virginia’s complaint included more detailed estimates. It said that approximately 6.4 million X accounts were linked to geolocations in the state, while the Grok application was downloaded 986,624 times by users with Virginia-based IP addresses.
“When xAI allowed Grok’s creators to sideline consent to turn a profit, they prioritized growth and engagement over safety, and they deceived the public about it,” Jones said. “Today’s filings are the first step towards holding this company accountable.”

性的な画像を生成できるAIやアプリは、Grokのほかにもあります。今回の訴えが問題にしているのは、生成できること自体ではありません。論点は3つです。
1つ目は、SpaceXがそれを売りにしていたという点です。両州は、同社がGrokを「反抗的な一面」を持つAIコンパニオンとして売り出し、その画像・動画生成技術がCSAMや同意のない性的画像の作成に使われると知っていたと主張しています。ヌードなどの性的画像を生成できる「spicy mode」を用意し、宣伝していたとも指摘しました。バージニア州は、同社がAI開発競争で差別化するために業界標準の安全策を無視したとしています。オレゴン州は、成長と利用の拡大を安全より優先して安全策を弱めたと表現しています。
レイフィールド長官は「SpaceXは、明らかに必要な安全策を備えていない製品を、それと知りながら設計した」と述べました。「必要な安全策がないことは、バグではなく機能とみなされ、『spicy mode』として宣伝されていた」とも話しています。
2つ目は、実在の人物が対象になり、それがSNS上に広がったという点です。バージニア州の発表によると、Grokは既存の写真1枚から、その人物が裸や半裸の姿、あるいは性的行為をしているリアルな画像や動画を、本人が知らないまま生成できました。両州は、GrokがXに組み込まれていたことで、性的画像の作成と拡散が広がったとしています。
両州の発表は、非営利団体CCDHの調査を引用しています。それによると、2025年12月29日から2026年1月8日までの間に、GrokはX上で性的画像を約300万枚作成・投稿し、うち約2万3000枚は子どもを描写したとみられるものでした。1分あたり約190枚、子どもの性的画像は約41秒に1枚のペースです。子どもの画像の3割近くは、特定から1週間以上たっても公開されたままで、「削除」された画像の多くも別のURLから閲覧できたとしています。
3つ目は、消費者への説明が事実と違ったという点です。同社は、こうした画像の生成を防ぐ強固な保護策があると公に説明していました。それでもオレゴン州とバージニア州を含む数百万人が、有害なコンテンツと、自分の画像が同意なく性的に加工されるリスクにさらされたと両州は主張しています。

Attorneys General Jones and Rayfield 
File Suit
 Against SpaceX, Parent Company of X, xAI, and Grok, Over Creation and Promotion of Child Exploitation and Non-Consensual Images on Platform 
Virginia and Oregon Attorneys General seek accountability for harms and significant new protections for consumers 
***Read the full filing 
here
*** 
RICHMOND, Va
. – Today, Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones took a significant step in holding operators of X and its AI tool, Grok, accountable for knowingly failing to protect Virginians, while falsely promoting the safety of its platform and AI tool.  
In the 
lawsuit
, the Commonwealth of Virginia alleges that SpaceX, the telecommunications and space transportation conglomerate that operates the social network X and its artificial intelligence company, xAI (now called “SpaceX Super Intelligence”), and developed the Grok family of large language models, failed to prevent Grok from generating sexualized images of people, including children, without their knowledge or consent—while simultaneously promoting the safety of the social media platform and AI tool. The Grok-generated content includes non-consensual intimate imagery (NCII), other non-consensual sexualized images, and Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM). Today’s suit was filed in collaboration with Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield, who filed a separate but similar action.  
“This lawsuit is about seeking justice for the millions of Virginians who maintain accounts on X and were misled by the company’s false reassurances that their data and images would be safe on the platform,” 
said Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones. 
“When xAI allowed Grok’s creators to sideline consent to turn a profit, they prioritized growth and engagement over safety, and they deceived the public about it. Today’s filings are the first step towards holding this company accountable.”    
With a single existing photograph, Grok could, and did, fabricate a realistic image or video of a person nude, or partially nude, or engaged in sexual activity, all without the individual’s knowledge or consent.  This violation is not acceptable.  
The company ignored industry-standard safeguards so xAI could distinguish itself in the AI arms race. Grok’s ability to generate sexualized content was leveraged as a promotional opportunity. These exploitations of Virginian’s safety enabled the widespread creation and distribution of sexualized images through Grok’s integration with X.   
“SpaceX allowed Grok and the X platform to create, host, and share unacceptable and dangerous content—and in fact knowingly designed a product without the obviously necessary safeguards in place,” 
said Attorney General Rayfield.
 “It’s no surprise that Grok and X became a haven for perpetrators using AI to create and share child sexual abuse material. The lack of needed safety measures was considered a feature, not a bug, which they promoted as ‘spicy mode.’” 
In his filing, Attorney General Jones shows that SpaceX and its subsidiaries marketed Grok as an AI companion with a “rebellious streak,” knowing that its video and image generation technology would be used to produce CSAM, non-consensual intimate images of adults and children (NCII), and other sexualized imagery. In fact, X and its subsidiaries created and promoted a “spicy mode” that specifically allowed for the creation of nude and otherwise sexualized images. 
A study by the Center for Countering Digital Harm (“CCDH”) estimated that, between December 29, 2025, and January 8, 2026, Grok created and posted on X approximately three million sexualized images, including approximately 23,000 images that appeared to depict children. That’s an average pace of roughly 190 sexualized images per minute, and one sexualized image of a child approximately every 41 seconds. Nearly 30% of the sexualized images of children identified by CCDH in its study remained publicly accessible in posts on X a week or more after they were identified—and even many of those that were “removed” could still be accessed via separate URLs. 
SpaceX and its subsidiaries publicly assured consumers that robust protections were in place to prevent Grok from creating CSAM, NCII, and other sexualized images. In reality, millions of people — including in Oregon and Virginia — were nevertheless exposed to harmful content and the risk that their own images could be manipulated into sexually explicit content without their consent. 
“Time and again, xAI and X told consumers they were being protected from abuses. That was false. Instead, the tool permitted users to routinely create and circulate non-consensual intimate imagery and sexualized images, and child sexual abuse material (CSAM), causing harm to individuals and children,” 
added Attorney General Jones
