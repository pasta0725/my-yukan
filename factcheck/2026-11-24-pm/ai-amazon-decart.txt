Amazon snaps up Decart’s AI optimization tech for $7.5B as it prepares to sell Trainium chips
Amazon has agreed to acquire a good chunk of Israeli startup Decart.
The e-commerce and cloud giant is paying
 $7.5 billion
 for the startup, according to Bloomberg. 
Decart has made a name for itself building interactive real-time world models — 
most recently Oasis 3
 — and AI video-generation tools. But under the hood, the company stands out for its Decart Optimization Stack (DOS), software that helps AI models run efficiently on Nvidia, Amazon, and Google’s hardware. 
As part of the deal, Amazon will acquire the technology behind DOS, as well as Decart’s founding team and team of AI researchers and engineers, 
Amazon said in a blog post
.
“Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip,” said Peter DeSantis, SVP of foundational AI models, custom silicon, quantum computing, at Amazon. “More efficient compute means better training, better reinforcement learning, faster inference, and better models for customers at a lower cost.”
Amazon, which has so far been using its Trainium chips at its own data centers, is preparing to start selling them to customers as a competitor to Nvidia. Decart’s technology could make it easier for customers to run their workloads on Amazon chips, rather than Nvidia’s, which has been the gold standard so far. 
Decart and Amazon did not immediately return requests for comment.

Amazon is buying Decart’s AI optimization technology and team for a reported $7.5 billion. (GeekWire Illustration / ChatGPT)
Amazon 
confirmed Monday morning
 that it will acquire Decart’s AI optimization technology, known as the Decart Optimization Stack, and bring on the 3-year-old startup’s founders and team of AI researchers and engineers.
Amazon didn’t disclose financial terms. Amazon agreed to pay about $7.5 billion for the assets, 
Bloomberg reported
, citing a person familiar with the matter. The deal is tied to performance targets over five years, 
Globes reported
.
The deal could boost Amazon’s push to compete with Nvidia, which sells most of the chips used for training and running AI models today. Amazon makes its own AI chips, called Trainium, and has signaled plans to start selling them directly to other companies, beyond its own AWS data centers.
“We believe we’re going to have a very significant business selling chips externally,” CEO Andy Jassy 
said on the company’s earnings call
 last month.
Amazon announced the deal the same day Nvidia’s board 
reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO
, ending a leadership shakeup at the chipmaker that put CFO Colette Kress 
in the interim role
.
Decart’s software is designed to get more performance out of each chip, one of the main selling points Amazon needs to win those external customers. Microsoft is building its own 
Maia chips
, mainly for its own AI services, and Google has its TPUs.
Decart’s software has also run on chips from Nvidia and Google. Amazon didn’t say whether that will continue.
“Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip,” said Peter DeSantis, Amazon’s senior vice president of foundational AI models, custom silicon and quantum computing, in the 
blog post announcing the acquisition
.
Decart’s three co-founders are joining Amazon: CEO Dean Leitersdorf, his brother Orian Leitersdorf and Moshe Shalev. Amazon isn’t buying Decart’s AI models, 
Bloomberg reported
. Those include Lucy, which edits live video in real time, and Oasis, which generates interactive virtual worlds.
The deal runs through Annapurna Labs, Amazon’s chip unit, and Decart’s technology will be used to improve performance on Amazon’s Trainium and Inferentia AI chips, 
according to Globes
. The Israeli business publication reported that Annapurna co-founder Nafea Bshara led the negotiations for Amazon.
The founders, who own about 64% of Decart, have committed to stay at Amazon for five years and could receive about $4.8 billion collectively, 
Globes reported
.
Amazon 
bought Annapurna
, also founded in Israel, in 2015 for a reported $350 million. Its engineers went on to design Trainium and Amazon’s other custom chips.
Amazon and Decart have worked together since 2025. Decart was among the first customers on Trainium3, and Amazon 
said last December
 that Decart was running real-time video four times faster on Trainium at half the cost of GPUs.
The deal follows Amazon’s release last month of 
TorchNeuron
, a tool that runs common AI code on Trainium with minimal changes. Both moves target the software advantage that has kept developers on Nvidia hardware, as GeekWire 
explained when the talks surfaced
 in October.
Decart drew interest from other big buyers before Amazon. Anthropic was in talks to acquire it for about $6 billion, 
Bloomberg reported in August
, before it 
walked away in September
. Nvidia held earlier acquisition talks, 
according to CTech
, and invested in Decart’s 
$300 million funding round
 in May.
“From day one, our mission has been to redefine the boundaries of what AI can do and to make it dramatically more efficient along the way,” Dean Leitersdorf said in Amazon’s announcement. “Joining Amazon gives our team the scale, the customers, and the infrastructure to pursue that mission faster than we could on our own.”
The deal is subject to regulatory clearance. Amazon didn’t say what happens to Decart’s AI models or to its existing customers, such as eBay, which is also an investor in the startup.

Key takeaways
Decart’s technology will help Amazon further optimize both its inference and custom silicon performance so more customers can train and run AI models faster and at lower cost.
Since 2025, Decart's team has worked closely with Amazon to deliver strong performance results on Trainium, using a full-stack approach that spans software and hardware.
Today, Amazon announced it has signed an agreement to purchase the proprietary technology behind the Decart Optimization Stack (DOS), and bring the Decart AI co-founders—Dean Leitersdorf, Moshe Shalev, and Orian Leitersdorf—along with their team of AI researchers and engineers, to Amazon. With their deep expertise in full-stack performance optimization across hardware and software, Decart brings a strong connection between technology and talent that will help further 
Amazon’s investment in custom silicon
 and optimize AI inference for its customers.
Amazon has long believed that 
inference speed and compute efficiency
 are central to the future of AI, and has advanced that vision through innovation across its custom silicon portfolio, from 
Graviton
 to 
Trainium
. That work spans large language models, multimodal and agentic systems, and world models, alike. Amazon began working with Decart in 2025, and the collaboration quickly showed how much the two teams shared, from a full-stack view of performance to an appetite for solving some of the world’s most challenging science and engineering problems.
“Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip,” said Peter DeSantis, SVP of foundational AI models, custom silicon, quantum computing, Amazon. “More efficient compute means better training, better reinforcement learning, faster inference, and better models for customers at a lower cost. That's the pillar Trainium and our AI efforts are built on, and from our earliest work with Decart, we knew they were the kind of technologically fearless team who would help us push it even further.”
“From day one, our mission has been to redefine the boundaries of what AI can do and to make it dramatically more efficient along the way,” said Dean Leitersdorf, co-founder and CEO of Decart. “Joining Amazon gives our team the scale, the customers, and the infrastructure to pursue that mission faster than we could on our own. We’re looking forward to exploring what we can build together.”
Completion of the transaction is subject to customary closing conditions, including regulatory clearance.
