Opening the keynote at its Ignite event, Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella outlined the company’s vision for its new 
“frontier
 ecosystem,” announcing Tuesday the general availability of Word, Excel, and PowerPoint inside Copilot, which he described as “the new OS for work.”
The announcement is a striking inversion of how we’ve traditionally used software. Rather than an app like Word being the attraction and 
Clippy the assistant
 to help you use it, Microsoft envisions the new Copilot integrations will see agents driving things, with the software packages riding shotgun. Applications will become plugin components of the agent’s work, backed by an organizational knowledge layer that draws context from emails, meetings, calendars, chats, and files. Users will now be able to create documents, spreadsheets, and presentations directly in Copilot, with the resulting files remaining editable in their respective Office applications.
The rollout marks a major step toward the 
Copilot redesign the company first announced
 in September. Parts of that vision are now becoming “generally available,” as Nadella puts it, with autonomous agents and new governance tools arriving alongside them.
All about agents
Copilot Autopilot (not to be confused with Windows Autopilot) is a “persistent, proactive agent for personal productivity” according to Microsoft. In a nutshell, Autopilot is a cloud-based autonomous AI agent that users can delegate responsibilities to, not just single tasks. Autopilot can be authorized to work across services like email and calendars and complete tasks assigned to it as part of its role. Autopilot will use usage-based billing via Copilot Credits, but final pricing has not been announced ahead of general availability.
Also announced as generally available at the keynote speech was 
Microsoft IQ
, an intelligence platform that houses 
Work IQ
, a context layer that helps agents understand a company’s communications, relationships, and ongoing work. Nadella described Work IQ as perhaps a company’s most valuable database: “It has a deep understanding of the people, their relationships to other people, your emails, your meetings, chats, projects that make up all the fabric of work.” Whether companies want autonomous agents to have access to internal documents and messaging data will have to be determined by their own internal IT review.
Copilot Workspaces is a collaborative environment currently available through Microsoft’s Frontier early-access program, but coming soon will be the ability to bring personal Autopilot agents into Workspaces. Early in the keynote address, Nadella said that “Until now, AI interactions have mostly been single-player. Workspaces makes it a truly multiplayer experience, where people and agents drive towards a common goal, with shared context.”
Governance over trust
Trust, the third pillar of Microsoft’s new frontier ecosystem, was something that Nadella continued returning to during his presentation, and not in a way that some might expect. “We find ourselves in a place where alignment is elusive. Mechanistic understanding of these models is elusive. The question is: How can you even trust all this powerful technology? The reality is, by definition, you can’t,” he said.
In a departure from the norms of “AI is magic” corporate hype sentiment, Nadella conceded that the internal workings of AI thought processes remain difficult to understand, and models cannot be trusted to govern themselves, regardless of whether their answers are accurate. Nadella described the solution as “an architectural imperative to separate the supply of intelligence” from the authority granted to it. Microsoft says each Autopilot agent will have a unique ID, and administrators can restrict permissions or block it entirely. All agent activities are logged for human review, and Autopilot can ask for human approval before engaging in certain actions.
              Human ambition, intelligence, and trust are said to be the three pillars of the frontier ecosystem.
      Human ambition, intelligence, and trust are said to be the three pillars of the frontier ecosystem.
The promised agent controls and admission that AI systems can’t always be trusted are meaningful, but Microsoft is asking businesses and users to place their trust in the company’s own safeguards instead, though the effectiveness of those safeguards against mistakes, rogue actions, or data exposure will still have to be demonstrated. In Microsoft’s view, the solution to untrustworthy intelligence is not to make AI agents trustworthy, but to control what they’re permitted to do. Businesses will have to decide how much autonomy they delegate to agents.
Along with the guardrails, Microsoft announced what it calls the “
AI Transparency Initiative
.” The effort has partnered with 40-plus major companies, including Adobe and Mastercard, to develop open, industrywide standards for AI governance and security. Nadella described the initiative as a way for industry to “help establish an open standard that makes AI safer, more transparent, and more accessible.”
Minding and measuring token-spend
Along with the other announced additions, Microsoft also announced expanding capabilities for 
Agent 365
, its existing control plane for managing AI agents across enterprise environments. The new agent sheriff extends existing identity, security, and management systems to AI agents. Microsoft describes the objective as providing “observability at every layer of the stack.” Agent 365 offers governance controls over agent behavior in what Microsoft says is “autonomous in execution, but bounded and supervised.”
Along with Agent 365 comes 
Agent 365 Business Analytics
: “You can simply think of this as an ERP system of AI usage. You can see exactly what’s driving the spend and connect the spend to the outcomes that drive business value,” said Nadella. Customers will be able to see real-world costs associated with AI token-spend, and compare those metrics to the value of work performed. Microsoft acknowledged that the estimations of assisted 
work hours are determined by a model
, and not a direct measurement of time that may have been saved. This is a telling concession for Microsoft to make as businesses evaluate whether there will be an ROI on AI implementations, but according to Judson Althoff, CEO of Microsoft Commercial Business, “anything you can do with Claude or ChatGPT, you can do better with Copilot, 
for less
.”
The outage in the room
Nadella offered only a brief acknowledgment of 
last week’s outage
 that disrupted access to services like Exchange Online and Teams for users across the globe. “Last week was a reminder of how critical digital infrastructure is as the dial tone for the world,” he said. “We know the trust you place in our services and take that responsibility extremely seriously, and in that context I know we have work to do.”
              Some big promises
      Some big promises
That statement was all the time the elephant in the room was given, but Microsoft did seem to acknowledge the public’s growing concern over data center projects by highlighting the previously discussed “
community-first AI infrastructure plan
.” Not a surprise in a presentation that asks organizations to extend more autonomy to software agents, at a time when Microsoft’s existing infrastructure is under increased scrutiny.

Satya Nadella, Microsoft Chairman and CEO, delivered the opening keynote at 
Ignite in San Francisco
 this morning. Here is an edited version of his remarks.
This week is about two things: First is how we capitalize on new capabilities that are growing exponentially. And second is how do we build the capability to deal with the consequences of these exponentials.
At times like these, I always find it helpful to be grounded in our mission: To empower every person and every organization on the planet to achieve more. In today’s world, that means building a frontier ecosystem. We need to parlay any technological advance into real outcomes and new points of leverage for people, organizations and society at large.
For us, that comes down to three things: Keeping human ambition at the center, ensuring your control of your organization’s intelligence and learning, and trust.
And so, our goal is to build a stack that addresses these three core architectural principles.
Copilot
Copilot is the 
new OS for work
. Copilot brings together all the new form factors of human-computer interaction, whether it is Chat, Cowork, Code or Autopilot, into one experience. It also brings together the disparate categories of knowledge work, coding and business process. That is why we have all of Office with its richness built into Copilot. And underlying it is a model system that matches the right intelligence to the right context to the right form factor to accomplish the task.
Home gives you one place to start, whether you need a quick answer or want to assign and complete complex, multi-step tasks. Code brings the power of an infinite SaaS factory to every individual. It lets you build your own custom SaaS apps, dashboards and much more. And Autopilot is an autonomous agent that keeps moving your work forward 24×7 in all the places where you and your team collaborate.
Underlying all this is Work IQ. You can think of this as the most important database for any organization, with a deep understanding of people, their relationships, documents, emails, meetings, chats and projects that make up the fabric of work.
And we are going further. We are extending Copilot for specific roles: sales, legal, finance, service. We are organizing the skills, connectors and plugins for these roles across the entire surface area of Copilot.
And that includes third-party systems, too. In fact, today we are announcing new plugins for business applications, starting with Dynamics 365. And it is not just Dynamics. We are very excited to partner with Salesforce to 
bring Salesforce to Copilot
.
And we are going beyond that as well. Up until now, we have mostly been in a single player world of people and their agent loops and swarms. But the time has come for a true multiplayer form factor, where people, their agents and other people are all driving towards a common goal, with shared context. This is Copilot Workspaces:

Microsoft IQ
The idea behind IQ is to help every organization 
build, retain and continuously improve
 its own intelligence. Essentially, we are taking the system we built underneath Copilot and externalizing it as a first-class platform that embodies your organizational intelligence as an asset that you continuously build and control.
We are bringing together all of the data you have across Fabric, Foundry and Microsoft 365. And, of course, all the third-party context too. Microsoft IQ comes with hundreds of new connectors we are announcing today, so agents can get the full enterprise context where business gets done. And all this rich context manifests as business plugins that can be shared and inherited across the organization.
Models
For us, it is about mapping intelligence to a given task, with the best economics. This is something we can uniquely do in the context of real-world tasks by tuning our products, harness and tools, and the model system, end-to-end.
And today we are thrilled to announce 
Microsoft-Decision-1.1
, which is now generally available and ready for enterprise deployment. We have added multimodal support. And we have improved performance, making decisions more accurate and responses faster. Foundry of course has all the most popular closed and open weight models, too. We are very excited to bring GPT 6.2 to Foundry. It is a great model that sets a new bar for AI in professional work. And today we are announcing that Meta’s latest models are all coming to Foundry as well.
We are also innovating at the frontier with our own MAI model family that is both compute-efficient when it comes to pre-training and token-efficient when it comes to product deployment. Our goal is to put additional points on the frontier curve informed by real world task-performance.
And we are going further. We are announcing MAI Licensed Weights, meaning our trusted customers will now be able to get access to Microsoft AI model weights through our Frontier Co.
Today, we are 
introducing lots of new models
. But I want to highlight two in particular:
First, Code. We are introducing our new flagship family of agentic coding models: Code 2. Code 2 is at the frontier of cost-performance for everyday enterprise coding tasks. It excels in taking code to production, deep debugging, multifile implementation, repo-wide reasoning and UI at a fraction of frontier cost.
Code 2 Flash is designed for fast, high-volume work like code explanations, targeted edits, routine fixes and rapid iterations. It brings near-flagship quality for a much lower cost. These models are both generally available today on GitHub Copilot, both in the model picker and as part of Copilot Fusion. They are also available in Foundry.
We are taking the same approach in cyber. Cyber 2 Flash is our most capable security model yet. It is a flash-sized model that delivers performance ahead of frontier models at a fraction of the cost per task. And, just as we have done with coding, we are co-designing the model with the tools and systems where it will be used. We are integrating Cyber 2 with Perception.
Agent 365
Successfully deploying AI systems starts with making sure you have observability at every layer of the stack. That is where Agent 365 comes in. It is a control plane that extends your existing governance, identity, security and management systems to the agents in your enterprise. We already have over 50 million agents in Agent 365.
And it includes FinOps, too. As AI scales, every organization needs a clear line between what it spends and the value AI creates. That is why today we are announcing Agent 365 Business Analytics. You can think of it as your ERP system for AI usage. You can see exactly what is driving spend. You can connect spend to outcomes to drive business value. And you can optimize ROI across different scenarios.
Security
The next thing I want to talk about is how you take the environment you have set up, from Copilot to IQ to Agent 365, and secure it. And that is where Perception and our security stack comes in. We are building an end-to-end trust platform. We are helping customers both secure their AI deployments and use AI to strengthen their security posture.
Perception is a 
complete agentic security system
 with teams of red, blue and green agents that run through playbooks to deliver security outcomes at machine speed, with you always in control.
Essentially, think about it like this: red team agents simulate attacks, blue team agents detect attacks and green team agents fix and remediate. With Playbooks, you can compose teams of agents to tackle complex jobs and trigger them to take action autonomously.
Perception helps connect all the signals, sensors and actuators across your identity, client, cloud, apps and agents to intelligence to secure your end-to-end environments. And I am very excited to share that Perception is now generally available.
In addition to the work we have been doing across our products, we are also working across the ecosystem to ensure everyone can innovate at speed and scale, safely. Today, we can see almost everything models touch. But we cannot see what happens inside the model. And what the model tells us about itself is not independent evidence. These nested black boxes are simply unacceptable. We need to be able to examine what AI sees and does, verify its actions independently and control what it is allowed to do.
That is why today we are also announcing the 
Microsoft AI Transparency Initiative
.
There are three parts to this: First, we will expand our collaboration in NIST’s Agent Standards Initiative, drawing on its research to help lead the development of open, industry-wide standards for AI transparency, security and governance.
Second, we are taking a full ecosystem approach. Together, we can enable AI systems that are accountable, independently verifiable and safe by design, regardless of the model or provider. Already, 40 organizations are working with us on defining and applying these standards.
Third, we are working to incorporate the same standards in our products, including MAI models, Foundry and Copilot. Our ambition through our research, our product work and our ecosystem is to help establish an open industry standard that makes AI safer, more transparent and more accessible.
Cobalt 200 VMs
Underneath all of this is unbelievable silicon. We are building a heterogeneous platform working across the ecosystem to bring you all the latest innovation. We are also advancing our own hardware systems, co-designing silicon, servers and infrastructure to get more from every watt, chip and datacenter.
We just announced Maia 300, which will deliver 40 percent more tokens per watt compared with leading GPU-based systems. Azure Boost and Azure Integrated HSM move critical infrastructure and security functions into purpose-built silicon. And today we are announcing general availability of our 
next-generation Cobalt 200 VMs
, which are our highest-performing general-purpose VMs with leading price-performance.
So, that is a quick walk through some of our news. When you look at all that is coming, it is clear that we are entering an era of ubiquitous intelligence. The key imperatives for us are to ensure human ambition, a firm’s control of its own intelligence and the diffusion of the benefits broadly. That is the promise of a frontier ecosystem. And that is what we are here to build together.

Book of News

Microsoft has made Word, Excel and PowerPoint generally available inside its Copilot app, moving a central part of its Office software into the AI interface. The release was announced at Ignite on 17 November, alongside wider availability of Home, which combines Copilot's Chat and Cowork experiences.
Users can create files, edit them and work on them with colleagues without leaving Copilot. The change follows an earlier rollout to Frontier customers in October, so this week's news is broader availability rather than the first demonstration of Office inside the app.
Microsoft is presenting Copilot as a starting point for work that previously began in separate applications. That does not mean those applications have disappeared: the documents, spreadsheets and presentations remain editable in the standalone Office programs.
Office files stay connected to OneDrive
The company says work created in the Copilot app is saved in OneDrive. Files keep the sharing permissions, security labels and compliance controls that organisations already apply through Microsoft 365.
The Office experience includes comments and coauthoring, letting colleagues continue working on the same file as a conversation with Copilot develops. Microsoft's earlier product announcement also described a library for reopening recent Office files and continuing to edit them beside the chat.
That continuity matters to the release. A presentation or spreadsheet is meant to remain a working Office file, rather than becoming a separate output that has to be rebuilt before a team can use it.
Home brings Chat and Cowork together
Home is also generally available. It puts short questions and longer delegated tasks behind one prompt box, with a shared history for Chat and Cowork conversations.
Microsoft says Copilot can recommend Cowork when a request needs more work than a chat answer. If the user agrees, it carries over the request and relevant context instead of requiring them to start again.
Projects, another generally available feature, groups related conversations, tasks and outputs. Users can add instructions and source material for a continuing piece of work, while the resulting files remain stored in OneDrive.
Not every Ignite feature is broadly available
The wider announcement includes several products at earlier stages. Today, a view covering email, meetings, Teams and tasks, is in private preview. Shared Workspaces and the proactive Autopilot agent are reaching customers through Frontier, Microsoft's early-access programme.
Code, which lets people describe an application they want built, is due to become generally available in the coming weeks. Those separate timetables mean the Office release should not be read as a launch date for every capability shown at Ignite.
Microsoft says the subscription licence covers the creation of Excel spreadsheets and PowerPoint presentations, with no separate usage meter for that work. That statement does not make all of its autonomous-agent services free or remove their separate access conditions.
The bottom line
Office inside Copilot is now a generally available way to create and collaborate on familiar Microsoft files. The broader shift towards ongoing AI work is still arriving in stages, with preview features and future releases sitting alongside the newly available Office tools.
