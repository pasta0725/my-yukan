===== https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/cuda-x-libraries-agents-accelerate-scientific-discovery/
(status 200)
With expanded 
NVIDIA CUDA-X
 libraries, including cuPhoton, cuPotential and cuSpa, researchers can sift through vast amounts of data to accelerate research, from astrophysics and materials science to single-cell biology.
The speedups are helping scientists analyze larger datasets and model more complex systems.
The new libraries give AI agents the tools to help researchers make discoveries faster than ever.
Read All NVIDIA Announcements From SC26
Bringing Black Holes Into Focus
Seeing a black hole takes a telescope the size of Earth. The Event Horizon Telescope links radio dishes worldwide, generating petabytes of data that can take months to process.
With NVIDIA cuPhoton.xBeam, a module within the 
cuPhoton Toolkit
, black hole observations from the Event Horizon Telescope can now be correlated orders of magnitude faster than ever. Analysis that once took over a month now takes eight minutes.
By combining global radio dishes into Earth-sized aperture, cuPhoton delivers unprecedented astronomical imaging resolution. cuPhoton successfully correlated observations of powerful black hole jets J1924 and 3C279 in the night sky — making it a reality to reconstruct a Messier 87* black hole galaxy in real time. 
The entire pipeline, from data loading and processing to visualization, runs on distributed multi-GPU systems or a compact 
NVIDIA DGX Spark
 system, helping scientists study how gas spirals inward under a black hole’s powerful gravitational pull.
Steering Fusion Simulations
Sustaining plasma at millions of degrees is essential to achieving stable, continuous fusion energy. But simulating its behavior is costly: on petascale systems, a single time step can take days, with simulations running for weeks. Experiments also generate data faster than traditional pipelines can process it, often leaving scientists waiting until experiments end to analyze results.
Pacific Fusion researchers used NVIDIA cuPhoton.xLens module to extract insights while plasma simulations run, helping them visualize what’s happening as results emerge. 
The NVIDIA cuPhoton.xLens module extracts insights while plasma simulations run, helping researchers understand what’s happening as results emerge. Researchers can couple the entire pipeline with an 
NVIDIA Nemotron
-powered agent running locally on the same system, enabling interactive analysis that can help steer simulations in real time to inform fusion reactor target and chamber design.
Exploring Potential Medicines
Molecular simulations help scientists evaluate drug designs before costly laboratory experiments. NVIDIA cuPotential, built on work by researchers at 
Simons Foundation’s Flatiron Institute
 and 
D. E. Shaw Research
, accelerates existing simulation software by up to 3x while improving calculation stability and accuracy. It expedites synthetic data creation for generative AI models, finding previously unknown molecular binding sites and calculating the strength of molecular interactions.
NVIDIA is working with 
Schrödinger
 and the 
AMBER
, 
NAMD
, 
GROMACS
, 
LAMMPS
and 
Open Molecular Software Foundation
communities to bring cuPotential acceleration to molecular dynamics engines and workflows.
Cadence
, 
Fathom Therapeutics
, 
Topos Bio
, 
Genesis Molecular AI
 and 
Terray Therapeutics 
are adopting it for drug discovery.
Accelerating Materials Research
Daviator
, a collection of batched GPU kernels for the widely used Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package, is coming to 
NVIDIA ALCHEMI
 — delivering quantum-mechanical calculations up to 22x faster and at up to 75% lower cost. With Daviator, researchers building simulation models can now use GPU acceleration across atomistic systems of all sizes to generate the millions of calculations of atomic interactions needed for model training and inference, including for small systems that previously ran faster on CPUs.
At 
Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory
, the NVIDIA DAQIRI networking library connects a high-speed electron microscopy detector to GPU processing, moving analysis into the experiment itself. Researchers can identify useful signals, discover previously unknown regions of interest and decide what to measure next while measurements are being taken. 
The workflow supports atomic-resolution materials studies that can distinguish elements, laying the groundwork for future automated experiments.
Revealing Biology at Scale
The newly open source cuSpa accelerates cell polygon calculations, enabling developers to generate massive spatial graphs for next-generation patient and cancer models at 
Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center
, with a 900x speedup compared with using CPUs. 
Codeveloped with 
scverse
, the rapids-singlecell framework accelerates single-cell analysis — and is now available in a single, agent-ready container. Using the tool, 
Therna Biosciences
 processed data 200x faster for therapeutic design. 
10x Genomics
 integrated rapids-singlecell in its new Sentira platform, helping academic researchers, biopharma teams and foundation model builders accelerate the path from large-scale spatial data to biological insights. 
And the NVIDIA GPU-accelerated HMMER library, also open source, accelerates the annotation of predicted protein sequences, supporting data preparation for biological foundation models. At 
Basecamp Research
, benchmark searches ran up to 84x faster than with standard HMMER, reducing the time required to annotate the large biological datasets used to train the company’s therapeutic design models.

===== https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/ian-buck-sc26/
(status 200)
NVIDIA’s Ian Buck kicked off the company’s week at SC26 in Chicago on Monday with new tools for AI agents, software that can plan experiments, run code and check results on researchers’ own systems.
In NVIDIA’s special address, Buck announced
Nemotron for Science
, the Rubin HPC GPU and an
expanded Vera CPU platform for high-performance computing
. He also detailed a new collaboration in quantum computing with 
IBM and Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory
.
“The frontier of computing and the new scientific stack is going to be simulation, as always, but combined with AI and the future of quantum,” said Buck, vice president of hyperscale and high-performance computing at NVIDIA. 
Buck opened the talk by looking back to his first public talk on CUDA, at an SC06 tutorial in Tampa, Florida, 20 years ago. NVIDIA now offers more than 1,000
CUDA-X libraries
, he said.
Open Models for Science Agents
“[Agents] have been just a game changer in how we interface with our supercomputers, with our devices, our instruments and how we can do the research,” Buck said.
Simulation remains the foundation of scientific computing, “which will always be on the top,” Buck said, with AI, quantum and now agents working alongside it.
Nemotron for Science combines the Nemotron 3.5 Super VL open model with a new open chemistry dataset and post-training recipes for life sciences and chemistry, Buck explained. Researchers can run it locally on a DGX Spark, a workstation or their own supercomputer.
At Argonne National Laboratory, Buck said, an agent built with the toolkit took a single prompt, planned and tested hypotheses, chose which simulations to run and identified two new binding sites.
“You own your data. You own the model,” Buck said.
Buck highlighted other agents already in use:
GROMACS:
 An agent from KTH Royal Institute of Technology repairs broken GROMACS setups on its own. In one overnight run on a single DGX Spark, it fixed 81% of test cases.
SETI Institute:
 At the Allen Telescope Array, an agent took a one-line prompt to plot a methanol line. Buck explained that it then retuned for Doppler shift on its own [NGC 869 vs. 896, check slide].
Nsight AI Porting Agent:
 NVIDIA’s new agent ported a routine from the Weather Research and Forecasting (WRF) model to GPUs, Buck said, speeding it up more than 5x using about 150 million tokens.
CPUs for the Agent Loop
Agents lean on CPUs to run tool calls between model steps, Buck explained.
“That’s exactly what agents need,” he said. “They need a CPU that can run, evaluate, run the tool, perform the operations, gather the results.” 
DKRZ, the German Climate Computing Center, has selected Vera CPUs for its HLRE-5 supercomputer, expected in early 2028. Buck said it joins Vera systems already announced at Texas Advanced Computing Center. 
A New GPU for Science
Rubin HPC is a new GPU in the Vera Rubin platform, built for science, engineering, analytics and AI, Buck said. 
It supports precisions from FP64 to FP4, adds sparse-data acceleration and connects over seventh-generation NVIDIA NVLink. With more than 50,000 CUDA cores, it has about three times as many as Hopper, Buck said. It is expected in 2028.
Quantum Error Correction
Buck said a simulation on Jülich’s JUPITER supercomputer showed that a technique called magic state cultivation can cut the operations needed for one logical step tenfold. The work is being added to CUDA-Q.
NVIDIA, IBM and Fermilab announced plans today for a quantum computing co-design platform to support error-correction and applications research. NVIDIA and IBM are pairing NVIDIA DGX B200 systems with IBM quantum computers, including those powered by IBM Quantum Nighthawk. 
Buck also introduced a new breakthrough for quantum decoding. The goal, Buck explained, is to cut decoding latency from about 300 microseconds to 10. A new CUDA-Q decoding algorithm, TCBP-JOBS, cuts error-correction research computation from 12 weeks to 45 minutes, he said.
Networking
Buck also showed one of the first deployments of 
NVIDIA Photonics co-packaged optics switches, at Lambda
. He said they are 15x more reliable on bit errors and link flaps.

NVIDIA Introduces Nemotron for Science to Accelerate Discoveries With Agents
New Open Dataset and Post-Training Recipes Help Researchers Adapt Nemotron 3.5 Super VL for Life Sciences and Chemistry
          November 16, 2026
News Summary:
NVIDIA Nemotron for Science brings together the Nemotron 3.5 Super VL model, scientific evaluations, a new open chemistry dataset and recipes to help researchers build domain-specific science agents.
LitmusBench v0.2 supports both evaluation and post-training for chemistry reasoning, helping researchers assess model capabilities and adapt models to scientific tasks.
NVIDIA DGX Spark and data center infrastructure take Nemotron 3.5 Super VL from researchers’ desktops to supercomputing centers, enabling secure scientific workflows on infrastructure organizations control.
Leading global research institutions, including the U.S. Department of Energy’s Argonne National Laboratory, are using Nemotron to build agents to speed scientific discovery.
NVIDIA today introduced NVIDIA Nemotron for Science, bringing together scientific applications of its 
NVIDIA Nemotron™ 3.5 Super VL
 open model, a new open chemistry dataset and post-training recipes to help researchers build specialized agents for scientific discovery.
Built on Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, Nemotron for Science helps researchers develop agents that reason through scientific problems and put specialized research tools to work. With post-training, scientists can adapt the model to specialized tasks such as conducting experiments for chemistry, protein design and more.
Part of Nemotron for Science, 
LitmusBench v0.2
 is a new open dataset with nearly 50,000 questions for training and evaluating language models on chemistry reasoning. Drawing on molecular properties and experimental drug discovery data, it helps researchers improve models’ ability to predict how molecules behave, including their solubility and how the human body processes them.
Life sciences companies Lila Sciences, Terray Therapeutics and Therna are using and customizing NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Super VL for science applications. Use cases include extracting chemical knowledge, planning experiments and designing therapeutics and laboratory protocols. Major research centers like Argonne and Jülich Supercomputing Centre are integrating it into their drug and scientific discovery AI agents.
“Accelerated computing is transforming how scientists tackle the world’s most complex challenges,” said Ian Buck, vice president of hyperscale and high-performance computing at NVIDIA. “Nemotron for Science gives researchers open models, scientific data and post-training recipes to build specialized agents that reason through scientific problems and use CUDA-X accelerated tools to turn ideas into discoveries.”
Post-Training Nemotron Open Models for Scientific Discovery
Researchers can post-train Nemotron open models with domain-specific data to improve performance on specialized scientific tasks. They can integrate the resulting model into agent workflows that interpret scientific information, generate code and call computational tools. These workflows can use 
NVIDIA CUDA-X
™ accelerated tools to perform scientific computation.
Terray Therapeutics
 post-trained Nemotron to extract reactants, products and reaction conditions from scientific diagrams, surpassing previously published results on two benchmarks. On the RxnScribe test, it recovered about 76% of annotated reactions under the benchmark’s matching criteria, helping turn published chemistry discoveries into structured data for synthesis planning and scientific agents.
Lila Sciences
 used Nemotron to design execution-ready laboratory protocols for its automated labs, matching frontier-level performance without requiring additional fine-tuning. Post-training with laboratory feedback nearly doubled the model’s accuracy for making quantum dots — nanoscale crystals used in screens and solar panels — to 61%, exceeding GPT-6 Astra’s 54% at 10-20x lower inference cost. These gains help agents and materials scientists translate desired material properties into synthesis recipes, advancing the discovery of new materials.
Therna
 collaborated with NVIDIA to post-train Nemotron for agentic mRNA therapeutic design, nearly doubling accuracy in predicting protein-expression ranges over frontier models. Scientists preferred its explanations over those from the base model in 96% of comparisons. These gains could help scientists tailor mRNA designs for the desired protein production in specific cell types as they scale to human use.
Researchers Speed Science With Nemotron Models and Datasets
Leading research institutions are using NVIDIA Nemotron models and datasets to advance scientific discovery at every scale, from individual researchers’ desktops to labs and supercomputing centers.
By helping researchers rapidly design and evaluate novel molecules across the periodic table, the work has the potential to open new pathways for applications in critical materials and minerals, national security and cancer treatment.
Argonne is integrating Nemotron into its StructBioReasoner multi-agent system to design and evaluate protein-based therapeutic candidates for treatment of cancer and other diseases.
KTH Royal Institute of Technology has built an AI agent on Nemotron that repairs broken GROMACS molecular dynamics setups on its own. In one overnight run on a single 
NVIDIA DGX Spark
™ system, the agent fixed 81% of test cases with reasoning enabled, and every fix was checked independently by GROMACS itself.
RIKEN has made NVIDIA Nemotron, including Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, available on its RIKYU AI supercomputing platform through RiVault, its inference gateway, enabling researchers to access the models for scientific research and the development of accelerated applications.
Availability
NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Super VL
 and the 
LitmusBench v0.2
 chemistry dataset are available now on Hugging Face. The Nemotron 3.5 Super VL model was also optimized to run locally on a 128GB 
NVIDIA DGX Spark system
.
