「Style Advisor」の仕組み
米国のファッションブランドJ.Crew（ジェイ・クルー）は、Anthropicの「Claude Code」を活用して、新たなスタイリング支援ツールを自社開発し、自社ECサイト「JCrew.com」に導入しました。AI基盤にはAnthropicの「Claude」を採用。消費者がECサイト上で入力した質問や要望を「Claude」のモデルに送信し、回答を生成する仕組みです。
「Claude」を活用した新たなスタイリング支援ツール「Style Advisor」とは
「Style Advisor（スタイル・アドバイザー）」と呼ばれるこのツールは、オンラインショッピング利用者1人ひとりに合わせたパーソナライズされた商品提案（レコメンデーション）を提供します。11月12日付で「Claude」の公式ブログに掲載された発表で明らかになりました。
なお、米国のEC専門誌『Digital Commerce 360』の各種データベースにおけるJ.Crewの順位は次の通りです。
「北米EC事業 トップ1000社」データベース：80位
（北米の大手EC事業者を年間EC売上高などに基づいて順位付け）
AI Commerce Rankings（AIコマースランキング）：135位
（『Digital Commerce 360』とReFiBuyによるランキング。「Top 1000 Pro with AI Database」で提供）
J.Crew Groupのリビー・ワドル（Libby Wadle）CEOは、導入の背景について次のように説明しています。
消費者の期待が変化し、AIによって小売業に新たな可能性が生まれるなか、
J.Crewは従来のオンラインショッピングのあり方を見直しています。個別の商品を検索するだけでなく、よりつながりがありインスピレーションを重視した体験へと進化させようとしているのです
。私たちは以前から、顧客が将来どのような買い物を望むのか、そしてその変化に後から対応するのではなく、
自ら主導するにはどうすればよいか
を考えてきました。
最先端モデルを用いた自社チームによる開発プロセス
Anthropicによると、「Style Advisor」の開発はJ.Crewの製品開発チームが主導し、次のモデルを活用しています。
プロトタイプ（試作品）の構築：
「Claude Code」と「Claude Opus 5.5」を使用
本番サービスへの開発：
Anthropicが
「複雑で自由度の高い作業」に適していると位置付ける「Claude Fable 5.1」モデルを活用
し、試作品を基にエンジニアが本番稼働できるサービスへと仕上げた
最終的に「Style Advisor」は、
J.Crewのスタイリストが店頭などで顧客に対応するような形で商品レコメンデーションを提供すること
をめざしています。
ワドル氏はさらに次のように語っています。
私たちは、
ショッピング体験を根本から見直す機会がある
と考えています。Anthropicとの協業を通じて、
J.Crewが数十年にわたって培ってきたスタイリング、商品構成（マーチャンダイジング）、クリエイティブの専門知識を結集し、今後の消費者行動のあり方を形作ることに役立てています。
「Style Advisor」の仕組み
「Style Advisor」は現在、J.CrewのECサイト「JCrew.com」
で利用できます。サイトのメニューから選択するとWebブラウザ内にアシスタントが起動し、画面上には
消費者が入力する質問やプロンプトの候補を提示します。
「Style Advisor」のイメージ（画像は編集部が「JCrew.com」からキャプチャ）
実際の運用では、処理の性質に応じて異なる「Claude」モデルが役割を分担しています。
「Claude Sonnet 5.5」モデル：
消費者が入力したプロンプトを受け取り、
スタイリングに関する回答
を生成。
「Claude Haiku 5.5」モデル：
より短く、特定の目的に絞ったタスクを担当。
また、J.Crewによると、レコメンデーションの際に参照・活用されるスタイルは
すべて同社のチームが考案したもの
であるため、AIによる提案内容が
ブランドの方針や優先事項と一貫性を保つよう設計
されています。
Anthropicのポール・スミス（Paul Smith）最高商務責任者（CCO）は、J.Crewの取り組みを次のように評価しています。
J.Crewは、ファッション業界でも特に独自のスタイル（視点）が確立されたブランドの1つです。J.Crewのチームは「Claude」を活用し、その専門知識を新たな形で顧客に届けています。
最先端のモデルを使って体験を構築し、実際の運用にはより高速で効率的なモデルを採用したことで、顧客はブランドのスタイリング方針に忠実な回答を迅速に得ることができます。

Online, shopping usually happens one product at a time, so a customer who finds a sweater has to search again for the pants and shoes that go with it. To change that, J.Crew today launched Style Advisor, an AI stylist on 
JCrew.com
 (opens in new tab)
 that helps customers shop by complete outfit. J.Crew’s team built it with 
Claude Code
 (opens in new tab)
 using multiple Claude models, from Fable to Haiku, each matched to the work it does best. It is available now for women’s and men’s styles in the U.S.
For more than 40 years, J.Crew’s own stylists have built complete looks by balancing proportion, color, and layering and by pairing pieces in unexpected ways. Style Advisor brings that expertise online. Customers describe what they need, such as an outfit for a beach wedding or a first job interview, and it answers with complete looks drawn from J.Crew’s own photo shoots. They can keep refining from there, so shopping becomes a visual conversation.
How it's built
J.Crew’s product team used 
Claude Fable
 (opens in new tab)
 5.1, Anthropic’s model for complex, open-ended work, to plan how Style Advisor should reason like a stylist, then built a working prototype with 
Claude Opus
 (opens in new tab)
 5.5 in Claude Code. J.Crew’s engineers took that prototype to production. In each conversation, 
Claude Sonnet
 (opens in new tab)
 5.5 acts as the stylist and decides each step of the response, while 
Claude Haiku
 (opens in new tab)
 5.5 handles quick, focused tasks so Style Advisor answers quickly. Because every look comes from J.Crew’s stylists, its recommendations stay consistent with J.Crew’s styling point of view.
How customers use Style Advisor
Shop complete outfits.
 Every recommendation starts from looks J.Crew stylists created for the brand's catalog shoots, and Style Advisor shows them in photos so customers see the full outfit.
Build from a single piece.
 A customer can start with one item and get suggestions for the pieces that complete the look.
Dress for the occasion. 
Customers can say where they're going, whether that's a vacation, a holiday party, or a wedding, and get recommendations for it.
Get J.Crew's point of view. 
Style Advisor applies the same styling principles a J.Crew stylist would use in a store.
What's next
At launch, Style Advisor helps customers find new outfit ideas and shop with more confidence. Over time, J.Crew plans to make it more personal by tailoring suggestions to each customer's preferences and past purchases, and to bring it to more markets and product lines.
“As consumer expectations evolve and AI opens up new possibilities for retail, J.Crew is challenging the traditional model of online shopping, moving beyond individual product searches toward a more connected and inspiration-led experience,” said Libby Wadle, Chief Executive Officer of J.Crew Group. “For a long time, we’ve been thinking about how customers will want to shop in the future, and how we can lead that evolution rather than react to it. We see an opportunity to fundamentally rethink the shopping experience. Through our work with Anthropic, we're bringing decades of J.Crew styling, merchandising and creative expertise together to help shape what’s next for customer behavior.”
“J.Crew has one of the most recognizable points of view in fashion, and its team used Claude to bring that expertise to customers in a new way,” said Paul Smith, Chief Commercial Officer, Anthropic. “They used a frontier model to build the experience and faster, more efficient models to run it, so customers get quick answers that stay true to the brand’s styling.”

===== https://fortune.com/2026/11/11/jcrew-anthropic-ai-style-advisor/?trk=public_post_comment-text

Over the course of four decades, J. Crew’s style has woven cashmere cardigans, tweed jackets, and other preppy staples that have attracted high-profile fans including Michelle Obama, Demi Moore, and Meghan Markle.
Recommended Video
Throughout the apparel brand’s history—beginning as a catalog, then growing to become a physical and omnichannel retailer—those styling decisions were made by humans. But beginning today, there’s artificial intelligence at work too.
On Wednesday, J. Crew announced a new consumer-facing, AI-enabled tool called Style Advisor, which runs on a collection of Anthropic’s models. Style Advisor is trained on data from thousands of outfit pairings from the company’s photo shoots and J. Crew’s styling principles, outfit and accessories pairings.
“It’s not just about the piece that you’re looking for, but how to style it,” says Danielle Schmelkin, chief information officer at J. Crew. “What those stylists bring to the table—and really recommend more than just the item that you may have picked up when you walked into the store—we are really looking to create that online.”
As more shoppers gravitate to agentic shopping, with more than 
50% of global consumers
 using AI to buy goods within the past three months, new tools have proliferated ranging from 
Meta
 Platforms’ Muse agent and Alexa for Shopping from 
Amazon
 to AI shopping assistants created by retailers including Lowe’s, 
Ulta Beauty
, and Macy’s.
But while 56% of consumers say they use AI chat or search tools including Anthropic’s Claude or OpenAI’s ChatGPT, only 27% are gravitating to retailer-created AI shopping assistants, according to consulting firm McKinsey.
“I think the visual piece is really important,” says Schmelkin of the distinctive edge that she promises Style Advisor will offer. In the future, the tool will also pull in data from a consumer’s past purchase history, allowing Style Advisor to make even more personalized recommendations, replicating that’s already possible during the omnichannel shopping experience.
She says Style Advisor is also grounded in contextual details, asking questions to better understand a shopper’s intent. If a J. Crew customer were to tell it that they were looking for an outfit to attend a wedding—in, say, Miami, but not too formal—the tool will make recommendations based on these nuances.
Style Advisor will debut on J. Crew’s desktop website and is expected to later be added to the retailer’s mobile app.
J. Crew’s Style Advisor runs on a variety of Claude’s models, including Fable 5.1, which is leveraged to handle the more complex and ambiguous judgment that allows the tool to think and respond like a stylist. Claude’s Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 4.5 support the speed and efficiency of the back-and-forth responses between consumers and the AI stylist, while Claude Opus 5.5 was utilized by J. Crew’s team to build a working prototype. 
“What is the actual experience that we want the shopper to have, and then, how do we think about making sure that we’re mapping the right models to be able to deliver that,” says Lauren Collett, Anthropic’s enterprise go-to-market leader. 
Schmelkin says J. Crew built in a back-end observation layer that allows the retailer to closely monitor the costs. “We want to make sure we are constantly refining and using these models in the right way, both in terms of the outputs for our customers, but also the efficiency we want to see to make this scalable,” says Schmelkin.
Internally at J. Crew, to bolster AI adoption, the retailer has created an AI fluency program that begins with the basics and advances up through vibe coding. An internal AI lab, called “Second Thread,” began with just the tech team but has broadened to incorporate all corporate employees to discuss AI use cases and propose other technology solutions.
For four years, J. Crew has also run a two-day tech summit that features workshops, hackathons, guest speakers, and vendor presentations. Initially, this programming was only available to the tech team, but J. Crew has more recently democratized access. Employees are also encouraged to use AI enterprise tools from Anthropic and OpenAI.
The Style Advisor was conceptualized by a J. Crew digital product designer, who developed a working prototype before engineers were pulled into the project. The fact that the tool wasn’t mandated from the top of J. Crew’s C-suite reflects Schmelkin’s efforts to encourage employees to chart their own path on AI. 
“I’ve been screaming to anyone who will listen for years now to get in there and build a habit,” says Schmelkin. “You have to find your a-ha moment and then all of a sudden…this could really change the way you think about your own life, work, and team productivity.”

Yet encouragingly, 40% of enterprises plan to increase their IT headcount in 2027, according to a 
survey
 of 2,002 CIOs and other tech leaders conducted by Gartner. The biggest changes will be for data and analytics and cybersecurity roles.
Alicia Mullery, VP analyst at Gartner, tells 
Fortune
 that much of the concern about AI in the workplace is that these systems will save organizations so much time, they’ll need less people. But that’s not always the case: AI can create a lot of so-called “slop” that workers need to rummage through. And, even when the outputs are more advantageous, there are still plenty of instances where human-machine collaboration is needed.
IT departments tend to be further along on their AI journey and this is where CIOs can continue to step in and guide, remaking workflows across other parts of the business, says Mullery. “I see the CIO as really being at the forefront of being able to say, ‘Hey our teams went through this first,’” adds Mullery. “Here’s what we learned, here’s what worked, and here’s what didn’t work.”

J.Crew has launched Style Advisor, an AI stylist on its website built with Anthropic’s Claude, Anthropic 
announced
 on Thursday. It is available in the US for women’s and men’s clothing.
Instead of searching for a sweater, then trousers, then shoes, shoppers can describe what they need, such as an outfit for a beach wedding or a first job interview. Style Advisor replies with complete looks, and shoppers can keep refining them. They can also start from one item and ask what goes with it.
Every suggestion comes from outfits J.Crew’s own stylists put together for the brand’s catalogue shoots. The AI picks and shows those looks rather than inventing new ones, which keeps it in line with the brand’s style.
“
We see an opportunity to fundamentally rethink the shopping experience,
” said Libby Wadle, CEO of J.Crew Group.
J.Crew built the tool using several Claude models. In each conversation, the mid-sized 
Sonnet 5.5
 acts as the stylist, while the smaller Haiku 5.5 handles quick tasks so answers come fast. For now, it does not tailor suggestions to each shopper’s past purchases, but J.Crew plans to add that and expand to more countries.

late last year

, while Zalando’s agent will
