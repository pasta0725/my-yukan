Canva will let users switch off most generative AI
The platform is adding new controls for its editing tools and its image library.
By
Anna Washenko
Nov. 19, 2026 7:30 am EST
Canva

Like most companies, Canva has added a fair number of AI tools and integrations to its platform over the past year. Today, the company is giving its users the option to strip the generative AI out of their experience. Canva is adding two new settings, one to turn all the gen AI features off or on, and a second to reduce how much gen AI content appears in a user's library. Free and Pro users will both be able to access the new controls.
There are some limitations to these AI settings. Some of the AI-powered editing tools, such as the Background Remover and Magic Grab, will remain available even when the broader AI tools are disabled. That's because those options don't leverage generative AI, only the content of an existing image, and using those features doesn't add the AI tag to the resulting image's metadata. And the library option will "reduce" the odds of an AI-generated image from Canva appearing in searches, but the company doesn't go so far as to say it will completely block them from the results.
Both new settings can be changed under the "profile" tab. For managing the platform-wide AI tools, it's under the "permissions" section of the settings menu, with the text "Enable generative AI features." The library setting can be reached by navigating to "Your Account" and then the AI personalization section.

Canva introduces new controls to hide generative AI features
Marcus Mendes

In light of growing pushback from users who want more control over how AI is integrated into their apps (if at all), 
Canva
 is introducing new settings that let them hide generative AI features and see less AI-generated content. Here are the details.
Canva adds an off switch for generative AI
Canva today announced new controls that will let Free and Pro users choose whether to see generative AI features across the app, as well as how much AI-generated content appears in search results and in recommendations for templates and design elements.
The company notes that both controls are available at no additional cost and can be turned on or off at any time.
According to Canva, “the future of creativity isn’t a choice between AI and no AI.” For this reason, it is introducing the following controls:
Generative AI feature control
. A new switch to turn supported generative AI features on or off. When it’s off, those features are hidden across Canva, and can be switched back on any time. It can be found under 
Profile > Settings > Permissions
 > then switch 
Enable generative AI features
 on or off.
AI-generated content control
. A new option to see less AI-generated content while browsing the Canva library, without changing any existing designs. It can be found 
Profile > Your account > AI personalization
, and enable showing less 
AI-generated content
.
The company says that, while it knows that there is an ongoing debate between users with opposing views on whether AI has a place in creative work, it thinks “both views are valid”:
“It’s not our place to tell anyone how to create, so today we’re launching two new controls that put that decision firmly with our community […].”
Importantly, the company noted that select features that use AI without generating new content will remain available even when generative AI features are turned off:
“Some of Canva’s everyday editing tools use AI to make simple tasks faster, without generating anything new. Background Remover, one of Canva’s most popular tools, lifts a subject cleanly out of a photo in a single click, and Magic Grab lets creators select an object in an image and move it somewhere else in the design. Both remain available when the generative AI switch is off, because they work only with what’s already in a design.”
Canva added that these tools also won’t add an AI label to a design’s metadata, since that label is reserved for AI-generated content.
As for the company’s design content library, Canva said that while “a small portion of the library is AI-generated content made by Canva, including some abstract backgrounds and illustrated graphics,” its community has told it “they want an easier way to find design assets made by real people, so the content control reduces the number of AI-generated templates and elements that appear in search results and recommendations.”
With today’s announcement, Canva is essentially expanding on the AI permissions already available to administrators of Business, Enterprise, Teams, and Education accounts, which let organizations control how their teams use AI.

Canva has introduced new settings that allow users to disable its generative AI features and reduce the amount of AI-generated material shown in its content library. 
The Verge
 reports
 that the controls are available to both free and paid subscribers, giving users greater choice over how AI is incorporated into their design experience.
The generative AI setting disables a range of tools, including 
Magic Expand
, Magic Write, Magic Design, Magic Layers, Magic Edit, Dream Lab, Canva Code, and AI Shapes. It also prevents users from generating images through Canva’s AI chatbot. 
However, the change does not disable every AI-powered feature on the platform. Tools such as Background Remover and Magic Grab will remain available, even when generative AI is switched off. Canva says these tools will stay enabled because they “make everyday tasks faster without generating anything new.”
The distinction is based on how the tools operate. While generative AI features create new content, Background Remover and Magic Grab work with existing images. The latter allows users to select an object within an image and reposition it elsewhere in the design. 
A second setting allows users to reduce the amount of AI-generated material displayed in Canva’s content library. When enabled, the platform will prioritise human-created templates, graphics, and other design elements in search results. 
However, the setting does not guarantee that AI-generated content will be excluded entirely. Canva says that a “small portion” of its library consists of AI-generated material, and designs containing these elements will carry an AI label in their metadata.
Both settings can be accessed through the profile menu. To disable generative AI tools, users should go to Profile, select Settings, open Permissions, and switch off Enable generative AI features. To reduce AI-generated content in search results, they can navigate to Profile, Your Account, and AI Personalization, then enable the option to show less AI content.
