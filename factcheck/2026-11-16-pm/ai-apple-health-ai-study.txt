Big AI labs like 
OpenAI
 and 
Anthropic
 have made their bets on offering chatbots that can answer health-related queries with specialized features. Apple is debuting a new study to understand how people use AI for health. But it isn’t quite following other AI startups just yet; it wants to study how clinically grounded answers can help people.
The iPhone maker is partnering with healthcare-focused AI company OpenEvidence to launch a new study app. Apple is inviting users in the U.S. over 21, using iPhone 15 or later, and running iOS 26 or later, with Apple Intelligence enabled. The company is aiming to run this study with up to 200,000 participants for more than 12 months.
The new app will allow users to ask health-related questions, as they do with ChatGPT or Claude, with answers grounded in clinical data, from sources including The New England Journal of Medicine, Journal of the American Medical Association, and Nature. Apple said that the app will try to give simplified answers to everyday medical questions. Users can rate those answers and provide feedback about the responses; they’ll also be asked to participate in monthly surveys.

Image Credits:

Participants can share data from HealthKit such as resting heart rate, activity, sleep, or lab results to get more personalized and pointed suggestions from the app. The company said the app can also help prepare participants better for a doctor’s visit, and it will guide users to specific providers for answers related to concerning medical issues.
Apple specified that the data will be anonymized before sending it to a secure third-party cloud provider, and it won’t be used to train any kind of AI models. The company said that OpenEvidence deletes the query and response data after 24 hours.

“At Apple, we believe responsible innovation starts with scientific rigor and clinical grounding. Health is deeply personal, and conducting a dedicated research study is the most thoughtful way to understand how people engage with, comprehend, and evaluate health AI in everyday life,” Sumbul Desai, M.D., Apple’s vice president of Health and Fitness, said in a statement, adding, “Insights from this study will help advance the broader scientific conversation and ensure that as technology evolves, we continue building trusted tools that genuinely support people wherever they are on their health journey.”
The app is 
available to download
 starting today for U.S. users. It’s not clear if Apple wants to release AI-powered answering to its Health app or Siri yet. The potential is huge — earlier this year, OpenAI said users were asking over 
300 million health-related queries each week
.

Apple’s new study looks to improve the effectiveness of AI health tools
The study will be conducted through a new app where participants can ask health questions and rate the responses.

Apple announced a new study today with the goal of better understanding how people interact with conversational AI health tools and how useful their responses actually are. The company is conducting the Apple Health AI Usage Study in collaboration with 
OpenEvidence
, a clinical AI platform used by healthcare professionals that generates answers based on medical information from sources like 
The New England Journal of Medicine
. Participants will use a new iOS app to ask medical questions and then rate the responses based on criteria including accuracy, detail, and relevance.
The app is now available for download, and enrollment starts today, with Apple looking for up to 200,000 people to contribute. Participants 21 and older must be based in the US, and although the app doesn’t use Apple Intelligence, it does require an iPhone 15 Pro running iOS 26 or later.
Study participants can use the app to ask health-related questions and receive easily understandable responses with links to peer-reviewed clinical sources. The app relies on text-based prompts, and while dictation is an option using the iOS keyboard, the app isn’t integrated with Siri or Apple Intelligence.
Participants in the study will be given the opportunity to rate responses to health questions from the app.
Image: Apple
During enrolment, participants are given the option to share Apple HealthKit data, like their activity, sleep, or clinical lab results, so responses will include more context about their own personal health. But privacy is a priority, and Apple says it has taken measures to ensure users’ information remains confidential and the data collected won’t be used to train AI models. The aim is to improve the design and functionality of AI-powered health tools so they can deliver relevant health information that’s easier for users to understand and act on.
“At Apple, we believe responsible innovation starts with scientific rigor and clinical grounding. Health is deeply personal, and conducting a dedicated research study is the most thoughtful way to understand how people engage with, comprehend, and evaluate health AI in everyday life. Insights from this study will help advance the broader scientific conversation and ensure that as technology evolves, we continue building trusted tools that genuinely support people wherever they are on their health journey,” said Sumbul Desai, MD, Apple’s vice president of Health and Fitness.
