Transform notes, research, and sources into interactive sites with Gemini Notebook.
Maria Ines Aranguren
Software Engineer, Google Labs
Nov 16, 2026
Starting today, 
Gemini Notebook
 can turn your sources into interactive sites to help you quickly make sense of complex information.
To get started, simply type “make me a site” in your Gemini Notebook chat along with a description of what you want to build for work or study, such as:
Visual explainers to help you prepare for exams, like an interactive periodic table or a historical timeline.
Interactive dashboards to explore your data, such as quarterly sales performance.
Project management trackers to outline your tasks and deadlines.
Once built, select “Open in a new tab” to interact with it, or share it with others.
This capability is rolling out globally on the web today for Gemini Notebook users (18+), including Google Workspace subscribers, and is coming soon to the mobile app.

Google has added site generation to Gemini Notebook, allowing the research tool to turn material in a notebook into an interactive page. The feature began rolling out on the web on 16 November, with study aids, sales dashboards and project trackers among the company's examples.
The change gives Notebook another output beyond a written answer or a static summary. Users can describe the kind of site they need in chat, then open the result separately and share it with other people.
Access is being introduced worldwide for adults using Gemini Notebook, including Workspace subscribers. Google says the mobile app will follow, but its announcement does not give a date for that release.
Notebook sources become something to explore
Google's announcement describes the feature as a way to work through complex information using sources already gathered in Notebook. The request combines that material with a description of the intended task.
For study, the company points to a periodic table that people can interact with and a timeline for history. Its workplace examples are a dashboard built around quarterly sales and a tracker showing project deadlines and tasks.
Those are examples supplied by Google, rather than independently measured results. The launch post does not provide an accuracy benchmark for the generated sites or establish how reliably a page will represent every detail in its sources.
A site can leave the chat window
The starting prompt is "make me a site", followed by a description of what the user wants. Once Notebook produces it, a control opens the site in another browser tab, where the user can interact with it.
Google also says the result can be shared. That makes the addition different from a response intended only for the person asking the question: the generated page can become an output for someone else to use.
The announcement does not spell out the permissions available for sharing a site, whether it can be exported as code or whether it will refresh automatically after a source changes. It would be premature to treat those capabilities as part of the launch.
Web rollout includes Workspace users
The initial release applies to users aged 18 or above. Google's explicit inclusion of Workspace subscribers puts the business examples alongside the study features, rather than limiting the announcement to personal accounts.
Its existing help pages describe Notebook as a source-based research assistant. Supported material includes documents, websites, videos and audio, although individual source types have their own limits. The new announcement concerns what Notebook can produce from collected information, not the removal of those input limits.
For now, the company has confirmed a web rollout and a later mobile arrival. It has not published a completion timetable for all eligible accounts in this announcement.
The bottom line
Gemini Notebook is adding interactive sites to the ways it presents research. The immediate change is on the web: a notebook can become a shareable study or work page through a chat request. Mobile timing and several details about managing the generated sites remain unspecified.
