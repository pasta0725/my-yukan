Rocket Lab To Launch Milestone 100th Electron Rocket This Week
November 12, 2026
PDF Version
Less than a decade after its debut, Electron is set to reach 100 launches faster than any other commercially developed rocket in history.
LONG BEACH, Calif., Nov.  12, 2026  (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- Rocket Lab Corporation (Nasdaq: RKLB), a global leader in launch services and space systems, today announced that its 100th Electron rocket is scheduled for launch at the end of this week. The launch will be a historic moment for the the global space industry, setting a new record for reaching 100 launches faster than any other commercially developed launch vehicle.
The mission, named “The Mountain God Rises”, is scheduled for lift off from Rocket Lab Launch Complex 1 in Mahia, New Zealand, to deploy a single Earth-imaging satellite to orbit for Japanese company the Institute for Q-shu Pioneers of Space, Inc. (iQPS).
Lift-off is currently scheduled for November 14 at 6:30 p.m. New Zealand Daylight Time. The mission will be broadcast live at 
www.youtube.com/@RocketLabCorp
 with coverage beginning at around 5:30 pm NZDT.
Since its first launch in 2017, Electron has become one of the world’s most frequently launched rockets and has redefined access to space for small satellites. Electron has achieved a string of world-firsts and industry-leading achievements along the way including:
The first carbon composite orbital launch vehicle.
Powered by the first 3D-printed orbital rocket engine, Rutherford, which is also the first rocket engine to use electric fuel pumps instead of traditional gas turbines.
Launched from two hemispheres. Rocket Lab operates launch pads in Virginia, USA, as well as New Zealand, where the company built the world’s first private orbital launch complex.
Achieved a new responsive space record earlier this year when Electron was launched with just 16 hours and 42 minutes notice for the U.S. Space Force.
On track to be the world’s first commercial small-lift orbital rocket to reach 100 launches.
Follow Rocket Lab on X (@RocketLab) and other social channels for real-time mission updates.
Interviews available on request as schedules allow.

Rocket Lab Hits 100 Electron Launches, Fastest Commercial Rocket to Reach the Milestone
November 14, 2026
PDF Version
LONG BEACH, Calif., Nov.  14, 2026  (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- 
Rocket Lab Corporation
 (Nasdaq: RKLB), a global leader in launch services and space systems, today announced mission success for its 100
th
 Electron launch, setting a new record for reaching 100 launches faster than any other commercially developed rocket.
“The Mountain God Rises” mission launched from Rocket Lab Launch Complex 1 in New Zealand at 6:30 p.m. on November 14, 2026, deploying the YAMATSUMI-II synthetic aperture radar (SAR) Earth-imaging satellite to a 575km low Earth orbit for the Institute for Q-shu Pioneers of Space, Inc. (
iQPS
). This mission marks Rocket Lab’s 11
th
 Electron launch for iQPS with 100% mission success since 2023. iQPS has booked seven more dedicated Electron launches through 2030 to complete its satellite constellation.
Reaching 100 launches in under a decade is a major milestone for commercial spaceflight. What started as a vision to open up access to space with the world’s first carbon-composite rocket has grown into an unmatched launch cadence across the small-launch industry.
Rocket Lab founder and CEO, Sir Peter Beck, says: “Few rockets reach 100 launches in their total operational lifetime. Not only has Electron now reached 100 launches, but it reached the milestone faster than any other commercially developed rocket. That record pace comes down to the relentless dedication of the Rocket Lab team, and I want to congratulate everyone behind Electron. This is the team that pioneered 3D-printed rocket engines, built the world’s first carbon-composite orbital rocket, launched back-to-back from two hemispheres, and shattered responsive space records along the way, putting Electron in a league of its own. To our customers who have trusted us with their spacecraft, many of them across multiple launches, thank you. The next 100 Electrons are already booked so we’re only accelerating from here.”
Since its first launch in 2017, Electron has deployed hundreds of satellites for commercial constellation operators, national security agencies, and scientific institutions from launch pads in New Zealand and Virginia. Backed by vertically integrated manufacturing and a packed manifest extending well into the next decade, Electron continues to deliver access to orbit while laying the operational foundation for Rocket Lab’s next medium-lift rocket, Neutron.

Rocket Lab just hit the century mark.
An 
Electron
 rocket topped with a private Japanese Earth-observing satellite lifted off from 
Rocket Lab's
 New Zealand site on Saturday (Nov. 14) at 12:30 a.m. EST (0530 GMT; 6:30 p.m. local New Zealand time).
The mission, which Rocket Lab called "The Mountain God Rises," was a big one: It marked the 100th launch to date for the California-based company.
A Rocket Lab Electron rocket launches from New Zealand on Nov. 14, 2026 on "The Mountain God Rises," the 100th mission for the company to date. 
(Image credit: Rocket Lab)
Rocket Lab launched for the first time in May 2017, sending the brand-new Electron up on a test flight from New Zealand. That effort failed, but Electron bounced back on its second-ever liftoff, delivering several cubesats to 
low Earth orbit
 (LEO) in January 2018.
In the years since, the 59-foot-tall (18 meters) Electron has become one of the most prolific and reliable 
rockets
 in the world, carrying small payloads to the final frontier for a variety of customers.
And it hit the century mark on Saturday in record-breaking fashion: No commercially developed rocket has gotten there faster, not even SpaceX's workhorse 
Falcon 9
, according to Rocket Lab founder and CEO Peter Beck.
"It's an unprecedented milestone for a commercial small-lift orbital rocket, and a threshold rarely crossed in modern spaceflight," Beck said on Monday (Nov. 9), during Rocket Lab's third-quarter earnings call with investors. "But what matters most to us and our customers is that we have achieved a 96% reliability rate across 99 launches."
The first stage of Rocket Lab's Electron rocket falls back to Earth during "The Mountain God Rises" mission on Nov. 14, 2026. 
(Image credit: Rocket Lab)
Electron met the moment in style on Saturday morning, thanks to some fancy livery.
"Black rocket 🤝 Golden milestone. Electron is all dressed up for launch 100," Rocket Lab said via X on Tuesday (Nov. 10), in 
a post
 that shared a photo of the specially painted first stage.
Not all of Rocket Lab's missions have gone orbital, it should be noted: A suborbital variant of Electron known as 
HASTE
, which customers use to test technologies in a hypersonic flight environment, has nine missions under its belt to date.
The Electron rocket that flew Rocket Lab's 100th mission sported some special livery. 
(Image credit: Rocket Lab)
"The Mountain God Rises" was Rocket Lab's 11th launch to date for the Tokyo-based company iQPS, which is building a constellation of three dozen synthetic aperture radar (SAR) satellites in LEO. And all went to plan on the mission: Electron's upper "kick stage" deployed a single iQPS spacecraft 357 miles (575 kilometers) above Earth.
SAR satellites can see through cloud cover and image our planet at night as well as during the day, offering key advantages over spacecraft that are optimized to view in optical wavelengths.
