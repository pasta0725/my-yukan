　米国の複数の大手銀行で現地時間11月16日、口座にアクセスできないなどの問題が利用者から報告された。Microsoftのサービスや航空管制システムに影響を及ぼす大規模障害が起きてから、わずか数日後のことだ。
     提供：Thomas Fuller/SOPA Images/LightRocket/Getty Images
  ※クリックすると拡大画像が見られます
　障害情報サイト「
Downdetector
」によると、16日午後に最も多くの報告が寄せられたのはJPMorgan Chaseで、米東部時間午後2時前には4600件を超えた。Bank of Americaでは2000人近く、Wells Fargoでは800人以上が不具合を報告した。
　原因はJPMorgan Chaseで発生した技術的な問題にあるとみられる。同行の担当者は米CNETへの電子メールで、技術的な問題により、一部の取引の完了に通常より時間がかかっていると説明した。同行によると、口座情報や残高は引き続き確認できるはずだという。
JPMorgan Chaseの公式Xアカウント
は、同行のシステムの問題が他行のサービスに影響している可能性を認めた。
　「他行が関与する取引に当行も加わっていた場合、その銀行にも影響が及んだ可能性がある。ただし、これは金融システム全体に及ぶ問題ではない」（同行）
　同行は16日中に障害を解消する見込みだとし、原因はサイバー攻撃や悪意ある第三者ではなく、自社施設内のハードウェアシステムで発生したソフトウェアのバグだと説明した。

Customers report difficulty accessing online services at major US banks
Bank of America, Chase, US Bank, Wells Fargo and Truist were listed by users who have been experiencing issues
Customers reported service outages and difficulty accessing their online accounts at several major US banks and the money transfer service Zelle on Monday after a technical issue at JPMorgan Chase caused ripple effects throughout the financial system.
Chase said the technical problems were not a systemic issue and were not the result of a cyber-attack, and that it was working to bring banking services back online.
“Due to a technical issue, some transactions are taking longer than usual to complete but customers still have access to their accounts and funds,” a Chase spokesperson said in a statement. “We are working to resolve this as quickly as possible.”
The disruption comes after a major outage by Microsoft that began on Thursday and lasted into the next day, which crippled a host of services including Outlook, Teams and AI assistant Copilot. The outage had serious repercussions for millions of people and organizations all over the world, including schools, hospitals, businesses, courts, banks, prisons, police forces and governments.
Microsoft said it had resolved the issue and restored service by Saturday, also issuing an apology. The office software giant did not attribute the blackout to a cybersecurity breach, identifying 
in an initial report
 on Monday that a combination of a months-old bug, malfunctioning internal connections, and server capacity sparked the outage.
Also on Friday, ageing equipment led to a temporary grounding of all commercial airplanes in the US, 
sparking travel chaos
 as thousands of flights were delayed or canceled. The Trump administration later said the four-hour 
ground stop
 stemmed from routine maintenance.
At a briefing on Saturday, US transportation secretary Sean Duffy said the outage began after “a contractor was doing routine maintenance to switch telecommunication lines from old copper wire … to fiber” and a newly installed router “started flooding other routers in our air traffic control system”.
The FAA, he said, was still working to determine whether the fault was “incompetence” by the contractor, or “a faulty router”.
Monday’s technical issues seem to center on Chase customers and customers at other banks trying to transact with Chase. Those banks, including 
Bank of America
 and Citigroup, were not having issues with their banking services.
A spokesman for US Bank said its systems are not directly affected but that “clients attempting to send or receive Zelle payments with another financial institution may experience transaction delays.”
Zelle, the peer-to-peer payment system that major US banks use to allow customers to send money near-instantly to friends and family, is owned by Arizona-based Early Warning Services, which in turn is owned by financial institutions including Bank of America, Capital One, JPMorgan Chase, PNC Bank, Truist, US Bank and 
Wells Fargo
.
A spokesperson for Early Warning Services said the Zelle network was fully operational. “An issue at the financial institutions may be delaying Zelle payments for some of their customers,” they said.
Some Chase customers received a message that said: “Some services aren’t available. We’re working on them. Please try back later.”
