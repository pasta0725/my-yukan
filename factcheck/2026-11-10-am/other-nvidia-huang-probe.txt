NVIDIA、フアンCEOへの中国スパイ工作疑惑で独立調査　本人は不倫認め謝罪

2026年11月09日 07時44分

　米NVIDIAは11月8日（現地時間）、創業者でCEOのジェンスン・フアン氏が外国の情報機関による工作の標的になっていたとする報道を受け、取締役会が独立調査を開始すると
発表
した。
米New York Times
などの報道によると、米情報当局は、フアン氏と親密な関係にあった中国人実業家アリス・フアン（Alice Huang、中国名はHuang Le）氏（ジェンスン・フアンCEOと同姓のため、以下「アリス・フアン氏」と表記）が、中国の情報機関である国家安全部に協力していた疑いを把握していたという。
　アリス・フアン氏は、NVIDIAのAI半導体を購入するシンガポールに拠点を置くAI向けクラウドサービス企業Megaspeedの元幹部。米当局は同社について、中国への輸出が制限されている先端AI半導体の入手に関与した疑いでも調べている。米下院の外交委員会、中国特別委員会、情報特別委員会の3委員長は11月5日、フアンCEOに対し、アリス・フアン氏との接触やNVIDIAのスパイ対策について、18日に非公開で説明するよう要請したと
発表
している。
LinkedInに掲載されているアリス・フアン氏とみられるLinkedInプロフィール
　NVIDIAの取締役会は、独立した取締役で構成する委員会を設置し、外部の独立した法律顧問の支援を受けて調査を進める。声明では「米国の国家安全保障やNVIDIAの機密情報が侵害されたと考える理由は現時点でない」としながらも、入手可能なすべての情報を精査するとしている。
　取締役会はフアンCEOと経営陣への信頼を表明し、調査の進展に応じて追加情報を公表する方針だ。フアンCEOも調査を全面的に支持し、協力を約束している。
　フアンCEOは同日、従業員向けのメッセージを公開し、妻以外の女性との関係を認めて謝罪した。「私の行動は妻と子供たちを傷つけた。弁解の余地はない」と述べ、責任を全面的に認めた。また、国家安全保障上の懸念については、独立調査に全面的に協力すると表明した。

Alleged Jensen Huang spy plot highlights Nvidia export concerns
Business news
|
November 2, 2026
                                                By
                                                Brian Tristam Williams

US intelligence agencies reportedly concluded that a Chinese businesswoman with access to Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang was passing information to Beijing, raising questions about the security of the company’s commercial relationships and the enforcement of US semiconductor export controls.
The alleged Jensen Huang spy plot was 
reported by The New York Times
 on 29 October 2026. The newspaper identified the woman as Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, a Chinese national formerly associated with Singapore-based data centre operator Megaspeed International.
Citing people briefed on classified surveillance, the newspaper said US intelligence officials believed Huang was gathering information for the Chinese government. Surveillance reportedly established that she had developed a close relationship with Nvidia’s chief executive. President Donald Trump was allegedly briefed on the findings in 2025 but did not intervene or arrange for the Nvidia executive to receive a warning.
Jensen Huang spy plot allegations and Megaspeed
Megaspeed had already attracted attention from US authorities investigating whether advanced Nvidia processors were reaching Chinese customers despite export restrictions. The company operates in the market for AI computing infrastructure, where data centre operators can purchase GPUs and provide computing capacity to customers remotely.
According to the investigation, a Malaysian company affiliated with Megaspeed purchased at least $4.8 billion worth of equipment incorporating Nvidia technology between June 2024 and February 2026. US investigators reportedly suspected that some of the hardware might have been diverted to China, although the allegations of illegal shipments have not been publicly substantiated.
Huang rejected suggestions that his dealings with Megaspeed were improper. In comments 
reported by Reuters
, he described the company’s relationship with Megaspeed as normal and said Nvidia had examined the deployment of its GPUs to establish whether processors had been diverted.
Nvidia spokesperson John Rizzo said Huang routinely meets thousands of customer executives each year. He maintained that regional cloud customers receive ordinary commercial information rather than confidential Nvidia technology or material relevant to US national security.
Singapore disputes claims about the investigation
Singapore’s Ministry of Home Affairs issued a 
clarification on 31 October
, rejecting suggestions that Singaporean authorities had investigated Alice Huang’s alleged connections to Chinese intelligence or her relationship with Jensen Huang.
The ministry said no such investigations into Huang had been conducted. It nevertheless confirmed that investigations into Megaspeed itself remained ongoing, distinguishing the company’s potential regulatory problems from the separate espionage allegations.
Megaspeed told The New York Times that its current management had retained outside legal counsel to investigate the claims and intended to cooperate with US authorities.
China also rejected the allegations. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun 
dismissed the reports
 on 30 October as unsubstantiated accusations against the country.
Nvidia chip exports remain politically sensitive
The controversy comes against a background of changing US restrictions on the sale of advanced AI accelerators to China. As 
previously reported by eeNews Europe
, China’s own restrictions on purchases of Nvidia H200 accelerators have complicated efforts to reopen authorised sales, even as Washington has considered permitting selected exports.
The Jensen Huang spy plot allegations introduce a separate intelligence concern into that debate: whether individuals connected to semiconductor customers could gain access to commercially or politically sensitive information through senior industry executives.
The publicly available reporting does not establish what information Alice Huang allegedly supplied to Beijing, whether it included confidential Nvidia material, or whether the company’s technology was illegally transferred to China through Megaspeed. Those questions remain unresolved.

===== https://thehill.com/policy/technology/6181938-nvidia-huang-china-intelligence-report/amp/
(status 200)
Nvidia dismissed a new report that CEO Jensen Huang was targeted by a Chinese intelligence asset, stating the diversion allegations were “thoroughly investigated and debunked last year.” 
The New York Times 
published a report
 Thursday finding American spy agencies last year discovered Alice Huang, an executive at the Singapore-based data center Megaspeed, funneled information to Beijing while developing a “close relationship” with Jensen Huang.
According to the report, President Trump was briefed about the intelligence about a year ago but did not take further action. Trump, the Times reported, was hesitant to interfere with Nvidia’s chip sales and the U.S.’s trading relationship with China. 
The Nvidia chief, a 
key Trump ally
, has navigated a delicate trade war between the U.S. and China over the past year and a half. Amid fierce competition 
on AI development
, the U.S. has restricted most advanced tech infrastructure sales to Beijing, though the Trump administration allowed Nvidia to sell some of its older AI chips to China last year. 
Citing anonymous sources briefed on the surveillance, the Times reported American officials were concerned about Alice Huang’s relationship with the Nvidia CEO, and it “left questions about whether she could be gleaning information” about the U.S. tech company. 
“The diversion allegations against this regional cloud were thoroughly investigated and debunked last year,” an Nvidia spokesperson told The Hill. 
Jensen Huang’s interactions with Alice Huang were “not unusual or unexpected,” the spokesperson added. “Megaspeed has been no different than many other regional cloud customers. Meetings with customers are part of a CEO’s job; Jensen talks with thousands of customer executives every year.”
The spokesperson maintained Nvidia’s regional cloud customers receive “ordinary business information” and “not sensitive Nvidia trade secrets and zero information relevant to national security.”
Jensen Huang has not received a defense briefing, the Times reported. The company spokesperson said the tech giant is “confident that if the U.S. government believes that any person is a threat to American industry, it would let us know.”
“We often receive updates from the U.S. government regarding parties and entities of concern and always act accordingly,” the spokesperson added. 
The anonymous sources told the Times that companies associated with Alice Huang have continued to buy Nvidia chips. The Hill reached out to Megaspeed for comment. 
Following years of export controls on the U.S.’s most advanced AI chips and chipmaking materials to China, the country has 
sought technological self-reliance
 through chip giants like Huawei. The spokesperson for Nvidia pointed to this dynamic, writing, “The PRC government is not seeking NVIDIA products from regional clouds. Instead, the PRC government blocked U.S. AI chips, protecting its own industry and resulting in the largest write-offs in U.S. tech history.”
The White House did not immediately respond to request for comment.
