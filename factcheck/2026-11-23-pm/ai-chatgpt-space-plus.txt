===== https://ctrlmag.com/blog/openai-brings-chatgpt-space-to-plus-subscribers-replacing-library-with-collaborative-ai-pages
(status 200)
OpenAI began rolling out ChatGPT Space to paying subscribers worldwide on Thursday, November 20, 2026, widening access to a collaborative workspace the company first showed at its September developer conference. Until this week, Space had been confined to Pro, Business, and Enterprise customers. Plus subscribers, who pay roughly twenty dollars per month, can now open the feature from the sidebar and build shared hubs of pages, slides, and files with other users who have Space access. The move marks one of the largest product expansions OpenAI has shipped to its mainstream consumer tier since the launch of custom GPTs, and it formally retires Library as the home for saved work inside the chat interface.
From Dev Day demo to default sidebar
ChatGPT Space debuted publicly at OpenAI Dev Day in September 2026 as a dedicated environment where people, teammates, and—on higher tiers—Dot agents could co-own documents and other artifacts. Igor Bonifacic reported for Engadget on November 20 that Plus users may now “create your own Spaces starting today” by choosing Space in the left-hand navigation bar, in the same slot where Library used to appear. OpenAI’s own release notes, timestamped the same day, state that Space is rolling out globally to Plus users ages eighteen and older, and that the section replaces Library as “your new home for pages, slides, images, and uploaded files.”
The product shift is more than a label change. Library had functioned largely as a passive archive of chats, uploads, and generated images. Space reframes ChatGPT as a lightweight productivity surface: users create “pages,” a document type OpenAI says is built for agent-assisted writing, visualization, research, and coding. Pages can nest subpages, sit inside named spaces, and—critically—stay synchronized with connected external tools so that, for example, a project plan might refresh when decisions land in Slack. Slides are rolling out alongside pages for eligible accounts; spreadsheets branded as Sheets are listed as coming soon in OpenAI’s help documentation.
What Plus users gain—and what stays gated
According to OpenAI’s help center, anyone creating or editing in Space needs Plus, Pro, Business, or Enterprise access and must be at least eighteen. Collaborators who only hold a Free account cannot open shared pages or presentations at all, and the company has not enabled public links—every share is permissioned and account-bound. That design choice limits viral distribution but gives OpenAI and enterprise administrators tighter control over data handling, especially in regulated industries where anonymous view links would be unacceptable.
Plus subscribers inherit most of the creative tooling Pro customers have used for the past two months, with one conspicuous exception in the near term: Dots, OpenAI’s personal agent layer, remain unavailable to typical Plus accounts. Engadget noted that Plus users “can’t use a Dot with your Spaces (or at all, for that matter)” as of the rollout, though OpenAI began testing Dots with a select group of Plus subscribers outside the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, and Switzerland on November 19. When Dots do arrive broadly on Plus, Space is positioned as one of their primary collaboration venues—users will be able to invite a Dot into a shared page and @mention ChatGPT in comments to request edits.
Scheduled Tasks, another feature OpenAI has been iterating in late 2026, integrates with Space so automated workflows can refresh content on a timer or trigger. That pairing turns a static export from a chat into a living dashboard: OpenAI’s release notes cite examples such as a self-updating to-do list, a study guide with an explorable 3D model, or a roadmap that tracks Slack decisions without manual copy-paste.
Collaboration mechanics and privacy boundaries
OpenAI’s documentation draws a careful line between collaborative page content and private chat memory. Each collaborator works with their own ChatGPT instance; sharing a page does not expose another person’s private conversations or Memory entries. Anything written onto the page, however, is visible to everyone with view or edit rights, which means teams must treat Space like any other shared drive—sensitive prompts belong in private chats, not on a jointly edited brief.
Sharing can happen at the page level or across an entire space. Subpages inherit permissions from parents, and removing a direct invite does not always revoke access if rights flow from a higher-level folder. Business and Enterprise deployments inherit organization-wide sharing policies, and administrators in those tiers may need to enable collaboration before members can invite external emails.
Real-time co-editing behaves similarly to mainstream office suites: multiple editors can work simultaneously, tag ChatGPT in comments, and ask the model to revise a section or an entire document. If a Plus user exhausts their message quota, manual editing remains available—Space does not block hand edits when model calls are throttled.
Platform coverage and regional caveats
Space is available on the web, desktop clients, and mobile apps, though creation and heavy editing are optimized for larger screens. OpenAI advises Plus users who do not see the sidebar entry to update the desktop application; staged rollouts mean some accounts will lag by hours or days even after the global announcement. Enterprise and Business workspaces with data residency in Canada or the United Arab Emirates are told to expect Space “coming soon,” reflecting the complexity of matching new collaboration features to sovereign hosting requirements.
Projects, a separate ChatGPT organizational primitive for instructions and file bundles tied to chat threads, remain distinct from Space. OpenAI explicitly states that adopting Space does not retire Projects; the two coexist so power users can keep long-running conversational contexts while also maintaining polished outward-facing documents.
Industry context and competitive pressure
The Plus expansion arrives as every major model provider races to own the interface where knowledge work happens. Microsoft has embedded Copilot across Office; Google has pushed Gemini into Docs and Slides; Anthropic and others pitch team workspaces with audit logs and enterprise retention. OpenAI’s bet is that customers already habituated to ChatGPT will prefer extending the same UI rather than exporting to a separate SaaS stack. By replacing Library with Space, OpenAI signals that uploaded files and generated artifacts are first-class products, not accidental by-products of chat history.
Analysts watching consumer AI pricing will note that Space effectively bundles lightweight CMS, presentation, and light project-management behaviors into the existing Plus subscription without a list-price increase—though inference costs for richly agent-edited pages could push heavy users toward Pro tiers or API billing. For small businesses that standardized on Plus during 2025 and 2026, the rollout may delay upgrades to Business plans unless they need SSO, retention policies, or Dot governance.
Use cases OpenAI wants to popularize
OpenAI’s marketing materials and Engadget’s walkthrough emphasize approachable scenarios: a city guide that friends co-edit before a visit, a student-built quiz deck with embedded visuals, or a product requirements document that updates when engineering posts a decision. The through-line is that Space rewards structured prompts and connected apps; a page that never links to external data sources will behave like a static wiki.
Third-party developers may eventually connect tools through OpenAI’s connector ecosystem, though the November 20 documentation focuses on first-party integrations and manual uploads—spreadsheets, images, links, and slides. The company’s September Dev Day narrative positioned Space as the human-facing layer atop Codex and other agent builders; pushing it to Plus is a population-level experiment in whether tens of millions of subscribers will adopt shared AI documents as casually as they adopted solo chat.
What to watch next
Several open questions will determine whether Space becomes sticky or lapses into another sidebar experiment. Will Free users ever receive view-only access via enterprise sponsors, or will OpenAI keep the paywall hard to drive upgrades? How quickly will Dots arrive for Plus outside test markets, and will agent edits in shared pages be attributable in audit logs for compliance teams? When Sheets launch, does OpenAI challenge Excel Online in small-business finance workflows, or stay confined to AI-generated summaries?
OpenAI has not published adoption metrics for the Pro-only preview period, so the Plus rollout will be the first broad stress test of concurrent editing, connector reliability, and support volume. If Engadget’s early hands-on is representative, the UX is mature enough for personal planning but may still feel sparse for teams accustomed to Notion databases or Figma comments.
For subscribers, the immediate action item is simple: update clients, look for Space where Library lived, and decide whether a shared page is a better home for ongoing work than an endless chat thread. For the industry, November 20, 2026, is the day OpenAI stopped treating collaboration as an enterprise upsell and declared it part of the default ChatGPT experience for anyone paying twenty dollars a month.
