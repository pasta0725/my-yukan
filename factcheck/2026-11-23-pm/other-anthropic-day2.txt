対話型AI「クロード（Claude）」を手掛ける米アンソロピック（Anthropic）の株価は、米国時間2026年11月20日の取引で前日比8.5%安の88.91ドルで引けました。取引時間中には一時、前日終値から約14.5%安となる83.05ドルまで下落し、公開価格（83ドル）まであと5セントの水準に迫りましたが、終値では公開価格を7.1%上回る水準を維持しました。
同社は11月19日、生成AI基盤モデルの開発を中核とする米大手企業として米ナスダック市場へ新規株式公開（IPO）しました。事前の需要が想定を上回ったことから、公開価格は当初提示していた70〜78ドルのレンジを上回る1株83ドルに決定し、売り出し株数も約8億2500万株から約9億8000万株へ増額されました。上場初日は公開価格を34%上回る111.50ドルで寄り付き、一時115.10ドルまで買われたものの、利益確定の売りに押されて97.17ドル（公開価格比17%高）で初日を終えました。初日終値時点の基本発行済株式ベースの時価総額は約1兆2700億ドル（完全希薄化ベースでは公開価格時点で約1兆5000億ドル）となり、2026年6月に上場し初日終値ベースで約2兆1000億ドルに達した米スペースXには届きませんでした。
一方、資金調達額では過去最大規模を更新しました。アンソロピックは20日、引受証券会社が追加購入権（オーバーアロットメント）を完全行使したことで、IPOの調達額が当初の約813億ドルから約122億ドル増え、約935億ドルに達したと発表しました。これはスペースXが6月のIPOで記録した約857億ドル（追加行使前は約750億ドル）を上回ります。さらに、アルファベット（グーグル親会社）、エヌビディア、マイクロソフト、AMD、ブロードコム、マイクロン、TSMC関連会社の7社に対する同時第三者割当増資（260億ドル）を合わせると、総調達額は約1195億ドルに上ります。
2021年に米オープンAIの元研究者らによって設立されたアンソロピックは、上場目論見書によると2026年1〜9月期の売上高が332億4000万ドルと前年同期（25億ドル）の13倍超へ急拡大しました。一方で、計算資源への巨額投資や非現金費用の計上が重荷となり、2026年7〜9月期は営業損益が5億6700万ドルの赤字（4〜6月期は9億7400万ドルの黒字）、米国会計基準（GAAP）の最終損益は233億ドルの赤字となっています。上場記念式典でダリオ・アモデイ最高経営責任者（CEO）は、商業面・政治面のいずれの圧力にも屈することなく、強化された財務基盤をもとにAIの安全性確保と適切な開発ペースを貫く方針を表明しました。ただ、追加購入権の行使完了に伴う引受証券会社による株価下支えの終了やAI関連株全体の軟調な地合いも重なり、上場2日目は値動きの荒い展開となりました。
AI投資の採算懸念が再燃、米ハイテク株が全面安に
アンソロピックの歴史的な大型上場を経ても、AIインフラへの巨額投資が収益に見合うのかという市場の警戒感は和らがず、20日の米国株式市場ではハイテク株を中心に幅広い銘柄へ売りが広がりました。ダウ工業株30種平均は前日比949.73ドル（1.93%）安の4万8147.33ドルと5営業日連続で下落し、およそ7か月ぶりの安値水準に沈みました。ハイテク株比率の高いナスダック総合指数は同1261.47ポイント（5.28%）安の2万2617.73で取引を終え、年初来の上昇分をすべて吐き出しました。S&P500種株価指数も3.70%安の6910.39と大きく下げています。
個別銘柄では、半導体大手のエヌビディアが14.0%急落しました。11月12日のジェンスン・フアン前CEOの辞任を受けた先行き不透明感が続くなか、AMDが13.3%安、ブロードコムが12.2%安となるなど半導体関連株が軒並み大幅安となりました。市場の不安心理を映す米CBOEのVIX指数（通称「恐怖指数」）は取引時間中に一時40.03と2025年4月以来の40台をつけ、終値は前日比26.2%高の35.89でした。なお、20日の米国市場に先立つ同日の東京株式市場でも、19日のダウ平均5万ドル割れなど米株安の流れを引き継ぎ、日経平均株価（225種）が前日比3007円56銭（5.06%）安の5万6436円65銭と、1日の下げ幅として過去5番目の急落を記録しています。

Wall Street is debating what caused a sharp swing in Anthropic's shares two days after the developer of artificial intelligence (AI) tool Claude completed a record initial public offering (IPO).
Anthropic logo.
The Wall Street Journal (WSJ) reported on the matter on the 21st, local time. According to the report, factors cited in the share price decline included the exclusion of some investors from the IPO allocation, the decision to allow employees to immediately sell large amounts of stock, and the underwriters' pricing at the start of trading.
Anthropic shares closed at $88.91 on the New York stock market the previous day, down 8.5% from the previous close. That would wipe $109 billion off its market capitalization in a single day. The stock fell as much as 15% during trading, dropping to just 5 cents above its $83 offering price.
One issue investors have raised is how the IPO shares were allocated. More than half of the $81 billion in shares allocated in the IPO went to existing investors, including Altimeter Capital and Lightspeed Venture Partners. Although the allocation was intended to reward early investors, critics said the proportion was unusually high.
By contrast, about 60% of institutions that sought IPO shares reportedly did not receive a single share. BlackRock reportedly received only about half of the roughly $10 billion in shares it requested.
The allocation of substantial amounts to major investment firms such as Citadel also prompted speculation that large-scale users of Claude may have been given priority.
Concerns were also raised that the opening trading price may have been set too high. An excessively high opening price could lead to sustained selling, making it difficult to reverse a decline in the stock, according to the analysis.
The decision to allow stock sales immediately after the listing is also seen as contributing to the decline. Anthropic allowed some current and former employees to immediately sell approximately 300 million shares they held. Those shares were worth about $26 billion at the offering price.
Companies typically impose lockup periods that restrict employees from selling their shares for several months after a listing. But Anthropic made an unusual decision to allow some employees to sell immediately.
Under these circumstances, underwriters have limited means to support the price if it falls below the offering price. Anthropic said the overallotment option, or greenshoe, that underwriters can use to stabilize the share price has already been exercised.
The WSJ said approximately 300 million additional shares are scheduled to become available for trading on the 15th of next month, potentially increasing selling pressure.

Today Anthropic, PBC announced the closing of its initial public offering of an aggregate of 1,127,000,000 shares of its Class A common stock at a public offering price of $83.00 per share, including the full exercise by the underwriters of their option to purchase an additional 147,000,000 shares of Class A common stock from Anthropic at the initial public offering price. The offering consisted of 1,103,351,354 shares sold by Anthropic and 23,648,646 shares sold by certain selling stockholders. The aggregate gross proceeds from the offering, before deducting underwriting discounts and commissions and other offering expenses, were approximately $93.5 billion, of which approximately $91.6 billion was received by Anthropic. Anthropic did not receive any proceeds from the sale of shares by the selling stockholders.
Anthropic’s Class A common stock began trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market on November 19, 2026 under the symbol “ANT.”
In addition to the shares of Class A common stock sold in the initial public offering, Anthropic closed the concurrent sale of an aggregate of 313,253,007 shares of Class N common stock to Advanced Micro Devices, Inc., Broadcom Inc., Google LLC, Micron Technology, Inc., Microsoft Corporation, NVIDIA Corporation, and TSMC Partners, Ltd. at the public offering price of $83.00 per share, in a private placement for an aggregate purchase price of approximately $26.0 billion. The sale of these shares of Class N common stock was not registered under the Securities Act of 1933, as amended (the “Securities Act”). Aggregate gross proceeds from the initial public offering, including the shares sold by the selling stockholders, and the concurrent private placement were approximately $119.5 billion, before deducting underwriting discounts and commissions and other offering expenses.

BTIG, Cantor, Capital One Securities, Macquarie Capital, and Societe Generale acted as co-managers for the offering.
