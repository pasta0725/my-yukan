iPhone Duo

最適化アプリの条件、何が変わるか
「
iPhone Duo
向けに最適化されたアプリ」とは、
iOS 27
.1以降のSDK（ソフトウェア開発キット）でビルドされたアプリを指す。インナーディスプレイとアウターディスプレイの両方で画面いっぱいに表示され、画面上部のステータスバー部分には縦に並んだ操作ボタンが表示される仕組みだ。
対応していないアプリでも使えなくなるわけではない。
iOS 27

iOS 26

対応アプリかどうかで画面の使われ方が変わる

Apple Says More Than 100,000 Apps Optimized for iPhone Duo, Here's What's Still Missing
Friday November 6, 2026 9:18 am PST
 by 
Joe Rossignol
Apple today announced that more than 100,000 apps have been optimized for the iPhone Duo, released two weeks ago. We confirmed that this figure refers to apps 
built with the iOS 27.1 SDK or later
, meaning these apps fill the full width of the iPhone Duo's inner and outer displays and have vertical controls in the status bar.
A statement from Susan Prescott, Apple's Vice President of Worldwide Developer Relations:
It's been wonderful to see how quickly developers have embraced iPhone Duo, and we're grateful for their creativity and innovation. Today there are well over 100,000 apps built for iPhone Duo already live on the App Store, and thousands of optimized apps are being added to the store each day. In the last month alone, thousands of developers have joined us for workshops in 19 cities around the world to experience iPhone Duo firsthand and bring updated apps to their users. The enthusiasm has been incredible, and we're excited to see the pace of updates continue to accelerate as more and more apps optimize for iPhone Duo.
Apple said thousands of apps optimized for the iPhone Duo are being added to the App Store each day, with recent examples including 
Microsoft's Excel, OneNote, PowerPoint, and Word apps
, along with the Etsy, Goodnotes, Halide Mark III, NBA, NHL, and Zoom apps. Other apps optimized for the device include Amazon Prime Video, Fantastical, Flighty, Instagram, MLB, Netflix, SmartGym, WhatsApp, YouTube, and more. There are too many to list.
Apps optimized for the iPhone Duo have a "Made for iPhone Duo" badge in their App Store listings on the device, making them easy to identify.
iOS 27.2 added 
Apple Pencil (USB-C) support to the iPhone Duo
, enabling users to draw in apps like Procreate Pocket, take notes in Apple Notes, Fineliner, and TypeDraw, and mark up documents in apps like Preview on the device.
On the other hand, with millions of apps available in the App Store, there are unsurprisingly many apps that are still not optimized for the iPhone Duo, with the following examples listed in alphabetical order: AMC Theaters, Amazon Fire TV, Apple Store, Best Buy, Bluesky, Delta, DoorDash, Duolingo, Fandango, FedEx, Goodreads, Google Docs, Google Home, Google Sheets, Google Slides, GrubHub, JetBlue, Libby, Microsoft Authenticator, Microsoft Teams, MTA, Nike, PayPal, PictureThis, Ring, Southwest, Spectrum TV, Starbucks, Steam, Uber Eats, UPS, USPS, Venmo, Verizon, Wells Fargo, Whole Foods, and YouTube Music.
Apple said developers can easily optimize their apps for the iPhone Duo. Apps that use standard SwiftUI and UIKit components adapt automatically to each display, with controls moved to the status bar and multi-column support on the inner display. New APIs let developers optimize their apps for the device's various layouts and poses.
Apple has hosted more than 50 in-person iPhone Duo app workshops in 19 cities worldwide, and there are 
online resources
 like the 
Apple Developer Forums
.
In the meantime, apps built with the iOS 27 SDK resize to fill most of the inner display on the iPhone Duo, but they avoid the status bar on the right edge. Apps built with the iOS 26 SDK or earlier run on the iPhone Duo, but appear in the center of the inner display, with lots of empty black space on the left and right sides of the apps.
iPhone Duo app optimization has been a hotly debated topic on social media platforms, with some people pointing out apps that are not yet optimized and others saying the issue is overblown. It is clearly a mixed bag, with some apps optimized and some not. More apps are being updated by the day, though, so the situation is improving rapidly.

Three steps to make your app shine on iPhone Duo
Apps built with the iOS 27 SDK and later resize dynamically on iPhone Duo. Unfold it and your app expands to fill the inner display; fold it and your app returns to the outer display. Hardcoded screen sizes, fixed orientations, and device assumptions can break layouts, leading to stretched, clipped, or misplaced content. Follow these steps to make sure your app is ready for iPhone Duo.
Before you begin
While all apps will run on iPhone Duo, building with the latest SDKs let your app use more of the display and adapt gracefully across layouts. The following examples show what your app will look like on iPhone Duo depending on which SDK you build with.
iOS 26 SDK
Apps built with the iOS 26 SDK or earlier run on iPhone Duo, but won’t adapt to all layouts. When the device is open, your app will appear in the center of the display, with empty space surrounding the window. When the device is closed, the outer display will show your app’s content to the left of the status bar and camera.
iOS 27 SDK
Apps built with the iOS 27 SDK resize to fill most of the inner display on iPhone Duo, but still avoid the status bar on the right edge. This provides an improved experience, as empty space is minimized.
iOS 27.1 SDK or later
Apps built with the iOS 27.1 SDK or later are optimized for iPhone Duo. Apps will extend to use the full display, with toolbars and tab bars appearing vertically below the status bar. This provides people with an optimal and consistent experience as they move across displays.
Before shipping an app build with the iOS 27.1 SDK, make sure your app’s content adapts to the bars becoming vertical.
Step 1: Run the app resizability skill
In 
Xcode
, ask the coding assistant to “
get my app ready for iPhone Duo.
” The skill scans your project for the patterns described in Step 2. For each issue it finds, it explains the problem and suggests a fix.
If you use a different coding agent, export the skill by running this in Terminal:
xcrun agent skills export
The skill finds most issues, but not all of them. Use the list below to review what it flags and catch anything it misses.
Step 2: Fix patterns
Stop sizing from the main screen
On iPhone Duo, your app’s size changes whenever the device folds or unfolds, and it doesn’t always match the size of the screen. Search your project for 
UIScreen.main
 and replace each use:
Size from the view or window.
 Replace 
UIScreen.main.bounds
 with 
view.bounds
 or 
window.bounds
. In SwiftUI, use 
GeometryReader
 or 
onGeometryChange
.
Re-read the size whenever it changes.
 Reading the size once at launch or in 
viewIsAppearing
 isn’t enough, because a fold can resize your app in the middle of a session. Do size-dependent layout in 
layoutSubviews
 or 
viewDidLayoutSubviews
, and use 
viewWillTransition(to:with:)
 for work that needs to run when the size changes.
Get scale from the trait collection.
 Use 
traitCollection.displayScale
 in UIKit or 
@Environment(\.displayScale)
 in SwiftUI.
Create windows from a scene.
 Replace 
UIWindow(frame: UIScreen.main.bounds)
 with 
UIWindow(windowScene:)
.
Don’t store a 
UIScreen
.
 When screen information is needed, access it dynamically with 
view.window?.windowScene?.screen
.
Avoid using orientation to make layout decisions
On iPhone Duo, the device’s orientation doesn’t tell you what shape your app is. Search for these properties and remove any layout logic that depends on them:
UIDevice.current.orientation
statusBarOrientation
interfaceOrientation
, including 
windowScene.effectiveGeometry.interfaceOrientation
.
To find out whether your app is wider than it is tall, compare the width and height of the space you have: 
view.bounds
 in a view controller, 
superview.bounds
 in a view, or the size from 
GeometryReader
 in SwiftUI.
Stop assuming idiom or size class identifies the device
With iPhone Duo and resizable iPhone Mirroring, an iPhone app can have 
any combination of size classes
. On the inner display, your app should look like a natural extension of its iPad layout. For more information, see 
Prepare your app for iPhone Duo
.
Don’t choose a layout by idiom.
 Search for 
userInterfaceIdiom == .phone
 and 
== .pad
. Base layout choices on size classes or available space instead.
Don’t treat “regular width” as “iPad.”
 Code that shows an iPad-only layout when 
horizontalSizeClass == .regular
, or that assumes iPhone is always compact, will now run that layout on iPhone too.
Remove checks for specific device sizes.
 Comparisons such as 
bounds.height == 844
 or a list of known iPhone sizes won’t match iPhone Duo.
Check full-bleed media.
 Hero images and video set to 
.scaleAspectFill
 or 
.aspectRatio(contentMode: .fill)
 can lose important content on wider displays. Use the current size classes or aspect ratio to choose between fill and fit, or set a focal point.
Step 3: Test resizing
Test resizing using these tools to make sure your app runs as expected.
The iOS resizable simulator
 in Device Hub.
iPhone Mirroring
 on macOS 27. Resize the window to extreme sizes in both directions.
The iPhone Duo simulator
 in Xcode 27.1.
Note:
 The iPhone Duo simulator is the best way to test the full native experience.
Go further: Stand out on the App Store
Update your App Store product page to show that your app is ready for iPhone Duo.
Showcase iPhone Duo support.
 Share app previews and screenshots that highlight your app across orientations. When you submit iPhone Duo screenshots in App Store Connect, your App Store product page will display a badge to let people know your app is optimized for iPhone Duo. 
View screenshot specifications.
Preview your assets.
 Use the new preview tool in App Store Connect to see how your assets — including 
new creative assets
 — look on iPhone Duo.
Share your app with us.
 Submit a featuring nomination in App Store Connect to let the App Store editorial team know your app supports iPhone Duo. In the Helpful Details section, you can specify that your app is optimized for iPhone Duo, including support for all device poses. 
Learn how to create a featuring nomination.
