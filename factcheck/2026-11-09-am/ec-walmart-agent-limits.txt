米EC売上高2位のWalmart（ウォルマート）は、AIショッピングエージェントがECで果たす役割が急速に拡大するなか、消費者自身が買い物を主導・管理できる安全な環境づくりを強化しています。
AIエージェントによる自動購入の管理機能と支出上限を導入
Walmartは10月21日、「Walmart」および傘下の会員制倉庫型スーパー「Sam's Club」において、AIエージェントによる自動購入の管理や買い物の効率化、アクセス制限ルールを定めた新機能を表しました。
AIに買い物を完全に任せることに慎重な消費者が多い現状を踏まえ、購入プロセスに厳格な安全対策を組み込むことで、「最も信頼されるAIショッピング体験」の提供をめざしています。
この方針に基づき、Walmartは次の3つの機能を導入します。
Agent Profiles（エージェント・プロフィール）：
消費者がAIエージェントを承認し、支出上限を設定したり、購入履歴を確認したりできる機能。
Agent Gateway（エージェント・ゲートウェイ）
：AIエージェントがWalmartのショッピングサービスに、より直接的に接続できる仕組み。
新たな購入ルール
： AIエージェントが実行できる操作や、顧客情報へのアクセスに制限を設けるもの。
Walmartは消費者向けの機能に加え、マーケットプレイスの出店事業者がAIを活用した商品発見に対応できるよう支援。これにより、出店事業者が消費者にリーチする新たな機会が生まれる可能性があります。
Walmartは現在、自社のAIアシスタント「Sparky」をはじめ、Metaの「Muse」、OpenAIの「ChatGPT」、Googleの「Gemini」などとの連携を進めています。なお、Walmartは米国の大手EC専門誌『Digital Commerce 360』の「北米EC事業 トップ1000社」で
2位
、「AIコマースランキング」で
37位
にランクインしています。
「Walmart」に実装されているAIエージェントのイメージ（画像は編集部がWalmartのWebサイトからキャプチャ）
支出上限や個別承認を設定できる「Agent Profiles」
Walmartは、消費者がAIエージェントによる購入を主体的に管理・確認できる「Agent Profiles」を今後1か月以内に順次導入します。
現在、AIエージェントが実行した購入手続きは、顧客自身の通常注文と同じように購入履歴に表示され、両者を明確に区別する手段がありません。WalmartのAI推進・製品・デザイン担当エグゼクティブバイスプレジデントであるダニエル・ダンカー（Daniel Danker）氏は、LinkedInへの投稿で、新機能について次のように説明しています。
エージェントは顧客のアカウントに紐づく専用プロフィールを使って買い物をします。これにより、顧客は購入を承認したり、支出上限を設定したり、どの購入がエージェントによるものかを確認できるようになります。
今回の新機能により、AIエージェントは
顧客アカウントに紐づく専用プロフィール経由で買い物を行う
ようになるのです。
Agent Profilesの主な特長と導入手順
ダニエル・ダンカー氏によると、支出管理において次の選択肢が提供されます。
購入承認の選択肢
1件ずつの手動承認：
AIによる購入ごとに消費者が内容を確認して承認する。
上限額までの自動許可：50ドル、100ドル、200ドル
など、あらかじめ設定した上限額までエージェントによる支出を許可する。
また、「Agent Profiles」のセットアップは次の3ステップで完結します。
エージェントのアプリから開始：
消費者が初めてAgent Gatewayを通じて買い物をすると、Walmart側でプロフィールを自動生成。
Walmartとの接続：
消費者がWalmartのアカウントにログインし、エージェントのプロフィールを承認してアカウントに紐づける。
支出管理の設定：
消費者は支出上限を設定し、エージェントによる購入を確認。プロフィールはいつでも削除可能。
なお、
AIエージェントがアクセスする際も人間が目にするのと全く同じ価格を表示
し、購入手続きの前に割引特典や手数料が提示されます。
エージェントの支出限度額を設定し、承認が必要な購入項目を指定できる（画像は編集部がWalmartのWebサイトからキャプチャ）
70%の消費者が望む「購入前確認」、AI買い物に対する安全対策ルール
ダンカー氏によると、Walmartは「AIエージェントが顧客やWalmartとどのように関わるべきかについて、ルールを明確にする」という取り組みも進めています。
こうした安全対策の背景には、消費者が「AI任せの買い物」に対して抱いている根強い警戒感があります。
決済テクノロジー企業NMIが8月に公表した調査（米国の成人1000人を対象に実施）によると、次の実態が明らかになっています。
AIを利用して取引を完了した経験があると回答した割合は11%
チェックアウト前にAIによる購入判断を確認・変更したいと回答した割合は70%
AIに購入を完全に一任してもよいと回答した割合は10%
このような消費者の不安に対応するため、Walmartの新たな利用規約では
初期設定で購入ごとの消費者承認を義務付け
ています。消費者は設定した上限額の範囲内で支出を許可できますが、Walmartが追加の承認を求める場合もあります。
さらに、AIエージェントに対する制限ルールとして次のルールを定めています。
ユーザーの指示を超えた独断の行動禁止
許可されていない顧客情報や決済カード情報（クレジットカード詳細）へのアクセス禁止
支払い方法の追加・変更の禁止
仮にAIエージェントが意図しない商品を購入してしまった場合でも、
Walmartの通常の返品手続きを適用
します。利用規約に違反した悪質なAIエージェントは、Webサイトへのアクセスが即座に禁止されます。
ただし、消費者がサードパーティーのAIアプリに直接提供した情報については、引き続き各サービス提供事業者のプライバシーポリシーが適用されます。
AnthropicのMCP規格を採用した「Agent Gateway」の開発
Walmartは、AIシステムが自社のショッピングサービスへより高速かつ直接的に接続できるようにする「Agent Gateway（エージェント・ゲートウェイ）」の開発を進めています。
現在、
多くのAIエージェントは人間用に設計されたブラウザ操作（スクレイピングなど）を通じて買い物を代行していますが、この方法では処理に時間がかかりエラーが発生しやすいという課題がありました。
「Agent Gateway」は、Anthropicが開発したオープン接続規格「Model Context Protocol（MCP）」を採用することで、AIアプリケーションとECサービスのスムーズな接続を実現します。ダンカー氏によると、このゲートウェイは「商品発見、カートへの追加、購入、注文の追跡」に対応しているそうです。
商品発見
カートへの追加
購入手続き
注文の追跡
また、プライバシー保護の観点から、
ユーザーの広範な購入履歴全体を開示することなく、普段購入している牛乳やパンの好み（特定ブランド）をAIエージェントが安全に特定できる設計となっています。
なお、「Agent Gateway」は企業や開発者向けに限定ベータ版として提供する予定です。ただし、提供開始時期や参加企業については、まだ発表していません。
エージェントが購入した商品を確認し、追跡できる（画像は編集部がWalmartのWebサイトからキャプチャ）
「Walmart Marketplace」出店事業者に生まれる新たな商機
AIエージェント経由の買い物の広がりは、
「Walmart Marketplace」に出店するサードパーティ事業者（出品者）にとっても新たな顧客獲得のチャンスをもたらしています。
ダニエル・ダンカー氏が3月のMorgan Stanleyのカンファレンスで明かしたところによると、「ChatGPT」経由でWalmartから購入された商品のトップ2カテゴリーは「ビタミンサプリメント」と「プロテインサプリメント」でした。
また、こうした購入が特定の商品を探す検索ではなく、
「健康や栄養に関するAIとの幅広い会話」から始まった可能性がある
と指摘しています。
Walmart Global Marketplaceシニアバイスプレジデントのマニッシュ・ジョネジャ（Manish Joneja）氏は、『Digital Commerce 360』にこう説明しています。
エージェント型コマースによって、まだ広く知られていないブランドや商品を含め、出店事業者が適切な商品を適切な消費者に届ける機会が増える可能性があります。
一方で、AIに自社商品を正しく理解・推奨してもらうためには、次の要素が今後より一層重要になると指摘しています。
正確な商品情報
競争力のある価格
在庫状況
信頼できる配送予定
こうした変化に備え、Walmartは現在マーケットプレイスの出店事業者と協力し、商品掲載情報や商品カタログの品質向上に取り組んでいます。これにより、AIを活用したショッピングサービスが、出店事業者の商品を「より理解しやすく、レコメンドしやすくなる」としています。

Walmart Makes It Possible for Agents to Shop on Behalf of Customers, While Keeping Customers in Control

When Sam Walton opened the first Walmart more than 60 years ago, he set out to help people save money and live better. He believed new ideas and technology could make that possible – and that spirit still drives us today.
Today, we serve customers through stores and clubs, online shopping, pickup and delivery, Sparky and conversational commerce integrations with Muse, ChatGPT, Gemini and others. We want to be wherever customers choose to shop, bringing Walmart and Sam’s Club’s broad assortment, convenience and everyday low prices into the experiences they already use.
Shopping keeps changing, and Walmart has never been one to stand still. As AI agents become more mainstream, we’re not waiting to see what shopping becomes – we’re helping shape it, guided by our values – respect, integrity, service and excellence – and by listening and responding to our customers.
That’s why we’re introducing Agent Profiles, the Walmart Agent Gateway and terms for AI agents. Together, these tools will let customers shop with Walmart and Sam’s Club through the apps and agents they choose, with clear permissions, controls and protections built in.
Shopping with an agent won’t be for everyone, and it doesn’t have to be. But for customers who choose to use one, our goal is to make our experience the most trusted by building clear controls and protections into the experience from the start.
Agent Profiles: A dedicated profile for agents
Today, when a customer uses an AI agent, it signs in using a customer’s account. This means that all purchases – whether made by the customer or the agent – appear together, without any differentiation.
Agent Profiles, launching in the next month, will offer a new level of transparency for customers. Customers will be able to authorize each AI agent with a dedicated profile within the customer’s account, so the customers will be able to see the agent’s purchases separately from the rest of their purchases.
In addition, the customers will be able to determine how they want to approve purchases. They can require approval for every purchase or provide permission for the agent to make purchases within a spending limit they set – for example, $50, $100 or $200. That will give the agent a way to help while the customer stays in control.
Think about it like adding someone as a cardholder on your credit card. You can give them a way to pay, set a spending limit and keep track of how the card is used. Here, an agent gets its own profile to shop, with permissions and spending limits set by the customer and the ability to turn them off at any time.
And whether customers choose to shop directly or through an agent, our commitment to Every Day Low Price remains the same. We price products, not people, so the prices that an AI agent sees are the same prices that a human would see on Walmart.com, with applicable offers and fees shown before purchase.
Agent Profiles: How it Works
Start in the agent’s app
. The first time you use an agent to shop through the Walmart Agent Gateway, Walmart will automatically create an Agent Profile for that agent.
Connect through Walmart
. You'll authorize and link that Agent Profile to your account by signing into your Walmart account. Only Walmart sees this.
Set your controls
. Choose a spending limit and see purchases from every agent.  Delete an Agent Profile at any time.
The Walmart Agent Gateway: Talking to agents, like agents
Today, agents may shop on Walmart’s site through a web browser, following steps designed for people. This can be slow and error-prone, and agents may get stuck along the way. But the website is built for people to browse, click and sign in themselves. The way agents shop is different, and so we are building the Walmart Agent Gateway which will give agents a faster, more reliable way to shop by using an MCP (model text protocol). We'll begin with a 
beta for companies and developers
.
Another benefit of the Walmart Agent Gateway will be the ability to help agents shop for a customer’s usuals without having to share their personal information or purchase history with the agent. For example, if a customer tends to buy sourdough bread or a particular brand of milk, Walmart will help the agent find those usual items for the customer without sharing the customer’s broader shopping history.
Terms for AI Agents
In addition to profiles and a gateway designed for agent shopping, we are also setting clear expectations for how agents must operate, establishing what agents can and cannot do when shopping on behalf of a Walmart customer:
Customers are in control, and Walmart’s returns process applies – including if an agent doesn’t act as intended.
Customers must approve agent purchases by default, and Walmart may require additional approvals even for purchases within an agent’s spending limits.
Agents must not take actions beyond what the shopper intends.

NMI Research: Consumers Want AI to Help Them Shop, Not Control Their Spending
Aug 26, 2026
Just 11% of U.S. consumers have used AI to complete a transaction, as trust, control and transparency remain barriers to agentic commerce 
The technology industry is racing towards AI agents that can browse, choose and buy on a consumer’s behalf, but American shoppers are not there yet. They want AI to help them find products, save money and remove friction from checkout, but they still want a human hand on the final purchase. New research from 
NMI®
, a global leader in embedded payments infrastructure, reveals a significant gap between the industry hype surrounding agentic commerce and the authority consumers are currently prepared to give AI.
NMI’s 
Embedded Payments: Agentic Commerce Reality Check
, a survey of 1,000 U.S. adults, found that consumers are increasingly using AI to start their shopping journeys, but few are ready to let it finish the job. While Salesforce reports a 
200% rise in consumers using AI 
as their first step in shopping, NMI found that just 11% have used it to complete a transaction. 
Consumers are embracing AI as a shopping assistant, using it to find information, discover products and save money, but most are not ready to let it make the final decision or spend on their behalf: 
Just 10% would give AI full control over a purchase
45% would not trust AI to make purchases on their behalf at all
70% say they must be able to review or override AI’s decision before a purchase is made
69% do not trust AI to process payments securely
Tiffany Johnson, Chief Product Officer at NMI, said:
“The industry is designing for autonomy, while consumers are still asking for assistance. There’s a big difference between using AI to quickly surface the right information to help you choose a pair of shoes and AI taking over the entire shopping journey.  With back-to-school shopping underway and Labor Day sales around the corner, we expect more consumers to turn to AI to search, compare and find the best deals, but most are still not ready to surrender control of the purchase itself. For agentic commerce to move from an impressive demonstration to an everyday behavior, earning that permission will matter just as much as building the technology. Consumers need to know that AI understands what they want, stays within the limits they set and cannot spend beyond the authority it has been given.This approach to agentic commerce can help build consumer trust while allowing businesses to make the painful parts of shopping easier, without taking away what people value most: the satisfaction of a good purchase and the sense that they had the final say. ”
Consumers want AI to remove friction, not remove their involvement
More than half of U.S. consumers, 53%, used AI for research or information searches in the past month, while 33% used it for shopping or product discovery. Its most common role remains that of a “better search engine,” selected by 39% of respondents. 
The research shows that consumers are not rejecting AI in commerce. Instead, they are drawing a clear boundary between tasks that assist them and decisions that commit their money. 
63% would use AI to insert discount codes automatically during checkout
59% are comfortable with AI recommending products
58% would allow AI to fill in their shipping address
57% believe AI can make shopping faster and more convenient
56% would use it to make checkout faster and more seamless
That willingness declines sharply as AI begins to make more personal or consequential decisions. Fifty-eight percent are uncomfortable with AI recommending clothing sizes based on customer reviews, while 55% do not want AI recommending which payment method they should use. Seventy-six percent are uncomfortable with AI completing purchases on their behalf, and only 3% currently describe AI as a personal shopper that manages every stage of the shopping journey.
Parents and younger consumers are moving faster, but still want boundaries 
Parents, Millennials and Gen Z are adopting AI-powered shopping more quickly than other groups. Almost half of parents with children at home, 48%, have used AI for shopping or product discovery, compared with 25% of adults without children at home. Parents are also nearly three times as likely to have completed a transaction using AI, at 19% compared with 7%. Yet only 16% of parents would give AI full control over a purchase.
Similarly, 48% of both Millennials and Gen Z say they are excited about agentic commerce and believe it could make their lives easier. But even among these earlier adopters, relatively few are prepared to hand over complete purchasing authority. Only 16% of parents, 14% of Millennials and 13% of Gen Z would give AI full control over a purchase. 
The findings suggest that greater familiarity with AI increases enthusiasm for agentic commerce, but does not remove the need for strong safeguards. 
Trust, control and transparency will determine adoption 
Consumers appear more comfortable with AI operating invisibly within commerce than acting autonomously at the moment of purchase. Sixty-eight percent would not be surprised to learn that AI is already being used behind the scenes to streamline payment experiences. However, when AI is given the ability to spend, consumers’ concerns center on three clear imperatives:
Trust: 
consumers need confidence that their money and payment information will remain secure:
69% do not trust AI to process payments securely
61% cite security or fraud as a concern about AI completing purchases
84% have never uploaded payment details to an AI tool to enable purchases
The consequences of a failure would extend beyond the company providing the AI. If an AI tool made a purchase before a consumer was ready, 62% would blame the AI provider, but 34% would blame the retailer and nearly one in four, 24%, would hold the payments provider responsible.
Control: 
consumers want to set clear boundaries around what an AI agent is permitted to do:
70% say it is important that they can review or override an AI decision before a purchase
60% worry AI could spend more than they intended
57% are concerned about losing control over purchases
Transparency:
consumers also want to understand what AI has selected and why before their money moves:
58% worry an AI shopping agent could purchase the wrong item, size, color or brand
50% are concerned about having limited visibility into AI’s decisions
55% do not want AI recommending their payment method
Johnson added: 
“Payments is where the promise of agentic commerce meets real consumer risk. If an AI agent is going to spend someone’s money, it has to be clear what that person agreed to, what the agent is allowed to do and when it needs to ask for approval again. That means payment infrastructure will need to carry much more context. It will have to connect identity, authentication, permissions and secure credentials so that every transaction reflects what the consumer actually intended, not simply what the AI was technically able to do.”
About the research
NMI’s 
Embedded Payments: Agentic Commerce Reality Check
 surveyed 1,000 U.S. adults about their current use of AI, their attitudes towards AI-powered shopping and payments, and the factors that would influence their trust in agentic commerce.
