Educators can now add Guided Learning to assignments in Google Classroom to help students get extra support
Google Classroom
Rapid Release
Scheduled Release
Building on our 
announcement
 earlier this year, a new Google Classroom integration that lets teachers add 
Guided Learning
 to any assignment is now rolling out globally in all Gemini-supported languages. Guided Learning is a mode in the Gemini app designed to help students deepen their understanding with step-by-step guidance, rather than giving the answer. Similar to how teachers can 
assign Gemini Notebooks in Classroom
, this feature enables educators to embed structured and curriculum-informed AI-guided sessions directly into their lesson plans while maintaining visibility into student progress.
Key benefits:
Informed by your curriculum: 
Educators can select specific sources from Classroom to serve as context, helping ensure that the assigned Guided Learning is relevant and directly aligned with what is being taught in their classroom.
Grounded in learning science: 
Grounded in Google’s 
LearnLM research
, Guided Learning is built to support learners’ work - stimulating curiosity, providing scaffolding, facilitating productive struggle, and offering graduated hints.
Helping educators focus their support: 
Guided Learning serves as a supportive 1:1 tool to field students' initial questions about independent practice or homework, freeing up valuable teacher time to focus attention on the learners who need direct support.
Focused view for students: 
When students open the assigned Guided Learning from Classroom, the Gemini side navigation panel is automatically hidden, new chats are disabled within the tab, and they remain in Guided Learning mode.
Actionable insights for the entire class and individual students:
 Teachers can see  AI-generated insight summaries in Classroom based on students’ conversations with the assigned Guided Learning activity at both the class level and individual student level, helping educators inform instruction. Additionally, teachers can access individual student chat transcripts.
Seamless integration with Class Tools on Chromebooks:
 For educators and students using ChromeOS and 
Class Tools
, Guided Learning in Classroom can be launched directly to maintain a secure, unified, and locked-down environment where students are focused on a single tab and teachers can remotely view students’ screens to monitor progress.
How educators can use Guided Learning:
Provide scaffolded support for STEM problem-solving: 
For complex math or science problems, attach a Guided Learning activity so students can get extra support when they get stuck. This helps students navigate speed bumps independently, while giving teachers insights into where students are still struggling.
Deepen inquiry and conceptual extensions: 
Use Guided Learning for open-ended exploration and extension tasks, so students can develop their understanding of core concepts at their own pace and make meaningful cross-curricular connections.
Support collaborative brainstorming: 
Ask students to discuss and talk through their project ideas with help from Guided Learning to build confidence and refine their thoughts before participating in wider group discussions.
Extend support beyond the classroom:
 Offer Guided Learning as an on-demand tutoring assistant during homework and remote study, ensuring help outside of standard class time.
Educators can learn more about Guided Learning and ways to bring this tool into instruction in the 
Guided Learning in Classroom Guide
.

 Teachers and students must be in a group or OU with Gemini set to On. Visit the Help Center to learn more about 
turning Gemini on or off for users

 Guided Learning will be available if Gemini is enabled by your admin. Visit the Help Center to learn more about using Guided Learning in Classroom:

We’re sharing two new studies that show how Guided Learning supports students and teachers in real-world settings. Alongside these findings, today we’re starting to 
roll out Guided Learning in Google Classroom
, giving teachers even more control over how and when this tool is used in their classrooms.
Guided Learning is designed to help students work through complex concepts step by step, prioritizing deep understanding over providing direct answers.
Boosting statistics scores
To see how Guided Learning impacts real performance, we conducted a randomized controlled trial (RCT) with more than 300 students at the University of Tokyo. We wanted to test whether Guided Learning provided extra value beyond standard study tools. All students were given half a day to independently study a statistics lesson using standard online resources. Half of the group was also given access to Guided Learning in the Gemini app.
Instead of looking for quick answers, students used Guided Learning to dig deeper. They frequently asked for practice quizzes, step-by-step help, and custom visual aids. When we compared pre- and post-test scores, the difference was clear. Students using only standard web tools improved their scores by 5.3 percentage points. But students with access to Guided Learning improved by 8.1 percentage points, representing 1.5x higher learning gains in just a single study session. While that number might seem modest — these gains were achieved in just half a day of study, pointing to growing evidence that when technology guides students rather than shortcutting the process, it can lead to more learning.
Giving teachers more insight and control
Bringing Guided Learning into Google Classroom requires a specialized approach that is purpose-built for the classroom, keeps teachers in control, and grounds the experience in relevant lesson materials.
To test this experience, we partnered with 
Ark
 and ran a four-week pilot across three UK secondary schools, allowing teachers to use Guided Learning in Google Classroom across four different subjects. Feedback from post-pilot surveys and interviews was encouraging: teachers found Guided Learning handled initial student questions effectively during independent work, so they had more time to offer targeted, one-on-one help. Meanwhile, the majority of surveyed students said they felt comfortable asking Gemini for help when stuck, and over half said it helped them solve problems on their own.
We also received important feedback on areas of improvement. Some teachers wanted clearer visibility into how their students were engaging with the course materials so they could better tailor future lessons. Others voiced a desire to keep their students focused on specific assignments during class time and reduce distractions. We took this feedback back to the team and made direct improvements — including adding insights for educators to show how their students interacted with Guided Learning, and improving the ability for teachers to anchor conversations on uploaded curriculum.
Shaped by real educator and student feedback in the classroom, today we're rolling out teacher-led Guided Learning globally in Google Classroom. This feature will help teachers assign their students a Guided Learning experience that will coach students step-by-step on their course materials and track student progress with actionable insights.
By grounding our tools in learning science, working alongside educators, and conducting research in real classroom settings, we're eager to continue innovating on tools that support active learning and a deeper understanding.
