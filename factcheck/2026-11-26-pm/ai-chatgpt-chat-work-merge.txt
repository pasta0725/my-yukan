OpenAI
 has started folding ChatGPT’s Work tab into its main Chat experience. The first users to see the change are a small group of Pro subscribers.
The merger also comes with a new shared usage allowance. That second part is what has some paying customers reaching for the comment box.
What’s changing inside ChatGPT
Product lead Thibault “Tibo” Sottiaux confirmed the limited rollout in a post on X on Tuesday. OpenAI had already told users about the merger in an earlier email.
The rollout began on November 24, 2026, and initially covers Pro subscribers only. OpenAI says it wants a simpler user experience by January 2027.
Until now, ChatGPT has effectively run two lanes. Chat handled everyday back-and-forth, and Work handled longer, multi-step jobs.
The bigger operational shift is how usage gets counted. Starting in December 2026, Plus, Pro, and Business plans will draw from a single token usage allowance.
That shared pool covers tasks run in both ChatGPT and Codex, OpenAI’s coding product. Tokens are the small chunks of text that AI models read and write, and AI companies use them as the basic unit for metering consumption.

There is one exception. Instant Chat, the fast-response mode, will not count against the shared pool.
That carve-out matters for users who mostly fire off quick questions. Their routine usage should be unaffected by the new accounting.

OpenAI expects most users to see roughly the same reset frequencies they had before. Users running heavier workloads, however, might hit limits sooner.
How we got here
ChatGPT Work launched in July 2026. OpenAI pitched it as an agent for complex tasks, powered by models such as GPT-5.6.
Agents differ from standard chatbots because they carry out sequences of actions instead of giving single replies. They also tend to use far more compute per request.
Usage has been a sore spot for months. OpenAI had planned to reduce usage limits for Pro subscribers, then reversed course after user feedback.
On October 29-30, 2026, the company went further and doubled usage limits for Pro and Business Premium users. That was just weeks before this merger began rolling out.
OpenAI is not the only company moving this way. 
Anthropic
 has also combined its offerings, which suggests AI companies are moving toward unified interfaces instead of separate products.
What this means for users and OpenAI
For power users, the picture is less clear. Anyone relying heavily on both agent tasks in ChatGPT and coding work in Codex now has to budget across a single allowance.
Previously, heavy use in one area did not necessarily eat into the other. Under the new structure, a long Codex session could leave less room for Work-style tasks later in the cycle.
OpenAI’s defense rests on its own projection that most people will notice little difference. Whether that holds will depend on how usage actually plays out once the shared allowance takes effect in December.
The recent doubling of limits gives OpenAI some cushion. Pro and Business Premium users are entering the shared-pool era with more headroom than they had a month earlier.
The phased rollout is telling too. Starting with a small group of Pro subscribers lets OpenAI watch how heavy users behave before the change reaches more people.

Why some users may hit their limits sooner
OpenAI product lead Thibault Sottiaux said on November 25 that the company had 
begun rolling out
 an integrated Chat and Work experience in ChatGPT, with Work allowing users to delegate tasks to AI, to a 
small group of Pro subscribers
. He said OpenAI would gather feedback before expanding access.
The 
shared ChatGPT and Codex usage allowance
 planned for December had already been announced. Following the start of the early rollout, OpenAI provided new details about how the shared allowance may affect users.
An OpenAI spokesperson 
told TechCrunch
 that the company expects most users will not need to wait for their usage allowance to reset more often than before, while 
some particularly heavy users may hit their limits sooner
.
The outlook given for most users does not establish whether people who regularly use nearly all of their allowance will be able to keep working as they did before.
The English-language 
official help article
 also says some users may see the usage changes before December. As of November 26, Joho no Todai could not confirm whether the new limits had been applied to everyone who received early access to the integrated interface.
Sharing an allowance between research and development
The shared allowance applies to Plus, Pro, and Business plans. Usage from ChatGPT and the Codex programming assistant will be combined and measured in 
tokens, the units of information a model processes and generates
. Consumption varies by model and task, with more advanced models and complex tasks generally using more of the allowance.
On plans with a shared allowance, Work and Codex already drew from the same allowance. OpenAI's previous description of Pro plans said the GPT-6 Pro message limit for Chat was managed separately from the Work and Codex allowance. Under the December change, 
advanced chat will also consume the shared allowance
. Chats handled by Instant are excluded.

Consider a developer who investigates a design problem using an advanced reasoning model in ChatGPT and then implements the solution with Codex. If the design research consumes the shared allowance first, less will remain for the implementation that follows.
Conversely, using up the shared allowance during development could also affect research that relies on advanced models. Users will need to consider how much of the allowance to spend on each task across their entire workload.
An environment where users can develop an idea in chat and then delegate the work directly has clear appeal. The more naturally it connects the process from initial thought to completion, the more important it becomes for the allowance to support that entire process through the end.
Remaining usage is visible, but how much work will it cover?
In October, OpenAI 
expanded the Work and Codex allowance for Pro plans
 and canceled a planned reduction for some Pro 200 subscribers.
Determining whether the shared system effectively increases or decreases available usage requires comparing the size of the old and new allowances and the consumption of each task. The official help article on the shared allowance that Joho no Todai reviewed on November 26 did not disclose 
the total size of the new shared allowance
 or specific per-model consumption under the new system, and it did not identify the number or percentage of users who may hit their limits sooner.
In OpenAI's official community, a support account 
responded
 to a user's question on November 23. It said there was no confirmed information about whether the shared allowance would exceed the previous weekly Work and Codex allowance or how the separate Pro chat allowance would be handled.
According to OpenAI's guidance, users will be able to see their combined remaining usage and reset times in Settings. If they reach the limit, their options are to wait for the allowance to reset, upgrade their plan, or purchase credits for eligible features.
The spokesperson said 
Instant remains available
 after the shared allowance is exhausted. Because the previous 
official description of Pro plans
 also limits the features eligible for purchased credits, users should check whether additional usage is available for the models and features they want to use.
Displaying the remaining shared allowance will make it easier to recognize excessive consumption. But to determine from that figure how many document analyses can be completed or how far a software fix can be taken, users will also need a reasonable forecast of consumption.
The value of a flat-rate AI subscription also depends on 
how much work can be completed
 within the included allowance. The new experience of moving from consultation to execution in a single environment delivers its full value only if the allowance is large enough to avoid interrupting the work before it is finished.
