TITLE: Waymo suspends freeway rides nationwide after California DMV order

PUBLISHED: 2026-11-18 04:49 (page: 2026-11-17T19:49:41+00:00)

TEXT:
Waymo robotaxis are no longer allowed to operate on California freeways after the state’s Department of Motor Vehicles restricted the Alphabet-owned company’s operations following several incidents that raised safety concerns.
California’s DMV, which regulates the testing and deployment of autonomous vehicles in the state, issued the operational restrictions on Waymo on November 11. However, the restrictions weren’t made public until Tuesday. San Francisco Business Times was the
first to report
the order.
The DMV confirmed the new restrictions to TechCrunch, stating that the department took action after three Waymo autonomous vehicles entered an active construction closure on U.S. Route 101 near Burlingame, and another separate incident involving California Highway Patrol vehicles on Highway 280 in San Francisco.
Waymo has complied and is preventing its robotaxis from accessing freeways in any of the markets it operates in, including those outside of California. Waymo currently has commercial operations in 15 U.S. cities.
The DMV said Waymo’s freeway operations pose an imminent hazard to public safety.
“The Department determined that Waymo’s driverless freeway operations pose an imminent hazard to public safety due to recurring incursions by its vehicles into active construction zones where construction workers, vehicles and equipment are present, despite a prior restriction and corrective actions implemented following similar incidents that occurred on May 18, 2026,” according to an emailed statement sent to TechCrunch.
The DMV said the restrictions will remain in place until Waymo can demonstrate that it has taken “effective corrective actions” to address the deficiencies.
Waymo spokesperson Dana Gallagher told TechCrunch the company has temporarily restricted freeway operations across its other operating markets to evaluate its performance.
“Safety is at the foundation of everything we do,” Gallagher said in a statement. “Following recent freeway construction zone encounters on U.S. 101 in California that did not involve any injuries or collisions, the California DMV has implemented an operating restriction on freeway operations in the state. In alignment with our safety-first principles, we have also temporarily restricted freeway operations across our other operating markets to evaluate our performance. Our ride-hailing service continues to operate safely on surface streets, serving over half a million trips each week. We are working with the California DMV and other state officials diligently and responsibly to resume freeway operations.”
Waymo began offering
freeway rides in late 2025
, a commercial milestone that has been central to its expansion in major metro areas. Freeway access allows Waymo to connect riders to airports and other destinations more efficiently, slashing travel times by avoiding slower surface streets.
That has been particularly important in the San Francisco Bay Area because Waymo’s service area stretches from the city south to Silicon Valley. Freeway access has dramatically shortened trips across the Peninsula that previously took 45 minutes or more than an hour.
In May, Waymo abruptly
stopped operating
on highways. The company didn’t cite a specific reason at the time, but several social media posts showed Waymo robotaxis driving through traffic cones and struggling to navigate construction zones.
Waymo robotaxis
returned to freeways
in July after the company said it updated the self-driving software system to improve performance around freeway construction zones.
