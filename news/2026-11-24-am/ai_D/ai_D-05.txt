TITLE: Trump reportedly called into Nvidia board meeting to advocate for Huang’s return

A close ally of Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang, President Donald Trump
reportedly
dialed into an Nvidia board meeting to push for the ousted CEO’s return, Semafor reported on Monday. Trump got his way, as Nvidia announced Huang’s
reinstatement
shortly afterwards.
“I spoke to them, let me put it that way… And I convinced them,” the president
told
CNBC.
Huang was forced to step down from his role as CEO, which he held for 33 years, after news broke that he had
affairs
with both an alleged Chinese spy and two Nvidia employees, including a direct report.
“President Trump was totally involved and this is a great decision by the NVIDIA board. We have to protect our geniuses,” White House communications director Steven Cheung told Semafor.
The president took to his social media app, Truth Social, to
remark
that, “the United States government has no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to Nvidia poses any risk to national security.”
According to Nvidia’s press release about Huang’s reinstatement as CEO and president, the company claimed that “the Board received input from senior levels of the United States government, and based on that input, the Board is confident that Mr. Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk.”
While Trump has
declared
that Huang “did absolutely nothing wrong,” many government officials aren’t buying the president’s point of view. Huang is still expected to testify in closed-door conversations with three committees of the House of Representatives on December 1. He is also slated to appear before the Senate Banking Committee to testify publicly, though that hearing has not been scheduled yet.
“People say, is it a threat to have him? No, it’s a threat to lose him,” Trump said to CNBC.
Trump has maintained this position through the scandal, even
denying
that he was ever told about Huang’s relationship with an alleged Chinese spy named Alice Huang (no relation). The New York Times
reported
, on the contrary, that Trump’s secretary of state and national security adviser Marco Rubio briefed him aboard Air Force One in May.
Earlier this spring, Rubio also
supervised
the drafting of a memo which circulated among top policymakers, warning that Alice Huang was spying for China’s Ministry of State Security.
