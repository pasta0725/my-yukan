Amazon snaps up Decart’s AI optimization tech for $7.5B as it prepares to sell Trainium chips
Amazon has agreed to acquire a good chunk of Israeli startup Decart.
The e-commerce and cloud giant is paying
$7.5 billion
for the startup, according to Bloomberg.
Decart has made a name for itself building interactive real-time world models —
most recently Oasis 3
— and AI video generation tools. But under the hood, the company stands out for its Decart Optimization Stack (DOS), software that helps AI models to run efficiently on Nvidia, Amazon, and Google’s hardware.
As part of the deal, Amazon will acquire the technology behind DOS, as well as Decart’s founding team and team of AI researchers and engineers,
Amazon said in a blog post
.
“Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip,” said Peter DeSantis, SVP of foundational AI models, custom silicon, quantum computing, at Amazon. “More efficient compute means better training, better reinforcement learning, faster inference, and better models for customers at a lower cost.”
Amazon, which has been so far been using its Trainium chips at its own data centers, is preparing to start selling them to customers as a competitor to Nvidia. Decart’s technology could make it easier for customers to run their workloads on Amazon chips, rather than Nvidia’s, which has been the gold standard so far.
Decart and Amazon did not immediately return requests for comment.
