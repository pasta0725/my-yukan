Terrence O'Brien
is the Verge’s weekend editor. He’s covered the tech industry for over 18 years and knows a thing or two about synths.
Over the last week or so, we have seen a nationwide FAA ground stop, a multi-day Outlook outage that brought entire companies to a standstill, and a banking outage at
JPMorgan Chase
. But the consensus is that
there are more to come
. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told
CNN
:
“The American people should be prepared for a great deal of investment in upgrading our systems; they should be prepared for investment in cybersecurity, and they should be prepared that any time there is a step change in technology, a step change in both compute and applications, there will be bumps in the road.”
Microsoft President Brad Smith issued a similar warning about further outages on
CBS News’
Face the Nation with Margaret Brennan
, but sounded a much more ominous tone.
“I think we have to be prepared, frankly, for the coming months because this isn’t the type of thing that just gets done in a couple of weeks … The whole information infrastructure of the world is in effect being upgraded right now.”
While the outages over the last week were not believed to be caused by cyberattacks, Smith said that
AI
has made it
easier to find flaws
, “and then you can either use that knowledge to patch the code, or if you’re a bad actor, you can use that knowledge to try to hack into systems,” he said.
The last few months have also seen an increase in attacks on government and commercial systems, including those at the
USDA
,
JP Morgan
, and
supermarkets
.
Whether outages are brought on by AI-assisted hackers or
human error
during the
rush to patch an aging system
, one thing seems certain: there will be more in the coming months.
Update November 22nd:
Added clarification that Smith was speaking on Face the Nation, not to CBS generally. Due to a typo, this article originally stated that the outages were believed to be caused by cyberattacks, the missing word has been corrected.
