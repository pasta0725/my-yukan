Signal is restricting the ability to take screenshots on Android and iOS devices in an effort to prevent AI agents from reading users’ messages and other data. The agentic AI capabilities built into operating systems that analyze on-screen information to learn more about the user are “undermining Signal’s ability to independently guarantee privacy,” Signal president
Meredith Whittaker said in a blog post
, while urging OS providers to make it easier for app developers to control what data agents can access.
“We want to be very clear: it is a serious problem that Signal – and application developers beyond – lack robust tools to prevent AI agents from undermining application-level privacy,” Whittaker said. “Even where we are able to detect and block AI data access, Google, Apple, Microsoft, and others do not provide ways for developers to easily restrict such AI data access without making drastic compromises to the core user experience. This is also the reason we are currently unable to implement these protections on macOS.”
Signal is addressing the privacy concerns by blocking the ability to take screenshots in the app on
iOS 27
and Android, which should prevent
Siri
, Gemini, and other Android AI assistants from reading on-screen content. Users can restore screenshot capabilities in Signal’s app settings, but Whittaker warns that doing so will “potentially expose your (and your contacts’) Signal content to these AI agents, and break our privacy guarantees.” Whittaker admits that Signal’s screenshot-blocking remedy is “incomplete,” as it doesn’t address alternative ways that AI agents can access app data, such as retrieving it via the device file system.
Signal’s app security settings on Android devices will display this message for the screenshot-blocking feature.
Image: Signal
Signal took a similar approach last year to prevent
Microsoft’s controversial Recall feature
from capturing secured chats. The encrypted messaging service says it’s struggling to develop a better solution due to the lack of available information regarding how AI agents are actually accessing application data, and is now calling on Apple, Google, and Microsoft to provide “simple technical tools and accurate documentation” to app developers. Signal also says more tools are needed to prevent third-party agents, like
Meta’s Muse,
from using deep accessibility settings in apps like a “skeleton key” to access application data.
“We all need to recognize that the current integration of AI agents into the operating system, without these common-sense remedies, represents an existential threat to privacy at the application layer, and the ability of independent developers like Signal to protect your sensitive data,” said Whittaker. “Signal is the canary in the coal mine, but this affects the entire ecosystem and should galvanize anyone who cares about these fundamental rights.”
