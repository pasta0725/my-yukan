Toyota is looking beyond the automotive industry as it places bigger bets on robotics, physical AI, energy, aerospace, and other emerging technologies. Through its venture arm,
Woven Capital
, the automaker is investing in startups that could help shape what the company looks like decades from now. But as startup valuations climb and competition for promising companies intensifies, figuring out where the next big opportunities lie is becoming increasingly important.
On this episode of TechCrunch’s
⁠Equity⁠
podcast, transportation editor Kirsten Korosec is joined by
⁠Ro Gupta⁠
, head of
⁠Woven Capital⁠
and former founder of autonomous vehicle startup Carmera, to discuss Toyota’s investment strategy, the changing venture capital landscape, and what’s next for emerging technology. Listen to the full episode to hear about:
Why Ro thinks today’s sky-high startup valuations may be more justified than they first appear, and whether there are enough worthwhile companies to warrant the investment.
How the autonomous vehicle boom of 2017 and 2018 compares to today’s AI frenzy, and why future technology hype cycles could move faster.
Why Toyota is investing beyond its traditional automotive business, from robotics and physical AI to energy, aerospace, and manufacturing.
What Woven Capital looks for in startups, why manufacturing and supply chain technology are suddenly attracting attention, and how Toyota’s industrial expertise can give portfolio companies an advantage.
Why corporate venture capital has become more attractive to founders, and how strategic investors can offer more than just money through customers, manufacturing capabilities, and global distribution.
Where the next opportunities in autonomous vehicles are emerging, and why the biggest investments may now be in the infrastructure and operations that make autonomous fleets work at scale.
