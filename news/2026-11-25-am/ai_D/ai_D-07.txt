TITLE: Amazon’s Decart acquisition is a bet on using software to narrow the silicon gap with Nvidia
SITE: TechCrunch AI
PUBLISHED: 2026-11-25 00:51 (page: 2026-11-24T15:51:07+00:00)

TEXT:
Jensen Huang’s comeback as Nvidia’s CEO is understandably making waves, leaving people wondering about the
longer-term implications
of his quick reinstatement and the U.S. government’s active role in his return. But there was another development yesterday that could also matter for Nvidia down the road: Amazon’s decision
to acquire Decart
‘s tech and its team for a reported
$7.5 billion
.
The broader industry seems to agree that Decart’s tech for optimizing AI models to run on non-Nvidia chips is worth several billion dollars. Anthropic reportedly tried to acquire the startup
in the same ballpark
, and Nvidia, which invested in the startup’s $300 million funding round
at a $4 billion valuation
, was also rumored as a prospective buyer.
Amazon was ultimately the one to pull the trigger and bring on board Decart’s co-founders Dean Leitersdorf, Orian Leitersdorf, and Moshe Shalev
and their team
, but the company is not interested in the
world models they had been working on
. At the heart of the deal is the startup’s tech that helps AI models run better on all kinds of chips.
By incorporating the startup’s Decart Optimization Stack (DOS), Amazon is now hoping to extract more performance out of its Trainium chips, as the company tries to turn
AWS into a $1 trillion revenue business
.
In its
announcement
, Amazon said it has worked with Decart since 2025 to optimize the Trainium chips. This deal is ostensibly aimed at accelerating that work, which could eventually result in the company offering its Trainium-based data centers, which Anthropic already uses, to more customers. Amazon has already been
working to make Trainium easier to switch to
.
One way Nvidia managed to leapfrog its competitors was by spending
$6.9 billion
to acquire Israeli chipmaker Mellanox, which eventually grew to become
a core piece of its international manufacturing strategy
. Amazon could well be following a similar path.
Annapurna Labs makes for a more interesting parallel than Mellanox, however, as the latter formed the basis of
Amazon’s custom chip-designing unit
following its
2015 acquisition
. Add
Intel into the mix
, and this becomes a broader story. “Most of the hardware powering the AI wave was built in Israel. Annapurna; Mellanox; Habana Labs; etc…,” VC Eric Reiner
observed
on X a few months ago.
In an email to TechCrunch, Eze Vidra, co-founder and managing partner at Remagine Ventures, said the deal is  “a strong signal for the Israeli deep-tech ecosystem, showing that specialized local talent continues to attract major global players.”
Amazon still has a ways to go before it can make a dent in Nvidia’s near-monopoly on AI chips, but the deal could help the e-commerce giant speed up its efforts. Dean Meyer, a partner at Sequoia who co-led the firm’s seed investment into Decart, said
on X
that the deal “will be looked back on as one of the most strategically important acquisitions in many, many years.”
