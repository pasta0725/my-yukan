An Amazon delivery drone that clipped a fiber-optic line before crashing onto a garage roof is now the subject of an investigation by the US Federal Aviation Administration. It’s the latest delivery drone mishap caught on video as Amazon and other companies are ramping up such aerial delivery operations across the United States.
The November 20 incident began with Jim Brennan and his wife, residents of Huntington Woods, Michigan, placing an order for shampoo and being surprised by a notification about the package arriving by drone within seven minutes, according to
WXYZ 7 News Detroit
. After the drone dropped the package into Brennan’s backyard, it lost a propeller when it caught on fiber-optic cable and floundered sideways before getting pinned between the neighbor’s garage roof and some power lines.
“Like a sick bird, it tried to go up, it tried to get out of here,” Brennan told WXYZ 7.
Amazon Prime Air currently operates a fleet of battery-powered
MK30 drones
that can deliver packages weighing up to five pounds and have onboard sense-and-avoid technology to avoid obstacles. However, the drone clearly failed to avoid the thin fiber-optic cable in this case—so hopefully the FAA investigation would take that into account along with Amazon delivery drone procedures.
The FAA
preliminary accident and incident notification
briefly describes the incident as “UAS struck a fiber cable, a residential garage, and became entangled in power lines during takeoff.” That notice was posted on November 23.
The National Transportation Safety Board is also gathering information about the incident but has not yet decided whether to open its own probe,
according to CNBC
. That US government agency holds primary responsibility for investigating aviation accidents and other transportation incidents.
Logan Coomes, the neighbor whose garage was struck by the drone, described an Amazon Prime Air team arriving within 10 minutes and coordinating with Detroit Edison workers to eventually remove the drone by early evening, in an interview with WXYZ 7.
The Amazon Prime Air team also reportedly told Coomes that the company would help with repairs to his garage roof, and gave the homeowner a $100 gift card on the spot. Amazon claimed that this was the first such delivery drone crash from its operations near Detroit.
However, this would also add to the handful of Amazon drone incidents already under investigation by the US government. In October 2025, two Amazon delivery drones
crashed into a construction crane
in Tolleson, Arizona, while another delivery drone
downed an Internet cable
in Waco, Texas, the following month.
The FAA has also been investigating an Amazon Prime Air drone crash
involving an apartment building
in Richardson, Texas, that occurred in February 2026.
In response to the latest crash in Michigan, an Amazon spokesperson described Prime Air drones as making nearly 6,000 flights per day and already having delivered “hundreds of thousands of packages this year” in a
statement shared with CNN
.
Amazon has been growing its Prime Air service with the goal of having delivery drones
reach nearly 500 cities and towns
across the United States by the end of 2026. The company has described delivery drones as enabling faster deliveries through potentially lower-emission and lower-cost operations compared with relying solely on ground vehicles.
But the growth of delivery drone services has also drawn noise complaints from people living in neighborhoods where drones operate. As Sean Gallagher has written for Ars, the
constant buzz of drones could even trigger post-traumatic stress disorder
or other anxiety-related disorders—especially for people who have directly or indirectly experienced some aspect of modern drone warfare.
