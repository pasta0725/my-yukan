In separate but similar lawsuits filed on Monday,
Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield
and
Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones
accused SpaceX of violating state consumer protection laws by ignoring industry-standard safeguards and falsely promoting the safety of its X social media platform and Grok AI features. The
complaint filed in Virginia
accuses Musk of willfully ignoring growing concerns around AI-generated sexualized deepfakes, and “instead saw a market opportunity for more profits,” describing the weak safeguards around Grok as “not a failure but a feature.”
“Time and again, xAI and X told consumers they were being protected from abuses. That was false. Instead, the tool permitted users to routinely create and circulate non-consensual intimate imagery and sexualized images, and child sexual abuse material (CSAM), causing harm to individuals and children,”
Jones said in a press statement
. “Companies that deploy powerful tools don’t get to ignore consumer safety and call it innovation.”

The complaints largely center around Grok Imagine, a tool that allows Grok and X users to generate images and videos. In August 2025, Grok maker xAI (which Musk has
since merged with SpaceX
) launched
a “spicy” mode for Grok Imagine
that spat out sexualized content including
nude deepfakes
of celebrities like Taylor Swift. In January 2026, Grok gained a feature that
allowed X users to instantly edit any image
without requiring consent from the original poster — resulting in X becoming flooded with nonconsensual, sexualized deepfakes of
adults and minors
.
xAI made several changes to restrict access to Grok image editing, moving some capabilities
behind a paywall
and blocking
certain requests
for sexual poses, swimwear, or explicit scenarios. Our testing at
the Verge
following these restriction updates found they did little to prevent the bot from creating sexualized deepfakes, however.
Both of the Oregon and Virginia lawsuits are seeking financial damages.
The
X Safety team
said in a statement released after the lawsuits were announced that it takes appropriate measures to address users who generate sexual content involving minors:
“The people who tried to use Grok Imagine to generate this material did so by deliberately violating our Terms of Service and working aggressively to circumvent our safeguards. When some of these bad actors found ways to misuse Grok Imagine earlier this year, we acted promptly to tighten its protections, which are designed to refuse any request to generate explicit images of minors or real people. To stay ahead of this disgusting and indefensible conduct, we keep testing and adjusting those protections as these abusers change tactics. We also implement sophisticated classifiers that screen every prompt before an image is generated and check every output after.”
