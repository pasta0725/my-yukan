The Ukrainian Film Academy, With the Support of Netflix, Marks Five Years of Support for Ukrainian Filmmakers
Social Impact
13 November 2026
Global
Ukraine
Link copied to clipboard
The Ukrainian Film Academy, supported by Netflix, has marked the fifth anniversary of their joint support programmes for Ukrainian filmmakers. On 5 November, programme alumni and partners gathered for an online session dedicated to celebrating the local industry and its talent. At its centre was a conversation with Volodymyr and Anna Yatsenko, producers and co-founders of ForeFilms, whose projects have received grants within the support programme eight times. The conversation was led by Scarlett Lacey, Emmy Award winner, Netflix Creative Consultant and Executive Producer of
The Empress
.
Since 2022, over 1,200 creative industry professionals have taken part in the programmes. Over this period, filmmakers have received over 180 grants, more than 250 participants have received tailored training, over 45 film projects have received development support, from which over 30 went on to be pitched at international festivals and 17 received awards. Alumni projects have reached Cannes, Venice, Berlin and Toronto, and documentary film
Traces
has been selected as Ukraine’s official submission for the 99th Academy Awards (Oscars) for Best International Feature Film.
During the conversation, the producers reflected on what the programme meant for the industry in the first months of the full-scale invasion. When Ukraine’s film support system came to a halt, the grants gave filmmakers hope and the opportunity to rethink projects conceived before the invasion, together with international experts. For screenwriters who had lost their work, the support became a lifeline and allowed them to carry on.
Volodymyr and Anna Yatsenko also spoke about how Ukrainian cinema changed over the past five years. At the start of the war, films mostly documented evidence and portrayed Ukraine as a victim. Today, they believe fiction films should focus on people and relationships and approach the war indirectly. “All great war stories are essentially anti-war. At their heart they are about people, their humanity and resilience” Volodymyr said.
They identified reaching European audiences as one of the industry’s biggest challenges. “Our biggest challenge is to become a true member of the European film community: to tell stories that European audiences see not as something exotic, but as stories of other Europeans who are living in slightly different circumstances,” said Anna and Volodymyr Yatsenko.
In an inspiring call to creatives, the two producers emphasised the value of building enduring relationships: “Find your people, and nurture those connections over time. Generosity, honesty and openness are the foundation of lasting creative relationships.”
The impact of the filmmaker support programmes can be seen in specific films:
Traces
by Alisa Kovalenko and Marysia Nikitiuk
is a documentary about Ukrainian women who survived sexual violence during the war. In 2026, it screened in the Berlinale Panorama and was selected as Ukraine’s submission for the Academy Awards for Best International Feature Film.
U Are the Universe
by Pavlo Ostrikov
received a post-production grant in 2023. The film had its world premiere at the Toronto International Film Festival in 2024. In 2026, it won 10 Golden Dzyga national film awards, including Best Film, Best Director and Best Screenplay. The Best Film award went to its producers, Anna and Volodymyr Yatsenko.
Noah
by Marysia Nikitiuk
received a screenwriting grant in 2024. The project went on to secure production funding from the European Solidarity Fund for Ukrainian Films and was selected for KVIFF Central Stage in Karlovy Vary. It then reached the pitching programme of the 2026 Odesa International Film Festival, where it won support from the BRIGHT POINT Skalyha Foundation and additional promotional support from Kyivstar TV. The second instalment of the planned trilogy has also received a development grant.
Songs of Slow Burning Earth
by Olha Zhurba
was selected for the Venice International Film Festival.
Militantropos
by Yelyzaveta Smith, Alina Gorlova and Simon Mozgovyi
was selected for the Directors’ Fortnight in Cannes.
“Over the past five years, the programmes we run together with Netflix have grown far beyond grants alone, adding training, mentoring and access to international experts. They have given Ukrainian filmmakers the opportunity to continue working, to develop their projects, find international partners, and present Ukrainian films on the world’s leading stages. For us, this is an example of how support at a moment of crisis can gradually evolve into a long-term system for developing the entire industry,” said Anna Machukh, Executive Director of the Ukrainian Film Academy.
“Ukrainian cinema needs consistent support to grow: funding, training, professional connections and the ability to plan the next project. The partnership between the Ukrainian Film Academy and Netflix brings these opportunities together. It helps keep professional teams together during the war and creates the conditions for new stories to emerge. We value and thank our partners who invest in the talent of Ukrainian filmmakers and the future of our film industry,” said Tetyana Berezhna, Deputy Prime Minister for Humanitarian Policy – Minister of Culture of Ukraine.
The latest joint programme of the Ukrainian Film Academy and Netflix focuses on director-driven feature-length fiction films. This is one of the slowest-developing segments of the industry, yet it shapes the international reputation of Ukrainian cinema. In 2026, 15 projects received script development grants of $15,000 each, along with mentoring and script doctoring sessions with experts from EAVE.
About the programmes.
Since 2022, Netflix and the Ukrainian Film Academy have supported Ukrainian filmmakers who have been affected by the war or forced to leave the country. Over this period, five grant initiatives have run, offering script development and post-production grants, stipends, educational programmes and mentoring by international experts. House of Europe, EAVE (European Audiovisual Entrepreneurs) and the New York Film Academy have partnered on individual programmes.
About Netflix.
Netflix is one of the world’s leading entertainment services, with over 325 million paid memberships in over 190 countries enjoying TV series, films and games across a wide variety of genres and languages. In Ukraine, Netflix has supported filmmakers affected by the war since 2022 as part of its global talent development initiative.
About the Ukrainian Film Academy.
The Ukrainian Film Academy was founded in 2016 on the initiative of the Odesa International Film Festival to support the development of Ukrainian cinema and its film industry. The Academy presents the annual Golden Dzyga national film awards. Since 2022, it has run support programmes for Ukrainian filmmakers together with Netflix, combining grants, training and mentoring.
