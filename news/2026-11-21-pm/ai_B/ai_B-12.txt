アンソロピック上場、時価総額1.27兆ドル 「AIによるリスクは避けられる」
臼田勤哉
2026年11月20日 15:47
Anthropicは19日(米国時間)、米国NASDAQ市場に上場した。ティッカーシンボル(銘柄コード)は「ANT」。終値は97.17ドルと公開価格の83ドルを上回り、時価総額は1.27兆ドルとなった。
Anthropic(Google Finance)
上場にあわせて、ダリオ・アモデイCEOがNASDAQ市場で挨拶。同社のXでは、スピーチと同文の声明も投稿されている。
声明では、「この時代で最も強力な技術は人類の利益のために開発されるべきだという信念のもと、Anthropicを設立した」とし、アモディ氏は、数年後には治療可能になる病気で父親を亡くしたことに触れ、AIにより、科学と医学の進歩を大きく加速できると強調している。
医療に加え、経済成長の促進、貧困からの脱却、民主主義と自由な社会の保護、日常の行政サービスの改善などに活用できることにも言及。AIによる「リスク」についても、「個人や国家による技術の悪用から、経済の混乱、AIシステムの制御不能にまで多岐にわたる」としながらも、「避けられると考えているからこそ議論している。避けるかどうかは、AIを開発する人々の選択にかかっている」とし、安全性と社会への利益にこだわることがAnthropicのDNAだとしている。
そのため、AIシステムが安全かつ、人間の制御下に置かれ続けることに確信を持てるよう、「必要なだけの時間をかけ、訓練と導入のペースを調整する」とし、ビジネス上の圧力や政治的な圧力に屈せずに、「正しいことをする」と強調している。
また、「機械の知能(machine intelligence)の登場は、人類の歴史における画期的な瞬間の一つであり、我々はそれをより良いものに形づくる機会を得ている。この瞬間は、どの一社、どの一国よりも大きなもの。この技術が我々と、これから続く多くの世代にとって良いものとなるよう、私たち全員が力を合わせて取り組む必要がある。今日から、この使命に加わってくださることを願っている」と締めくくっている。
Remarks from Anthropic CEO @DarioAmodei on our initial public offering:  My co-founders and I started Anthropic because we believe that the most powerful technology of our time should be built for the benefit of humankind.  Daniela and I lost our father to a disease that, just a few years later, became curable. I started my career in biology, hoping to help future cures come sooner. In the lab, I saw the vast thicket of complexity that scientists face when they’re trying to understand disease and develop new treatments.  So many of the scientific tools that can cut through that thicket—tools for gene editing, gene synthesis, controlling brain cells with light—started as pure ideas. In other words, they came from thinking. From intelligence.  Human intelligence has already made diseases like plague, smallpox, and polio history for most of the world. But progress on other diseases has been too slow for so many patients and their families. Going from a great idea to a treatment can take decades: it took many years from the first discovery of a strange repeating pattern in DNA to the approval of the first treatment for a disease using CRISPR.  Nature is full of these patterns. But we’ve had too little intelligence to decipher most of them; too few scientists, with too little time. Now we can build that intelligence. Whether it’s protein folding, drug design, cancer genetics, or clinical trial administration, scientists across many areas of biology and medicine are finding Claude can help them do their research in a fraction of the time it would’ve taken just a couple of years ago. I believe sufficiently powerful AI, working in concert with humans, could compress a century of scientific and medical progress into a decade.  I don’t want to get ahead of myself. At this point all we have are early results. Experiments and clinical trials will still take time. And although they involve slow and ponderous processes, I believe those processes can be substantially accelerated by AI. We hope that in the future fewer families miss a cure by just a few years.  Medicine is just a particularly vivid example, but there are many others. Our customers—the world’s most ambitious companies, nonprofits, and individual people—are already applying the intelligence we’ve built to stimulate economic growth; lift people out of poverty; protect democracy and free societies; even make everyday services like the DMV work better. We’re proud to work alongside them. And I’m extremely optimistic about what we can do together in the future.  At the same time, we have always been honest that this technology has very real risks. These range from misuse of the technology by individuals or nation-states, to economic disruption, to losing control of AI systems. We discuss these risks not because we think they are inevitable, but because we think they are avoidable.  Avoiding them depends on the choices made by the people building AI. There is a gigantic difference between how AI will go if we pay little attention to safety, versus how it will go if we are obsessed with safety. And an obsession with safety and with public benefit has been woven into Anthropic’s DNA from the start. From safeguards against biological and cyber risks, to alignment training and testing, to looking inside our models like an X-ray, Anthropic has pioneered methods for understanding and steering the behavior of AI systems.  There is much work still to be done, and the most difficult safety work lies ahead of us. The incredible talent of the company that has enabled us to build the most capable AI systems will need to be directed, even more so than it has already, towards double- and triple-checking that those AI systems are safe and secure.  And to state it unequivocally, we will pace the training and deployment of AI systems, taking as long as is necessary to have confidence that they are safe and will remain under human control. We will not bend to either business or political pressure. Today puts the company in a stronger financial position, and in doing so only strengthens our resolve to do the right thing.  If we can show it’s possible to make the right choices on safety and still build an incredibly successful company, others in our industry will be inspired to do the same. We call this a race to the top. When the race to the top is working properly, it doesn’t matter which company has the best models—everyone wins because everyone is building safely. The race to the top works best when driven from the front of the pack, and we fully hope and expect to do so, but the goal is to make the whole industry better, regardless of whether Anthropic is winning or losing at any given time.  The advent of machine intelligence is one of the seminal moments in human history, and we have a rare opportunity to shape it for the better.  This moment is bigger than any one company, or any one country. It will take all of us, working together, to ensure this technology goes well for us and the many generations to come. Starting today, we hope that you’ll join us on this mission.
