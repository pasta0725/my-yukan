Nvidia has stopped sending its advanced AI chips to China despite the approval it received from the White House to do so
in December last year
. The announcement was made by interim Nvidia CEO Colette Kress during an
earnings call on Tuesday
, marking a notable shift for the company that, under former CEO Jensen Huang, fought for the right to ship its powerful Hopper architecture
H200 chips
to China.
“Our guidance ⁠does not have any licensed Hopper data center products included, and we are no longer shipping ​licenced Hopper data center products to China,”
Kress said
. She did not explain why Nvidia was ceasing its H200 processor shipments or clarify whether Nvidia would pursue other business with China in the future.
The pivot comes after
Huang stepped down
from his position as Nvidia CEO last week following reports of multiple extramarital affairs, one with a woman who was suspected by US intelligence to be
a Chinese spy
. Nvidia already faced difficulties around offloading the AI chips prior to this scandal, however. Concerns were raised about
failings to properly vet Chinese companies
that were approved to handle the H200 AI chips.
Reuters
also reported earlier this month that around 600,000 ⁠H200 chips ​were stuck in Hong Kong after Beijing withheld approval for large-scale imports, with China seeking to reduce its dependency on US technology.
