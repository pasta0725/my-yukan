Instagram is filled with people posting deepfaked content — including, as of late, from one of Meta’s most prominent executives.
Earlier in November, Alexandr Wang, Meta’s chief AI officer, shared a short clip to promote Muse, the company’s consumer AI agent. In the AI-generated clip, Wang, Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, and Jolly, the anthropomorphic mascot of Muse, are on a four-way video call. When Altman joins the call he’s dressed like Jolly, wearing a furry hooded suit and face paint to look like the cutesy character. The clip is captioned “When the homies meet about openai product strategy,” a reference to the AI agent race and the recent popularity of Muse.
Just a few years ago, AI content portraying real people led to strong public statements rebuking the use of the technology to imitate humans. In some cases legal action
was threatened
. Now, AI content has become part of the texture of a new reality, especially on social media. Leading up to the midterm elections, for example, political groups
ran millions of dollars’ worth of AI-generated ads
attacking opponents.
Wang has been doing a lot of what some have
described
as “anti-PR” to promote Muse, tweeting hundreds of times and
referring
to more traditional marketers as “corposlops.” Still, a major tech executive posting an AI video of a competitor is notable, especially on a platform that’s been criticized recently over AI-generated content. After an AI-generated image of actress Mia Goth gained traction in October, the
Pearl
star’s representative
told
The Verge
it was “incomprehensible” that Instagram allowed the image to spread instead of focusing on the “protection and integrity of a public figure’s likeness.” Other celebrities, including Scarlett Johansson, are
regularly the subject
of sexualized AI imagery; Meta has removed some accounts in the past that it found to be posing as someone else and using their likeness.
Meta requires AI-generated videos depicting people to be labeled as AI, like Wang’s post is. Static images, however, have no such requirement, meaning the AI image of Goth had no disclosure. To make matters worse, the image was shared by a fan account with a blue verification badge given by Instagram.
Reached for comment, Meta spokesperson Tracy Clayton noted that Wang’s post is labeled as AI content. Clayton also pointed out that Altman has made his likeness available for AI content generation, as when OpenAI launched its Sora app (and it
was quickly flooded
with fake Altman videos). OpenAI didn’t respond to requests for comment.
