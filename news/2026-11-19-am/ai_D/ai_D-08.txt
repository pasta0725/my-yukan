TITLE: Nvidia’s startup stakes ballooned to $65B, but Huang’s exit puts future deals in doubt

PUBLISHED: 2026-11-18 10:44 (page: 2026-11-18T01:44:21+00:00)

Nvidia’s equity in private companies continues to grow: It revealed its stakes are now worth $65.5 billion
on Tuesday
, up from the $22 billion reported in January. The increase includes both new investments and significant markups of existing portfolio companies.
The company invested $16 billion into equities in the past quarter alone, it said, and $58 billion in the past nine months of its fiscal year. This amount includes buying shares in public and private companies, yet it shows how the chipmaker made massive bets over the course of the year, especially
compared to last year
; in November 2025, the company reported spending $5 billion buying equity in the first nine months of that fiscal year.
But it’s unclear if this growth will continue under the interim CEO and Jensen Huang’s successor.
Nvidia
stated
in late 2023 that the goal of its corporate investing is to expand the AI ecosystem by backing startups it considers to be “game changers and market makers.”  Over the nearly three years that followed, the chipmaker has invested in nearly 350 startups, according to PitchBook. The companies Nvidia backed include Anthropic and OpenAI; neolabs like Thinking Machines and Reflection; neoclouds, like Lambda, Nscale, and Crusoe; as well as other companies developing AI infrastructure, robotics startups, and applications.
It’s also become a big financier of the data center build-out, reporting $118 billion in guarantees with $105 billion of that the backstop it supplied for OpenAI’s $500 billion data center in Ohio.
The company made those commitments by allocating a meaningful portion of its excess cash flows. In Tuesday’s earnings call, Kress said Nvidia would continue providing financing assistance to strong customers and that “as the market matures and additional sources of capital become available,” the company would be “rigorously evaluating future investments, commitments, and guarantees.”
But the question for the VC ecosystem is whether the company will continue to back startups at anywhere near the same level without Huang at the helm.
Huang’s rampant dealmaking has reportedly been a source of concern for Kress. Investors have told TechCrunch that they anticipate she will take a more conservative approach to startup investing, becoming more selective with new investments and writing smaller-sized checks into its existing portfolio companies.
“We intend to return all excess free cash flow net of strategic uses to shareholders during this last quarter and going forward,” Kress said on the earnings call on Tuesday. Just how strategic startups will be under her leadership, and under the future permanent CEO, remains to be seen. But for now,
VCs are bracing
for a meaningful pullback in capital.
