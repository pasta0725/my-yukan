OpenAI CEO Sam Altman is once again addressing his controversial comments on PC RAM purchases. “I want to be clear, I want people to have a lot of RAM, I think everybody should be able to buy a lot of RAM for their own computer,” Altman said today. “I think the home tinkering is great.”
The comments were
made onstage
at Semafor’s
Silicon Valley & The World
summit on Monday.
Altman’s remarks on RAM stirred up controversy last month. In an appearance
on the
Cheeky Pint
podcast
, Altman said that it’s “quite terrible” for everyone to have RAM in personal computers rather than in data centers. He later followed up on those comments in posts on X
earlier this month
, saying, “i want you to have so much ram” and saying the original remarks had been “taken out of context.”
AI companies have been buying up the world’s memory supply for data centers, leading to ongoing price hikes on everything from
phones
to
game consoles
. Altman commented on supply chain issues, saying OpenAI has “been constrained by different things at different times,” like memory, rack assembly, data center capacity, or “some random component.”
Onstage today, Altman went on to say that “a few years ago we started saying the world needs to build a lot more memory supply chain in particular.” He acknowledged how
RAM prices have soared
over recent months, saying:
“The world did not build out that much, and now we have seen DRAM prices — I don’t know exactly, but maybe they went up 5x, 7x, something like that. I think it would be much better if the world just had a lot of memory. That comment, which I think got misinterpreted, but there is a true part of it, which I will stand by is that, if we’re going to be in this memory supply shortage, then in some sense — whether or not we’ll decide to do it, because I think there are other tradeoffs like people having their own at-home inference — the optimal thing would be, can we share this memory and have it working 100 percent of the time rather than sometimes off in someone’s edge device? Again, optimal use of this limited supply. Maybe it’s not the reason to make the decision but that’s what I was trying to say.”
Altman also
commented on Jensen Huang’s resignation
as Nvidia’s CEO, saying he “doesn’t know enough” to say if Huang should be reinstated.
