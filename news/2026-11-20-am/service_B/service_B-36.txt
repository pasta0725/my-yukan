Netflix has long delivered Japanese unscripted series that capture raw human emotion and unpredictable moments, often surprising even their own creators. The slate spans dating reality shows such as
Badly in Love
,
Love Village
and
The Boyfriend
; comedy titles such as
Last One Standing
,
Love Village
and
Kiss or Die
; and competition series such as
Final Draft
.
Both the genre range and the audience have grown fast: global viewing hours for Japanese unscripted titles increased by 3.5 times from 2021 to 2025.
The Boyfriend
Season 2 won Best Non-English LGBTQ TV Show at the 17th Dorian TV Awards, hosted by GALECA: The Society of LGBTQ Entertainment Critics.
Badly in Love
Season 1 took home Best Reality & Variety at the Global OTT Awards 2026, one of Asia’s largest streaming content showcases. Locally, memorable lines from the shows have gone on to become some of Japan’s buzzwords of the year, sparking widespread conversation.
Netflix’s early years in Japan centered on exclusive early access to existing hits. Since 2022, the focus has shifted toward original titles developed and produced in-house, expanding across dating, reality, comedy, survival and talk shows. Netflix released five new Japanese unscripted titles in each of 2025 and 2026.
To bring that growing unscripted slate to a wider audience, Netflix is launching the
#ネトフリでアンスク
campaign this week, with a range of programming for fans to dive into. Today, November 17, Netflix is announcing its latest lineup, including new titles, and more announcements still to come.
Netflix’s goal isn’t simply to repeat today’s popular formats, but to expand what ‘unscripted’ can be by embracing new themes, new people and new creators. By creating unscripted stories rooted in Japan that resonate with people globally, Netflix will continue to take on new challenges and grow the unscripted format as a new kind of culture.
