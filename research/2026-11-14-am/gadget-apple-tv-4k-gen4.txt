best streaming devices
 generally don’t get updated all that frequently; there’s not too much that changes year-to-year. It’s been four years since Apple released its last device, but now it’s back with the Apple TV 4K (4th gen).
This fourth-gen streamer has the company’s newest A19 processor — the same as in the iPhone 17 — which means it’s not only faster, but has access to the new-and-improved Siri, which in theory should mean that it’s easier for you to find shows to watch. And, you can also create a fully immersive surround-sound setup using HomePod smart speakers. But, the Apple TV 4K also comes with an exorbitantly high $299. Yes, it’s a nicer setup all around, but it’s hard to justify the price if you’re not in the Apple ecosystem.

Setup was a cinch; once I plugged in the Apple TV 4K, all I had to do was bring my iPhone near the device, and I was good to go within minutes. The Apple TV 4K even remembered all my installed apps from my previous Apple TV device. However, I did have to re-login to every app. If you have a lot of streaming services, this can get a bit tedious, no matter how easy they make it with QR codes.
Where’d my remote go?
(Image credit: Future)
One of the nicest upgrades is the new FindMy built into the remote; Apple’s remotes have long been easy to lose in the couch. You can use any iPhone to help locate the device and even have it play a little tone, but if you have an iPhone with Bluetooth 6 (the iPhone 17 or later), you can get super-precise location tracking.
I tried this with both an iPhone 14 Pro and an iPhone Air; with the 14 Pro, FindMy knew when I was close, but told me to look around. With the Air, the phone started buzzing more urgently the closer I got, and even gave me distance estimates. Finally, when I was within a few inches, it said “here.”
One thing that’s still missing from Apple’s remote though: backlit buttons.
Siri: Still a work in progress
(Image credit: Future)
Another benefit of the A19 chip in the Apple TV 4K (4th generation) is that it supports Apple Intelligence, which can be used for, among other things, finding what to watch. However, in my testing, it could use some work, especially when I compared it with Gemini on the Google TV Streamer and Alexa+ on the 
Fire TV Stick 4K
.
My first stab with Siri was “Find me scary movies for kids.” I got a response saying “I’m not finding anything for that,” and offered to let me search YouTube.
(Image credit: Future)
I next tried “Find scary movies good for kids,” which did bring up some options, but I wouldn’t call “Poltergeist,” “Alien,” or “Seven” appropriate for my 8-year-old. I don't want her to know what's in the box just yet.
When I performed a similar search on the 
Google TV Streamer
 and on the Fire TV, I received much more relevant movies, such as “Coraline,” “Goosebumps 2,” “Hocus Pocus,” and “The Addams Family.”
I tried the same search another day on the Apple TV, and I got a few better suggestions, such as “Gremlins” and “Arachnophobia,” but the results still fell well short of what I hoped.
(Image credit: Future)
“What’s that movie with Audrey Hepburn that takes place in Rome” just returned a bunch of Christmas movies, none with Hepburn. On the Fire TV, Alexa+ brought up “Roman Holiday” in an instant, along with suggestions for other Hepburn movies.
Siri performed better when I asked it to “find action movies with Tom Hanks.” “Saving Private Ryan,” “Captain Philips,” “Angels & Demons,” and “Greyhound” were listed as the first few options.
Siri’s responses appear as a scrollable column along the right side of the screen, but there’s no audio reply. By comparison, on the Google TV Streamer, Gemini delivers the list, as well as a bit of dialogue, which makes everything feel friendlier overall.
Siri seemed to have a blind spot when it came to Netflix. I searched directly for “Top Gun: Maverick,” but Siri only showed that it was available through Paramount, when in fact you could also stream it on Netflix. Similarly, when I asked Siri for “The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo,” it only gave me options to buy or rent the movie, even though it was free to stream on Netflix.
A search for “KPop: Demon Hunters” was also fruitless. I either got a list of other movies with “demon hunters” in the title, or received this response: “I don’t have the answer for that, but I can search the web if you ask on your iPhone.” Siri was, however, able to find “The Queen’s Gambit” and “Love is Blind.”
(Image credit: Future)
Other things worked mostly as expected. “Turn off subtitles” worked within Apple TV, but not within the Netflix app. “Show my video Doorbell” brought up a live view from my doorbell’s camera. I could even place it in a small window in the corner while I watched other programs.
Interface and performance
One of the best things in the Apple TV 4K’s favor is its far, far cleaner interface when compared with devices like the Fire TV, which are just littered with ads and sponsored content.
However, I wish it would go a little further than simply displaying a grid of apps; I do like how, on the Google TV Streamer and Fire TV, they both display a row of movies and shows currently airing, things I’ve recently been watching, and recommendations based on my viewing habits; it makes it easier to resume a binge session, and makes discovering new content a little easier.
The Apple TV 4K (4th generation) definitely felt a little snappier than the older model; while I didn’t time it, apps seemed to open faster.
Impressive surround sound
Like the previous generation Apple TV 4K, the fourth-gen model lets you wirelessly connect Apple HomePods to use as an audio output. But, while the older model only let you connect two speakers, the fourth-gen Apple TV 4K lets you connect four, so you can have two front channels and two rear channels, for true surround sound.
The one caveat is that each pair has to match - that is, you need to have two HomePods or two Homepod minis as front channels. If you go the HomePod Mini route, you’ll spending around $600; I had two HomePod 2s as my front channels, and two HomePod Mini 2’s as my rears, which would mean an investment of just under $1,000. At that price, you may as well look at one of the 
best soundbars
.
Amazon offers a similar feature with its streaming sticks, in that you can arrange a number of Echo Dots or Echo Studios to create a surround-sound system. I tried it with four Echo Studios, and it was pretty impressive.
Bottom line
(Image credit: Future)
The Apple TV 4K (4th generation) has some incremental upgrades over the previous model, but like I found with the HomeView, Siri as an AI assistant falls short of its rivals in Gemini and Alexa+.
In an era where there’s more movies and shows than ever to watch, an AI that’s capable of digging up something catered towards your tastes is essential. While I’m sure that Siri will improve over time, right now, you’re better off with a less expensive streaming stick, even if you do have to put up with some ads. The 
Roku Ultra
, for example, costs half as much, yet offers support for Dolby Atmos and Dolby Vision.
One argument in the Apple TV 4K's favor is that it has 128GB of storage, which is far more than you get with other premium devices like the 
Google TV Streamer
 (32GB) and 
Fire TV Cube
 (16GB). But how many of us are still downloading movies?
The Apple TV 4K has some necessary upgrades, but until Siri gets better, it’s not a necessary upgrade.

The 
Apple
 TV

 (2026) has been a strange product to review. I’ve been an Apple TV user since the Apple TV 3rd Gen, released in 2012 — in over a decade of sticking with it, I upgraded to the first 4K version, and the 2021 4K version with the better remote. I skipped the 
Apple TV 4K 2022 
version, since the upgrades were minimal.

Its picture quality is best-in-class, its format support is excellent, and it offers more privacy protection (and an ad-free home screen) than its rivals from the likes of 
Roku
, 
Amazon
 and

 — even though it’s more expensive than those models. The premium was worth paying.
But the premium has changed. The 2022 model launched at $129 / £149 / AU$219 as the starting price. In the middle of 2026, the price rose to $199 / £249 / AU$399. Now the new model is $299 / £299 / AU$449.

Let’s start with the basics. The box runs tvOS, which is software specific to these devices, and is a spin-off from the iOS software used on iPhones. As a result, the Apple TV 4K is more versatile than most other streaming boxes. Yes, you have all the big streaming apps, but you also have practical apps that might be useful for some people — though obviously 99% of people will be focused on streaming.
The Apple TV 4K (2026) also supports games, in the sense that it works like a mini console, and you can connect games controllers to it (including the official PlayStation and Xbox controllers). This all works well enough, and there are some pretty decent games on there… though there are no game streaming services, due to restrictions in Apple’s policies, and I think this is a huge miss here in 2026.
We didn’t mind this too much when Apple released the last version, but cloud gaming has only grown in that time — and with the price of consoles and PCs spiking too, it’s becoming an increasingly tempting option.
It’s one of the slicker bits of streaming software in terms of performance, opening apps super-quickly and generally feeling perfectly smooth to move around. It does have its foibles — rearranging apps is theoretically an intuitive process, and yet suddenly this is where performance became less predictable. But I would say it’s generally the least annoying of the streaming systems.
That’s partly because it doesn’t have the same aggressive advertising right on the home screen that all the other platforms do. When you highlight a particular app, it might show recommendations from the app at the top of the screen, but you never have shows pushed on the home screen unless you’re already hovering over the app.
Apple also has, predictably, included some excellent integrations with its own products. The most useful is easily the ability to share passwords saved on your iPhone's Passwords app instantly with the Apple TV when you try to log into a service or app, though the ability to your iPhone's on-screen keyboard to search or type information in is also excellent.
There are lots of other integrations as well, some of which will be more useful than others to individuals. You can see your 
iCloud
 photos and use them as a screensaver (though the Apple TV's beautiful built-in screensavers are one of its highlights). You can use your iPhone for FaceTime calls, where you see other people on the big screen, and your iPhone camera beams your image over to the TV and then to the person you're calling. You can beam audio or video over to the box from your phone using AirPlay.

The remote that comes with the latest Apple TV is relatively easy to use. You don’t have lots of buttons for directly launching streaming services, for better or worse — this is, let’s be clear, a form of advertising of its own, but it’s one that can be pretty useful. That said, there is a button that looks like a TV, and pressing this once launches the Apple TV app, which includes the Apple TV streaming service’s wares — but it’s also a hub for things you’ve been watching in other services, so it’s not purely a play to get you to watch Apple’s own stuff.
Unfortunately, the Apple TV hub doesn’t work with every major streaming app, with Netflix being the most egregious omission. Netflix also doesn’t work with Siri’s ability to play movies or the universal search function. So it’s possible to ask Siri to find a particular movie, the system will tell you which of your apps have it so you can watch it right away… except you have to check Netflix separately.
There’s a button on the side of the remote to invoke Siri if you want to use your voice to find and control things, but Apple has now also added microphones to the Apple TV box itself, so you can — in theory — use the device hands-free. Just say ‘Siri’ and ask what you want to ask.
(Image credit: Future)
And the reason Apple has included these new mics is because Siri has now been upgraded to Siri AI, and the company is surely hoping you’ll want to use Siri way more now that it’s smart. The problem is that while Siri itself may have become smart, its implementation often still isn’t.
The most obvious gripe for me was the information presentation, and the density of that information. Sometimes it’s too crowded, packing paragraphs of information with no formatting to aid readability into a small window on the side of the screen. At other times, it’s too simple, taking over the full screen yet displaying text so large that only a few words fit in before the cut off (and if you click the remote hoping it will open the rest of the text, it doesn’t — a different part of the screen is selected by default). It’s always too much or not enough, and it’s somehow never just right.
There's text I can't see, yet at least 50% of the screen is empty…

Watching Netflix, it told me it couldn’t see what I was viewing, and asked me to just tell it the name of the movie — but if I paused it, causing the name to appear on screen, and asked the same question, it was now able to see the title. A system that responds so differently per-app is not really a reliable system.
I asked it to identify the music playing in the background of a scene of 
Tony

Tony

It worked better when I watched a documentary about chefs, and the system was able to identify Marco Pierre White instantly when I asked — but that was on a service that used the native player, so I don’t know if that helps Siri have better visibility of what’s on-screen.
Later, I asked the system to show me some good Jason Statham movies available on the streaming services I have. It was able to suggest some movies on the services I have, but there was no visible info on how they were rated, even though the results took up the whole screen. So I asked a follow up question about whether any of them were actually worth watching, and then it showed movies it considered to be good, but 
not
 whether they’re available on my services. So I asked a third time if any of these good movies were available on my services, and it finally narrowed down to a list of what I originally asked… except the first movie on the list wasn’t accessible on any of my streaming apps.

still

And sure, it’s technically in beta still, so Apple might say it should be no surprise that it’s been half-baked — but what are we paying so much more for the elaborate chip inside the new Apple TV 4K for, if not the AI powers? I know that the mics that are always listening will be contentious with people. And while it’s possible to turn this functionality off in the settings, there’s nothing like the switch

I mentioned early on that image quality is one of the most important elements that draws enthusiasts to the Apple TV, and that’s for a few reasons. One is its support for both Dolby Vision and HDR10+ advanced 
HDR
 formats, not to mention the HDR10 and HLG standard formats.
It can also change formats on the fly, including switching to 24fps output for movies, so that you get more natural movie motion, especially if you have a 120Hz TV.
But it also has excellent upscaling of lower-resolution content, better than the vast majority of TVs people own at home. The latest flagship TVs with good neural-network-based upscaling might beat it, but there’s also a lot more mediocre ‘AI upscaling’ in TVs — the Apple TV takes non-4K content and makes it look naturally sharp, consistently, without looking like it’s been messed with.
On top of this, the other factor is that the Apple TV movie store offers basically the highest-quality streaming you can get outside of expensive specialist services such as Kaleidescape. These moves come at a higher bitrate (meaning the amount of data transferred per second) than anything like Netflix or Disney+ offers, so they look cleaner and more detailed, especially in scenes with lots of motion.
And while you can buy movies from the Apple TV store on other devices, the highest bitrate quality is reserved for Apple TV 4K boxes only — and the movies look fantastic. It’s a big part of why I use them to this day. The problem is that these movies look exactly the same as they do on the previous 4K model, which was less than half the price at launch.
The ports on the back of the Apple TV 4K — the HDMI port is HDMI eARC, meaning it can actually receive audio from your TV and beam it over to HomePods

So let’s talk about whether there’s really anything here for upgraders. I already said I think there basically isn’t, but I should at least show my working. When it comes to picture and sound performance, there are no meaningful changes.
Even if I thought Siri AI was a great upgrade, it is coming to the last two generations of Apple TV 4K too, so that isn't a reason in itself. Some people obviously might like the hands-free mics, and the remote that can play a noise so you can find it. The latter is a truly useful upgrade; it just isn’t worth the price if you have one of the previous models.
The new model does have faster Wi-Fi. Again that doesn’t mean much in itself, but it appears to have enabled this version to support a full surround system using HomePods for the first time (seemingly due to lower latency in the wireless tech, based on what Apple's said about it).
You can have four HomePod or HomePod Mini 2nd Gen speakers (two at the front, two at the back), or two HomePod 2nd Gen (at the front) and two HomePod mini 2nd Gen (at the rear). Because these are separate products, I’m not going to factor them into reviewing the Apple TV 4K — however, I have been very impressed with the HomePod 2nd Gen as TV speakers with Apple TV in the past.
The price of getting a setup like these used to be highly competitive too, though after HomePod price rises they’re a little more middle of the pack compared to wireless home theater speaker options from the likes of 
Hisense
 and Sonos.
Ultimately, I'm dropping our score for this model to 3.5 stars from 4.5 stars for its predecessor, and it all comes down to value. Yes, prices are rising everywhere, but I think this Apple TV box has simply fallen outside of the point where I can argue that it's good value despite being more expensive than the competition.

Price

The new Apple TV 4K has been given a price tag of £299 / $299 / AU$449. That’s an increase of £100 / $100 / AU$150 over the entry-level version of the previous model when it was discontinued.

Features
(Image credit: What Hi-Fi?)
Getting the new Apple TV 4K up and running is an absolute doddle. If you have an iPhone, simply hold it close to the streamer, enter the four-digit code displayed on screen, and most of the set-up process takes care of itself. You’ll be asked to read a short phrase aloud if you want to use the far-field microphones, allowing the Apple TV to check that it can hear you clearly.
There’s one important setting to change before you start watching, too. Match Content is switched off by default, so head into the Video And Audio menu and enable both Match Dynamic Range and Match Frame Rate. This ensures that content is played in its original HDR or SDR format, rather than being unnecessarily upconverted, and at the correct frame rate to minimise judder.
Frame-rate matching is offered by most video streamers these days, but it doesn't always work consistently across every app, and that can mean unwanted judder when watching films and TV shows. The Apple TV 4K, however, is exceptionally dependable in this respect, with its Match Content settings working correctly across all of the streaming apps we test. It's a significant advantage, and one that makes a tangible difference to the viewing experience.
App support is, as you’d expect, excellent. Of the 32 apps we check on every video streamer and smart TV, the Apple TV 4K has 26. The six missing apps are all related to either cloud gaming or music streaming: GeForce Now, Amazon Luna, Xbox, 
Qobuz

Dolby Vision

There is one very disappointing exception for UK viewers, though. BBC 
iPlayer

This is a legacy issue for Apple TVs, and it's never been entirely clear whether Apple or the Beeb is more to blame, but most buyers won't care: the upshot is that most devices deliver 
HDR content 
via the iPlayer app, but the Apple TV 4K does not, and for many, that's a significant flaw. Here’s hoping it is, one day, resolved.
As you would hope, the new Apple TV 4K is incredibly snappy in use. Apps open almost instantly, and asking Siri to find one for you produces similarly immediate results. But it’s the new Siri AI experience that represents the biggest change here. It needs to be enabled in the General menu, and is still officially in beta, but it transforms the assistant from a fairly basic voice-control tool into a much more capable, conversational way of finding things to watch.

Instead, it might tell you it can't play the programme, or it will take you to its listing in the Apple TV app, where you'll be invited to buy or rent it. You can ask Siri to open Netflix, of course, but you then have to find the recommended title yourself. Thankfully, the other apps we tested have much deeper integration, allowing Siri to recommend and play content directly.

One neat new addition for the latest Apple TV 4K is the remote's Precision Finding feature, which works through Apple's Find My app to guide you to its hiding place down the back of the sofa. The chirpy little tune it plays at the same time further aids rescue.

Given the Apple TV 4K's fairly mainstream intentions and Blu-ray-ripping's dubious legality, though, this isn't something we're going to punish the device for.
Picture quality
(Image credit: What Hi-Fi?)
The Apple TV 4K delivers a consistently accomplished picture, with a clean, detailed presentation that makes the most of the material it's given. Its performance with native 4K content is particularly refined, but it's the way it handles lower-resolution video that really impresses.
With 
Kiss Kiss Bang Bang
 in HD, the Apple TV 4K does a lovely job of making the most of the available detail. Edges are clean and well defined, while compression artefacts are kept in check. Colour gradients are also smoother, with less of the banding and blockiness that can make older or more heavily compressed material look rather rough. It's a polished, convincing picture given the limitations of the source.
Things get even more impressive when we switch to 
Peep Show
 in SD from 
Disney+
. There's no disguising the fact that this is a low-resolution source, but the Apple TV 4K does a remarkable job of upscaling it. The picture is surprisingly clean and smooth, with refined edges and shading that help make the most of the limited information available. You could almost mistake this for HD.
Comparing the new model directly with its predecessor reveals just how much its processing has improved. The older Apple TV 4K is no slouch itself, and it still does a good job of both HD and SD content, but its successor is consistently cleaner and smoother, particularly around fine edges and subtle changes in shading. The difference isn't night and day, but it's a worthwhile improvement for anyone who regularly watches older or lower-resolution material.
With native 4K HDR content, the differences are much less pronounced. Initially, there's very little to separate the two models, but look more closely, and the new Apple TV 4K reveals some subtle refinements. Shading is handled with a little more nuance, colours have slightly greater subtlety, and edges look fractionally sharper, with a touch more fine detail coming through.
These are small gains, certainly, but they combine to give the new model a slightly richer, more three-dimensional appearance. The previous generation remains an excellent performer with high-quality 4K content, so don't expect a dramatic transformation. But the new Apple TV 4K does squeeze a little more from the best sources, while making a much more convincing job of the less impressive ones.
Sound quality
(Image credit: What Hi-Fi?)
The previous Apple TV 4K is already a very capable source of movie sound, but the new model manages to extract a little more from a film soundtrack. We test through a 
Denon AVC-A1H
 AVR and 
KEF R Meta speaker package
, and the improvements are subtle but worthwhile, with a cleaner, more detailed and dynamically assured presentation.
The post-explosion sequence in 
Deepwater Horizon
, as Mark Wahlberg's character regains consciousness, is a particularly good demonstration. The film's excellent surround effects are even better resolved by the new Apple TV 4K, with individual elements given a little more clarity and definition.
The presentation is also subtly more dynamic, making the scene feel that bit more realistic and doing a better job of drawing you into the action. It's not a night-and-day difference, but there's a tangible improvement in the way the new model communicates the detail and scale of a busy cinematic soundtrack.
That extra sense of precision and energy carries over to music. Listening to 
Marianne
 by Fontaines D.C. in stereo through the 
Denon
 AVC-A1H in Pure Direct mode, the new Apple TV 4K sounds a little more upfront rhythmically and dynamically, giving the track extra drive without upsetting the nicely balanced, effortlessly listenable character of its predecessor.
The central vocal has an appealing airy spaciousness, and when the busy chorus arrives, the new model makes better sense of the arrangement. The older streamer sounds good, too, but its delivery is a little more homogeneous and less likely to get the toe tapping.
There's a worthwhile improvement in the organisation of complex recordings, too. 
Dionysus
 by President has greater separation between its layered vocals, making it easier to pick out the synthetic backing vocals and background growls and screams. There's a touch more weight, too, with extra fullness through the midrange and bass.
The difference is perhaps most obvious with 
Save It For Later
 by Eddie Vedder. There's more depth and richness to the guitar picking and soulful vocals, while Vedder's delivery has greater focus and directness. The performance feels more textured and immediate, with each guitar pick more solidly resolved and the vocals fuller and meatier.
None of these improvements is especially dramatic in isolation, but they add up to a more engaging and accomplished sound. The previous Apple TV 4K remains a very good performer, particularly with movie soundtracks, but the new model offers that bit more insight, precision and richness across the board.
Verdict
(Image credit: What Hi-Fi?)
The new Apple TV 4K doesn't represent a huge leap over its predecessor, but it makes worthwhile improvements to picture and sound quality while adding some genuinely useful features.
Its upscaling of HD and SD content is particularly impressive, while the subtle gains with 4K and the more noticeable improvements to audio make it an even more accomplished all-round performer.
The extra processing power should also help keep the streamer feeling slick for years to come, while making more demanding games run better. The addition of far-field microphones makes Siri AI more convenient, and support for up to four HomePods opens up an intriguing alternative to a traditional home cinema system.
There are still a few frustrations, not least the continued lack of 4K HDR support in BBC iPlayer, and the price has gone up considerably. At £299 / $299 / AU$449, it's far from cheap, and there's little reason for owners of the previous model to rush out and upgrade unless the new features are particularly appealing.
But for anyone buying a video streamer from scratch, the new Apple TV 4K is an excellent choice. The previous model was the best mainstream video streamer we've tested, and this latest generation makes it better still.

Apple、新しいApple TV 4Kを発表
より高速なパフォーマンスと、一段と臨場感あふれるホームシアター構成により、エンターテインメント体験が向上します
カリフォルニア州クパティーノ
 Appleは本日、Apple史上最も臨場感あふれるホームシアター体験を提供する、次世代の
Apple TV 4K
を発表しました。新しいApple TV 4Kは、さらに高速なパフォーマンスを提供し、4台のHomePod mini 2またはHomePod（第2世代）スピーカーと組み合わせると、Apple史上最も臨場感あふれるホームシアターサラウンドサウンド構成が実現します。また、新しいApple TV 4Kは、ホームハブとして、アクセサリとシーンのシームレスなコントロールを提供するため、Apple Homeの管理もかつてないほど簡単になります。
新しいApple TV 4Kは本日より予約注文を開始し、11月13日より販売を開始します。
「新しいApple TV 4Kは、Apple史上最も臨場感あふれるホームシアター体験です。HomePodと組み合わせれば、Apple史上最高のサラウンドサウンド構成を実現でき、Apple Homeのアクセサリの管理もかつてないほど簡単になります。HomeViewやHomePod mini 2とともに、Apple TV 4Kはこれまでに私たちが築いた中で最も連係したApple Homeの一部になります」と、Appleのワールドワイドプロダクトマーケティング担当バイスプレジデントであるボブ・ボーチャーズは述べています。
より速いパフォーマンス、より賢いリモコン
新しいApple TV 4KのA19チップは、前世代と比較して最大50パーセント高速なCPUパフォーマンスと最大2倍高速なGPUパフォーマンスを提供し、より優れた反応速度、より高速なナビゲーション、よりなめらかなアニメーションを実現します。A19チップはまた、新しい専用のAV1デコーダを備え、より少ない帯域幅を使用しながら、より効率の高い高品質なストリーミングを可能にします。
新しいSiri Remoteは、使い慣れたデザインはそのままに、「探す」アプリの「正確な場所を見つける」機能とリモートで音を鳴らすオプションが追加され、一段と探しやすくなります
1
。
Apple史上、最も臨場感あふれるホームシアター
HomePodとApple TV 4Kのペアリングはすでに、設定が簡単で臨場感あふれる視聴体験をユーザーに提供していますが、新しいApple TV 4Kはそれを一段と高めます。今回初めて、ユーザーはApple TV 4K（第4世代）とHomePod mini 2で4スピーカーサラウンドサウンド体験を実現することができます。4台のスピーカーはApple TV 4Kと連係し、それぞれの部屋の中での位置を賢く認識します。これにより、音声のタイミングとレベルが、その空間に合わせて自動的に調整されます。前方と後方のペアごとにモデルが同じであれば、HomePod（第2世代）もこの4スピーカーの構成に使うことができ、究極のオーディオ体験が得られます
2
。
パワフルなホームハブ
新しいApple TV 4Kは引き続きパワフルなホームハブとして機能し、ユーザーはどこからでもスマートホームアクセサリを操作できます
3
。対応するドアベルがある場合、通知がテレビに直接表示されるので、ユーザーは視聴を中断することなく、誰がドアの前にいるのかを確認したり、その人と話をしたり、ドアの解錠といったほかのアクセサリの操作を行ったりすることができます。Apple TV 4Kは、オートメーション、ゲストモード、HomeKitセキュアビデオの安全なローカル処理にも対応するため、ユーザーはさらに多くの方法で自宅とつながることができます。Apple TV 4Kは、内蔵のWi-FiとThreadにより、ユーザーのスマートホームにシームレスに接続してコントロールできます。
リビングルームを楽しむためのより多くの方法
Apple TV 4Kは、観るためだけにあるのではありません。その瞬間にユーザーが何を求めているかに応じてリビングルームを一変させることのできる、家に欠かせない存在です。Apple Fitness+を使えば、リビングルームがワークアウトスタジオになり、Apple ArcadeやApp Storeのゲームライブラリを使えば、リビングルームがゲームセンターに変わります。Apple TVアプリを使うと、スリリングなドラマ、壮大なSF、気分爽快なコメディなど、何百本ものApple Originalの作品をストリーミングでき、さらに「Formula 1®」（米国のみ）や「メジャーリーグサッカー」「フライデーナイト ベースボール」などのスポーツの生中継も視聴できます
4
。また、連係カメラを使ってFaceTime通話をしたり、写真を観たり、ほかにも多くのことを大画面で楽しむことができます
5
