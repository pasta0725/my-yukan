OpenAI
, the United States-based developer of ChatGPT, has pledged 
$2 million
 to research examining how artificial intelligence affects children, following its participation in a child-safety initiative at the Paris Peace Forum in France.
The company 
announced the contribution
 on 
November 12, 2026
, saying it had signed the Charter of Engagement for Safer AI for Children earlier in the week. The money will support a scientific research network under the Coalition for Children’s Rights and Protection in the Age of AI.
Its contribution is intended to help researchers develop ways to measure both risks and opportunities for young people. OpenAI said the work would inform product safeguards and public policy, rather than announcing a new consumer service.
“Research teams retain control over their conclusions, and findings will be published, including negative or inconclusive results,” the company said.
Okay News reports
 that the announcement follows a wider set of commitments at the forum’s Child Summit, held on November 10 and 11. The company’s statement did not give a payment timetable or name individual research grant recipients.
In a 
separate release dated November 10
, the 
Paris Peace Forum
 said AI developers and safety organisations, including OpenAI, Anthropic and Common Sense Media, would collectively pledge 
€8 million
 for research and tools addressing AI’s interaction with child development.
The forum reported a collective pledge in euros, while OpenAI disclosed its own contribution in dollars. The figures should not be added together as separate funding commitments. Neither announcement described the funding as money already paid to researchers.
The summit also promoted a broader Children First Declaration covering health, nutrition, education, protection and wellbeing in the digital age. The forum said participating governments and organisations aimed to deliver results by 2045.
African countries listed among the declaration’s signatories included Kenya, South Africa, Rwanda, Benin, Egypt and Tanzania. Nigeria was not on the organiser’s published list as of November 10.
The forum said the summit would also launch a framework of indicators and practices, alongside a hub connecting public and private organisations working on children’s rights.

OpenAI pledged $2 million to support a scientific research network focused on artificial intelligence and children’s safety, the company 
announced on November 12
. The network is being established under the Coalition for Children’s Rights and Protection in the Age of AI.
The commitment is intended to support shared measures and open-source tools, including classifiers and graders, for technical teams and independent researchers to test and validate. The coalition’s 
Charter of Engagement for Safer AI for Children
 says the tools are meant to make research comparable across products, countries and time. OpenAI said the work could help researchers assess risks and opportunities for young people and inform 
safer products
 and better policy. Those benefits have not yet been demonstrated.
The company said it signed the charter earlier that week at the Paris Peace Forum. The document says the coalition is led by France, Spain and Kenya, involves 10 United Nations agencies, and brings together governments, researchers, civil society and industry.
Under the planned process, companies may run shared measurement tools within their own systems and provide coded results, reducing the need to transfer full conversations. The charter assigns scientific priorities, methods, analysis and conclusions to independent scientific responsibility. It also requires publication of methods and results, including null, negative or inconclusive findings, and says financial or in-kind contributors will not receive a veto over publication.
The charter limits industry funding to the Shared Measurement workstream. It says other parts of the programme—including the Observatory, research on evidence and developmental outcomes, Scientific Committee remuneration, and assessment and standards—must be funded exclusively through public and independent philanthropic sources, with separate accounting. The opened materials do not identify who will administer OpenAI’s payment, when funds will be transferred or when the tools will be delivered.
