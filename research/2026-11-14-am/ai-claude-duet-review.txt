Claude’s Duet agent is a little neurotic and surprisingly helpful
November 13, 2026
Normally, the products I test don’t offer to help me write up my experience with them. Duet is different.
Claude Duet is Anthropic’s AI agent for consumers, the latest attempt by an AI company to try to convince us that agents can be useful for people who don’t write code. It’s been an interesting couple of months: Instinct gave us 
an impressive text-based assistant
, Meta offered us its cute little guy, Muse, 
to help us buy things
, and OpenAI gave us 
the more work-oriented Dots
. Claude Duet is even more focused on work than those. It’s also more consumer-friendly than 
Claude Cowork
, which was more consumer-friendly than Claude Code — the coding agent that 
kicked off a whole hype cycle
 earlier this year. I’ve been testing it over the past week as I navigate a busy travel and work schedule. At turns it has been truly useful, kind of a nag, and very eager to write about itself.
Duet is still a “research preview” according to Anthropic, and access is limited to Pro, Max, and Ultra subscribers in the US, with a waitlist to join. It’s not here to order you burritos, either; it’s for “professionals and small business owners” and is meant to use its judgment about what needs your attention most.
Like Meta’s Muse, Duet has its own app. (If you’re keeping score at home, yes, it has been updated to fully support the iPhone Duo). Duet uses Claude’s characteristically cheery, minimalist design. The app is consumer-friendly and approachable. Once you’ve connected all the productivity and chat services you want, it’ll look for ways to help out with and suggest them as cards you can swipe left or right to hide or accept — kind of a to-do list Tinder.
In the name of testing, I went all in on Duet and gave it everything: my work email, calendar, Google Docs, and Slack. This gave it plenty of grist for the mill in more ways than I’d even realized at first. Because our biweekly staff meeting is automatically summarized in an email from Gemini, and Duet had access to my email, it was able to connect the dots between something said in a meeting and a direct message in Slack. Duet also worked out who my boss is because we have a shared Google Doc for our weekly 1:1 meetings. AI is at its most useful when it can suck up lots of data, and Duet wasted no time doing so.

Duet has started each morning by greeting me with a brief message and some quick highlights for my day, like reminding me to pack an item required for a video shoot before my flight. Below that, it would suggest to-dos, usually ending in an offer to help draft a message or add an item to an agenda.
This has often been helpful, though it sometimes felt like interacting with a slightly neurotic personal assistant. In one instance, I’d emailed someone I interviewed for a story that the article was likely to go up on Tuesday and that I’d send a link when it did. I submitted my draft to my editors late at night on Monday, and Tuesday morning Duet was beside itself. It wasn’t quite 9AM yet when it pointed out that the story was still waiting for an edit and I needed to get on that — or else I wouldn’t be able to send the link I promised. The concern was a bit misplaced. And it didn’t pick up on the nuance in my message implying that this was not a hard deadline, nor the fact that it was only 7AM local time for my editor.
This sometimes felt like interacting with a slightly neurotic personal assistant
In other cases, it was surprisingly helpful. In planning out the next few weeks I’d missed the fact that upcoming time off would only leave me with a couple of working days before another big draft was due. Duet proposed a solution, offered to update the Google Doc with weekly notes for my editor, and drafted a Slack message for him noting the update. Reader, the message was a little 
too
 good. It copied my millennial habit of writing in all lowercase — which it pointed out in a slightly self-congratulatory aside — and used an abbreviation we often use to refer to our CMS. But it also shortened the word “signature” to “sig,” which I never would and made me laugh out loud. In any case, there’s no fooling anyone; the Slack message was tagged with a “sent with Claude” label.
Duet often volunteered to help start my drafts, which is always a hard no for me. I’ll type up my own little blogs, thank you very much 
Claude.
 If you’re not a writer, I can see how taking care of little writing chores here and there would be helpful. But this started feeling like a Spider-Man-pointing-at-Spider-Man moment when the task it wanted to help with was reviewing itself.
Maybe that’s how CEOs live
It was a little uncanny, too. Even setting aside the fact that I was writing about Duet, it’s kind of weird knowing that it can read every email, draft, and Slack I send. It can read all of that 
because I opted in to sharing that data
, and I knew full well what I was doing. But it’s another thing to see it in practice. I wonder if it would change how I work if I knew I didn’t really need to take responsibility for putting things on my calendar, writing down a to-do list, or paying attention in a meeting because Duet will just handle it. Maybe that’s how CEOs live. Not that I cherish creating calendar entries, but I wonder if I would end up feeling a bit disconnected from my workplace.
I don’t think Duet or any other AI agent is quite ready to make us feel like we’re all the CEOs of our own little worlds. There’s a key word in the title “personal assistant”: personal. A person can pick up on nuances in tone to understand what’s a big deal and what isn’t. A person will pick up on communication norms and deploy them sensibly. A person can be tactful. Anthropic’s blog post mentions that the company is continuing to “test its judgment about when to message you,” so it’s possible Duet will calibrate a little better over time. But AI overall isn’t good at understanding nuance or tone, and it’s not clear if it ever will be.
For me, Duet made it a little more clear what I want AI to do for me and what I don’t. Communication happens all over the place in a modern workplace — in emails, DMs, meetings, shared docs. Having AI find discrepancies that have been overlooked between all of these sources and point them out proactively is really helpful. I like it as a backstop for my own organizational systems, not as a replacement for them. I don’t like it in the creative process. Like, at all. I want to find “my own ways into and around a topic,” 
as Hank Green said of his own reliance on AI
. If you send a robot to do the legwork, you miss the opportunity to stumble across something unexpected. It’s part of what makes the work enjoyable. And I’m certainly not about to let an agent review itself for me. You hear that, 
Duet?

Context from Industry Pulse
The release of Claude Duet occurs during a period of rapid diversification for AI assistants. Tech firms are increasingly moving away from general-purpose chatbots toward specialized agents. Meta recently updated its Muse assistant with paid calling features for businesses, while OpenAI introduced the work-centric Dots. This trend is unfolding alongside significant shifts in the broader tech leadership landscape, highlighted by Jensen Huang's resignation from Nvidia and subsequent discussions regarding Tim Cook as a potential successor. Anthropic’s decision to target small business owners with a judgment-based agent reflects an industry-wide effort to monetize AI through deep integration into professional productivity stacks rather than standalone consumer novelties.
Anthropic is expanding its presence in the competitive artificial intelligence sector with the launch of Claude Duet. This new agent represents a shift in strategy toward professionals and small business owners rather than software engineers. Unlike the previous developer-focused Claude Code or the more general Claude Cowork, this iteration features a minimalist mobile application specifically designed for the iPhone Duo. The interface utilizes a card-based system where users can swipe to accept or dismiss suggested tasks, functioning as a dynamic to-do list.
According to 
The Verge
, the agent functions by connecting to a variety of digital tools, including Google Docs, Slack, calendars, and email accounts. By analyzing these data streams, Duet can synthesize information across different platforms. For instance, the system can identify organizational hierarchies by examining shared meeting documents or correlate information from automated meeting summaries with private chat messages. This deep integration allows the agent to monitor deadlines and nudge users about incomplete work, though testers have noted the tool can be persistent in its reminders.
As The Verge reports, the current release is categorized as a research preview with restricted access. Availability is limited to users in the United States who maintain Pro, Max, or Ultra subscriptions. Those who do not meet these criteria are required to join a waitlist. Anthropic has positioned the tool as a decision-making assistant that uses its own judgment to determine which items require a user’s immediate attention, rather than acting as a simple service for consumer errands like ordering food.
The launch follows a broader trend of companies releasing specialized agents to capture different segments of the market. While competitors have focused on shopping assistance or heavy work tasks, Anthropic’s latest entry prioritizes a approachable design meant to feel more consumer-friendly. By leveraging the existing Claude aesthetic, the company hopes to convince non-technical users that autonomous agents can provide tangible benefits in managing a busy professional schedule.
The Industry Pulse Bottom Line
Anthropic’s Claude Duet signals a move toward autonomous agents that prioritize cross-platform data synthesis over simple chat, aiming to become an indispensable layer in the professional productivity ecosystem.

Claude Duet の仕組み
Claude からのコンテキストが基盤となります。
 Claude Duet は、メモリ、スキル、接続されたアプリを Claude アカウントから引き継ぐため、設定に必要な作業は最小限です。 変わるのは協働の仕方です。Claude Duet が先にあなたをサポートしますので、タイピングや音声を使って1つの継続したスレッドでメッセージを送ることができ、簡単な返信も1週間にわたるプロジェクトも同じ場所から始められます。
離れている間も作業は継続されます。
 Claude Duet は独自のブラウザを搭載した仮想マシンを備えているため、ノートパソコンやスマートフォンをオンにする必要はありません。 短いメッセージでタスクを説明すると、どのアプリを使い、どのような手順を踏むべきかをバックグラウンドで判断します。 タスクが完了したとき、または確認が必要なときに通知が届きます。
対応が必要なときは、メッセージが届きます。
 Claude Duet は、接続されているアカウントを数時間おきに確認します。常時確認すると、プランの利用上限をはるかに早く使い切ってしまうためです。 どのようなタイミングでメッセージを表示するべきか、引き続きその判断を検証しており、テスターからのフィードバックに基づいて調整を続けていきます。また、どのような内容をどのくらいの頻度で知らせてほしいかを指示することもできます。
重要な決定権は、あなたにあります。
 Claude Duet は、作業内容の読み取り、下書きの作成、準備を単独で行います。 ただし、メッセージを送信、支払いを行う、文書に署名する、または何かを削除をする前には、これから実行する内容を正確に提示し、あなたの承認を待ちます。証券の売買や口座間の送金など、承認があっても実行できない操作もいくつかあります。 Claude Duet がアクセスできるアプリは、いつでも接続解除することが可能です。

リサーチプレビューに参加
これは Claude Duet の初期バージョンであり、スレッドを誤読したり、緊急度を見誤ったりする可能性がまだあります。 当社は業務を中心に中核機能を構築しているため、チケットの予約など個人的な用事への対応はまだ十分ではなく、ブラウザを表示することもまだできません。現時点では、Claude Duet はメインの Claude アプリとは独立した独自のアプリ内にあります。 初期ユーザーから学んだことが、両者がどのように連携していくかを形作ります。
リサーチプレビュー期間中、Claude Duet はプランの使用量制限を Claude の他の部分と共有しており、通常の会話とは異なる方法でこれらの制限を適用します。そのバックグラウンドチェックはこれらの制限に含まれます。財務モデルやプレゼン資料の作成など、より大規模なタスクでは、より多くのモデルの実行回数やバックグラウンド処理が必要になります。
