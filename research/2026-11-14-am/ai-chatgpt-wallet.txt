OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet, enabling ‘dot’ agents to make purchases
Zac Hall
 | Nov 13 2026 - 7:46 am PT																
								1 Comment							
OpenAI is launching ChatGPT Wallet, which it says is “setting a new bar for secure payments by agents.”
ChatGPT Wallet works with OpenAI’s “dots” agent feature and includes a partnership with American Express, MasterCard, and Visa. Here’s how it works.
ChatGPT Wallet lets your dot agent make purchases on your behalf
OpenAI says ChatGPT Wallet is the “first wallet that lets agents pay without access to users’ actual card details and that requires a security check for every purchase, while also preserving existing card rewards and protections for eligible disputes.”
To start, ChatGPT Wallet is only available to ChatGPT Pro subscribers who are 18 or older in the United States.
“We’re starting with limited availability to ChatGPT Pro users,” OpenAI explains, “so we can learn from real-world use and work with businesses, banks, and payment networks to improve compatibility and reliability.”
The feature won’t work with websites that block agents, however, which has increasingly become an issue online. 
More details below:
Before each purchase, the user reviews the items, total, and selected card, then approves it with a security check such as Face ID or a fingerprint scan. Each approval creates a clear record of the purchase the user authorized the agent to make.
After the user approves, the payment network provides a payment token—a substitute for the user’s actual card details—with a card number, security code, and expiration date. The payment is charged to their selected card without ChatGPT Wallet sharing their actual card details with the business or agent.

  By Katherine Hamilton 
  ChatGPT has launched a payment feature that lets users pay with an artificial-intelligence agent. 
  Users can add a debit or credit card to ChatGPT Wallet, which can then be used for payments without the need to re-enter card information. 
  Before each purchase, the customer reviews the purchase and approves it with facial or fingerprint identification, similar to Apple Pay. Each approval creates a clear record of the purchase. 
  The payment is made with a token, which is a substitute for the user's actual card details, so those details aren't shared with the business or agent. 
  ChatGPT owner OpenAI said it expects users will eventually be able to delegate purchases entirely to their AI agent by setting parameters, such as spending limits and purchase categories. 
  ChatGPT Wallet began rolling out Friday via the platform's new agent offering, Dots. The product will initially be available to Pro subscribers aged 18 and older in the U.S. with eligible U.S.-issued cards. 
  The wallet platform was built in partnership with American Express, Mastercard and Visa, OpenAI said. The company also released a set of tools and guidance to help businesses figure out how to accept payments from AI agents.
