Helping families navigate controls for teens on Pinterest
November 23, 2026
Trust + safety
Company
At Pinterest, we strive to lead on youth online safety by understanding 
how 
young people use our products and build protections that address the risks they may face. We’ve 
thoughtfully designed the platform
 to be age-appropriate from the start, with positive experiences for teens, including private-only accounts for users under 16, distraction-free school prompts, turning off notifications during late night hours, content controls and ongoing testing of new features and safeguards.
Young people should have experiences that are appropriate for their age, and caregivers should have one simple place to access granular settings and make additional protection choices that they feel are right for their family.
“We’ve built Pinterest to be a positive, age-appropriate place for teens from the start, with protections built in, not bolted on,” said Bill Ready, CEO of Pinterest. “For parents who want more involvement, the controls are easy to find and simple to use. Families can choose the experience that is right for them, while teens can continue to explore their interests and feel inspired.”
New Parental Controls Page﻿
Parents and caregivers shouldn’t have to navigate a maze of settings to guide their teen’s experience. That’s why our new Parental Controls page features everything in one location. 
It includes direct links to help update a teen’s device-level Screen Time and Location settings. On iOS and Android, this extends the experience beyond Pinterest and connects app-level settings with protections across a teen’s device. Parents can also find existing controls for private-by-default profiles, limits on ads personalization and controls for social features. 
More Parental Controls ﻿
We’re also continually looking for new controls to offer that customize the experience for younger users. Now on the Parental Controls Page, you’ll find introducing new settings for: 
AI-generated content:
 adjust the amount of AI-generated content appearing in a teen’s recommendations.
Notifications:
 adjust the volume of push, browser, email and in-app notifications.
Video autoplay:
 adjust whether videos can autoplay.
The page also brings existing protections front and center, including controls for social features like comments and mentions. Ads personalization can also be configured. Every setting on this page is locked by the Parental Passcode. When the Parental Passcode is enabled, teens need permission to make settings less restrictive.
Teen safety isn’t about adding the longest list of controls; it’s about making relevant protections accessible, clear and customizable for families.

Pinterest's header image for its Nov. 23, 2026, announcement on teen parental controls. 
Image: Pinterest
Pinterest has gathered its teen settings into one Parental Controls page and added three new options: one for AI-generated content in recommendations, one for notifications and one for video autoplay. The company announced the changes in a 
newsroom post
 dated Nov. 23, 2026.
What's new on the page
According to Pinterest, the page includes direct links to a teen's device-level Screen Time and Location settings on iOS and Android, so a parent can connect Pinterest's own controls to protections on the phone itself. It also pulls together controls that already existed: private-by-default profiles, limits on ads personalization and settings for social features such as comments and mentions.
The three new settings, as Pinterest describes them:
AI-generated content:
 adjust the amount of AI-generated content that appears in a teen's recommendations.
Notifications:
 adjust the volume of push, browser, email and in-app notifications.
Video autoplay:
 adjust whether videos can play automatically.
Pinterest's image of the feature shows a "Parental controls" entry in the account settings menu. Inside, a "Parental passcode" switch sits above a list of categories: account management, profile visibility, refining recommendations, social permissions and activity, notifications, and privacy and data.
"We've built Pinterest to be a positive, age-appropriate place for teens from the start, with protections built in, not bolted on," said Bill Ready, Pinterest's CEO, in the post. "For parents who want more involvement, the controls are easy to find and simple to use. Families can choose the experience that is right for them, while teens can continue to explore their interests and feel inspired."
The passcode is the lock
Pinterest says every setting on the page is locked by the Parental Passcode. When the passcode is on, teens need permission to make a setting less restrictive.
The company's help center explains how the passcode works. A parent or caregiver of a teen under 18 creates a four-digit code and enters an email address, which they'll need later to change the passcode. Pinterest sends a confirmation email, and the link inside it is valid for 24 hours. After the passcode is set, both the parent and the teen receive a confirmation email.
A few details from the 
help page
:
The passcode expires automatically when the teen turns 18, based on the date of birth on the account.
Pinterest deletes the parent's email address after the passcode is turned off or expires.
The code isn't required when someone changes a setting in a way that increases the teen's privacy.
Ads personalization and video recommendations are among the settings it protects, and the options available can depend on where the teen is located.
On a computer, the steps start by logging into the teen's account and choosing Account management in the left-side navigation. In the mobile app, a parent opens the teen's profile from the picture at the bottom right, then taps the profile picture at the top left, and follows the prompts.
What Pinterest already does for teens
Pinterest requires users to be at least 13, and in some countries the minimum is the age of consent, which can be higher, according to its 
resources page for parents
. Accounts for teens under 18 are private by default, so only the teen and people they invite to follow can see saved Pins and boards, and profile details such as name, age or location aren't discoverable by others, the company says.
Teens under 16 can exchange messages with mutual followers and may receive message requests from group board collaborators, Pinterest says, and settings adjust as teens get older. A teen can always choose a more restrictive setting. The company also says it's testing a prompt that may appear if a teen opens the app during the school day, suggesting they put Pinterest down, pause notifications and focus on school.
In the newsroom post, Pinterest wrote: "Teen safety isn't about adding the longest list of controls; it's about making relevant protections accessible, clear and customizable for families."
What to know
Pinterest's announcement doesn't give a date by which every account will see the new page, and it doesn't say how it defines AI-generated content or how the AI setting changes what a teen sees. The company's own description is the source for all of the above.
Other platforms have also been updating family tools. Launchpad reported on 
TikTok's redesigned Family Pairing
 on Sunday.
Families can check whether the new page has reached a teen's account by looking for a Parental controls entry in the account settings menu, where Pinterest's image shows it.
