November 24, 2026
By 
Murray Stassen

Tidal
 is banning wholly AI-generated music from its platform.
Continue to article...
The 
Block
-owned streaming service says it will reject and remove “any wholly AI-generated track that’s submitted to Tidal,” starting on 
December 9
.
Tracks that were “assisted by AI tools, but not the sole outputs, will be allowed on the platform,” according to the company.
Tidal
 says that 
43%
 of all tracks distributed to it over the past 
90 days
 were detected as wholly AI-generated.
The company has also named 
Ircam Amplify
, the commercial subsidiary of French audio and music research institute 
IRCAM
, as the technology partner behind its AI detection.
The policy update was announced on X on Tuesday (
November 24
) by 
Tidal
 and by 
Robert Andersen
, Tidal Lead at 
Block
.
“This year we introduced Tidal’s AI Policy to protect artists and keep listeners informed,” 
Tidal
 said in its post. “Since then, we’ve been identifying, labeling, and demonetizing tracks we determined are 100% AI generated outputs of tools like Suno, Lyria, and more.
“But that’s not enough. We think a more drastic change is in order.”
“Tidal will always fight for artists to get paid more.”
Tidal
Under the update, 
Tidal
 is also banning distributors from delivering wholly AI-generated tracks, and says it “will reject deliveries if their metadata says they were generated solely using AI tools.”
On 
November 6
, 
Tidal
 says it saw “the greatest influx of AI music yet,” with over 
60%
 of the deliveries it received that day being wholly AI-generated.
“Today, approximately 27 million tracks in our catalog are tagged as such, representing 6.3% of all available music on Tidal,” 
Tidal
 said. “Yet Tidal subscribers are not listening to these tracks: 96% of them have received zero streams in the last three months.”
Tidal
 says 
94%
 of the tracks carrying its AI badge are attributed to 
Suno
.
The company added that, to date, it has automatically taken down more than 
65,000
 AI tracks over suspected impersonation of artist names.
Tidal
 said: “Wholly AI-generated music intended to mislead listeners and impersonate artists is another serious problem that has only increased in scale: It diverts streams and royalties away from the artists, and confuses fans.”
Tidal
 added: “It’s true that not all songs catch on immediately, and artists should have time to build their fanbases. But this situation is different – at this scale, the authentic music and artists that Tidal subscribers are looking to listen to and discover get buried.”
“Tidal will always fight for artists to get paid more. Everything in this update comes back to that. If you make music, you should be able to put it on Tidal knowing it won’t be buried under tracks no one asked for, or copied by someone pretending to be you,” the company said.
Tidal
‘s ban goes further than the 
AI policy it introduced in June
, which tagged wholly AI-generated music in its app and blocked it from earning royalties.
At the time, 
Tidal
 said it “will accept AI-generated music,” provided it met the standards in the policy and in its agreements with rightsholders and distributors.
According to 
Tidal
, enforcement of the updated policy is “made possible with technology provided by Ircam Amplify, our AI detection partner.”
Ircam Amplify
 describes itself as “the independent trust infrastructure for music in the age of AI,” and says it analyzes audio to assess the use of AI in tracks for streaming platforms, distributors, and collective management organizations.
Its AI Music Detector can analyze more than 
250,000
 tracks per hour, according to the product’s listing on 
IRCAM’s website
.
Ircam Amplify
launched its AI-Generated Detector in May 2024
, when it said the tool could scan up to 
5,000
 tracks in under a minute with an accuracy rate of 
98.5%
.
“When a streaming service tells listeners that a track is AI-generated, it puts its reputation behind that decision.”
Nathalie Birocheau
, 
Ircam Amplify
“When a streaming service tells listeners that a track is AI-generated, it puts its reputation behind that decision,” said 
Nathalie Birocheau
, CEO of 
Ircam Amplify
. “A false positive can cast doubt on the authenticity of an artist’s work, with consequences for both the artist and their rightsholders. This can also affect the trust a listener places in their streaming service.
“That is why AI detection at scale demands the highest standards of accuracy and transparency. The industry needs a neutral, independent partner it can trust to assess the audio, deliver consistent results across a catalog and be clear about where the evidence is inconclusive. The same rigor matters upstream: distributors and labels can use this same detection system to evaluate content before it reaches the platforms, thereby ensuring that their deliveries meet the new standards and prerequisites in the age of AI.
“Our detection technology is built on nearly 50 years of audio and music research at IRCAM: half a century of studying how sound is made and perceived, working alongside artists to understand the language of music, and developing tools to analyze audio with the highest precision. That depth of expertise is what Ircam Amplify brings to AI detection, helping us achieve a market-leading false positive rate below 0.02%.
“We are proud that 
TIDAL
 has chosen us to help put its artist-centric AI policy into practice.”
Tidal
‘s 
43%
 figure is close to what 
Deezer
 has reported. The French streaming service 
said in April
 that fully AI-generated tracks accounted for more than 
44%
 of its daily deliveries.
In July, 
Deezer
 said it was 
receiving nearly 90,000 fully AI-generated tracks every day
 in June, accounting for more than 
50%
 of new music delivered to the service on peak days. 
Tidal
‘s own peak was higher, with over 
60%
 of deliveries on 
November 6
 being wholly AI-generated.
Tidal
‘s roughly 
27 million
 AI-tagged tracks compare with the more than 
42 million
 AI-generated tracks that 
Deezer
says it detected
 between January 2025 and August 2026.
Listening data from both services points the same way. 
Tidal
 says 
96%
 of its AI-tagged tracks have received zero streams in the last three months, while 
Deezer
 says fully AI-generated music accounts for just 
1%
 to 
3%
 of all streams on its platform, and that up to 
85%
 of the streams on those tracks were fraudulent in 2025.
Deezer
 keeps fully AI-generated tracks out of algorithmic recommendations and editorial playlists, and said in July that it would take down AI-generated tracks used to inflate streams fraudulently, as well as those that had not been streamed for six months or more.
In October, it 
said it would let users block
 fully AI-generated music from its search results by the end of 2026, but it has so far stopped short of the outright ban that 
Tidal
 is introducing.
In January, direct-to-fan platform 
Bandcamp
banned music and audio
 created “wholly or in substantial part by AI” from its platform.
Tidal
 also said in its post: “We’ll keep sharing what we find and we’ll keep acting on it.”

Tidal is the first subscription streaming platform to ban songs wholly generated by artificial intelligence. The company has 
announced
plans
 to remove some 29 million songs identified as such on December 9; from then on, it intends to automatically reject the output of song generators like Suno and Lyria, amounting to more than 80 thousand tracks per day. A company statement cited eye-watering data from regulation efforts. In the past 90 days, 43 percent of Tidal uploads were detected as wholly AI-generated. Of those songs, 96 percent have received zero streams.
Tidal’s detection software is supplied by ⁠a French company called 
Ircam Amplify
, which advertises 99 percent accuracy with a 1 percent rate of false positives. By that metric, somewhere close to 290,000 songs will be mistakenly pulled from the platform in the cull on December 9. Pitchfork has emailed Tidal to determine what recourse will be provided for artists whose music is falsely flagged as AI.
The move follows Tidal’s June rollout of plans to tag and demonitize music that is “wholly or substantially generated by generative artificial intelligence.” The new statement, conversely, refers only to music that is entirely AI-generated. Pitchfork has asked for clarity on whether Tidal will continue to demonetize music that is substantially, but not wholly, made with AI. The platform says it has so far removed 65,000 tracks flagged for impersonating artists.
Back in January, Bandcamp became the first major streaming service to wholly 
ban
 AI-generated music. Apple Music, and to a lesser degree Spotify, have publicized plans to tag, but not demonetize, some forms of AI-generated music.

TIDAL is set to ban wholly AI-generated music from its platform from December 9th.
Announced in a post shared to 
Instagram
 today, November 24th, captioned "Bye AI music," the streaming service will begin removing fully AI-generated tracks from its catalogue next month, with all such music expected to be gone by the beginning of 2027. TIDAL says the move could see 29 million tracks removed, with between 80,000 and 85,000 songs rejected daily.
According to figures shared alongside the announcement, 43 percent of music delivered to TIDAL over the past 90 days was identified as AI-generated, while 96 percent of AI-generated tracks received no plays. Users of the service have long complained that the DSP was overwhelmed by AI impersonations of popular musicians.
The decision expands on TIDAL's 
policy
 introduced in June, when it began labelling wholly AI-generated recordings and preventing them from earning royalties. The company also pledged to remove AI music linked to fraud or artist impersonation. TIDAL says it has already removed 66,000 tracks automatically over suspected artist-name impersonation.
