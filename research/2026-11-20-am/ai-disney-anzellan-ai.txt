Picture this: You're walking through Black Spire Outpost on Batuu, the setting for 
Star Wars
: Galaxy's Edge
 at 
Disneyland
 or 
Disney World
, and you step into a shop, maybe looking for a certain color of kyber crystal, a cold brew black caf, or a new piece of 
Star Wars
 merchandise. But before you can find what you're after, you're stopped by a small creature that starts talking to you.
Maybe it lets out a shriek of concern because you're wearing an outfit that screams 'dark side', or cheers with excitement after spotting your birthday button. These are the kinds of spontaneous, memorable interactions Disney parks are known for, and in recent years, we've seen Imagineering steadily bring 
robotics
 and 
high-tech Audio-Animatronics
 closer together to make its characters feel more alive.
Now, Disney's latest creation takes that idea a step further.

Meet the Anzellans: Vilo Vele, Kado Bre'a, and Jibu Bre'a. These tiny 
Star Wars
 creatures, the same species as Babu Frik from 
The Rise of Skywalker
, have been brought to life as three Audio-Animatronics positioned together in a crate. They can move, look around, and interact with one another, but what's especially impressive is that they can also read the room.
Thanks to a partnership with 
Amazon
 Web Services (AWS), the Anzellans use cameras, microphones, sensors, and AI to understand what's happening around them and respond in real time. They can recognize what a guest is wearing, identify who's speaking to them, and even coordinate their interactions with one another.
(Image credit: Disney Imagineering)
These Anzellans now are our most advanced Audio-Animatronics in the world
Kyle Laughlin
And while AI is helping bring these characters to life, it's not writing their dialogue or dreaming up their personalities. That's still very much the work of Disney's Imagineers.
"These Anzellans now are our most advanced Audio-Animatronics in the world," Kyle Laughlin, Senior Vice President of Walt Disney Imagineering R&D and Technology and Engineering, told me in an interview alongside Samira Panah Bakhtiar, General Manager of Telco, Media & Entertainment, Games, Advertising, and Sports at AWS.

It's a pretty significant statement considering Disney's long history with Audio-Animatronics, dating back to Walt Disney's Enchanted Tiki Room, and continuing through increasingly sophisticated figures in attractions like 
Pirates of the Caribbean
.
But the Anzellans are doing something different. Rather than performing a fixed sequence of movements and dialogue, they're designed to understand what's happening around them and decide how to respond, without someone controlling them from behind the scenes.
And it's part of a much bigger effort underway at Imagineering.
Bringing robotics and Audio-Animatronics together
(Image credit: Disney Imagineering)
I've spoken with Laughlin previously about Disney's characters, including the 
BDX droids
 that have been roaming Galaxy's Edge and 
Olaf
, the free-roaming robotic snowman that Imagineering has brought to Disneyland Paris and Hong Kong Disneyland.
While these creations might look very different from the traditional Audio-Animatronics that populate Disney's attractions, they're increasingly connected behind the scenes.
"Our robots and our animatronics are coming closer and closer together," Laughlin explained.
In fact, the Anzellans are built on the same robotic character platform that powers Olaf and the BDX droids. That's an especially interesting detail, considering these three little characters aren't roaming around on their own. Instead, they're working together in one environment, responding to guests and each other. Notably, there isn't another Cast Member controlling the experience or puppeteering, but rather those decisions are made by the character, in real time.
This is our most intelligent character that we've created
Kyle Laughlin
Laughlin describes the Anzellans as a hybrid, bringing together elements of robotics and Audio-Animatronics with a level of intelligence that Disney hasn't previously delivered in one of these characters.
"This is our most intelligent character that we've created," he said.
The hardware is an important part of that distinction. While Olaf can move around and interact with his surroundings, Laughlin explained that he doesn't have onboard cameras or microphones. The Anzellans do, along with additional sensors that help them understand their environment.
Imagine approaching the crate with a group of friends. The Anzellans can understand that multiple people are standing in front of them, determine who's speaking, and look that person directly in the eyes while responding in their own mix of English and Anzellan.
It's the kind of detail that could make an encounter feel much more personal, and it's where AWS enters the picture.
AWS gives the Anzellans their intelligence, but Disney remains in control
(Image credit: Disney Imagineering)
Disney and AWS have worked together for more than a decade, including on the infrastructure supporting Disney+, which turns seven this week. But the Anzellans take that partnership in a different direction, bringing AWS's cloud and AI capabilities into a physical character experience.
And the project came together surprisingly quickly.
Laughlin told me the teams worked together over a matter of months, with the opportunity to collaborate with the filmmakers behind 
The Mandalorian & Grogu
 helping inform the direction of the characters.
AWS I think brought the intelligence, Imagineering brought the brawn,
Samira Panah Bakhtiar
AWS brought its AI and software expertise, while Imagineering concentrated on the physical characters, their personalities, and the storytelling.
"AWS I think brought the intelligence, Imagineering brought the brawn," Bakhtiar told me.
The Anzellans use Amazon Bedrock, AWS's managed AI service, to access different AI models that help them understand and react to the world around them. Laughlin confirmed that Imagineering is using vision models, large language models, and world models as part of the experience.
That doesn't mean the characters are using generative AI to make up their own dialogue, though.
Every line is written by Imagineers and prerecorded, and every character's personality and movements are designed by the creative team. AI selects the appropriate responses and animations based on what's happening in the moment.
So, if an Anzellan spots a birthday button, it can choose a response that fits the occasion. If someone speaks directly to one of the characters, it can respond accordingly. The interaction might feel spontaneous, but the actual words and character behaviors remain firmly in Imagineering's hands.
That distinction is important, particularly as Disney continues to experiment with AI in guest experiences.
"Human creativity leads and the guest experience is preserved, and AI and the cloud computing infrastructure really is there to support it," Bakhtiar said. "[Entertainment companies] need technology that's going to really serve their story and not the other way around."
There's also another layer to this experience: the three Anzellans aren't simply responding independently.
(Image credit: Disney Imagineering)
Bakhtiar explained that each character is simultaneously tracking three things: the guest, what's happening in the surrounding environment, and what the other Anzellans are doing.
"The system is working like a show director, and it's like keeping this ensemble together so the characters, they don't talk over each other, the story stays coherent," she explained.
That's a fascinating distinction from a traditional Audio-Animatronics show. Instead of following the same carefully timed sequence, the Anzellans can adjust their performances to the situation while still behaving like characters from the 
Star Wars
 universe.
AWS also used Kiro, its AI coding agent, to help turn Imagineering's creative briefs into working software. Bakhtiar explained that the teams reviewed and approved the work at each stage, allowing ideas to move into testing more quickly.
For Laughlin, that meant Imagineers could spend more time focusing on the mechanical and electrical engineering, character skins, and the finer details that make the Anzellans believable.
And because Bedrock isn't tied to one specific AI model, Disney can swap models as the technology improves, optimizing for performance, speed, latency, or cost without rebuilding the entire experience.
"We can be on the bleeding edge of whatever model is launched in order to bring the best experience possible," Laughlin said.
That's particularly valuable when a character needs to respond quickly enough for a conversation to feel natural.
A new kind of character encounter
(Image credit: Disney Imagineering)
Of course, there's a big question hanging over all of this: When will guests actually get to meet the Anzellans?
Disney isn't saying just yet. The characters are scheduled to appear at AWS re:Invent in Las Vegas from November 30 through December 4, where attendees can meet them at the Customer Showcase at Caesars Forum.
And while this won't be their first public appearance — Disney confirmed to TechRadar that an earlier version of the Anzellans shown at 
D23
 was also powered by AWS — there's still no word on when or where they'll become a regular guest experience at Disneyland or Disney World.
Laughlin did offer one encouraging indication when I asked about their eventual arrival in the parks.
"We’re excited for guests to meet them. We just won’t talk specifics today."
And while we've already seen the BDX droids roaming Galaxy's Edge and Olaf interacting with guests at Disneyland Paris, these experiences are still relatively limited compared with the many Audio-Animatronics and characters found throughout Disney's parks. The technology is impressive, but the next challenge is bringing these more interactive characters into everyday park experiences, and doing so at scale.
That's something Laughlin and I have discussed before, and he sees the Anzellans as part of a broader shift in how Imagineering develops and introduces its characters.
(Image credit: Disney Imagineering)
"More than ever, this is not about short-term playtests. It really is about populating our worlds with lots of different characters," he explained.
Laughlin also told me that guests should expect to encounter the Anzellans indoors first, and his description of the technology offers a pretty intriguing glimpse at where Imagineering might be headed.
"We've viewed this as an experiential merchandise platform," he explained.
In other words, imagine walking into a Disney shop and having an unexpected interaction with a character, rather than needing to visit a designated meet-and-greet location.
And the idea isn't necessarily limited to 
Star Wars
.
"When we think about the future of where it's headed, you can imagine that any type of merchandise experience could be powered with characters from any of our franchises," Laughlin said.
There's still plenty to figure out, though. Keeping a character responsive throughout a busy day at a theme park requires more than just sophisticated AI. There's also the question of what happens if the network connection drops or the primary system encounters a problem.
(Image credit: Walt Disney Imagineering)
It's something I asked Laughlin about, and he explained that Disney is combining AWS cloud and edge infrastructure to help keep the experience running.
"If something were to fail [...] then we could always fall back to another experience," he said.
The goal is to keep these characters operating reliably throughout park hours, though Disney is still learning what it takes to deliver these more intelligent experiences at scale.
Privacy is another consideration, especially with cameras and microphones involved. Laughlin told me that the information gathered during an interaction is used only for the immediate experience, isn't currently stored in the cloud for Disney to reuse, and isn't used to train AI models.
The specifics of the underlying models and the number of sensors remain undisclosed, but the broader approach is becoming clearer.
The next chapter for Disney's characters
(Image credit: Disney Imagineering)
Imagineering has continued to push its characters further, from free-roaming robots to the next-generation Audio-Animatronics appearing in attractions like Pirates of the Caribbean or 
Frozen Ever After
.
The Anzellans are another step in that evolution, but perhaps the bigger development is what's happening behind the scenes. Disney is building a shared foundation that can support different types of characters and experiences, rather than treating robotics, Audio-Animatronics, and AI as entirely separate technologies.
Bakhtiar described the AWS-powered system as a reusable framework, one that could allow Imagineers to focus on creating new personalities, moods, movements, and stories instead of rebuilding the underlying software every time.
And with Laughlin pointing to more than $60 billion of work ahead for Imagineering worldwide, there are plenty of opportunities to put these technologies to work.
Just look at Walt Disney World, where an entirely new 
Monsters, Inc
. land is coming to Hollywood Studios — complete with an octopus-like, impressive animatronic — while Animal Kingdom is getting Tropical Americas with new Indiana Jones and Encanto attractions. Over at Magic Kingdom, Villains Land and Piston Peak are on the way, while EPCOT will eventually take guests on a new journey into imagination with Figment and Dreamfinder.
And that's just Florida. At Disneyland Resort in California, Imagineering is working on the first-ever 
Coco
 attraction, a major reimagining of Tomorrowland, and an expansion of Avengers Campus, among other projects.
Of course, that doesn't mean we'll necessarily see the Anzellans or this exact AI technology in any of these attractions. But with so much happening across Disney Parks, it's easy to see why Imagineering is investing in a platform that could bring more interactive characters to life across its parks.
my hope is over the next 10 years you're going to continue to see an incredible pace of scale and delivering these experiences worldwide
Kyle Laughlin
For now, the Anzellans remain a glimpse at what's possible rather than a confirmed new fixture at Galaxy's Edge. But it's easy to imagine how a seemingly ordinary shopping trip could become something much more memorable when a character recognizes you, reacts to what you're wearing, or strikes up a conversation.
And when I asked Laughlin what this convergence of robotics, AI, and Audio-Animatronics could mean for Disney's characters over the next decade, he pointed to the growing range of possibilities, from robots and Audio-Animatronics to puppets and roaming characters.
"We now have more tools in the toolkit to bring these characters to life in all kinds of new ways," he said. "And so, my hope is over the next 10 years you're going to continue to see an incredible pace of scale and delivering these experiences worldwide."

Disney Imagineers Have Built AI-Powered 'Star Wars' Alien Audio-Animatronics With AWS Credit: CNET
Disney is celebrating 75 years of its Imagineers, who have been amusing and delighting guests with audio-animatronic characters since talking parrots debuted in the Enchanted Tiki Room at 
Disneyland
. But today, new robotic characters are being powered up with artificial intelligence.
In partnership with Amazon Web Services, AI has helped Walt Disney Imagineering create 
three new Anzellan animatronics
 that will live bring a Disney theme park retail space to life, presumably in Star Wars: Galaxy's Edge.
Anzellans are a race of tiny humanoid creatures that repair droids and starships in the Star Wars universe. They include Babu Frik in 
Star Wars: Episode IX — The Rise of Skywalker
 and the droidsmiths introduced in season 3 of 
The Mandalorian
. The droidsmiths take on larger roles in 
The Mandalorian and Grogu
, helping Baby Yoda rescue Din Djarin from the Hutts.

The new Anzellan animatronics built by Disney Imagineers use AWS cloud and AI capabilities in order to "understand the scene around them," Kyle Laughlin, SVP of Walt Disney Imagineering R&D and Technology and Engineering, tells me.
"If you imagine walking into an experiential retail store, you now will be greeted not by a pre-scripted animation or interaction, but a totally personalized experience where each of these Anzellans [has] their own personalities," Laughlin says, adding that they'll be attuned to "how many people are in the room, the ages of people, the general size and type of guest."
The animatronic aliens will be able to interact with guests, right down to commenting on people who are wearing a birthday button or even a certain Star Wars T-shirt. Speaking with these animatronics will give guests the same experience they would have when meeting a character walking around the park, Laughlin adds. They also play off each other and react to what each other are doing, with no one controlling them behind the scenes.
The three audio-animatronic prototypes, which will make their first appearance during AWS re:Invent in Las Vegas on Nov. 30 before moving into one of the Disney Parks, are named Vilo Vele, Kado Bre'a and Jibu Bre'a.

They're original characters created just for this project in partnership with Lucasfilm and the filmmakers, and they all have their own personalities devised by the Imagineers. Vilo is the boss, Kado is the droidsmith mechanic who wants to be the center of attention and Jibu, though Kado's twin, is the shy one. The animatronics speak a mix of English (or Galactic Basic in-universe) and Anzellan, as they do in the Star Wars movies and TV series, and have hundreds of thousands of prerecorded lines of dialogue to draw from when talking to guests and each other.
The three audio-animatronic Anzellans are original characters named Vilo Vele, Kado Bre'a and Jibu Bre'a.Disney
Samira Panah Bakhtiar, AWS general manager of Telco, Media & Entertainment, Games, Advertising and Sports, tells me this is "one of the most delightful examples" of 
physical AI
.
"When you talk about physical AI, it's AI that perceives, it reasons and it acts in the real world," she says. "It's not just AI on a screen."
Bakhtiar says Disney was able to use both AWS cloud computing and AI tools to "get from idea to implementation several magnitudes faster than they would have otherwise," with Laughlin specifying it took "mere months."

AWS staff used a 
coding agent called Kiro
 that built their creative briefs into software, which was then reviewed and approved by humans. Imagineers also used the 
Amazon Bedrock
 fully managed AI service, which allows them to choose between all the biggest AI models, including those from OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta.
"We're providing Amazon Bedrock to the Imagineering team to be able to leverage the right models that they need at the right time to be able to really enhance those character experiences," Bakhtiar tells me. "From multimodal models that focus on voice to world models, to large language models. The point is that they are able to get the right tool at the right time and then do so in a way that's secure, resilient, performant and globally available."
Bakhtiar says AWS is continuing to work with Disney, though the next project is under wraps.
"The future of entertainment is going to be highly personalized," she says. "It's going to be multidimensional, and it's going to be more interactive than ever. And I think this is an incredible example of that coming to life."

Haas-ja-v1.0
」や音声合成AI「
Irodori-TTS
」などを1つの実行環境にまとめた無料のオープンソースツールが「

nyosegawa/speech.cpp: Speech synthesis, speech recognition and voice activity detection for voice conversation, in C++ on ggml
https://github.com/nyosegawa/speech.cpp
◆speech.cppの導入
speech.cppはWindows・macOS・Linuxに対応しており、今回はWindows 11を搭載したPCにPowerShellから以下のコマンドを実行してインストールします。
irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nyosegawa/speech.cpp/main/install.ps1 | iex
インストールが完了したらPowerShellを起動し直し、以下のコマンドを実行してCPUだけで動作するように指定した状態でWebインターフェースを起動します。
speech serve --open --device cpu --host localhost
ブラウザで「http://localhost:8080/」が開き、上部に音声合成の「Speak」・音声ファイルからの文字起こしの「Transcribe」・リアルタイム文字起こしの「Live」という3つのタブが表示されます。
◆テキストから日本語の音声を生成
まずはテキストから音声を生成するため「Speak」タブにあるモデル選択ボタンをクリック。
利用できる音声合成AIモデルの一覧が表示されるので、今回は日本語を含む10言語に対応した音声合成モデル「Qwen3-TTS 0.6B」の「Fetch and use」をクリックしてダウンロードします。
モデルの読み込みが完了したら「Text」に文章を入力し「Voice」で日本語の話者「ono_anna」を選択して「Speak」をクリックすると、音声が生成され再生やWAV形式でのダウンロードが可能です。
◆音声ファイルから文字起こし
speech.cppでは生成した音声をそのまま文字起こしすることもできるので、音声波形の下にある「Transcribe this」をクリックしてみました。
「Transcribe」タブに切り替わり、先ほど生成した音声が文字起こしの対象として設定されます。「Choose a model」をクリック。
音声認識には日本語特化型音声認識モデル「
Haas-ja-v1.0
」の「Fetch and use」をクリックしてモデルをダウンロード。
モデルの読み込みが完了したら「Transcribe」をクリックすると文字起こしされた文章が表示され、処理にかかった時間も確認できます。「speech.cpp」の部分は誤認識されていましたが、それ以外の日本語はおおむね正しく文字起こしできています。
音声ファイルをドラッグ＆ドロップして文字起こしできるほか「Record」をクリックしてその場で録音した音声を文字起こしすることも可能です。
録音した音声を文字起こしした結果、録音した内容が正しく読み取られていました。
◆マイクからリアルタイムで文字起こし
録音の完了を待たずリアルタイムに文字起こしするには「Live」タブを開き、日本語対応の音声認識モデル「
ReazonSpeech v2
」を「Fetch and use」をクリックしダウンロード。
人間が話している区間を検出するモデルを選択するため「Find speech with」の選択欄をクリック。
「
Silero VAD
」の「Fetch and use」をクリックしダウンロード。
「Start」をクリックしてマイクに向かって話すと発話中の文章が灰色で表示され、話し終えると文字起こしの結果が確定します。「肌寒く。じます。」となっている箇所は「肌寒く」と「感じます」の間を少し空けて話したため文の区切りとして処理されていました。
◆自分の声で好きな文章を読み上げる音声クローン
音声合成モデルを日本語特化型の「
Irodori-TTS
」に切り替えると、録音された音声から声の特徴を抽出して別の文章を読み上げる「音声クローン」も利用できます。
「Speak」タブでモデル選択ボタンをクリックし「irodori-tts-mf」の「fetch and use」をクリックしてダウンロードします。
「Make a voice from a recording」を開き「Record」をクリックして自分の声を録音し「Name of the voice」に任意の名前を入力して「Add the voice」をクリック。
「Text」に録音時とは異なる文章を入力して、登録した声を「Voice」で選択し「Speak」をクリックすると…
自分の声の特徴を再現した音声を生成できます。

◆GPU不要で動作する仕組み
speech.cppはAIモデルの演算処理を行うためのライブラリ「
ggml

音声認識や音声合成をローカルで実行できるようになれば、取材音声や社内会議の記録を外部サービスに送信せずに処理したり、大量の文書を追加のAPI料金なしで音声化したりといった活用が可能です。

===== https://press.aboutamazon.com/2026/11/meet-the-anzellans-inside-imagineering-r-ds-newest-robotic-characters
(status 200)
They’re small, they’re bold, and they’ve definitely got personality, built with AWS.
Picture this. You step up to say hello, and three tiny figures turn your way at once. One starts speaking a mix of English and Anzellan. Another blinks and tilts its head at you. The third gives you a long look and finally says, “we have something special for you.”
Meet the Anzellans: Vilo Vele, Kado Bre’a and Jibu Bre’a, members of an alien species from the Star Wars galaxy. They are the newest Audio-Animatronics® character prototype from Walt Disney Imagineering R&D. And they mark a new step in R&D’s character platform innovation: Audio-Animatronics characters that don’t run on a fixed loop but decide for themselves what to say and how to move. No two moments with them are ever quite the same.
Bringing characters to life has been Imagineering’s specialty since Audio-Animatronics figures first sang in Disneyland’s Enchanted Tiki Room. Each new character builds on the last.
Kyle Laughlin, SVP, Walt Disney Imagineering R&D and Technology and Engineering, added, “At Disney, we always start with the story and how we want guests to feel. With the Anzellans, that feeling is surprise. By bringing together AWS’s incredible technology with the ingenuity of our Imagineers, we have an exciting opportunity to take on the next challenge in bringing characters to life in innovative ways, and at the size and scale guests expect.”
The next innovation: characters that think on their feet
For decades, Audio-Animatronics figures have performed carefully choreographed shows, featuring the same performance, in the same order, every time. The Anzellans are different. They notice what’s happening around them, decide how to respond and act, all on their own and in the moment.
The Anzellans can perceive what guests are doing in real time. Wave or say something to them, and they pick up on it fast enough for their reaction to feel completely natural. Each character behaves unmistakably like themselves and reacts to each other as well as to guests. And the extraordinary magic: there is no one puppeteering or controlling them.
Behind the scenes, AWS technology is powering the intelligence of each of the Anzellans in real time. Fast-decision models handle quick reactions, while larger models help weigh the bigger picture. AI built with AWS helps the Anzellans sense, think, and act autonomously, while the moments themselves, from every line of dialogue to every personality quirk, were written and designed by Imagineers.
Attendees of AWS re:Invent in Las Vegas (Nov. 30-Dec. 4) will be able to meet the Anzellans at the Customer Showcase at Caesars Forum.
"At AWS, we believe the most magical technology is the kind you interact with seamlessly in the real world. The intelligence behind the Anzellans runs on AWS, enabling the characters to perceive their environment, reason about what's happening, and respond dynamically in real time," said Samira Panah Bakhtiar, GM, Telco, Media & Entertainment, Games, Advertising and Sports, AWS. "What makes this innovation truly special is that it exemplifies physical AI: Every word, every gesture, every personality trait was crafted by Disney's Imagineers — with AWS powering the real-time decision-making that brings those creative choices to life. We're proud to support Imagineering as they write the next chapter of a tradition that started more than 60 years ago.”
This new innovation builds on a longstanding relationship between Disney and AWS as a public cloud infrastructure provider for Disney+. With the Anzellans, this collaboration extends into a new frontier: AWS's cloud and AI capabilities now power the real-time intelligence behind characters guests can one day meet.
The system delivers pre-recorded audio that is intelligently selected and played with dynamic animations. Each component of the experience is optimized for latency, ensuring optimal real-time response of the characters. The result is a set of characters that surprise the guest, without surprising its creators or artists.
Imagineers made the impossible possible
The Anzellans are the latest chapter in a story that began more than 60 years ago with the first Audio-Animatronics figures. From the Enchanted Tiki Room to Great Moments with Mr. Lincoln to the next-generation Audio-Animatronics pirate figure in the Pirates of the Caribbean at Disneyland Resort, Imagineers have steadily designed characters to move, balance, and emote like the characters Guests and fans have come to know and love. With Vilo Vele, Kado Bre’a and Jibu Bre’a, Imagineers are reaching for something new: characters that don’t just perform for you, but that come alive and turn every encounter into a memorable moment of their own.
