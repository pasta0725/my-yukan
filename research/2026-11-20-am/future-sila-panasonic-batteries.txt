Sila secures billion-dollar offtake agreement with Panasonic
US battery material manufacturer Sila will supply Panasonic Energy with its silicon-carbon anode material, Titan Silicon, in the future. The multi-year agreement could reach a volume of over one billion US dollars.
Image: Sila
By Florian Treiss

Sila intends to supply its anode material from its production facility in Moses Lake, Washington, USA. The multi-year offtake agreement is part of a long-term supplier arrangement that, according to Sila, has a potential volume exceeding one billion US dollars.
Under the agreement, the material will be used in battery cells manufactured at Panasonic’s US cell factories. Both companies also plan to collaborate on intellectual property to further develop their partnership. However, financial details, specific delivery quantities, and a timeline for the start of series production have not yet been disclosed.
“This offtake agreement is a major commercial milestone for Sila, a signal that our Moses Lake plant will be ready to supply high-performance materials for our key strategic customers once qualified, and expand as needed,” explained Sila co-founder and CEO Gene Berdichevsky. The company can scale production as needed.
Sila a key partner for Panasonic’s US production
Panasonic Energy North America views Sila as a key partner for its manufacturing plans in the USA. “Sila’s rapid progress at Moses Lake – from groundbreaking to production – has been exceptional,” said company CEO Yasuaki Takamoto. The partner’s technological expertise helps Panasonic meet its own high quality and performance standards.
This newly signed agreement is not the first collaboration between the two companies. In December 2023, Panasonic 
announced an order for silicon anode material from Sila
. At the time, the focus was on the basic integration of the material into Panasonic’s battery cells. The cell manufacturer aimed to gradually reduce the graphite content in the anodes.
Sila commissioned its Moses Lake production facility 
in April 2025
. Production has been running there 
since autumn 2025
. The first expansion phase is expected to supply enough anode material to support battery cells with a total capacity of around two gigawatt-hours per year. According to Sila, Titan Silicon can increase energy density by around 20 per cent compared to leading graphite cells. In July of this year, Sila then 
raised 300 million dollars
 from private investors.
Panasonic operates two battery cell factories in the USA
Panasonic is also expanding its US battery production. In July 2025, the company inaugurated its 
second US cell factory in De Soto, Kansas
. The facility produces 2170 cylindrical cells. In the long term, Panasonic aims for an annual capacity of 32 GWh at the site. Combined with the Nevada plant, US production is set to reach 73 GWh.
The new offtake agreement between Sila and Panasonic is intended to strengthen the regional battery value chain in North America. Sila estimates that scaling production will create around 500 jobs in manufacturing and construction. In August, the company also received a conditional 
loan commitment of up to $1.4 billion dollars
 from the US Department of Defense to advance the expansion in Moses Lake.

Sila Inks Long-Term Offtake Deal with Panasonic Energy to Power Domestic Battery Production
11.18.26
11.18.26
Agreement to deliver Sila’s Titan Silicon® Si/C anode to Panasonic’s U.S. manufacturing sites, fortifying domestic battery supply chain resilience and advanced technology capabilities.
ALAMEDA, CA — November 18, 2026 — Sila, a leading battery technology and materials company, today announced a commercial offtake agreement with Panasonic Energy Corporation of North America. The multi-year agreement is part of a long-term commercial relationship with the potential for over $1 billion of offtake over time. Sila will supply its proprietary Titan Silicon silicon-carbon (Si/C) anode material from its Moses Lake, Washington plant to power Panasonic battery cells. The partnership is also expected to support significant manufacturing and construction job creation in the United States. With Sila’s U.S.-based anode production underpinned by proprietary American technology and know-how, Panasonic and its OEM customers gain a clearer path toward a more secure, resilient, and sustainable domestic supply chain. 
"This offtake agreement is a major commercial milestone for Sila, a signal that our Moses Lake plant will be ready to supply high-performance materials for our key strategic customers once qualified, and expand as needed," said Gene Berdichevsky, Co-founder and CEO of Sila. "Partnering with a global industry leader like Panasonic validates our technology, ensuring anodes invented and manufactured in the U.S. are integrated into American-built cells to power critical industries for years to come." 
The agreement marks a significant step toward strengthening U.S. technological competitiveness and industrial resilience by manufacturing next-generation silicon-carbon anode materials here in the United States. Sila and Panasonic will also collaborate on intellectual property to further strengthen and develop their partnership. 
"Sila’s rapid progress at Moses Lake—from breaking ground to production—has been exceptional," said Yasuaki Takamoto, CEO, Panasonic Energy Corporation of North America. "Their technical leadership makes them a trusted partner as we work together toward meeting our highest quality and performance standards. This collaboration evidences Panasonic’s commitment to U.S. manufacturing while contributing to a more resilient battery supply chain in North America."
The contract announced today follows Sila’s recent $1.4 billion loan commitment from the U.S. Department of Defense’s Office of Strategic Capital (OSC). Securing strong commercial offtake agreements represents a key milestone and a natural next step toward demonstrating Sila’s execution and commercial readiness across the business. It also results in an estimated additional 500 manufacturing and construction jobs at Sila’s plant in Moses Lake, Washington, as the company scales production to meet volume requirements for this agreement and other commercial commitments. 
Sila’s Moses Lake silicon-carbon anode manufacturing facility is expected to play a pivotal role in strengthening domestic battery manufacturing and securing key energy supply chains. By scaling proprietary Titan Silicon Si/C anode production locally, the facility reinforces U.S. manufacturing leadership, fosters regional high-tech job creation, and lays the foundation of a secure, competitive battery supply chain in North America for global electric vehicle, AI, and critical technology markets. 
About Sila 
Founded in 2011, Sila is a battery technology and materials company that develops and manufactures products to power critical industries and physical AI. The Sila-invented Si/C anode technology (Titan Silicon®) launched in 2021 and drives performance across drones, satellites, electronics, robotics, AI, and electric vehicles. By combining American intellectual property with gigascale manufacturing, Sila delivers the power, supply chain security, and cost-effectiveness that nations require. Sila's battery materials are produced at its first-of-its-kind facility in Moses Lake, Washington. The company is backed by 8VC, Atreides Management, Bessemer Venture Partners, Coatue, In-Q-Tel, Matrix Partners, Sutter Hill Ventures, and funds and accounts advised by T. Rowe Price Associates, Inc.
