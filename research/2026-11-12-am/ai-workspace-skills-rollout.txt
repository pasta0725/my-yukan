Google reported on November 10, 2026 that Workspace Skills were rolling out to Gemini in Workspace and Workspace Studio. Skills let teams reuse instructions and reference material. A meeting summary draft is a small first task that exposes how a skill handles missing facts and inaccessible files.
The key points
Rapid Release began November 9, with Scheduled Release beginning November 30
Sharing creates a copy and Drive permissions are managed separately
Test on fictional inputs before connecting a flow
A skill stores instructions for repeatable work
Google describes creating and testing skills in Studio and drafting collaboratively in Docs. Once enabled, a skill can be selected by Gemini or invoked with @. The rollout covers invocation on the web in Gmail, Docs, Slides, Drive, Chat and Studio, plus mobile Google Chat. Sheets and Meet are outside the launch scope. Prompts and skill instructions currently support English.
Availability depends on edition and release track
The November 10 announcement gives November 9 for Rapid Release and November 30, 2026 for Scheduled Release, each with up to 15 days of gradual visibility. Listed editions include Business and Enterprise Starter, Standard and Plus, Google AI Pro for Education and AI Expanded Access for users aged 18 or older. Administrators can disable access at domain, organizational-unit or group level. Check the existing account’s track and policy before planning a team rollout.

Check copies and permissions from a recipient account
Studio shares an independent copy that the recipient can inspect, edit and enable. Updates to the original do not propagate to copies already saved. Drive references require their own access grants. Record the version, date and owner on distributed instructions. Test once as a typical recipient and once with an account that cannot open a referenced file, then compare the output against your expected behavior.
Check each flow when moving from Gems
Google’s administrator guide treats Workspace, the Gemini app and Gemini Enterprise as separate skill environments. The planned 2027 automatic Gem migration creates drafts in the Gemini app. For Studio, review the configuration and reference files on that platform. If a flow uses Ask a Gem, consult Google’s transition guidance and capture a known output for comparison. What happened
Google's Workspace Updates blog says skills, which are reusable custom instructions that tell Gemini to apply a team's rules, templates and reference files, are rolling out in Workspace Studio, Gmail, Docs, Slides, Drive and Chat, on by default for Business Starter, Standard and Plus and Enterprise editions. Rapid Release domains get it gradually from Nov. 9, 2026 and Scheduled Release domains from Nov. 30, 2026, each over up to 15 days, and skills do not yet work in Sheets or Meet or with non-English prompts.
What it means for a 10-to-50-person business
If you run Google Workspace on a Business plan, you can now write your house rules once and have Gemini apply them to every draft. Take an 18-person plumbing company where the office manager sends about 25 estimate follow-up emails a week. Assume each takes 6 minutes to write from scratch. A skill could hold the company's tone, warranty wording and follow-up template, and point at a price-list file in Drive. If that cuts each email to 2 minutes, it saves about 100 minutes a week. That is roughly 7 hours a month, or about $210 at $30 an hour. There are limits. Skills don't work in Sheets, so estimates kept in a spreadsheet get no help. Anyone using a shared skill also needs access to the Drive files it references. Shared copies don't update when you edit the original, so give each skill one owner. If you have Studio flows built on 'Ask a Gem', you can no longer add new Gem steps to them. Microsoft 365 shops can ignore this.

What Skills in Google Workspace Are and Who They Help
Google is rolling out reusable instructions for Gemini across Gmail, Docs, Slides, Drive, and Chat, so teams can share the same way of working.

Have you ever explained the same email style or report format to an AI tool for the fifth time this month? Skills are Google's answer to that repetition. They let you write your preferred way of working once, then reuse it.
What a skill actually is
A 
skill
 is a saved set of instructions. Think of it like a laminated checklist you hand to a new coworker: it says how things are done here, and the coworker follows it every time. In Google's announcement, a skill tells Gemini (Google's AI assistant) to follow your rules, use your templates, and check your reference files.
Skills are written in a format called 
SKILL.md
, which is a plain text file with Markdown formatting. Markdown is just a light way of marking headings and lists with simple symbols. You don't need to know it to use skills, but it is useful to know that the format is open, so instructions can be copied between tools.
Where you can use them
Google says you can create, test, and share skills in 
Workspace Studio
 (the place to build automated workflows) and 
Google Docs
. You can also 
prompt
 Gemini directly in Gmail, Docs, Slides, Drive, and Chat to create and refine skills without leaving the app you are working in.
Once a skill is turned on, Gemini can recognize when it fits your request. You can also call one or more skills yourself by typing an @ followed by the skill's name. Skills can also start Workspace Studio flows set to "Start manually" or "On a schedule."
Some limits to know now: according to Google, skills are not yet available in Google Sheets or Google Meet, and they currently support English prompts and instructions only. Skills are also limited to users age 18 and older.
Wrap-up
Skills turn your team's habits into reusable instructions that Gemini can apply across Workspace. Google's announcement says rollout is gradual, so your feature may appear up to 15 days after your release date. Your next step is simple: check the Help Center articles Google links to, then write down one instruction you repeat every week and turn it into a skill.

Following our

skills in Google Workspace
 — reusable, custom instructions that guide Gemini to apply your team’s rules, templates, and reference files — are now rolling out across Google Workspace Studio and Workspace apps. Here’s how to get started:
Create, test, and share skills in Google Workspace Studio and Google Docs: 
Build skills from scratch, start from new turnkey templates, or edit Markdown-native (
SKILL.md
) files directly in 
Google Workspace Studio
, and test how they generate email drafts, documents, or presentations before turning them on. You can also draft and collaborate on skills in 
Google Docs
, and share copies of your skills from Workspace Studio so teammates can customize and enable them for their own workflows.
Create and edit skills in context across Workspace apps: 
You can prompt Gemini directly in Gmail, Google Docs, Google Slides, Google Drive, and Google Chat to create, refine, and test skills without leaving the app you’re working in.
Use skills automatically or with an @, and connect them to Studio flows:
 Once you turn on a skill, Gemini in Gmail, Google Docs, Google Slides, Google Drive, and Google Chat can automatically recognize when that skill is relevant and apply it to your prompt, or you can invoke one or more skills explicitly at any time with an @mention (including combining multiple skills in a single request). Skills can also trigger 
Workspace Studio flows
 configured with a “Start manually” or “On a schedule” starter step.
These capabilities unlock powerful ways to standardize everyday work across your team. For example:
A marketing or sales team can maintain approved brand voice, tone, and structure across email drafts in Gmail, proposals in Docs, and pitch decks in Slides.
A finance or legal specialist can standardize recurring contract, proposal, or invoice reviews against reference guidelines stored in Google Drive.
A project lead can generate recurring status updates grounded in living meeting notes, Chat conversations, and Drive files without re-typing formatting instructions each week.
Create, test, and invoke skills across Google Workspace Studio and Workspace apps
A few things to know for end users:
Supported surfaces, mobile access, and language: 
Creating, editing, testing, and sharing skills are available on the web. Invoking skills is supported on the web in Workspace Studio, Gmail, Google Docs, Google Slides, Google Drive, and Google Chat, as well as in the Google Chat mobile app on Android and iOS. Skills in Workspace are not yet available in Google Sheets or Google Meet, and currently support English prompts and instructions only.
How skill sharing and Drive file permissions work in Workspace Studio:
 Sharing a skill from Workspace Studio gives recipients an independent copy that they can review, customize, and turn on in their own skills library (subsequent edits you make to your skill won’t overwrite copies others have already saved). If your skill references Google Drive files, you’ll also need to share access to those files with recipients.
Skills in Workspace are separate from skills and Gems in the Gemini app:
 Skills created in Workspace do not sync automatically with skills or Gems in the 
Gemini app
, and the upcoming automated migration of remaining Gems in 2027 will convert Gems to draft skills exclusively in the Gemini app — not in Workspace Studio or Workspace apps. Because both environments use the open 
SKILL.md
 format, you can manually copy instructions between the Gemini app and Workspace if you want to use a skill in both places.
