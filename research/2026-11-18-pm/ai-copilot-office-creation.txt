Microsoft makes Word, Excel and PowerPoint generally available inside Copilot
By 
Saptam Modak

Microsoft has made Word, Excel and PowerPoint generally available inside its Copilot app, moving a central part of its Office software into the AI interface. The release was announced at Ignite on 17 November, alongside wider availability of Home, which combines Copilot's Chat and Cowork experiences.
Users can create files, edit them and work on them with colleagues without leaving Copilot. The change follows an earlier rollout to Frontier customers in October, so this week's news is broader availability rather than the first demonstration of Office inside the app.
Microsoft is presenting Copilot as a starting point for work that previously began in separate applications. That does not mean those applications have disappeared: the documents, spreadsheets and presentations remain editable in the standalone Office programs.
Office files stay connected to OneDrive
The company says work created in the Copilot app is saved in OneDrive. Files keep the sharing permissions, security labels and compliance controls that organisations already apply through Microsoft 365.
The Office experience includes comments and coauthoring, letting colleagues continue working on the same file as a conversation with Copilot develops. Microsoft's earlier product announcement also described a library for reopening recent Office files and continuing to edit them beside the chat.
That continuity matters to the release. A presentation or spreadsheet is meant to remain a working Office file, rather than becoming a separate output that has to be rebuilt before a team can use it.
Home brings Chat and Cowork together
Home is also generally available. It puts short questions and longer delegated tasks behind one prompt box, with a shared history for Chat and Cowork conversations.
Microsoft says Copilot can recommend Cowork when a request needs more work than a chat answer. If the user agrees, it carries over the request and relevant context instead of requiring them to start again.
Projects, another generally available feature, groups related conversations, tasks and outputs. Users can add instructions and source material for a continuing piece of work, while the resulting files remain stored in OneDrive.
Not every Ignite feature is broadly available
The wider announcement includes several products at earlier stages. Today, a view covering email, meetings, Teams and tasks, is in private preview. Shared Workspaces and the proactive Autopilot agent are reaching customers through Frontier, Microsoft's early-access programme.
Code, which lets people describe an application they want built, is due to become generally available in the coming weeks. Those separate timetables mean the Office release should not be read as a launch date for every capability shown at Ignite.
Microsoft says the subscription licence covers the creation of Excel spreadsheets and PowerPoint presentations, with no separate usage meter for that work. That statement does not make all of its autonomous-agent services free or remove their separate access conditions.
The bottom line
Office inside Copilot is now a generally available way to create and collaborate on familiar Microsoft files. The broader shift towards ongoing AI work is still arriving in stages, with preview features and future releases sitting alongside the newly available Office tools.

Microsoft announced the next stage of its Copilot app at Ignite 2026, which runs from 17 to 20 November in San Francisco and online. The headline for office workers is a split. Office in Copilot, which lets you create Word, Excel and PowerPoint files from the Copilot chat, is generally available. Copilot Code, Autopilot and Workspaces are all in Frontier, Microsoft's early access program, which an IT admin has to turn on. Knowing which tool fits your job, and which ones you can actually open, saves a lot of trial and error.
What Microsoft announced at Ignite
The announcements build on a rebuild Microsoft revealed on 25 September, when Jared Spataro introduced a new Copilot app organised around Home, Code and Autopilot. At Ignite, Microsoft's Copilot blog and its Book of News set out the status of each part. Microsoft says Home, where Chat and Cowork sit together, and Office in Copilot are now generally available. Cowork is the mode that takes a multistep task, such as an RFP response, through to a finished result.
Three newer tools are still previews. Microsoft says Autopilot "is starting to rollout to customers in Frontier", that Workspaces "is now available in Frontier", and that Copilot Code will become generally available "in the coming weeks". The Book of News is more precise on Code and says general availability comes in December. A fourth preview, Today, a morning briefing of mail, meetings and tasks, is in private preview in the Copilot app and Outlook.
Which feature fits which job
Each of the four main tools suits a different kind of work. We matched them to roles using Microsoft's own descriptions; we have not tested them ourselves.
The writer: Office in Copilot. You can create, edit and co-author Word, Excel and PowerPoint files from a Copilot conversation. Files save to OneDrive and keep Microsoft 365 permissions and sensitivity labels. Status: generally available.
The builder: Copilot Code. You describe an app, an interactive dashboard or a desktop widget, and Copilot builds it, using the same underlying technology as GitHub Copilot. Microsoft says apps can be shared like Word documents. Status: Frontier now, on Windows, web, iOS and Android, with general availability in December according to the Book of News.
The delegator: Autopilot. A long-running agent with its own identity, memory and tools, which Microsoft previously called Scout. Each Autopilot gets a Microsoft Entra Agent ID and a named manager, and colleagues can @mention it in a Teams channel or group chat. Status: rolling out through Frontier.
The team lead: Workspaces. A shared space where people and agents work toward one goal with shared context. It evolved from Copilot Notebooks. Status: Frontier, with personal Autopilots joining "soon", according to the Book of News.
The switch: what Frontier means
Frontier is Microsoft's program for preview features. According to Microsoft Learn, an admin turns it on in the Microsoft 365 admin center, under Release preferences or the Copilot settings, for all users or for named groups. It can take three hours for features to appear. To use the Copilot features in Frontier, a person needs a Microsoft Copilot licence or a licence that includes Copilot.
Microsoft is clear about the trade-offs. Frontier features are previews and "subject to change", they may not be covered by standard support commitments, and the documentation says certain Frontier experiences "may only be available on a paid basis". It also notes that HIPAA business associate coverage does not apply to Frontier experiences, which matters for health care teams. The same page says Frontier features do not meet ISO 27001, 27002 or 27018 requirements, which matters for any team whose contracts depend on those certifications.
What it costs
Microsoft now runs two billing models. A per-user subscription covers everyday use such as Chat and Copilot in Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and Teams. Microsoft's Ignite announcements do not say outright whether Office in Copilot counts as that everyday use, so check with your admin before a wide rollout. Cowork, Code and Autopilot are billed by usage, so cost rises with how much you use them. The Book of News adds that Today's morning briefing is included with a Microsoft 365 Copilot licence, while its updates through the day use Copilot credits.
Microsoft published a cost study on 16 November, run with GigaOm, that put Copilot at $30 a month for an everyday user and $96 for an advanced user, and found it cheaper than ChatGPT Enterprise and Claude Enterprise. Readers should weigh that with care, because Microsoft says it commissioned and funded the research and ran the testing itself.
How we got here, and what is still open
Microsoft has been reshaping Copilot all year. TNW, reporting on the September launch, noted that Microsoft's Jacob Andreou described the coding feature as catching up with rivals that already bundle chat, coding and agents, and that CNBC had reported fewer than 7% of Microsoft's commercial Office seats carried the Copilot add-on. We could not confirm that seat figure independently. Andreou also said the autonomy that makes long-running agents useful is the same thing that worries IT admins.
Several questions remain. Microsoft has not given general availability dates for Autopilot or Workspaces, and WindowsForum noted that some live coverage of Ignite blurred the line between released and preview features. We think Office in Copilot is the one tool most people should use now, and the three previews are worth a small, capped test before anyone builds a process around them.
What to do this week
Open the Copilot app and try Office in Copilot on a real task, such as turning meeting notes into a one-page brief.
Ask your IT admin four questions: is Frontier on for you, do you have a Microsoft 365 Copilot licence, is there a spending cap on usage-billed tools, and which preview features are approved.
If you get Frontier access, give Code one small job, such as a dashboard of open requests by owner, and check every number against the source.
Before setting up Autopilot, agree with your manager what it may send on your behalf.
