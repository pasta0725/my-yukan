PC Watch
2026.11.19
Developer krisgfx9 has released BatteryHub, an open-source tray tool that shows battery levels for peripherals from Logitech, Razer, SteelSeries, Corsair and HyperX, plus PlayStation and Xbox controllers, in the taskbar. PC Watch reports it supports Windows 10/11, is about 350KB, needs no admin rights, and is GPL v3.0 or later.

PC Watch：マウスやキーボードの電池残量がタスクバーで確認できる「BatteryHub」
以下抜粋----------------------------------------------
 　krisgfx9氏は、ワイヤレス機器のバッテリ残量をタスクバーに表示するオープンソースツール「BatteryHub」を公開している。無料で利用でき、対応OSはWindows 10/11。Linux向けの実験的なビルドも用意されている。
　BatteryHubは、
マウス
や
キーボード
、ヘッドセット、ゲームコントローラなどの残量をまとめて確認できる常駐ツール。各社の専用ソフトを起動せずに機器と直接通信するほか、専用ソフトとの併用も可能だ。アプリ本体は約350KB、メモリ使用量は約2MBと軽量で、外部ランタイムや管理者権限を必要としない。
 　タスクバーの通知領域の横に機器のアイコンと残量を表示し、15%以下になると数値が赤く変わる。充電中は青い雷のアイコンで表示したり、スリープ中の機器は直前の取得値を薄暗く表示したりする機能も備える。Windowsのライト/ダークモードやDPIスケーリングにも対応する。
　対応機器には、Logitech、Razer、SteelSeries、Corsair、HyperXなどの周辺機器や、PlayStation/Xboxのコントローラが含まれる。Windowsの
Bluetooth
設定で残量を確認できる機器にも対応するが、メーカーや接続方法によっては未対応の製品もある。
　GitHubのReleasesページからインストーラをダウンロードでき、ポータブル版も提供されている。ソースコードはGPL v3.0以降で公開されている。

===== https://github.com/krisgfx9/Wireless-Device-Battery-Monitor
(status 200)
BatteryHub
Formerly 
Wireless Battery
. Version 1.1.0 renamed the app; an update keeps your settings, startup choice and battery history.
Battery levels of your wireless mouse, headset, keyboard and controller, right on the Windows taskbar.
Just want it working?
 Download 
BatteryHub-Setup
 from the latest release, run it and click through. That's it.
Experienced users
 can grab the portable zip, use the 
command-line options
, or 
build it from source
 with 
make
 or 
build.bat
.
Like it?
 BatteryHub is free and open source. If it saves you a trip into vendor software, you can support it on 
Ko-fi
, 
PayPal
 or 
GitHub Sponsors
. It helps me keep my apps free alongside my studies. Not able to donate? A ⭐ star, sharing it, or reporting an unsupported device with a 
--dump
 helps just as much.
Tiny
: a ~350 KB app using about 2 MB of RAM. It's plain C++/Win32, with no runtime and no background service, and installs in seconds without admin rights.
No vendor software needed
: it talks to the devices directly. G HUB, Synapse, iCUE, GG and NGenuity can stay closed (or keep running; they don't conflict).
Many brands
: Logitech, Razer, SteelSeries, Corsair, HyperX, Glorious, Jabra, JBL, PlayStation and Xbox controllers, Samsung Galaxy Buds, any Bluetooth device Windows knows a battery level for, and every headset the bundled 
HeadsetControl
 supports.
Display
On the taskbar (default):
🎧 30%  🖱 93%
 next to the notification area, drawn with the Windows icon font.
Low battery:
 a red number means 15% or less.
Charging:
 a blue ⚡ appears next to the number. Once the device is fully charged and still on power, it turns into a green ✓.
Asleep:
 dimmed text means the device is asleep and the last known value is shown.
Disconnected (optional):
 turn on 
Show disconnected devices
 to display a dimmed 
×
 for devices whose driver explicitly reports an offline wireless link. This does not infer disconnection from a missed battery poll; the default remains to hide offline devices.
Two devices of one kind:
 with two mice (or two controllers...) both would get the same icon, so each one gets a tiny caption of up to 3 letters in the corner of its icon, taken from its name (
MX
, 
PRO
), or a number when the names do not tell them apart. A device that is alone in its kind shows no caption.
Theme:
 it follows light/dark mode and DPI scaling.
Transparent taskbar themes
 (e.g. Windhawk's Taskbar Styler): turn on 
Background behind taskbar text
 in the menu to keep the text readable over the wallpaper.
Several monitors:
 the widget shows on every monitor's taskbar, next to its clock, so it stays visible while a game runs fullscreen. 
Show the widget on
 in the menu picks 
All monitors
, 
Main monitor only
, or one display (e.g. only 
Display 3
).
More display options:
Icons above percentages
 stacks each icon over its level to save width; 
Brand colors
 shows known brands in their color (Razer green, HyperX red, ...) with a small level bar; two devices of the same kind get a tiny caption so they can be told apart; left and right taskbars get a column of devices.
Languages:
 English, العربية, Deutsch, Español, فارسی, Français, עברית, 日本語, 한국어, Magyar, Polski, Português (Brasil), Русский, Svenska, Tiếng Việt, Türkçe, Українська, 简体中文 and 繁體中文. The app follows the Windows display language; 
Language
 in the menu picks another one. See 
Translations
 to add or improve a language.
Brand colors:
 devices of 15 known brands get their maker's color, with a soft glow and a thin level bar under the
percentage: Razer green, Logitech blue, SteelSeries orange, Corsair yellow, HyperX / ROG / ZOWIE red, PlayStation,
Xbox, Samsung, Apple, Jabra, JBL, Cooler Master and Steam. Each color has a dark- and a light-taskbar shade. The brand
comes from the driver that found the device, then the vendor ID, then the name; colors and rules are in
src/brands.cpp
. Turn it off with 
Brand colors
 in the menu.
Tray icons:
 one icon per device with the number drawn in it, plus a "Low battery" notification at 15%. The app switches to this automatically when the taskbar is vertical or can't be found.
Custom device names:
 open the device submenu and select 
Rename device...
 to set a name
(up to 64 characters). The name is shown in the taskbar widget's tooltip/caption,
tray tooltip and notifications, and the device menu. Use 
Reset to original name
to restore the driver's name. Names are stored locally in the current user's
Windows registry (
Software\WirelessBattery
, 
DeviceAliases
), using the stable
device key. Renaming never changes driver matching, protocol queries, or 
--list
 /
--dump
 diagnostic output. No account or network access is needed.
Hover for details. Click for the menu: every device (click one to hide or show it), display mode, 
Refresh now
, 
Start with Windows
, and 
Exit
. Levels refresh every 60 seconds (
Check battery every
 in the menu changes this), and right away when a device is plugged in or the PC wakes up.
Smart Battery Alerts (experimental)
You can set a 
per-device low-battery notification threshold
 from each device's
submenu: 
Battery alerts
 → 
Use default (15%)
, 
Disable notifications
,
or one of 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25%, 30%, 35%, 40%. The existing 15% default stays
unchanged unless you choose an override. Settings are stored by device key in
the current user's Windows registry (
Software\WirelessBattery
, 
AlertThresholds
).
An alert is triggered only from a real, fresh 0–100% battery reading when the
device is connected, awake and not charging. An alert is issued once, and will
re-arm after the level climbs more than 10 percentage points above the chosen
threshold (or when charging begins). Missing readings or sleeping devices do
not retrigger alerts. Hidden devices don't produce notifications. Disabling
notifications for one device does not hide it or change its percentage.
Both taskbar-widget and tray-icon modes support notifications. Since Windows
needs a notification-area icon to display a legacy balloon, widget mode may
show a temporary notification icon for up to one minute after an alert.
Widget colours and the low-battery highlight still use the project's
original global 15% threshold, independently of the notification setting.
Battery history & insights
Right-click a device in the taskbar widget or tray menu and choose 
Battery history...
.
The native, DPI-aware viewer (it follows the Windows light/dark theme) shows recorded battery levels with connected sample lines over the last 
24 hours
, 
7 days
, or 
30 days
, along with the latest reading, lowest/highest level, change in percentage points, and sample count. You can clear the selected device's history from the viewer.
History is recorded 
locally only
 for connected, awake devices reporting a numeric percentage. Readings are sampled at most once every 30 minutes per device (except charging-state transitions, with a 60-second minimum). Data is kept for up to 30 days and the local file is kept under about 2 MB. The app never probes hardware beyond its existing polling, sends no history data over the network, and makes a conservative, approximate time-to-empty estimate from recent discharge observations only. At least four points, two hours, a three-percentage-point decline, a fresh reading and a stable discharge segment are required. Charging, old readings or insufficient data suppress the estimate; device-reported runtime is not available. The estimate is not a guarantee. Missing chart data is shown as gaps, not interpolated.
Turn off 
Record battery history
 in the menu to stop saving readings; what was already saved stays until you clear it in the viewer. The file is stored in 
%LOCALAPPDATA%\WirelessBattery\battery_history.tsv
. Its device identities are encoded (not encrypted); the file is local to your Windows account. In 
--demo
, the chart uses synthetic data that is never saved. 
Note:
 history starts collecting only after this feature is installed and the app has been running for a while.
