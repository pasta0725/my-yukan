Adobe is updating Premiere with a ton of AI-based tools and assistance as well as promising a new, wholly revamped approach to color grading.
Color Mode is Out of Beta
The revamp to color grading has been in beta since last April but is now being rolled out to the production version of the software. Called Color Mode, it replaces the Lumetri Color Panel that has been Adobe’s approach to color grading for the last several years. This new color editor was touted as a “first-of-its-kind” color grading system and appears to be a direct response to the far more widely beloved system inside of DaVinci Resolve. 
“Most Color Mode controls are bi-directional. Move your mouse up and down for one control, left and right for the other. Or press Shift for precise single-axis adjustments. Each control activates a unique heads-up display with scopes, numeric info, and real-time animated feedback that guides your creativity with confidence and clarity,” Adobe touts. 
https://petapixel.com/assets/uploads/2026/11/Color-Mode.mp4
The company says that controlling this system is meant to be intuitive as “it works the way you think.” New bi-directional controls animate as they are used, and grading can be handled on single clips, groups, or entire sequences without layers. 
“No more copying and pasting attributes. Actually understand how color is flowing through your timeline,” Adobe says. 
AI Assistance and Generative Media Tools
Color Mode has been a point of discussion for months, but the other updates to Premiere are both newer and very much AI-based. Firstly, Adobe is deploying its Creative Agent, which is an AI assistant, into the video editor. The company says that this agent allows editors to direct and execute complex, multi-step workflows using natural language prompts. It can apply effects across every clip in a sequence, track masked subjects through a frame, and adjust color and exposure across an entire sequence simultaneously, for example. 
https://petapixel.com/assets/uploads/2026/11/PR-Creative-Agent-in-AI-Assistant.mp4
Adobe is also adding a Generative Media Tool, which brings generative AI directly into a timeline to create missing video clips (like b-roll), sound effects, soundscapes, and background music. The AI behind it can be Adobe Firefly, but can also use the likes of Google, Kling, Runway, and Luma. 
https://petapixel.com/assets/uploads/2026/11/Gen-Media-Tool.mp4
Adobe is also adding AI-powered audio tools that can clean up, separate, and refine audio. Premiere can now separate audio sources into stems that can be edited individually (a feature that was also recently added to Apple’s Logic Pro). Adobe says it is also capable of separating crosstalk, which means breaking overlapping speaking voices onto independent tracks. 
Coming to the beta version, Premiere Pro is also getting a new Generative Remove tool that can remove objects from video (Adobe provides examples such as boom mics, logos, or background distractions). Editors can mask and track the object, and Premiere can fill the space with “realistic detail” while leaving the rest of the clip untouched. 
The updates to Premiere Pro will start rolling out to Creative Cloud subscribers starting today. 
Premiere Mobile
Finally, as 
announced
 last week, Adobe is adding access to hundreds of thousands of licensed music tracks for use in non-commercial videos. 
“Music from UMG and WMG is available starting today in Premiere on iPhone for use in personal, non-commercial videos, with availability on Android coming soon. For projects that require commercially licensed music, you can continue to access Adobe Stock Audio,” Adobe says. 
Adobe is also promising that the Agentic editing experience it is bringing to Premiere Pro will also be coming to Premiere Mobile “soon.”

Adobe unveils new AI tools for Photoshop, Premiere, and more, with MCP support coming in 2027
Marcus Mendes
 | Nov 10 2026 - 6:18 am PT																
								0 Comments							
Adobe
 today announced a sweeping set of updates at its annual Adobe MAX conference, focused on giving users more ways to automate complex workflows, edit content, and work across its creative apps. Here are the details.
At the Adobe MAX event today, the company unveiled a series of features, many of them AI-powered or AI-assisted, that will begin rolling out across its suite of creative platforms as early as today.
Photoshop
In Photoshop, the new 
Relight
 tool offers a way to non-destructively and dynamically add, reposition, and resize light sources in the composition, while 
Generative Restyle
lets users** prompt the platform to generate variations of an image’s style and fine-tune the results using adjustable sliders.
Photoshop is also adding a Quick Compare slider to make it easier to track before-and-after results, and is getting several features in the beta app, including, per Adobe:
Mockups
 to quickly and intuitively composite an image or design onto an object or surface with realistic results.
Camera Angles (beta)
 to explore new viewpoints of an entire image through direct, on-canvas interactions.
Generative Recompose
 to reposition an image and its related elements into a new aspect ratio while maintaining the structural composition without manual editing.
Convert to Text Layers
 to extract text from a flattened image and turn it into a fully editable type layer.
Timelapse
 to capture and share your entire creative process.
Remove Ambient Effect Only
 to remove distracting atmospheric effects like lens flare, haze, or stray shadows or reflections, without removing any objects.
Following its acquisition of Topaz Labs, Adobe is also introducing two new AI image enhancement models: 
Wonder 4
, which combines upscaling, denoising, deblurring, and sharpening into a single operation, and 
Image HDR
, which converts standard images into HDR with improved brightness and detail.
Premiere and Lightroom
Adobe Premiere is also getting generative tools that make it easier to prepare raw files for a project, including 
Enhance Audio
, 
Separate Crosstalk
, and 
Separate Sound Source
. Adobe is also adding a 
Generative Remove tool
 to Premiere in beta, “bringing you the power to remove unwanted objects, like boom mics and logos or background distractions, directly on your timeline,” says the company.
Premiere is also getting Color Mode, a new color-grading tool starting today, which “supports everything from quick corrections to more advanced grades while keeping your video as the focus.”
Another interesting tool making its way into Premiere is 
Generative Media Tool
. Here’s Adobe:
The Generative Media Tool brings Generate Video, Generate Sound Effects, Generate Soundscape and Generate Music directly into your timeline in Premiere. You can generate missing b-roll, background music or sound for a scene using context from your edit, then keep refining the result in your timeline. You can also choose from Adobe Firefly and models from partners including Google, Kling, Runway and Luma based on the creative needs of your project.
As for iPhone users who rely on Premiere Mobile, Adobe announced that its 
newly established partnerships with UMG and WMG
 will bring hundreds of thousands of licensed music tracks to the app (for non-commercial use) starting today, with users able to explore trending songs, discover featured artists, and create videos using artist-inspired templates.
Here’s Adobe:
Starting today, creators can:
Find a song that fits the moment:
 Explore hundreds of thousands of licensed tracks, including music from artists like Olivia Dean, Noah Kahan and more.
Tap into what’s trending:
 Discover popular tracks, browse featured artists and explore the music people are listening to now.
Get inspired by the music:
 Start with artist-inspired templates tied to the songs, artists and cultural moments you love.
Premiere mobile is also getting agentic tools soon, including 
Quick Cuts
, which generates a first cut based on the user’s prompt and the materials they add, and an AI Assistant, which helps users find the right editing tools and execute tasks based on natural language instructions.
In Lightroom, the company is adding multiple new tools that make it easier to edit or more quickly fix small imperfections. That includes 
Glow
, which “adds soft, cinematic light to your photos,” 
Blemish Removal
 for quick skin fine-tuning, 
Open Eyes
 in beta, and an improved and expanded 
Camera Tethering
 on Desktop and the iPad, with support for more cameras and advanced shooting controls, plus the ability to connect cameras directly to an iPad for live previews and photo editing.
Illustrator and InDesign
Adobe is adding new brushes, gradients, patterns, and graphic styles to Illustrator, alongside new 
Blend
 controls, which “make it easier to develop and refine designs with a persistent control panel and live preview,” per the company.
As for InDesign, a new Co-editing feature, available in beta, streamlines collaborative work, while the new Flex Layout tool dynamically adjusts content based on the canvas size and artwork. InDesign is also getting 
Check Document
 and 
Rewrite
 that more quickly “help identify common editorial and accessibility issues while you’re still working.”
MCP integration
While it will not launch until 2027, this feature will enable users to connect Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere, and After Effects to the AI tools and agents they already use.
In practice, this will mean connecting Photoshop to ChatGPT and having them work in tandem to adapt an entire campaign based on new artwork you develop and attach to your prompt.
This integration will also account for other connected tools, so the user can ask ChatGPT to make edits, add new files to a Google Drive folder, update the team on Slack, and so on.
Here’s Adobe on the upcoming MCP integration:
Because it runs locally on your computer, your agent works directly in your native project files, and your layers, vectors, bins and sequences stay editable.
The company notes that MCP integrations will enter private beta in early 2027, and users can 
sign up here
 to learn more.
Adobe Firefly
According to Adobe, Firefly is getting even better at helping users organize and edit their raw material for tasks such as turning travel clips into a vlog, creating social media campaigns, or assembling videos from storyboards.
The new 
Firefly Video
 lets users organize scenes, assemble clips, add captions, and generate transitions and B-roll, while 
Firefly Design
 brings illustration, image editing, animation, and 3D tools into a single canvas.
Adobe is also adding 
Quick Actions
 for common editing tasks, 
Firefly Graph
 for creating reusable workflows, and 
Firefly Builder
 (in private beta) for prototyping websites and apps using natural language.
Firefly’s AI Assistant is also getting new capabilities, including 
Plan Mode
, which lets users review and approve an agent’s proposed actions, and 
Model Selector
, which lets them choose which AI model to use.
Adobe is also previewing 
Always-on agents
, which can handle recurring tasks such as organizing assets, checking quality, and preparing content for different platforms in the background, even when users step away.
Adobe Creative Agent
Adobe is also expanding its 
Creative Agent
 across Creative Cloud, with AI Assistants now available in beta in Illustrator, Premiere, InDesign, After Effects, and Frame.io, as well as the Photoshop beta app.
These assistants allow users to execute complex, multi-step tasks using natural language prompts. In Premiere, for example, users can ask the assistant to apply effects to an entire sequence, track subjects across frames, or adjust color and exposure across multiple clips.
To learn more about these and other features announced at Adobe MAX today, stay tuned to the company’s 
blog
.
