Wall Street is debating what caused a sharp swing in Anthropic's shares two days after the developer of artificial intelligence (AI) tool Claude completed a record initial public offering (IPO).
Anthropic logo.
The Wall Street Journal (WSJ) reported on the matter on the 21st, local time. According to the report, factors cited in the share price decline included the exclusion of some investors from the IPO allocation, the decision to allow employees to immediately sell large amounts of stock, and the underwriters' pricing at the start of trading.
Anthropic shares closed at $88.91 on the New York stock market the previous day, down 8.5% from the previous close. That would wipe $109 billion off its market capitalization in a single day. The stock fell as much as 15% during trading, dropping to just 5 cents above its $83 offering price.
One issue investors have raised is how the IPO shares were allocated. More than half of the $81 billion in shares allocated in the IPO went to existing investors, including Altimeter Capital and Lightspeed Venture Partners. Although the allocation was intended to reward early investors, critics said the proportion was unusually high.
By contrast, about 60% of institutions that sought IPO shares reportedly did not receive a single share. BlackRock reportedly received only about half of the roughly $10 billion in shares it requested.
The allocation of substantial amounts to major investment firms such as Citadel also prompted speculation that large-scale users of Claude may have been given priority.
Concerns were also raised that the opening trading price may have been set too high. An excessively high opening price could lead to sustained selling, making it difficult to reverse a decline in the stock, according to the analysis.
The decision to allow stock sales immediately after the listing is also seen as contributing to the decline. Anthropic allowed some current and former employees to immediately sell approximately 300 million shares they held. Those shares were worth about $26 billion at the offering price.
Companies typically impose lockup periods that restrict employees from selling their shares for several months after a listing. But Anthropic made an unusual decision to allow some employees to sell immediately.
Under these circumstances, underwriters have limited means to support the price if it falls below the offering price. Anthropic said the overallotment option, or greenshoe, that underwriters can use to stabilize the share price has already been exercised.
The WSJ said approximately 300 million additional shares are scheduled to become available for trading on the 15th of next month, potentially increasing selling pressure.
