Introducing unified tasks and plans for Copilot and Microsoft 365
Ashna_Patel
Microsoft
Nov 18, 2026
For years, customers have had to decide between using Microsoft To Do, basic plans in Planner, or premium plans in Planner for tracking their tasks and managing work. Capabilities varied across these experiences, creating confusion and limiting how easily work could flow across tools, integrations, and AI experiences. Moving forward, this will no longer be a hurdle.
Today, we are introducing 
unified plans
, a single system for tracking your work as an individual, as a team, and as an organization. The new unified plans service will be available across Microsoft 365—whether in Outlook, Microsoft Teams, Planner, and Loop. Additionally, unified plans are now available in Microsoft Copilot and Microsoft Cowork, with availability in Autopilot also planned.
A unified platform for people and agents across Microsoft 365
Unified plans bring features from To Do and basic and premium plan types together on one shared foundation, where premium features are available according to your assigned Microsoft Copilot, Planner Plan 1, or Planner and Project Plan 3 license. Premium features such as 
Goals view
, 
Timeline view
, and 
custom fields
 have already rolled out in unified plans, and additional premium features such as 
sprints and backlogs
, 
task hierarchy
, dependencies, and more are on 
the roadmap
.
Also to note, our licensing structure and collaboration capabilities are not changing. Planner users will be able to collaborate on tasks together in shared plans, regardless of which license they have. For example, a project lead with Planner Plan 1 can set goals for a plan while teammates with Planner in Microsoft 365 can track and update the tasks corresponding to those goals. This allows teams to coordinate work in a single shared plan without requiring all team members to have the same license. For a full list of features available by plan, see the 
Planner plans and pricing comparison
.
Additionally, this unified platform makes accessing Planner through Copilot, Cowork and Autopilot easier because it’s all part of Work IQ. In short, we’re building one platform for Microsoft 365 tasks and plans that connects the work people manage individually, the plans they execute together, and the tools and agents that help them move that work forward.
Build on a shared foundation
Unified plans bring task and planning capabilities onto a shared foundation to reduce the complexity of working across separate systems, and support ongoing Planner extensibility. As part of our strategy to move toward unified plans, we’re transitioning to a fully Azure-based service. We will no longer be investing in Dataverse-based plans and will be bringing premium capabilities, Microsoft 365, and Copilot integration to our Azure-based unified task and plan infrastructure.
Soon we will be redesigning the plan creation experience to minimize confusion for our users by removing the “Basic” label from unified plans and templates. Premium plans will remain supported as we transition, though we encourage our users to begin adopting unified plans (currently created as a basic plan).

Extend your Planner plans
Today, To Do and basic and premium plans have separate APIs and connectors, with differences in the planning data and capabilities they expose. With a shared foundation, we now have a single set of extensibility options across Planner. This also provides more consistent ways for organizations to build integrations without requiring a different approach for each plan type, with access to more planning capabilities as API support expands.
If you need to extend your Planner plans, we recommend using the extensibility options below:
Microsoft Graph APIs
 let developers work programmatically with plans and tasks—for example, by creating tasks from an organization’s intake process or connecting Planner data to a custom application. The first version of the 
unified task and plan Graph APIs are now in beta
. These APIs will also include the new ‘default plan’ where an individual’s tasks will be stored.
Power Automate
connects Planner to business workflows. The direction is toward a single Planner connector that works across the unified platform, reducing the need for separate workflows for different plan types.
Model Context Protocol (MCP) server
makes it easier to connect Planner capabilities into agent-driven workflows. Designed for custom agents, the Planner MCP server is a part of Work IQ and connects planning and execution across Copilot and other Microsoft 365 experiences.
If your organization is investing in extending or customizing Planner, we encourage you to build unified plans using these extensibility options wherever they support your requirements.
What’s new and what’s coming soon to unified plans in Planner
Since moving basic plans to the unified foundation 
back in February
, we’ve introduced more than a dozen features to unified plans, including 
Goals view
, 
task chat
, 
custom templates
, 
Copilot integration
, 
Capacity view
, 
Timeline view
, and 
more
. You’re likely familiar with several of these features which already exist in premium plans today.
Additionally, we’re excited to announce new features that are currently rolling out or will soon be rolling out in unified plans. Together, as part of one unified plan type, these features will help individuals and teams:
Organize and visualize work
Collaborate where work happens
Structure larger efforts and understand progress
Manage your portfolios
Recognize changes and team capacity
Work with Copilot and agents in your plans
Organize and visualize work
Alongside the recently rolled out custom templates, which enable teams to reuse their meticulously-structured plans instead of starting from scratch, we're introducing features such as 
conditional coloring
, 
bucket colors
, and 
custom views
 to allow users to more easily scan their plans and focus on the work that matters to them.
Conditional coloring
is currently rolling out in unified plans in Grid view. Similar to conditional formatting in Excel, this feature lets users visually highlight tasks based on selected fields, conditions, and values to ensure that important task details are easier to spot.
A screenshot of a plan in Grid view shows the Conditional Coloring window open with a list of options—Task name, Priority, Status, Due date, Start date, Finish date, Assigned to, Label, Completion (%)—to select from the Field drop-down.
We’ve also brought bucket colors to unified plans, so users can color code their buckets by selecting the paint palette icon that appears next to the bucket title on hover.
A screenshot of a plan in Board view shows the paint palette icon next to the bucket title. The color picker for a bucket has been opened, showing 25 colors to choose from.
Custom views
, once rolled out in unified plans, will allow users to save a customized view of a plan and return to it later simply by selecting the 
Custom views
 drop-down in the upper right corner and selecting 
Save current view
. With custom views, team members will be able to reuse saved views without having to reapply filters, sorting, grouping, and other customizations.
A screenshot of a Planner plan in Board view shows the

Collaborate where work happens
With 
task chat
, which rolled out in unified plans earlier this year, users can ask a question, share an update, or bring someone directly into a task using @mentions to notify team members in Outlook and in Teams. Users can also now manage their tasks and shared plans through the Planner app in Outlook.
Additionally, Planner can now be 
added as a tab
 in both private and shared Teams channels, allowing team members to access their unified plans where they already collaborate.
Finally, tasks assigned through collaborative recurring meeting notes in Microsoft Teams can 
soon be tracked in existing Planner plans
, alongside other work, helping users both remember and follow through with tasks assigned in Teams meetings.
Structure larger efforts and understand progress
Task hierarchy
, which is planned to roll out in unified plans, will allow teams to break down larger efforts into tasks with subtasks in Grid view. With task hierarchy, you’ll be able to more easily track your workstreams and define larger tasks in greater detail while maintaining a simple, low-maintenance structure using 
Add a new subtask
 or
 Make this a subtask
 from the 
More options
 (three horizontal dots) drop-down menu.

 and

You will also be able to easily undo this by selecting 
Promote task
.

 drop-down menu with options to

 and

. Please note, design may change.
Teams and leads need to see when work is happening and how that work connects to larger goals. With 
Timeline view
, which rolled out in unified plans in October, teams can view work over time, while 
Goals view
 in unified plans allows users to connect their tasks to overarching team goals and update goal details as work moves forward.
Manage your portfolios
Connected plans
 allows users to connect plans together, creating a portfolio view across the team's work. By combining this with custom columns and all the tools in Planner, managing a portfolio becomes easier, while allowing individual teams to manage subtasks, owners, and delivery dates in a dedicated plan.
All of this data is available in Copilot which allows you to build rich reports and workflows in seconds.
A screenshot of a portfolio of tasks in Grid view shows four project tasks, each linked to its own connected plan.
Recognize changes and team capacity
As plans grow, teams also need to understand what has changed and how work is distributed. 
Task history
, which is currently rolling out in unified plans, will help teams stay up to date on changes made to a task. With task history, you can view recent activity to see what changed, when it changed, and track progress over time.
Capacity view
, which rolled out in unified plans in October, allows team leads to review their plan’s work by assignee and time. With Capacity view, you can see scheduled and unscheduled tasks together in a single view, understand who is working on tasks when, and adjust work across weeks if plans change. Select the 
Show unscheduled
 toggle to view unscheduled tasks in a separate pane to the right of the capacity view, and drag and drop unscheduled tasks to assign the work based on capacity.
A screenshot of the new Capacity view in Planner shows task bars by assignee and a list of unscheduled tasks in the right pane.
Work with Copilot and agents in your plans
Unified plans are where AI capabilities in Planner come together. With a Microsoft Copilot add-on license, the Planner Agent can work directly in your plans. For example, teams can 
assign tasks to the agent
 or ask the agent to 
generate status reports
 grounded in their plan’s details. Additionally, users with a Microsoft Copilot license can manage Planner tasks directly in 
Microsoft Copilot
 and Copilot Chat.
The 
Planner Plugin allows Cowork
 to directly read and take action across plans at scale and the 
Planner MCP server
 is part of the Work IQ APIs bringing Planner capabilities into custom agent-driven workflows. Together, these experiences connect the tools that help people move work forward in the plans that organize it.
Personal task management in Planner and what’s next for Microsoft To Do
Alongside these improvements to unified plans, we’re making Planner a simpler place to manage your own work. We’ll soon roll out a 
redesigned My tasks experience
 that makes it easier to capture and track your own tasks, keep important work front and center, and stay on top of tasks assigned to you. For people using work or school accounts, Planner brings individual task management and team planning together in one place.
A preview of the updated My tasks experience in Planner for the web. Please note, design may change.
Going forward, new private tasks created in Planner will be stored directly in Planner, in a 
private plan
 just for you, rather than in To Do. Bringing individual and shared work onto the same foundation helps us deliver more consistent capabilities and connect your own tasks with Copilot, Cowork, and other agents.
As we bring personal task management into Planner, we will phase out To Do. The To Do apps will retire on June 30, 2027. Connections that other apps use to access To Do tasks will continue beyond that date, and we’ll provide separate notice before those connections are retired. We’re also updating existing task integrations across Microsoft 365 to connect with Planner, so you’ll be able to create or manage tasks in the Microsoft experiences you already use. Although To Do tasks are already visible in Planner, they will not be migrated to the new private plan. Customers using personal Microsoft accounts will need to choose another app. Our 
To Do transition guidance
  will help you understand what’s changing and plan your next steps.
What this means for your organization
When creating a new plan in Planner, we recommend creating a new basic (unified) plan which supports the capabilities and integrations your organization needs. Some scenarios may still require premium plans in the interim while premium features are being brought into unified plans. If you need to extend your plan, we recommend using the extensibility options 
highlighted above
.
A screenshot of the existing 
Create a plan
 dialog shows both options for creating a Basic plan or a Premium plan.
For admins, unified plans do not require a Dataverse environment or Dataverse storage capacity. Existing premium plans continue to use Dataverse.
For organizations using To Do, preparing for this change includes confirming Planner access for affected users and reviewing workflows and integrations that depend on To Do. Our 
To Do transition guidance
 will help admins prepare users for the move to Planner and understand what’s changing for existing tasks.
Unified plans are the foundation for our work to connect personal tasks, team plans, and agents across Microsoft 365. Bringing that work together gives people and agents clearer context about goals and ownership to carry work forward.
For upcoming Planner capabilities and estimated release dates, visit the 
Microsoft Copilot Roadmap
.
What’s happening with premium plans
One of the most common questions we’ve been hearing is, "What's happening with premium plans?" You've likely noticed that our most recently released features work only with basic plans. That includes AI capabilities such as 
Planner capabilities in Microsoft Copilot
, 
Planner Agent in Copilot
, the 
Planner plugin for Cowork
, and the 
Planner MCP server in Work IQ
. These capabilities rely on the Planner MCP server, which supports only basic plans and their data. Other new Planner features, such as 
Task chat
, 
Custom templates
, 
Capacity view
, have also rolled out in basic plans and will not be available in premium plans.
It’s important to note that basic plans are now unified plans, built on one shared foundation, that eliminate the division between plan types and the tradeoffs that come with it. Moving forward, all new Planner features and capabilities will be developed for and rolled out to unified plans—and over time, we will continue to incrementally bring premium features to unified plans.

Microsoft To Do is retiring, including Mac and iOS apps
Zac Hall
 | Nov 19 2026 - 7:55 am PT																
								10 Comments							
Microsoft is saying goodbye to its task management service To Do, including the Mac and iOS apps. Here’s what users need to know.
Microsoft sets June 30 shutdown date for To Do on Mac and iPhone
The last day to use the To Do apps is June 30, 2027. That date also applies to Windows, Android, and the web. The underlying service and sync support will retire later, according to 
Microsoft’s support document
.
“Microsoft To Do is retiring,” Microsoft explains. “For work or school accounts, Microsoft Planner will be the destination for managing personal work tasks alongside assigned work. The updated My tasks experience begins rolling out in December 2026.”
On the Mac, you can use Planner on the web or in Teams. Microsoft explicitly says it has no plans for a native Mac app.
You can still see your existing To Do tasks in Planner through June 30, but your tasks and lists won’t carry over. Microsoft plans to offer an export option before the apps retire.
Planner doesn’t support personal Microsoft accounts however. If that’s how you use To Do, you’ll need to pick another task manager, even if you pay for Microsoft 365 Personal or Family, according to the company.
The June shutdown will end a run of just over a decade for To Do on Apple platforms. Microsoft 
bought the company behind Wunderlist
 in 2015 and 
released the first iPhone preview of To Do
 in April 2017.
Looking for a proper replacement? Apple’s built-in Reminders app can keep lists in sync between Apple devices, including 
a grocery list you share with family through iCloud
.

Friday, November 20 @ 1:40 PM ET: A Microsoft spokesperson shared an official statement discussing the upcoming changes. That statement has been added to this piece in its own section.
Users of one of Microsoft’s best free apps will have to add “find a new to do list app” to their list of tasks. But they shouldn’t do so on Microsoft To Do, since all versions of the app are now destined for retirement.
Microsoft To Do will be retired on June 30, 2027
. While the apps will shut down on that date, the underlying To Do service, APIs, and related synchronization support will retire later on separate timelines. Microsoft detailed its plans in a 
support document
.
The writing was on the wall that Microsoft To Do was not a priority at Microsoft. The software felt like abandonware, rarely receiving updates or new features. Not that a task tracking app should require many updates, but the Microsoft To Do webpage still uses an icon from Windows 10.
Just a few months ago our Ben Wilson 
pleaded with Microsoft to keep Microsoft To Do around
. His worry that the app would be moved to the Microsoft Graveyard became a reality quickly.
Microsoft has focused on Planner recently, and that will be the path forward for business and education users who want to sync tasks across devices and platforms. Planner is not available for personal users.
Even those who switch to Planner will have extra work to do, since tasks will not migrate from Microsoft To Do to Planner.
Microsoft To Do was made by the Wunderlist team after Microsoft acquired that popular application. Wunderlist had a passionate user base and Microsoft To Do earned praise for its swift performance and cross-device syncing. Microsoft eventually 
shut down Wunderlist
 and pushed users to Microsoft To Do when the latter was ready for mainstream use.
Microsoft To Do has served as a natural extension to the 
Microsoft 365
 ecosystem for years. Its integration with Outlook felt like magic when it was new.
With Microsoft To Do set for retirement, users will have to look for alternatives. I’d recommend 
Todoist
or 
TickTick
as replacements for Microsoft To Do. 
Google Keep
 and 
Apple Reminders
 are also strong candidates depending on the platforms you use.
It’s frustrating to see Microsoft To Do placed on a path to retirement. It’s a useful app that performs well on the web, Windows 11, iOS, and Android. I’d argue it was the pinnacle of Microsoft’s cross-platform app strategy.

「Microsoft To Do」のWebサイト。2027年6月30日にアプリが廃止される
 　米Microsoftは、タスク管理アプリ「Microsoft To Do」を段階的に廃止すると発表した。Web、Windows、Mac、iOS、Androidの「Microsoft To Do」アプリは、2027年6月30日をもって終了する。

リマインダー：「Planner」の期限通知は、タスクごとに設定する「To Do」のリマインダーとは異なる
共有：既存の「To Do」の共有リストのメンバー、割り当て、権限は引き継がれない

添付ファイル：新しい「Planner」のプライベートプランでは、現時点でタスクへのファイル添付に対応しない

Moving from Microsoft To Do to Microsoft Planner
Microsoft To Do is retiring. For work or school accounts, Microsoft Planner will be the destination for managing personal work tasks alongside assigned work. The updated My tasks experience begins rolling out in December 2026.
Microsoft To Do apps will retire on June 30, 2027.
 This includes To Do on the web, Windows, Mac, iOS, and Android, for both work or school accounts and personal Microsoft accounts.
The underlying To Do service, APIs, and related synchronization support will retire on separate, later timelines. We’ll communicate those dates and any required preparation separately.
This article explains what’s changing, what happens to existing tasks, and how to prepare.
Note for personal Microsoft accounts:
 To Do apps are also retiring for personal Microsoft accounts, including Microsoft 365 Personal and Family. Planner is not available for these accounts. Follow the personal-account preparation guidance below.
If you use both work and personal accounts, review the tasks and lists in each account separately.
Updated My Tasks in Planner
Planner is available today for work or school accounts. The updated My tasks experience will begin rolling out in December 2026, making it easier to capture, organize, and focus on your private tasks.
Preview of the updated My tasks experience in Planner for the web. Design may change.
A simpler, list-first experience:
 Capture and manage your private tasks, with assigned work accessible in the separate Assigned to me view.
Focus and organize your work:
 Star tasks to help you focus. Organize tasks to suit how you work.
Personal tasks stored directly in a private Planner plan:
 Newly created private tasks in Planner will start to be stored in a Planner-backed private plan. Existing To Do tasks will not be migrated.
What happens to existing tasks and lists?

How Planner differs from To Do
Planner uses a different task and collaboration model. Review these differences when preparing your transition.
Area
What to know
Organizing work
Planner brings private tasks and assigned work into My tasks, which currently offers Grid and Board views. A new List view is coming with the upcoming refresh of My tasks.
Reminders
Planner’s due-date notifications differ from To Do’s reminders that you schedule for an individual task.
Sharing and collaboration
Planner supports shared work through plans and task assignments. Existing To Do shared-list membership, assignments, and permissions will not transfer.
Desktop and offline access
Planner offers web and Teams experiences. Native Planner apps for Windows and Mac are not planned as replacements for To Do’s desktop apps. The web and Teams experiences do not provide equivalent desktop offline functionality.
Categories and labels
Planner labels work differently from Outlook categories and do not synchronize with them.
File attachments
File attachments are not currently supported on tasks in the new Planner-backed private plan.
Personal starring and shared priority
Personal starring helps you focus on your own work; task priority communicates importance within shared work. Personal starring is part of the upcoming My tasks experience.
To Do list groups do not have a direct equivalent in Planner. Equivalent To Do printing and email-list capabilities are not currently planned for the replacement experience.
Exporting your To Do data
Microsoft will provide a way to export your To Do data before the apps retire.
 We’ll update this article with instructions, supported data, requirements, and limitations when those details are finalized.
Export preserves supported data; it does not migrate tasks or lists into Planner. An export does not mean that the content can be imported into Planner or another application.
If your organization has retention or compliance requirements, contact your administrator before deleting or reorganizing task data.

Frequently asked questions
Do I need to start using Planner now?
You can explore Planner now and review how your workflows will change. The updated My tasks experience in Planner will begin rolling out in December 2026. Follow your organization’s guidance for transitioning to the updated experience.
Will my existing tasks and lists move automatically?
No. Existing To Do tasks and lists will not migrate to Planner. Existing To Do tasks will remain accessible in Planner through June 30, 2027; after that date, you will no longer be able to view or manage them there.
Will my data be deleted on June 30, 2027?
The June 30 date applies to To Do apps and access to existing To Do tasks through Planner. It does not itself schedule existing task data for deletion. Retention and access are separate considerations.
What should I do if I use To Do with a personal Microsoft account?
Follow the personal-account preparation steps above to review your data, choose another task-management application, and prepare for export.
How will I export my To Do data?
Microsoft will provide a way to export your To Do data before the apps retire. Instructions, supported data, requirements, and limitations will be added to the 
Exporting your To Do data
 section.
Will I need an additional Planner license?
Planner access depends on your organization’s subscription, your assigned license, and administrator settings. Some premium capabilities require additional licensing. Check with your administrator to understand what is included for you.
What will replace the To Do desktop apps?
Planner offers web and Teams experiences. Native Planner apps for Windows and Mac are not planned as replacements for the To Do desktop apps.
What happens to applications that use To Do APIs or synchronization?
These dependencies have separate retirement timelines. Integrations may require changes; review guidance with your administrator or integration provider.
Does the same rollout apply to government environments?
Administrators should follow the availability, timing, and preparation guidance communicated for their environment.
What happens to Tasks in classic Outlook?
Classic Outlook’s native Tasks experience is separate from the To Do experience hosted in Outlook and will continue working and syncing with Exchange.
