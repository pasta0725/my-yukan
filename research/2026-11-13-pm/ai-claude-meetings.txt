Anthropic reveals a host of updates for Claude to help users at work
Claude can now join meetings, analyze data and more
It can also effectively connect to third-party tools such as Adobe, Box and more
Anthropic has unveiled a host of updates for Claude which could see the AI platform play a much more integral role in some of your most common work tasks.
Among the next expansions are tools which allow Claude to join video calls as a participant, meaning it learns first-hand from your meetings and can start work immediately.
Joining its existing suite of office software tools are new Whiteboards, Sheets and Data services looking to help users work and create more effectively - and new interactive connectors will now allow Claude to work inside the full versions of common workplace apps such as 
Adobe
, Atlassian and HubSpot.

What's new with Claude for enterprise?
(Image credit: Anthropic)
"A year ago, Claude changed how developers work," Anthropic wrote in a blog post announcing the new tools. "Engineers stopped asking AI for snippets and started handing it whole tasks, which freed them to spend their time deciding what to build. For most other teams, work has stayed much the same: the email gets written a little faster, the meeting comes with a summary."
"Now the frontier is moving to knowledge work, and people are starting to hand Claude whole tasks too."
The most striking new addition is undoubtedly Claude Meetings, which will allow Claude to join video calls as a visible participant.
Claude follows the conversation and starts on the work as people talk, taking notes, drafting plans, outlining timelines on whiteboards, answering chat questions, and utilizing connected work apps while conversations happen - and when the call ends, the notes, action items and everything Claude made are waiting in claude.ai.

Also new is Claude Whiteboards, a shared, real-time board which helps teams turn brainstorms and retrospectives into structured plans, timelines, and action items, and integrates directly into Claude Meetings, and Claude Sheets, a spreadsheet tool which allows teams to build and edit trackers and budgets collaboratively with Claude, complete with tabs, formulas, and dropdown menus.
Finally, Claude Data (formerly known as Claude Dashboards) connects company data sources (such as CRMs and warehouses) to generate dashboards, data stories, and narrated videos, featuring beta capabilities like certified queries and live sharing.
(Image credit: Anthropic)
Alongside this, new Interactive Connectors will allow full versions of partner tools (like Adobe, Atlassian, HubSpot, 
Salesforce
, NetSuite, and Workday) to open directly inside Claude, which can take actions directly within these apps with user approval.
Connectors respect existing user permissions, and admins can utilize features like Claude auto-connect, usage controls, and Smart Reports to monitor team productivity and costs.
Claude Meetings, Claude Whiteboard and Claude Sheets are all still in beta, with Claude Data and Interactive Connectors generally available now.

Claude can now join your video calls as a visible participant, follow the conversation and start on work during the meeting. Claude Meetings is now in beta.
Your team can ask Claude questions during the meeting, out loud or in chat. By default, Claude answers in writing, and it can use your connected tools with your permission.
Claude also works with you live to build docs, 
whiteboards
 (opens in new tab)
, or other artifacts, while teammates on the invite refine them. Claude writes the notes and action items, so no one on the call has to.
Claude Meetings is starting to roll out in beta on Team and Enterprise plans. Support for other video conferencing platforms is coming soon.
What it looks like
Say your team is planning a customer event, and Claude is on the call. You say, “Claude, start a doc for this plan.” Claude writes up what the team has said so far, and teammates on the invite can edit the plan while you keep talking.
When you start discussing the budget, someone asks Claude what last year’s event cost. Claude asks for their approval to look it up, posts it in the meeting chat, and compares it to the current plan. When you start discussing timelines, someone asks Claude to put it on a whiteboard, and the team works on the schedule alongside the doc.
Before the meeting ends, someone asks Claude to share a recap with the team. After the call, any notes, action items, plan, and whiteboard are on the meeting’s page in 
claude.ai
 (opens in new tab)
.
Later, you can go back to meetings you were part of and ask Claude about them, or add Claude to the next one from your calendar in claude.ai.
We’re working with partners like Gong, Attention, Granola, Fireflies.ai, Otter, and Plaud, so you’ll soon be able to send a meeting’s transcript to the tools your team already uses.
Security and privacy
Claude adds a separate page for each meeting in 
claude.ai
 (opens in new tab)
 with the notes, transcript, and anything it built. People from your organization on the invite can see it and edit what Claude made, and it can be shared with others in your organization from the page.
By default, Claude is set to stay out of calls when someone from outside your organization is invited, and to leave if someone from outside joins partway through. An admin can allow Claude on calls with outside guests. On those calls, Claude posts its notice in the call’s chat but doesn’t reply there, and what guests say is included in the notes and transcript.
Claude uses each person’s connected tools with that person’s permission. It asks the first time, and a yes applies going forward. For now, when someone asks out loud, Claude asks each time to confirm who’s speaking. An answer posted in the call’s chat is visible to everyone on the call.
When Claude joins, it appears as a participant and posts a notice in the call’s chat. It saves a transcript, summary, and screen descriptions, not audio.
Before you add Claude or turn on auto-join, make sure everyone on your calls is OK with being transcribed.
Getting started
Claude joins when someone adds it or has auto-join on. Support is currently available for Google Meet, with other platforms coming soon. On Team plans, inviting Claude to your meetings is available by default, and an admin can turn it off. On Enterprise plans, an admin turns it on for all or part of the organization. While it’s in beta, it isn’t available to some organizations, including those with zero data retention (ZDR), HIPAA-ready configuration, or customer-managed encryption keys (CMEK).
During the beta, Claude’s notes and transcripts are free on Team and Enterprise plans. So are its answers in your calls, unless an answer uses one of your connected tools, which counts toward your plan’s usage. The docs, 
whiteboards,
 (opens in new tab)
 and other artifacts Claude makes in a call are free through February 10, 2027.
