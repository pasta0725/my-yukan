Tesla Passes Waymo in Texas Robotaxi Registrations, 1,302 to 1,297
Tesla's count on the state's driverless vehicle register edged ahead of Waymo's late on Nov. 10, though the list shows vehicles allowed to operate, not vehicles carrying riders.
Sam Okonkwo, Robotaxi · Nov. 11, 2026 · 3 min
Photo: Steve Jurvetson / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 4.0)
EVwire
AUSTIN, Texas - Tesla now has more driverless vehicles on the books in Texas than Waymo. The state's automated vehicle register lists 1,302 vehicles under Tesla Robotaxi LLC and 1,297 under Waymo LLC, a lead of five, according to counts of the 
Texas Department of Motor Vehicles register
 published by 
EVwire
 and 
TeslaNorth
.
The lead changed hands just before midnight on Nov. 10, Texas time, when Tesla added a batch of 20 Model Y vehicles to its authorization and went from 1,282 to 1,302, EVwire reported. Until then Waymo had been 15 vehicles ahead. On Nov. 1, TeslaNorth counted 1,204 vehicles for Tesla and 1,259 for Waymo.
The climb has been steep. Tesla had 42 vehicles registered in late May, when Waymo had 619, according to EVwire's records of the register. Waymo has roughly doubled its Texas fleet since then, often adding vehicles in large batches, while Tesla's count has grown more than thirtyfold.
The register itself lists only vehicle identification numbers. Decoding those numbers through the federal vehicle database, EVwire counted 729 Model Ys and 573 purpose-built Cybercabs in Tesla's Texas fleet, and 874 Jaguar I-PACEs and 423 Ojai vehicles in Waymo's. Tesla's Cybercab count has held at 573 since Oct. 27, while it has added 222 Model Ys over the same period.
The figures have limits. A vehicle on the register is one a company is allowed to operate without a driver, not one that is carrying riders. The state does not publish how many vehicles are in service at any moment, which cities they serve or how many trips they complete, so the count does not show which company runs the larger active ride-hailing fleet. Under the state's program, companies must hold an authorization before operating driverless vehicles commercially and keep their own lists current by adding and removing individual vehicles, 
TeslaNorth noted
.
Tesla and Waymo together account for about three-quarters of the 3,424 vehicles that 17 companies have registered in Texas, according to 
EVwire's tracker
. Avride is a distant third with 344 vehicles, and the rest of the list is made up largely of autonomous truck operators, led by Aurora with 91.
Tesla now runs unsupervised Robotaxi service in five Texas cities: Austin, Dallas, Houston, Fort Worth and San Antonio, where it opened this week. The company said on its third-quarter earnings call that Cybercab rides would begin in Dallas this month and that it aims to run Robotaxi around the clock by the end of the year.
