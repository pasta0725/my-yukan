Try Tabletop: Customizable, collaborative spaces for groups to plan, play and do more together.
Roman Nurik
Design & Engineering Lead, Google Labs
Nov 11, 2026
Whether you’re planning a family trip, running a rec league or hosting a potluck, coordinating as a group is easier when everyone can pitch in, build and decide together in one place.
Today, we’re introducing 
Tabletop
, a new Google Labs experiment built for real-time collaboration.
Just add in your links, files, and ideas, then prompt @tabletop — or pick a starter template — to generate custom interactive trip itineraries, dinner polls, expense splitters, and tournament brackets. Once your space is ready, drop the invite link in your group chat to let everyone jump in and use it at the same time, like in a Google Doc.
Tabletop is rolling out in the U.S. (18+) via waitlist. Pull up a chair and sign up at 
labs.google/tabletop
.

Google Labs has introduced Tabletop, a new AI-powered tool that makes planning and organizing activities with others more interactive.

Making plans with friends isn’t always as easy as it sounds. Say you’re planning a weekend trip and soon, your group chat is filled with so many messages that keeping track of everything becomes a task in itself.
Google
 has introduced a new experiment called 
Tabletop
 that could make situations like this much easier to manage. Developed by Google Labs, Tabletop lets you bring links, files, and ideas into one shared space where everyone can plan things together. But unlike a regular document or spreadsheet, it makes the whole experience more interactive.
For instance, you can create polls, vote on ideas, react to suggestions, and make changes together in real time. An 
AI assistant
 can also help you create activities and organize information, so you don’t have to set everything up yourself.
You could also use it to organize a potluck dinner, put together an itinerary, create a chore chart for your family, or even put on a scavenger hunt to keep your kids entertained on a rainy afternoon. Google also suggests using it for things like fantasy league drafts and award show watch parties.
What makes Tabletop interesting is that it brings a little fun to things that usually feel like work. For now, 
Google Tabletop
 is available through a waitlist for users aged 18 and older in the US. To request access, you’ll need to provide a personal Gmail address. There’s no word yet on when it might expand to other countries.

Google Labs has launched Tabletop, an experiment that builds custom tools such as trip itineraries, dinner polls and expense splitters from a prompt. Groups share one space and edit it together in real time. It is open to US adults via a waitlist.
Google has launched Tabletop, an experiment from its Google Labs team that lets groups build their own planning tools with AI. Google 
announced
 the tool on Wednesday.
Users add links, files and ideas to a shared space, then ask @tabletop to build something. It can make trip itineraries, dinner polls, expense splitters and tournament brackets. There are also starter templates.
The creator shares an invite link in a group chat, and everyone can then use the space at the same time. Google compares it to working together in a Google Doc. Group members can vote, plan and track progress in one place.
The tools can also change as plans do. Members can ask @tabletop to change a rule or add something new without starting over. In one example on Google’s product page, a Pong game becomes Pickle Pong, with a bouncing pickle and a live scoreboard.
Google suggests uses such as road trips, sports leagues, weddings, weekly meal plans and school pickups. A chore tracker can even become a game with points and a shared reward.
Tabletop is rolling out in the US to adults aged 18 and over through a waitlist, with new users let in each week. Google has not said which AI model powers it.
Google Labs experiments sometimes become full products. NotebookLM started there and was 
renamed Gemini Notebook
 this year. Google has also opened its Play Store to 
AI-built apps
, while Slack now lets teams 
build with AI agents
 inside its chat app.
