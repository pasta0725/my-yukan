The AI agent can schedule appointments, make reservations and wait on hold for you.

Meta's Muse just got another ability that could make the AI agent feel a little bit more like an actual personal assistant. Muse is now able to 
place phone calls
 on your behalf, if you have a paid subscription.
With the change, Muse is able to call businesses to help with tasks like making reservations, scheduling appointments and other "calls you don't want to make," Meta's Chief AI officer Alexandr Wang said in 
an update
. The feature is available to those who have paid for a separate Muse subscription, which comes with additional tokens and some extra features.
Some users who have had access to the feature already have reported that Muse has been able to handle phone-related tasks with relative ease. Moltbook creator and Meta Superintelligence employee Matt Schlicht shared a recreation of a phone conversation his agent had with a Volvo dealership last month, noting that it was able to wait on hold in order to get the info he needed.

Interestingly, in the call shared by Schlicht, Muse doesn't explicitly identify itself as an AI agent though it notes it's calling his behalf and that it's transcribing the call. That may be enough for some straightforward tasks, but there's always a chance these types of calls could go off the rails or the person on the other end hangs up when they realize they're speaking with an AI.
Meta has been steadily churning out new features for Muse, which has already drawn more than 
10 million users
, according to the company. People can also now assign a 
dedicated email address
 to their agent in order to loop it into important emails without giving wholesale access to their inbox.
The company isn't the first to experiment with phone-calling abilities for AI. Google has introduced a number of similar features over the years, including "Call For Me" 
for Pixel phones
 and Instinct recently introduced a 
"Concierge" feature
 for its agent that's meant to handle more complex calls.

Meta has added outbound calls to its Muse AI agent for paid subscribers in the United States, letting the assistant book appointments and request information from businesses.
The calling feature began as a beta in September, with a public beta test opening by mid-September. It extends Muse’s existing tasks, which include sending emails, shopping and booking travel.
In an earlier limited test, human contractors helped when Muse struggled with a call. That setup raised privacy concerns because a person might hear a call that users expected software to handle. Meta rolled back the approach and indicated it would pursue calls handled by AI alone, with privacy disclosures.
The feature follows Meta’s Nov. 5 U.S. launch of 
real-time Muse voice chats and animated avatars
.

Meta's Muse Now Places Phone Calls to U.S. Businesses, Paid Subscribers Only
TL;DR
Meta's consumer AI agent Muse can now place calls to U.S. businesses on a user's behalf, but only for paid subscribers. The feature was tested with early adopters in September. Muse plans start at $20 per month for 500 million Muse tokens per week.

Meta's consumer AI agent Muse can now place phone calls to U.S. businesses on behalf of its users, and the feature is available only to paid subscribers, the company says. Alexandr Wang, Meta's Chief AI Officer and head of its Superintelligence Labs division, announced the rollout on X on November 12, 2026, writing that it is "out now to paid subs in the US."
What changed
Meta first tested the calling feature in September. During that phase, only a select group of Muse early adopters could use it, and they had to ask the AI for access. Now any Muse subscriber who pays can make this type of request.
Wang described the use cases as calls people don't want to make: insurance, cable, the DMV, or a restaurant that doesn't offer online reservations.
Pricing
Muse subscription plans currently start at $20 per month for 500 million Muse tokens per week. Heavier users can pay $100 per month for 3 billion tokens. Calls remain a paid-only feature for the time being.
Wang also said this week that the recently added option to create a Muse email address will soon roll out to free users.
Adoption and competition
Meta said last month that Muse has more than 10 million users. Per TechCrunch, which reported the news, Meta is working to make consumer AI a mainstream product for everyday users, and phone calls are one way to take another task off people's to-do lists.
Muse is not the first AI agent to offer this. According to TechCrunch, the startup Instinct has offered a similar option, as has Google's Gemini.
The reception problem
TechCrunch notes that results on the receiving end can be mixed. Many businesses and receptionists hang up as soon as they identify that the caller is not a human. The outlet also reports that as AI voices better mimic real people, distinguishing a customer from an AI acting on a customer's behalf will get harder.
What this means
Calling moves Muse from handling digital tasks toward handling tasks that normally require a person on the phone, and Meta is placing it behind its paid tiers. The free-tier email address planned for rollout suggests Meta is splitting agent features between free and paid users, with calling on the paid side for now.
The practical value depends on whether businesses answer and stay on the line. TechCrunch reports that many hang up once they detect an AI caller, so success rates will matter as much as the voice quality. Meta has not published figures on call completion in the reporting so far. The feature is limited to U.S. businesses and U.S. paid subscribers.
