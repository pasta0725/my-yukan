We’re excited to officially bring Automatic Super Resolution (Auto SR) out of preview on the ROG XBOX Ally X and the ROG XBOX Ally X20. Auto
SR is also now available on select Windows Copilot+ PCs, powered by AMD Ryzen, with several major new features beginning to roll out today.  
This release brings 
handheld play (undocked on battery),
 support for games running at 
1080p
, compatibility with 
Auto HDR
 and a new 
Performance Mode
 that can run up to 120 FPS. Handheld play, expanded support for Windows Copilot+ PCs powered by AMD Ryzen, and 1080p support are available today, while Performance Mode and Auto HDR support will be rolling out with 
Windows updates
 so they may take a little time to reach your device.
Performance Mode: Up To 120 FPS 
Until now, Auto SR topped out around 60 to 75 FPS. We hinted at increasing this capability in our 
last post
, and today we’re introducing a new 
Performance Mode. 
It raises the limit to up to 120 FPS, almost double what was possible before, so fast-paced and competitive games can feel even smoother. 
Switch between modes on the fly in Game Bar and watch your framerate change in real time for the right balance for each game.

Play in Handheld Mode (Undocked/Internal Screen)
During the 
XBOX Insider preview
, players could use Auto SR only while docked. We started there because that’s where Auto SR makes the biggest difference: 1440p-like detail alongside smooth framerates on larger screens where every frame matters. By popular demand, Auto SR now also works in handheld mode, right on your device’s built-in screen, even when you’re unplugged and playing on battery. The next time a demanding game feels a little choppy on the go, Auto SR can help smooth things out, no dock required.
Support for 1080p Base Resolution
Many games on your handheld run at 1080p by default to match the built-in screen. During the preview, you had to lower a game’s resolution before Auto SR could kick in, an extra step that was easy to miss and a common reason Auto SR didn’t turn on. Now Auto SR will work with games running at 1080p. Dock to a higher-resolution TV or monitor, and it can upscale your game up to 1440p for a sharper picture on the big screen, with fewer settings to change. 
*
Note: 
When using 1080p in handheld mode (internal screen), performance may not be as optimal as other configurations, but you’ll still see visual quality improvements especially in older games.
Auto SR and Auto HDR now work together 
Auto HDR is a Windows feature that brings brighter highlights and richer colors to games without native HDR support when you play on an HDR display. Until now, you had to choose between Auto SR and Auto HDR. Now they work together, so you can enjoy sharper, smoother gameplay and vivid HDR 
visuals at the same time, whether on an HDR TV, monitor or ROG XBOX Ally X20.

A few months ago, Microsoft opened up 
Auto SR support for the Asus ROG Xbox Ally X
, allowing it to use the AI-aided upscaling tech to improve frame rates in games. But that was only for people using a Preview version of Windows, and you could only enable it when the handheld was in docked mode. Now, 
Auto SR has officially launched
 in the main Windows OS for the Xbox Ally X and X20; plus, you can now run it when undocked.
The first question you might have is, 'Why would I use Auto SR instead of FSR?' The answer is: 'You wouldn't.'
You'll primarily use Auto SR for games that don't have FSR baked in by the devs, or when the FSR implementation doesn't work well for some reason. It should, in theory, actually give you better performance than FSR, assuming you have an NPU on board (like the Xbox Ally X and X20 do). That's because it uses the NPU rather than the GPU for its upscaling shenanigans, which frees up the GPU for other work.

FSR, however, is still preferred when possible, as it will look closer to what the game should look like.
When Auto SR originally came to the Xbox Ally X earlier in the year, you could only enable it in docked mode, presumably in part because that allows for more power draw for the APU (including the NPU that does the upscaling work). Microsoft explains the official reason, however, as being "because that’s where Auto SR makes the biggest difference: 1440p-like detail alongside smooth framerates on larger screens where every frame matters."
Now, you can enable it when running on battery.
You can also use it with games running at 1080p and at a higher frame rate. Before, you could only run Auto SR up to 60 or 75 fps, but in a new 'Performance Mode', you can get up to 120 fps. Oh, and Auto HDR is supported if you're playing games that can benefit from it.
