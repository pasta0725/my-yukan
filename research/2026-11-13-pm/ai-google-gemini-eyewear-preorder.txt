Today, pre-orders begin for the new Intelligent Eyewear collections, made in collaboration with Gentle Monster, Warby Parker, and Samsung. With help from Gemini, these frames are ready to bring you real-time assistance throughout your day, right when you need it.
1
The new Intelligent Eyewear collections
These collections, which were designed to fit a variety of styles, integrate helpful technology into frames you’ll want to wear every day, and work with Pixel and Samsung phones, as well as iPhones.
2
Gentle Monster’s high-end designs transform glasses into a means of self-expression. Their 
Intelligent Eyewear Collection
 includes six designs in bold colors — including dark red, pink, gray, and yellow — paired with tinted, clear, blue, or brown lenses. Prescription options are available for most frames. Starting at $449, the collection will be available in the United States and in South Korea.
Warby Parker’s Intelligent Eyewear lineup features three refined styles — Alden, Reid, and Wren — inspired by the brand’s most beloved silhouettes, with timeless design and lightweight comfort at the forefront. You can choose from optical or sun styles starting at $395, in Pine, Saddle, and Jet Black colorways, with a wide range of prescription, width, and lens options. The full collection is available at 
warbyparker.com

Intelligent eyewear was engineered to be comfortable and helpful all day. The glasses feature lightweight frames (weighing around 41 grams) and a battery that lasts up to nine hours.
3
 Each pair comes with a sleek, portable charging case that can recharge your frames up to seven times.
4
A smarter way to see the world
Gemini on intelligent eyewear helps connect the dots across the Google apps and services you use every day to get you hands-free help. Just say “Hey Gemini” or tap your frames to learn the story behind a street art mural you are passing by, ask for a tailored recommendation from a “New Arrivals” bookshelf, or look at an event poster to check your Calendar availability on the spot.
Gemini also helps handle your busywork in the background. Whether you’re walking between meetings or running errands with your hands full, you can ask Gemini to add what you’re looking at to your grocery list, or remind you where your next meeting is — all while keeping your eyes up and your day moving.
When you’re exploring somewhere new, Google Maps delivers turn-by-turn directions in your ear. And when you ask Gemini, Google Translate will deliver real-time speech translation with automatic language detection across more than 70 languages.
The glasses also feature 
Gemini’s new Guided Vision
, which offers conversational audio descriptions to people who are blind, have low vision, or want intuitive visual assistance as they read small print or find everyday items.

 And thanks to our collaboration with 
Aira
, users can say “Hey Gemini, open Aira Explorer” to stream their glasses' camera feed directly to a professionally trained human visual interpreter for real-time support.
Never miss a moment
Custom-tuned open-ear speakers direct clear vocals and balanced audio right into your ear while keeping you fully aware of what’s around you. You can soundtrack your day with YouTube Music, Spotify, or Apple Music, and jump back into your audiobook on Audible.
6
 Or use your glasses to catch up on your missed group chats, respond to messages, and take phone calls hands-free.
With the integrated 12-megapixel ultra-wide camera, capturing high-quality photos and videos is as simple as pressing a button — or just asking Gemini. Your phone stays in your pocket so you can capture the moment while staying in it. HDR processing and image stabilization ensure your images and videos stay crisp, vibrant, and steady, while spatial audio capture helps you relive the moments like you were there.
Privacy designed for both sides of the lens
When technology can see the world around you, it has a responsibility to the people around you, too. We set out to design intelligent eyewear so that personal help doesn’t come at the expense of anyone’s privacy.
You can read about our approach to privacy on intelligent eyewear 
in our blog
, and learn more about privacy features on the 
Google Safety Center
. Key protections include:
Camera visuals from Gemini on glasses are not saved in your Gemini History and aren’t used to train AI models or target ads. Your glasses are also designed to automatically redact bystanders’ faces from these visuals before Gemini processes them.
Data stored on glasses is encrypted, and if data leaves your glasses, it’s also encrypted in transit to keep it private and secure.
You control which apps access your glasses’ camera and mic, and you can change these permissions whenever you like. You can also review or delete your chats with Gemini, and adjust your settings at any time in Gemini History.
When you take a photo or video for your gallery, an indicator light lets people around you know. The glasses are designed to stop recording if the light is covered or disabled.
Every pair has an off switch so you can turn your glasses completely off whenever you want to wear them as regular glasses only.

Smartglasses have always faced an uphill road to acceptance. Not only is propping a computer up on your nose an uncomfortable proposition, but the 
privacy aspect
 has been vexing people since the 
Google Glass days
. Now, Google is ready to take another swing at “
intelligent eyewear
” for the Gemini era after 
previewing its plans
 a couple of times. The first glasses are up for preorder today from Gentle Monster and Warby Parker, but they’re no impulse buy.
These devices are a collaboration between Google, Samsung, and the aforementioned eyewear designers. Gentle Monster and Warby Parker have each designed a few different frames, with options for transparent and tinted lenses. Samsung is providing the computing hardware that fits inside them, and Google has the software covered.
Warby Parker has designed three frames for Google eyewear, called Alden, Reid, and Wren (see above). They are available with both standard optical and sun lenses in Pine, Saddle, and Jet Black colors at $395 and higher. Warby Parker is only selling devices in the US. Gentle Monster has gone a bit further with six styles, but Google hasn’t told us any names for those yet. They come in dark red, pink, gray, and yellow colors and are available with clear, tinted, blue, or brown lenses. The Gentle Monster styles start a little higher at $449, and they will be available in the US and South Korea.
Google says the frames all weigh about 41 grams, which is not terrible but is definitely near the top for glasses generally. They will, naturally, weigh much more when you account for lenses. That will also determine the final pricing. So far, we only have the starting prices for these devices, including non-prescription lenses.
The setup here is similar to the Ray-Ban Meta glasses we’ve seen for a few years. There are no screens in the lenses, but you can interact with the AI system via voice or by activating the integrated camera. The experience should be similar to using Gemini Live on your phone, but it will be a lot easier to fire up the AI. You can say “Hey Gemini” or tap a touch-sensitive area of the frame to start interacting with the robot.
                  Credit:
                                      Gentle Monster
          Credit:
          Gentle Monster
Since this is the same chatbot that operates across the Google ecosystem, it has access to your data and preferences. You can ask questions and get personalized answers, have Google Maps guide you by voice, or just talk with the AI to kill time. The glasses use custom open-ear speakers to limit distractions for those around you. Google notes the directional microphones should not pick up speech from other people, either.
Adding the camera feed is where things get interesting. When the AI can see what you see, it has the potential to do more work on your behalf. Google suggests asking Gemini for recommendations when looking at a bookshelf or glancing at an event poster to have the AI add it to your calendar. The glasses will also support Gemini Guided Vision, which provides conversational audio descriptions for people who are visually impaired.
The utility of these devices hinges on Gemini. I would be remiss not to point out that Gemini, like all AI systems, is not infallible. In fact, it can be 
frustratingly
 fallible at times. The chatbot still regularly screws up simple things like adding calendar appointments or identifying objects in an image in my testing, and when you don’t have a screen to verify the AI’s output, it may be harder to trust it. These devices will really only make sense if you can rely on Gemini to do things right, and that’s still not a guarantee.
Google’s privacy promises
All the Gemini-powered glasses from Gentle Monster and Warby Parker feature a 12 MP wide-angle camera capable of shooting photos and video. 
Google says
 it has implemented a number of features and limitations to ensure people don’t do creepy things with its intelligent eyewear, and that it doesn’t do creepy things to you—Google claims its approach respects privacy on “both sides of the lens.”
For starters, there’s a recording light that is on whenever the camera is active. Like the Meta glasses, Google’s eyewear will detect if the light has been covered up or modified in any way that would prevent it from illuminating. In that event, the glasses will refuse to activate the camera. All Google-powered glasses will have a physical off switch, too.
                  Credit:
                                      Warby Parker
          Credit:
          Warby Parker
The terms of use for Gemini eyewear also require users to agree not to record people without consent or in sensitive areas. If Google finds out someone has broken those rules, it may remotely disable the glasses. Google is going a bit further in restricting video functionality than Meta, too. Google says it will not allow any apps in the Play Store to live stream video to social media from the glasses (not even YouTube live is supported). The content you record is saved to your gallery, and you can upload that wherever you want, but going live is apparently not on the agenda. Real-time facial recognition is also off the table, according to Google.  The glasses also automatically redact faces before sending video to Google for processing.
On the user privacy side, Google says it will not save any images or videos captured by the glasses in your Gemini history. Additionally, none of that data will be used for training AI models or targeting ads. Your voice interactions with Gemini via the glasses are, as with all other Gemini endpoints, not guaranteed the same level of protection. You can delete your chats from the Gemini history, though.
Data on the glasses is encrypted, and it remains that way during transit to the cloud. However, it’s not encrypted on Google’s servers. The company does note that it’s working to bring Private AI Compute to glasses, which will isolate data during processing so not even Google can access it.
                  Credit:
                                      Gentle Monster
          Credit:
          Gentle Monster
Anyone looking to get Gemini on their face and test Google’s privacy promise can preorder the glasses from the Gentle Monster and Warby Parker websites. Devices should ship and appear on store shelves on December 10. Importantly, support for these devices is a bit narrow at launch. They will work with all iPhones from the 13 Pro onward. On the Android side, you’ll need a Pixel 7 or newer or a Samsung Galaxy S or Z series device running One UI 9 or later. If you’ve got a cheaper Samsung Galaxy or a device from another OEM, you’re out of luck at the moment.

グーグル、Gemini搭載スマートグラス「Intelligent Eyewear」　395ドルから
浅井 淳志
2026年11月13日 13:32
 Googleは、SamsungおよびアイウェアブランドのGentle Monster、Warby Parkerと協業したGemini対応スマートグラス「Intelligent Eyewear」を12月10日に発売する。予約受付を開始し、価格はGentle Monsterモデルが449ドル(約71,801円)から、Warby Parkerモデルが395ドル(約63,170円)から。日本での展開は未定。 
 Intelligent Eyewearは、Geminiによる音声操作やリアルタイム翻訳、写真・動画の撮影などに対応したスマートグラス。「Hey Gemini」と呼びかけるか、フレームをタップすることでGeminiを起動でき、目の前にある物体について質問したり、買い物リストに追加したりできる。 
 Google マップによる音声ナビゲーションや、70以上の言語に対応するリアルタイム音声翻訳も利用可能。視覚支援機能「Guided Vision」では、周囲の状況を会話形式の音声で説明し、小さな文字を読む際や日用品を探す際などを支援する。また、Airaとの連携により、カメラ映像を専門の担当者と共有し、リアルタイムでサポートを受けることもできる。 
 重量は約41gで、バッテリー駆動時間は最大9時間。付属の充電ケースで最大7回の充電が可能。また、1,200万画素の超広角カメラとオープンイヤースピーカーを備え、写真・動画の撮影、音楽再生、通話などに対応する。 
 プライバシー保護機能として、Geminiが参照したカメラ映像は履歴に保存されず、AIの学習や広告ターゲティングにも使用されない。写真や動画の撮影時はランプが点灯し、覆われると撮影を停止する。 
 Gentle Monsterモデルは、ダークレッド、ピンク、グレー、イエローなどのカラーを採用した6種類のデザインを用意。カラーレンズ、クリアレンズ、ブルーレンズ、ブラウンレンズを選択できるほか、多くのモデルで度付きレンズにも対応する。米国と韓国で販売する。 
 Warby Parkerモデルは、「Alden」「Reid」「Wren」の3種類のフレームを展開。カラーはPine、Saddle、Jet Blackを用意し、通常の眼鏡とサングラスから選択できる。度付きレンズやフレーム幅などの変更にも対応する。

Google and Samsung have opened preorders for their first Gemini smart glasses, made with Warby Parker and Gentle Monster. The nine styles cost $395 to $489, have a 12-megapixel camera and ship on 10 December. Google is stressing privacy features as it takes on Meta.
Google and Samsung have opened preorders for their first Gemini-powered smart glasses, 
Bloomberg reported
. The glasses, made with eyewear brands Warby Parker and Gentle Monster, ship on 10 December.
There are nine styles. Gentle Monster’s six cost $449 to $489, while Warby Parker’s three start at $395. Buyers order through the two brands’ websites and stores, and some Google and Samsung stores will offer demos.
All styles weigh around 41 grams and last up to nine hours on a charge, Google said in a 
blog post
. They run on a Qualcomm Snapdragon AR1 chip, have a 12-megapixel camera and work with iPhones and some Pixel and Galaxy phones. Warby Parker’s models launch only in the US, while Gentle Monster’s also go on sale in South Korea, 
Samsung said
.
Users can say “
Hey Gemini
” to ask about what they are looking at, add a flyer to their calendar or get recipe ideas from the food in front of them. The glasses also offer walking directions, live translation, music and calls.
Google is leaning on privacy. The camera stops working if its recording light is covered, and bystanders’ faces are blurred before Gemini sees an image. Users must be 18 or over and agree not to film people without permission, or their glasses can be disabled.
“
As glasses become more capable, our focus is on creating helpful experiences that make both wearers and bystanders feel protected,
” said Shahram Izadi, who leads Google’s XR unit.
Google first showed the glasses at 
I/O in May
, and Samsung 
showed them again
 in July. They now face Meta, which shipped 
81% of AI glasses
 in the first half of the year and sells 
own-brand glasses from $299
.
Meta’s own recording light has been beaten by a 
$2 sticker
, and Google has banned such accessories from its shopping and ad platforms.
