決算発表の前から、わずか5営業日で41％下落したキオクシア株。前編記事〈5営業日で株価41％安の「キオクシアショック」…ウラには東芝の“爆売り”、決算3日前にも1124万株〉では、元親会社・東芝の"爆売り"と、増収見通しでも売られた相方Sandiskの急落を追った。株価の背後には、メモリの好況がいつまで続くのかという疑念が漂う。

選挙の後、米国ではメモリ株の売りが加速した。Sandiskの株価も4日に7.9％、5日には22.3％下げ、同じメモリ大手の米Micron Technology（マイクロン）も、4日に7.0％、5日に17.5％下げている。日本の5日の取引は、米国でメモリ株が売られた4日の取引の直後にあたる。
キオクシアが13.1％下げた5日の東京市場では、半導体関連株が軒並み売られた。東京エレクトロンは9.4％、ディスコは12.5％下げた。米国で5日にメモリ株が一段と売られると、6日は日経平均も約6.0％、3600円ほど値下がりし、売りが市場全体に波及した。
筆者としては、AIへの巨額投資を後押ししてきた政策の追い風が弱まるとの警戒が、もともとくすぶっていた「AIバブル」への疑いに火を付けたとみている。
初配当90円は「次の株主」への招待状？
キオクシアは、9月末を基準日とする中間配当を1株90円とし、上場後初めての配当を決めた。10月1日付で1株を3株に分割しており、分割後に換算すると30円に当たる。11月6日の終値で計算した配当利回りは0.35％と、利回りとしては小さい。
注目したいのは金額よりも方針だ。同社は、手元資金が借入金を上回る状態を保ったうえで、配当を減らさず増やしていく累進配当と自社株買いを基本とし、中長期的に余った資金の100％を株主還元に充てる方針を示した。
この半年の動きを並べると、狙いは一貫している。1株を3株に分割したのは、投資単位当たりの金額を引き下げ、投資家層を広げるためだ。米国預託株式（ADS）による米国の証券取引所への上場も準備しており、こちらも投資家層の拡大を目指すとしている。そこに、累進配当という長期保有の理由が加わった。
東芝や投資ファンド系といった上場前からの大株主が持ち分を減らしていくなかで、下がっても14兆円という巨大な時価総額を受け止める「次の株主」を国内外の個人や長期の機関投資家に広げる狙いがある。配当の導入は、そのための招待状とでもいうべきだろう。
キオクシアの先行きを占う3つのポイント
まずは10〜12月期の実績が「横ばい」の見通しを上回るかどうかにかかっている。会社は円高を前提に置いており、為替次第で上振れの余地がある。
次に東芝の動向を開示から細かくチェックすべきだ。東芝の保有割合は10.46％まで下がったが、10月27日時点でまだ1億7000万株、（11月6日の終値換算で）1.4兆円あまりを保有している。東芝だけでなく投資ファンド系の株主の持ち分の変化も大量保有報告書で確認できる。仮に、売りのペースが一巡すれば、需給要因における株価の重しは相当に軽くなるはずだ。
最後に期末配当の金額を確認したい。期末配当の予想は、現時点で示されていない。累進配当を掲げた初年度に、どれだけの配当を示すかは、経営陣が今後の稼ぐ力をどう予想しているかを測る物差しになる点で先行指標となりうる。
営業利益率80％という数字は、AIがメモリ業界にもたらした好況の大きさを物語る。一方で株価は、米中間選挙の後に強まったAIバブルへの疑いと、元親会社の売りという2つの重しを抱え、ついに終値でも1万円を割り込み、8000円台まで下げた。株価はどこで下げ止まるのか。キオクシアの次の決算は、その両方が試される場になりそうだ。

Why US Memory Stocks Fell 27% in a Week as Memory Prices Rose
Published 
Nov 10, 2026 at 11:24 PM KST
By 
Binucle
In the week that ended November 6, Sandisk shares fell 27.4% in the US and Micron’s fell 22.0%. Over the same period, TrendForce, a Taiwanese market research firm, expected fourth-quarter contract prices for conventional DRAM to rise 10-15% from the third quarter, and NAND flash prices to rise 15-20%. 
It was not falling memory prices that dragged the stocks down first. It was worry that prices are rising more slowly and that data-center investment could be delayed.
What happened to memory stocks in a week?
Micron down 17% and Sandisk down 22% in a single day
The worst day was November 5, when Micron fell 17.46% and Sandisk 22.26%. In Japan, Kioxia fell 36.8% on the Tokyo Stock Exchange from October 30 to November 6.
⤢
Data: US regular-session closes (adjusted), Tokyo Stock Exchange closes, Oct 20-Nov 10, 2026; as of 2026-11-10
All three stocks peaked at the end of October and turned down together in the first week of November. On November 9, Micron and Sandisk each rebounded by more than 6%, and Kioxia rose 9.23% on November 10. Even so, they recovered only part of their losses.
The trigger was the US midterm elections
After the Democrats won both the Senate and the House, the market took the view that permitting and electricity-rate rules for data centers could get stricter. Dan Ives of Wedbush expected that around 10% of the 800 to 1,000 data centers planned in the US could fall through.
On November 5, Bloomberg also reported that Micron had halted construction of its plant in Taoyuan, Taiwan, because of a labor dispute. Micron said its Taiwan operations were running normally, and no new data-center policy has been announced yet.
Results were actually at record highs
Micron
: revenue of $54.23 billion for the June-August quarter (fiscal Q4), about 4.8 times the $11.32 billion of a year earlier
Sandisk
: revenue of about $11 billion for the July-September quarter, up 378% from a year earlier; data-center revenue of $4.8 billion, up 61% from the previous quarter
Apple
: on its November 2 earnings call, CEO John Ternus described memory prices as being at “unprecedented levels”
Apple purchases memory, and when even Apple talks about the cost, that points to a shortage that is still under way. What has turned is expectations, not results.
3 reasons the stocks fell while memory prices rose
Reason 1. The increases are getting smaller
Investors look less at whether prices are rising than at how fast they are rising. In July, TrendForce expected Q3 contract prices for conventional DRAM to rise 13-18%. For Q4 it lowered its forecast to 10-15%. Its September 30 report also said the rise in conventional DRAM contract prices would slow.
For NAND, the forecast went the other way, from 10-15% in Q3 to 15-20% in Q4. Even so, the NAND makers Sandisk and Kioxia fell further. That suggests this drop had more to do with worry about the pace of investment from next year on than with today’s prices.
Reason 2. Spot prices have stalled
Contract prices are set each quarter between memory makers and the server and smartphone companies they supply. Spot prices are what memory trades for on the distribution market at any given moment.
As of November 10, TrendForce put the average spot price of DDR4 16Gb at $79.99, down 0.31%, and DDR5 16Gb at $60.17, unchanged. Spot prices often show changes in demand before contract prices do, so the market tends to read a stall like this as a sign that the peak is near.
Reason 3. Kioxia was first to signal that profits have stopped growing
On October 30, Kioxia forecast operating profit of 1.995 trillion yen for October-December. That is almost the same as the 1.9947 trillion yen it earned in July-September, and below the market estimate of about 2.35 trillion yen that Bloomberg reported. On November 2, the first trading day after the release, its shares fell 15.78%.
Profits are large, but the signal that they are no longer growing spread to Sandisk, which also makes NAND, and then to memory as a whole.
⤢
Data: Micron earnings releases (Sep 2025-Sep 2026); as of 2026-11-10
Micron’s revenue jumped from $11.3 billion to $54.2 billion in a year, and the company expects it to grow further, to $61.5 billion, next quarter. What the market is asking is how much longer this climb can last.
4 indicators to watch
Micron’s December results
: whether revenue for the September-November quarter tops guidance of $61.5 billion (±$1.5 billion)
TrendForce contract prices
: whether its forecasts of 10-15% for Q4 DRAM and 15-20% for NAND hold
Enterprise SSDs
: the only memory product for which TrendForce expects price increases to get bigger in Q4. It expects bit demand this year to grow by more than 80%
US data-center policy and Big Tech capital spending plans
: where this round of declines began
In short, this plunge came not from falling memory prices but from 
doubts about how long, and how fast, the boom can run
. Even if contract prices keep rising, the stocks can be shaken first if the increases shrink and data-center investment is delayed.

Frequently asked questions
Q1. If memory prices are rising but the stocks are falling, do memory makers’ results get worse too?
A1. Not right away. Most of a memory maker’s revenue comes from contract prices, and Q4 contract prices are still heading up. But share prices reflect conditions several quarters ahead, so when the increases shrink, the stocks move before the results do.
Q2. How does Sandisk’s stock split affect its share price?
A2. After the close on November 19, Sandisk will split each share into eight, and from November 20 its shares will trade at the post-split price. Only the price per share falls; the value of the company as a whole stays the same. Charts drawn after the split are comparable with earlier prices only where past prices have also been adjusted for the split.
Q3. Is HBM unaffected by this plunge?
A3. HBM is high-performance DRAM used in AI accelerators, and it is the memory product with the tightest supply. TrendForce expects HBM’s average sales price to rise 121% in 2027, largely because the share of expensive HBM4 is growing. But HBM is also tied to data-center investment, so it did not escape this round of declines.
