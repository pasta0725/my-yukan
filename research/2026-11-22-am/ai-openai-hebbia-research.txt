Hebbia’s tests of GPT-6 Astra found that the model cited required source types more often than competing models and met more presentation requirements in financial research tasks.
The model led the next-best alternative by 13.6 percentage points in citing required source types. Hebbia also found that 99.5% of its cited excerpts were relevant to the questions asked.
For presentation tasks, GPT-6 Astra met 97.9% of applicable requirements, a 14.4-percentage-point lead over competitors. The tests took place in August and September 2026.
Hebbia’s Matrix platform works with large collections of documents, including customer contracts and financial statements. In a hypothetical industrial-software acquisition scenario, Matrix reviewed about 2,000 files and produced PowerPoint slides following user guidelines.
“Citations help with traceability as well as giving users confidence that the AI found the right answer,” said Kavita Anand, a member of technical staff at Hebbia.

Before a banker can recommend an acquisition to their client, they need to make sense of financial statements, customer contracts and management presentations. Hebbia’s Matrix helps bring that research together, linking answers to the documents behind them.
Hebbia tested OpenAI’s GPT‑6 Astra to improve two parts of that work: finding the sources a question requires and building a presentation that follows the banker’s brief.
“We’ve been strong partners with OpenAI over the course of Hebbia’s lifetime, and the leaps GPT-6 Astra has made in terms of quality outputs and accuracy have truly been surprising. GPT-6 Astra shines compared to any other model we’ve tested before on these two fronts.”
—Kavita Anand, Member of Technical Staff, Product, Hebbia
From source documents to presentations
It could work like this: a banker is helping their client assess a strategic acquisition of an industrial-software company, with roughly 2,000 files to review. What makes its revenue durable? How does it compare with peers? In Hebbia’s hypothetical example, the banker could load the data room into Matrix and examine customer concentration, contract terms and churn in cited cells. Matrix could bring in peer filings and industry news to help develop the deal thesis, then turn that research into a diligence summary and valuation deck in the firm’s template.
In that workflow, GPT‑6 Astra selects documents or Matrix cells to support an answer, then turns the analysis into native PowerPoint slides that follow the user’s structure and guidelines.
“Traceability is really important to our users because they’re basing millions of dollars of decisions on what the AI actually comes back with,” Kavita says. “Citations help with traceability as well as giving users confidence that the AI found the right answer.”
In Hebbia’s tests, GPT‑6 Astra combined broader source coverage with highly relevant citations. It scored 13.6 percentage points above the best alternative tested on citing the required types of sources, while 99.5% of the excerpts it cited were relevant to the question.
Hebbia tested a prerelease build in August-September 2026, using the same financial research cases and grading criteria for each model. Domain experts wrote or audited the criteria against source documents.
“When you’re looking at a data room with thousands of documents, that’s extremely important. Even a single percentage-point gain can yield a lot of marginal benefit,” Kavita says.
A presentation also has to look like work the banker would send to a client.
“I don’t want to create a deck and send that to my partner that looks like AI made it,” Kavita says. “I want it to be made in my firm’s branding, and I want it to look like a human-made slide.”
GPT‑6 Astra led on both following the brief and presentation quality. In Hebbia’s tests, it met 97.9% of the applicable presentation requirements, putting it 14.4 percentage points ahead of the best alternative tested on that measure. Other models traded off polish and adherence to the brief; GPT‑6 Astra ranked first on both.
“In the past, we’ve seen models that really shine on one or the other of these dimensions where they’re really good at craft, but the instruction following and adhering to guidelines is not strong, or vice versa. GPT‑6 Astra is the first model we’ve seen where it’s number one on both of those fronts,” Kavita says.
In Hebbia, users can open a citation to see the underlying document and highlighted passage; slide footnotes lead back to the source material, too. A review could be as specific as checking a customer-concentration figure against the source table and correcting the reporting period before the deck goes to the investment committee.
Hebbia wants bankers to spend less time reformatting decks and checking sources, and more time assessing a deal. Kavita describes what a better first draft could make possible:
“What’s amazing about GPT-6 Astra is its ability to create really precise outputs and artifacts that adhere to user guidelines. You can imagine the hours this will save professionals when you get a deck that is closer to your final output. The overnighters that you have to pull get reduced to a few minutes of reviewing GPT-6 Astra’s output.”
—Kavita Anand, Member of Technical Staff, Product, Hebbia
A future role for agents
Kavita sees a future role for agents that monitor information in the background and surface signals for finance professionals to assess.
“Imagine getting an important signal a few hours before your competitors,” she says. “You can get started thinking about it and see and process more opportunities that a human is not manually hunting. It can unlock a lot of value for our users.”
Hebbia wants to build on these results to help finance professionals assess deals with research they can trace back to its sources.
