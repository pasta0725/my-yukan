Shortical’s “Inevitable” places the entertainment industry’s fears about AI’s threats to their creativity centerstage in this fully AI-generated microdrama series premiering Friday. 
The psychological thriller stars an AI version of Israeli actor Aki Avni. After the actor, who is actively involved in the creative process for this microdrama, sells his face and likeness to an AI studio, he realizes that the AI version of himself is implicated in an attempted murder he didn’t commit.
The microseries is quite meta as Hollywood battles these same issues around AI, consent and likeness. The irony, of course, is that Avni was heavily involved in the production for this vertical and helped create his own digital double, working alongside the filmmakers.

“It is surprising,” Avni told TheWrap of seeing the AI version of himself act and make choices on screen. “As an actor, you see yourself on the screen, you have relation … so now it’s even weirder in a way.”
“We have this fear from this from the unknown because AI and this technology is still very unknown,” he said. “But if you really put your step in and know exactly what is a yes for you and what is a no for you, you can win the situation.”
In an exclusive clip obtained by TheWrap, Avni drives onto the Paramount lot as news coverage swirls about him giving over his likeness as his AI double stars in 12 projects at once. Ominous music plays as he realizes that no one is working in person on the set anymore. 
This marked Ofir Lobel’s first AI-generated project and first vertical series. The director and creator of the series had extensive directing experience before the project working on Netflix series “Blank Space” and “Trust No One.”
“It’s not a new lane for me because it’s just movie making,” he said. “It’s just a faster lane. It’s a lane with more possibilities.”
“‘Inevitable’ is a microdrama series about about losing control of your likeness, and we made it by giving our actor, our main lead, the control of it,” Ofir Lobel said. “That’s what I really like about it because we believe in actors, we believe in crew, we believe in movie making as a team and not just as a you know technology.”
The first trailer of the series, exclusively obtained by TheWrap, sees high energy police chases, sultry moments and Avni’s AI avatar on a Hollywood press tour. As his new lane in his career starts to go up in flames, Avni confronts his daughter and even his AI avatar in an emotional climatic moment.
“The future of entertainment isn’t AI or actors; it’s AI with actors,” Lobel said. 
“Inevitable” premiered Nov. 13 on the 
Shortical App
 with 24 episodes. New episodes of the 50-episode microdrama drop weekly. Avni teased that if the fans like it that there could be more episodes in store.
The microdrama is created and directed by Lobel, written by Sharon Ben Tovim,  and produced by Shortical, led by CEO Shimoni.

Most stories about AI actors start with someone who never agreed to anything. 
Inevitable
 starts the other way round. The Israeli actor 
Aki Avni
 helped build his own digital double, then let it play the lead in a fully AI-generated vertical series about an actor who loses control of his likeness.
The microdrama premiered on 
13 November 2026

TheWrap's exclusive report
 (Tess Patton, 13 November) and Shortical's own pages on 16 November 2026. For writers, the interesting part is not the tech. It is who decided what, and where that was written down.
What Shortical released
Shortical's page for Inevitable, captured on 16 November 2026.
The premise, in Shortical's words: after selling his likeness to an AI company to spend more time with his daughter, actor Aki Avni is framed for murder and has to clear his name before he loses his family, his freedom and his identity. In TheWrap's exclusive clip, he drives onto a studio lot while the news reports that his AI double is starring in 12 projects at once, and nobody works on set in person anymore.

The counts don't fully match. TheWrap describes a 50-episode series that opened with 24 episodes, with new ones weekly. Shortical's page, on the day we checked, listed 24 episodes of about 115 seconds each, marked "Complete series", with the first 10 free. Treat the episode count as unsettled.
TheWrap, captured on 16 November 2026.
Consent written into the production
The line everyone will quote is Lobel's: "The future of entertainment isn't AI or actors; it's AI with actors." The more useful one comes just before it. "Inevitable is a microdrama series about losing control of your likeness," he told TheWrap, "and we made it by giving our actor, our main lead, the control of it."
Avni's own take is practical. Seeing his double act on screen was "surprising", he said, and "even weirder" than watching himself. His advice to other actors: "if you really put your step in and know exactly what is a yes for you and what is a no for you, you can win the situation."
Shortical is also turning this into a casting pipeline. Its 
License Your Face
 page invites people to submit their social media links. If a profile picture fits a future AI micro-series, the company sends a contract naming the series, the character and the payment. If the person declines, the page says, Shortical won't use the image any further.
Shortical's License Your Face page, captured on 16 November 2026.
That page raises as many questions as it answers. It does not say how long a likeness can be used, whether it can appear in other series, or what happens to the model of a face after the contract ends. Those are exactly the clauses Avni's "yes and no" refers to.
What it changes for writers
A digital double does whatever the script says. That moves a decision that used to happen on set, between the actor and the director, back onto the page. Three habits follow from it:
Write the limits next to the character.
 If a performer has agreed to a double, note in the character description what that double may not do: certain scenes, certain words, nudity, real brands. Our guide on 
character descriptions that attract actors
 covers the rest of that page.
Flag every scene that goes beyond the agreement.
 A stunt the actor would refuse in person is still a stunt when it is generated. Mark it in the script before anyone renders it.
Keep one reference set per character, approved by the person.
 A double drifts across 50 short episodes unless every shot comes from the same approved sheets. That is the same discipline as 
keeping a series consistent
.

FAQ
What is Inevitable?
A fully AI-generated vertical microdrama from Shortical that premiered on 13 November 2026. Aki Avni plays an actor who sells his likeness to an AI company and is framed for a murder his double is linked to.
Did Aki Avni agree to his AI double?
Yes. TheWrap reports that he helped create his own digital double with the filmmakers and was involved in the creative process. TheWrap's headline calls it an "approved AI double".
Who made Inevitable?
Ofir Lobel created and directed it, Sharon Ben Tovim wrote it, and Shortical produced it, according to TheWrap.
How many episodes does Inevitable have?
The sources differ. TheWrap describes 50 episodes, 24 at launch and the rest weekly. Shortical's page listed 24 episodes of about two minutes on 16 November 2026, with the first 10 free.
What is Shortical's License Your Face page?
A casting call for faces to use in future AI micro-series. People submit social media links, and if their profile picture fits, Shortical sends a contract naming the series, the character and the payment. If they decline, Shortical says it won't use the image further.
What should a screenwriter take from it?
When a double does the acting, the limits a performer agrees to have to be on the page. Write them next to the character, flag scenes that go further, and keep one approved reference set per character.
