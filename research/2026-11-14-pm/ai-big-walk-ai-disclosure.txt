The statement specifically called out AI-generated code, saying it was "not an exception," adding: "Programming is a creative discipline whose labour deserves the same respect we extend to any part of making a game. Indie darling Big Walk has found itself in hot water as developer House House admits to previously undisclosed AI usage "in the final stretch" of its co-op game's development, which it says touched "well under 0.1%" of the code.
This has surfaced after a 
statement from a group of jurors
 on the Independent Games Festival (IGF) was shared on Bluesky, acknowledging the "significant increase in the number of submitted games developed with generative AI tools" for the upcoming awards show. The group – which doesn't speak for IGF leadership or every juror – outlines the many issues with AI, from "ecological harms" to "cognitive and psychological harms to its users" and "harms to labor and worker power in creative fields," and says "we are declining to vote for any game that uses generative AI in any capacity." That includes 
everything
, be it
artwork, voice acting, or indeed, code.
It's code that Big House developer House House now admits that it used large language models for help with in a 
public statement shared on the game's FAQ page
. The devs acknowledge that "a version" of this statement was sent to the IGF as "a suggested part" of its submission, but say that the reason there's no AI disclosure on its Steam page is because 
Valve
 "does not request disclosure of code or chatbot usage," which is what House House used the tech for.

"In the final stretch of Big Walk's development, LLMs were used sparingly by House House to help work through difficult technical challenges and bugs," its statement begins. "In some cases, snippets from LLM responses were copied as a starting point to be modified into game code – we'd estimate that well under 0.1% of Big Walk's code had anything to do with this usage, and generative AI was not used in any other context when making the game.
"The primary use case for House House's use of LLMs was to research, via chatbots, issues we encountered with third-party code."
The devs give an example of one use of AI. After running into an issue where the game was slowing down significantly at sunrise and sunset, they were unable to find a fix themselves or after asking an engineer working for game engine Unity. "Finally," House House says, the team asked an AI chatbot instead.
"After a few dead ends, it suggested a possible fix, and while we had to modify the suggestion extensively, it worked," House House says. "This fix represented about 10 lines of code inside URP [Universal Render Pipeline]."

Despite the studio's usage of the tech, House House says "this is not an endorsement of, or argument for, the use of AI tools, however minimal." It continues with an apology: "We have some concern that voluntary disclosures like this might contribute to the normalization of this technology, or its treatment as 'inevitable.' As you might expect, fans aren't exactly happy. Looking at Big Walk's reviews on Steam, there's been a huge spike in negative feedback posted today – compared to just one negative review yesterday and three the day before, at the time of writing, today's reviews are just 36% positive, with 156 giving it a big, red thumbs down, with a number also indicating that they've refunded the title.

.

As disappointing as the news is for many, Big Walk is far from the only well-loved game to find itself in the center of a, uh, Big Backlash for AI usage. Last year, Clair Obscur:

What is 
Big Walk

Big Walk

As of August 4th, 2026,

Big Walk

It’s available for Windows and Mac (via 
Steam

PlayStation 5

      Players
How many people can play?
#
Two or more players, with a maximum of 12.
Does the game work differently with different numbers of players?
#
A little bit, yes. Each time the host starts a session, they choose a 2 player, 3 player or 4+ player version of the world. Some things in the world change to adapt to each of these choices.
What if not everyone can play the whole session?
#
Think of this setting as the minimum number of players who will be participating, rather than the maximum. If some players won’t be joining for your whole session, it might be better to pick a lower number.
What’s the ideal number of players?
#
Any number in that range is good! We didn’t make the game with a specific number of players in mind.
With fewer players the game is a more focused co-op experience. Once you get closer to 12 it’s more of a fun, chaotic mess. But it’ll be good no matter how many people you’re playing with.
Is there a single-player mode?
#
No, sorry. 
Big Walk
 is entirely about teamwork and talking. Everything you do in the game is designed for cooperative multiplayer.
(You can start a game and explore a 
little
 bit as a single player, but you won’t get very far.)
Will there be online matchmaking?
#
No.
Big Walk
 is a long-form cooperative game. It’s made to be played with the same group over multiple sessions, so it doesn’t make sense to throw strangers together randomly. We think it’s a much better experience to organize and play 
Big Walk
 with people you know — or people you want to know better.
Can people on different systems play together?
#
Sure! 
Big Walk
 has crossplay support, so players on different systems can play together.
Why doesn’t 
Big Walk
 allow same-screen coop?
#
The fun of 
Big Walk
 only really works if players are physically separate from one another, and exclusively use the in-game voice chat to communicate. We even recommend that 
players avoid sharing the same room, let alone the same screen

If you’d like to know more about why physical separation and playing with the in-game voice chat is so important to the game, please take a look through the

(At this point, consider changing the Password for your session.)

Can I use a mouse and keyboard? A game controller?
#
Both will work well.
The computer version of the game supports most game controllers. And we even support the mouse mode of the Nintendo Switch 2.
Can I rebind my controls?

The game host saves your progress automatically.
How does hosting work?
#
A 
Big Walk

Once a host starts a session, the other players can then connect to it. When the host stops the session, all other players will be disconnected. If you want to continue your journey later where you left off last time, you’ll need your host to host again!

If you your friend is on your friends list on the platform you’re on — like Steam, or a console — your friend’s host game should automatically appear in a menu of active friend games.

Who can join my game?

Your session will be visible to your friends, and any other players’ friends as well.
Anyone who knows your join code will be able to join as well.
If you want to control which friends join your game, we highly recommend setting a password that you share with the people you intend to play with.
If you are streaming the game, be mindful of letting people see your join code and/or password.
Does 
Big Walk

#
Yes! 
Big Walk

Your unique join code is displayed

Yes. Open the in-game menu, and choose “Session Details” to change your game’s password.

A Big Walk version number is made of three numbers, for example, 
1.2.3

Can 
Big Walk
 help with my first-person-game motion sickness?

Hopefully. First, you should try one of our unique “Motion Reduction Overlays”, which you can turn on in Settings > General. This will add an overlay that may help you feel more grounded, and less motion sick.
Second, if you’re playing on Mac or PC, you can try adjusting your “Field of View” (FOV) in Settings > Graphics. A higher number allows you to see more of the world, which may make you feel less claustrophobic, and less motion sick.
What accessibility features does 
Big Walk

#
Big Walk
 allows you to:
Skip any challenge
Rebind all controls
Choose from multiple crosshair styles
Choose between “Hold” or “Toggle” for some controls
Adjust the FOV (PC and Mac only)
Select from various Motion Reduction overlays
Use text chat anywhere you might use voice chat, with all the same restrictions and possibilities

Certain challenges in 
Big Walk

      In-Game Chat
How do I talk to people in 
Big Walk

Big Walk

Can I play

Yes! 
Big Walk

I don’t have a standalone microphone. Can I still play?

Yes!
Switch 2, PS5 Dualsense controllers, Steamdeck, and most laptops all have built-in microphones.

NO WAY. Talking to players outside the game is ❌ 
NOT ALLOWED

Wait, why can’t I talk to people in a separate chat?
#
Big Walk

We know that in some games, in-game chat is totally optional, and the game is still fun without using it. But in

In-game communication is very important!

I can’t join or host any games at all.
#
If you’ve 
ruled out the most common issues
 but are still unable to join games (or host one yourself), it’s likely you are unable to connect to Epic Online Services (EOS) servers.
First, 
check your device’s system clock
 to make sure it’s accurate.
If the system clock varies from the online clock by a significant amount, it will prevent you from either joining a game, or hosting anyone else.
If your clock is synced and you still can’t connect, 
check whether your network can access EOS
 at all:
If you have access to a computer, try the following:
On PC:
 Open the “Command Prompt” app > type 
ping api.epicgames.dev
 > Hit enter.
On Mac:
 Open the “Terminal” app >  type 
ping api.epicgames.dev
 > hit enter.
(You can also do the same on your phone. Google “how to ping website on phone”)
If you can send and receive packets, your network is able to successfully connect to EOS.
If you are playing on computer and are unable to connect to EOS, 
you might need to reset your proxy settings:
On PC:
 In the Command Prompt again > type “netsh winhttp reset proxy” > Hit enter.
If you are playing on console, or the proxy reset doesn’t work, 
you may need to check your router settings.
Is it possible that epicgames is blocked? By parental controls for example.
If the 
ping
 command confirms your network can connect to EOS, but the game still doesn’t work, please 
contact us
 for further assistance.

The game audio is cutting in and out on macOS.
#
On Apple Silicon (M-chips), the CPU, GPU, and sound processing all share the same resources and hardware, so sound can cut out when the others are working too hard.
Try freeing up resources by turning off any programs or services running in the background.
Limitations of Bluetooth headphones connections can make the problem worse. Try swapping to wired headphones if possible.
If these don’t work, try lowering the in-game graphics quality by following 
these steps
.
Other players can’t hear my voice.
#
Open Settings > Audio, and check if the “Microphone Activity” is bouncing when you talk. If not:
See if your mic is working in other programs.
Make sure your microphone is properly plugged in fully. Hey, it happens.
Also make sure your microphone or headset isn’t muted on the device itself. Check your mute switch. Lower your boom mic.
Check if you have Settings > Audio > Mute Mode set to “hold”. If so, you will need to hold the button to talk.
Make sure no one is “silenced” in the moderation menu.
Make sure your Bluetooth headset is working properly.

My wireless Bluetooth headset is not working properly.
#
Due to the limitations of Bluetooth connections, a wireless headset operates as two distinct devices:
A Head
phone
 device (high quality stereo audio + 
no
 microphone input) or
A Head
set
 device (lower quality mono audio + microphone input)
Some Bluetooth headsets are able to dynamically swap between these devices. But others need to be manually assigned to work with the game. If you are using a Bluetooth headset and have audio but no microphone, or vice versa, it may be because of a miss-match between the headset and headphone devices.
On Windows 11 you can get your wireless bluetooth headset working with these steps:
Right-click the speaker icon in the Taskbar, and select Open Volume Mixer
Open Big Walk
Alt-tab to return to the Volume Mixer. You should see Big Walk in the list
Expand Big Walk by clicking the arrow on the right
Make sure the output and input device are both set to the head
set
 device
We recommend playing Big Walk with a standalone microphone or wired headset, if available. This will allow you to enjoy stereo audio, and better experience the highly spatialised and directional sound design.
My audio interface isn’t working with 
Big Walk
#
Some popular audio interfaces have XLR microphone inputs that default to Channel 2, but 
Big Walk
 can only recieve mic input from the Channel 1 audio stream.
Your audio interface may have utilty software that allows you to reassign or combine your mic input to CH1. Otherwise, you may have to use a program like 
Voicemeeter
 to create a virtual microphone that inputs to CH1.
Voices sound doubled, or there’s weird voice echo.
#
Someone likely has a feedback loop going, which means the microphone is picking up voices from a speaker.
Find out which player is causing the doubling. Each player could take turns muting themselves using the mute button and you can see when the doubling stops.
If it’s you, try muting yourself when you aren’t talking.
Consider switching to headphones if you’re not using them.
Big Walk
 is running slowly on my computer.
#
If your framerate or FPS is bad, it could be graphic settings. Every computer is different, of course, but here’s one way to optimize 
Big Walk
:
Go to Settings > Graphics and lowering your “Render Scale”. This reduces the GPU load.
Then, go to Settings > Graphics and lower the “Quality” setting. This lowers the CPU & GPU load.
Finally, try increasing “Render Scale” back up gradually.
This will help balance your CPU with your GPU, and should improve performance and make 
Big Walk
 run smoother on your computer.
On Windows 11, stuttering is a possible symptom of 
a broken driver being present on your system
.

We’ve had reports of Xbox wireless controllers not working well on Mac when using Steam. If this is happening for you, you could try the following steps.
Make sure you’re connected through Bluetooth, not through USB C direct connection.
In System Settings > Game Controllers > Xbox Wireless Controller, make a profile for the Steam app for your controller after connecting, and toggle on “Increase compatibility”.
Enable these two permissions for Steam:
System Settings > Privacy & Security > Accessibility > add Steam to the list.
System Settings > Privacy & Security > Input monitoring > add Steam to the list.
Enable Steam Input with the default controller profile for Big Walk.
