TL;DR
Google Gemini Notebook can now turn your research sources into interactive websites to help you understand complex information.
Users can create visual explainers, interactive dashboards, and project management trackers by typing “make me a site” and describing what they need.
The feature is now rolling out globally to web users, with mobile support coming soon.
Google is making 
Gemini Notebook
 more useful for understanding complex topics and organizing research notes with a new interactive websites feature.
Starting today, users can ask Gemini Notebook to create interactive websites by simply typing “make me a site” in the chat. They can also describe what they want the site to include, depending on what they’re working on.
Google 
says
 the feature can be useful for a range of tasks. For instance, students can create visual explainers to help them prepare for exams. These could include interactive periodic tables or historical timelines. Meanwhile, professionals can use the feature to build dashboards that make it easier to explore data, such as quarterly sales performance.
Adamya Sharma / Android Authority
Gemini Notebook’s interactive sites can also help with project management. Users can ask Gemini Notebook to create trackers that outline tasks and deadlines, giving them a more interactive way to keep tabs on ongoing work.
Once Gemini Notebook creates a site, users can select the “Open in a new tab” option to access and interact with it. Google also lets users share these creations with others.
The new capability is rolling out globally on the web starting today. It’s available to Gemini Notebook users aged 18 and older, including those with Google Workspace subscriptions. Google says the feature will also arrive on the mobile app, although it hasn’t provided a specific release date.

Google has rolled out a new capability for Gemini Notebook which will enable the users to transform their notes research materials and source documents into interactive websites.
 The feature announced by the tech giant allow users to generate websites directly from the information stored in their notebooks, making them better understand and explore complex topics. As per Google, users can simply type ‘make me a site’ in the Gemini Notebook chat and the give a description of the project they wish to create. The AI-powered tool will then create an interactive website on the basis of the sources and content.
“Starting today, Gemini Notebook can turn your sources into interactive sites to help you quickly make sense of complex information. To get started, simply type “make me a site” in your Gemini Notebook chat along with a description of what you want to build for work or study,” said Google in a blog post.
Designed for work and study
Google said the feature can be used across a variety of educational and professional scenarios. Examples include creating visual explainers for exam preparation, such as interactive periodic tables and historical timelines, as well as interactive dashboards for analysing data like quarterly sales performance. 
Users can also generate project management trackers to organise tasks and deadlines. The company says the goal is to help users quickly make sense of large amounts of information by presenting it in a more engaging and interactive format.

Google adds shareable interactive sites to Gemini Notebook on the web
By 
Saptam Modak
4
 min read
·
Published on 
Nov 17, 2026 · 7:50 AM IST
Representative archive photograph of a study desk, not the Gemini Notebook interface. Photo: Hippopx, CC0 public domain.
-Advertisement-
Google has added site generation to Gemini Notebook, allowing the research tool to turn material in a notebook into an interactive page. The feature began rolling out on the web on 16 November, with study aids, sales dashboards and project trackers among the company's examples.
The change gives Notebook another output beyond a written answer or a static summary. Users can describe the kind of site they need in chat, then open the result separately and share it with other people.
Access is being introduced worldwide for adults using Gemini Notebook, including Workspace subscribers. Google says the mobile app will follow, but its announcement does not give a date for that release.
Notebook sources become something to explore
Google's announcement describes the feature as a way to work through complex information using sources already gathered in Notebook. The request combines that material with a description of the intended task.
For study, the company points to a periodic table that people can interact with and a timeline for history. Its workplace examples are a dashboard built around quarterly sales and a tracker showing project deadlines and tasks.
Those are examples supplied by Google, rather than independently measured results. The launch post does not provide an accuracy benchmark for the generated sites or establish how reliably a page will represent every detail in its sources.
A site can leave the chat window
The starting prompt is "make me a site", followed by a description of what the user wants. Once Notebook produces it, a control opens the site in another browser tab, where the user can interact with it.
Google also says the result can be shared. That makes the addition different from a response intended only for the person asking the question: the generated page can become an output for someone else to use.
The announcement does not spell out the permissions available for sharing a site, whether it can be exported as code or whether it will refresh automatically after a source changes. It would be premature to treat those capabilities as part of the launch.
Web rollout includes Workspace users
The initial release applies to users aged 18 or above. Google's explicit inclusion of Workspace subscribers puts the business examples alongside the study features, rather than limiting the announcement to personal accounts.
Its existing help pages describe Notebook as a source-based research assistant. Supported material includes documents, websites, videos and audio, although individual source types have their own limits. The new announcement concerns what Notebook can produce from collected information, not the removal of those input limits.
For now, the company has confirmed a web rollout and a later mobile arrival. It has not published a completion timetable for all eligible accounts in this announcement.
The bottom line
Gemini Notebook is adding interactive sites to the ways it presents research. The immediate change is on the web: a notebook can become a shareable study or work page through a chat request. Mobile timing and several details about managing the generated sites remain unspecified.
