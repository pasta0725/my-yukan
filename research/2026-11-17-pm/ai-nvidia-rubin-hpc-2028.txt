NVIDIA announces new Rubin HPC GPU for scientific computing, launching in 2028
NVIDIA has announced Rubin HPC, a new GPU designed for high-performance computing, scientific simulations and data analytics. Revealed at SC26, the GPU is part of the Vera Rubin platform but targets workloads that require large amounts of general-purpose GPU computation rather than primarily AI inference and training.
The company confirms that Rubin HPC features 
more than 50,000 CUDA cores on a single GPU
, the highest count NVIDIA has announced for one GPU. The processor will also support seventh-generation NVLink technology, moving beyond the NVLink 6 interconnect used by the existing Rubin architecture.
Rubin HPC focuses on compute and data movement
Rubin HPC introduces sparse data acceleration designed to improve access to scattered data during computation. NVIDIA says this capability will keep its CUDA cores supplied with data rather than leaving them waiting for memory operations. The GPU targets operations including sparse matrix calculations, stencil computations, sorting and reductions.

The Supercomputing 2026 conference is under way, and despite continuing turmoil elsewhere in the company, Nvidia has lots to reveal at the show. Foremost among its announcements today, Nvidia took the wraps off a new member of the Rubin GPU family 
called Rubin HPC
, a chip that the company says is meant to accelerate agentic workflows and simulation for scientists, engineers, and analysts working with large data sets.
To accelerate those most demanding workloads, Nvidia says Rubin HPC has the highest number of CUDA cores on any of its GPUs - "more than 50,000" - which might imply that this chip uses a configuration similar to the four-die Rubin Ultra GPU, which links those dies together using NVLink-C2C.
Today's Rubin GPU has 224 SMs and 28,672 CUDA cores, so scaling compute resources to the degree Rubin HPC does would necessarily require more dies on a package. Nvidia didn't reveal more details of the chip's physical layout alongside the announcement. (The representative image shared by the company wouldn't appear to take this into account.)

That balance is what optimizes Rubin HPC for scientific computing. It keeps the GPU’s record number of CUDA cores supplied with data, so they spend their time computing rather than waiting.
