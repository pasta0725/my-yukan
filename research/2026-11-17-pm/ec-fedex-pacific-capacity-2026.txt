SINGAPORE, November 3, 2026
 – Federal Express Corporation, one of the world’s largest express transportation companies, has expanded its trans-Pacific network with an additional 567,000 kilograms (1.25 million pounds) of weekly capacity between Asia Pacific and the United States. The new five-times-weekly Boeing 777F service is expected to help businesses across advanced industrial manufacturing, semiconductor production, AI infrastructure, and premium e-commerce access additional capacity to support rising customer demand during one of the busiest trading periods of the year.
The new service operates through three routing variations connecting our 
Asia Pacific hub in
Guangzhou and sub-regional hubs in Shanghai, China; Osaka, Japan; with our gateway in Clark, the Philippines, via Anchorage, Alaska, to the FedEx World Hub in Memphis, Tennessee
. Businesses across 
Southeast Asia, including Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, and Vietnam, as well as Hong Kong,
 can access additional capacity to the United States through connections via Clark and Shanghai. This expanded network provides customers flexibility, capacity, and connectivity to reach U.S. customers and respond quickly to changing customer demands.
The enhanced network also introduces a 
new five-times-weekly connection for Asia Pacific customers to Dallas–Fort Worth via Anchorage
, providing an additional gateway to one of the United States’ leading technology and data center markets. The connection strengthens access between Asia Pacific manufacturing hubs and businesses across the Dallas-Fort Worth region, supporting industries that rely on the movement of high-value, time-sensitive shipments such as semiconductors, servers, and other critical components supporting the expansion of artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure.
The additional capacity comes as businesses navigate growing trans-Pacific trade volumes and increasingly interconnected supply chains. According to the International Air Transport Association (IATA) Air Cargo Market Analysis, the Asia–North America corridor recorded 9% year-on-year growth in July 2026, making it the fastest-growing major air cargo trade lane globally
[1]
. At the same time, Asia Pacific continues to play a central role in global technology supply chains, accounting for approximately 58% of global semiconductor industry revenue, according to a 2026 FedEx and Frost & Sullivan report
[2]
.
As retailers, manufacturers, and technology companies prepare for year-end demand spikes, reliable air cargo capacity has become a competitive advantage. By expanding trans-Pacific capacity, FedEx is helping customers move critical inventory, respond faster to market demand, and unlock growth opportunities driven by advanced manufacturing, the AI-driven digital economy, and premium e-commerce.
“Global supply chains are becoming increasingly interconnected, particularly across advanced manufacturing, AI-related industries, and premium e-commerce,” said Salil Chari, president, Asia Pacific, FedEx. “The addition of this capacity comes at a critical time as customers prepare for increased shipping volumes ahead of the year-end peak season. Businesses need reliable access to capacity, speed, and global connectivity to serve customers and keep supply chains moving. These additional flights strengthen links between Asia Pacific and key U.S. markets, including Dallas–Fort Worth, while supporting growth across semiconductors, technology, and AI-driven industries.”
The service builds on a series of network enhancements introduced across Asia Pacific over the past year, including expanded flight connectivity from 
Bangkok, Penang,
 and 
Sydney to the Guangzhou hub
. Together, these investments help customers access international markets more efficiently, strengthen supply chain resilience, and capitalize on emerging growth opportunities.

FedEx has added an additional 1.25 million pounds of weekly 
air cargo
 capacity between Asia Pacific and the United States 
by adding
 five weekly Boeing 777 
freighter
 flights ahead of the year-end 
shipping
 rush. The 
expansion
 targets 
manufacturers
, 
technology
 companies and online 
retailers
 shipping goods to U.S. customers.
What’s Related
The service uses three route variations that link FedEx facilities in 
China
, Japan and the Philippines to its World Hub in Memphis, Tennessee, via Anchorage, Alaska. FedEx also introduced a five-times-weekly connection to Dallas–Fort Worth through Anchorage.
The added capacity totals 567,000 kilograms each week. FedEx said the flights are intended for shipments tied to 
semiconductor
 production, 
AI
infrastructure
, advanced manufacturing, and premium 
e-commerce
.
“The addition of this capacity comes at a critical time as customers prepare for increased shipping volumes ahead of the year-end 
peak season
,” said Salil Chari, president of Asia Pacific at FedEx. The company described the service as operating but did not provide an exact launch date.

How the routes connect
The service links the company’s Asia Pacific hub in Guangzhou, China, its regional hubs in Shanghai, China, and Osaka, Japan, and its gateway in Clark, the Philippines. Flights travel through Anchorage to reach Memphis.
Shippers in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam, and Hong Kong can access the additional U.S. capacity through connections in Clark and Shanghai. That gives 
businesses
 in those markets access to the expanded service without being located at one of the main departure points named in the announcement.
FedEx did not publish individual schedules for the three route variations. It also did not list shipping prices or specific 
delivery
-time commitments for the new service.
Dallas–Fort Worth connection
The Dallas–Fort Worth connection gives Asian exporters another U.S. entry point for high-value goods that need to move quickly. FedEx identified semiconductors, servers, and other components used to build AI infrastructure as target shipments.
The company said the connection is intended to link Asian manufacturing centers with technology and 
data
 center businesses in the Dallas–Fort Worth area. The announcement does not break down how much of the added weekly capacity will go to Dallas–Fort Worth versus Memphis.
The expansion follows other FedEx flight changes in Asia Pacific over the past year. Those included additional connections from Bangkok, Penang, and Sydney to its Guangzhou hub, feeding shipments into the company’s international routes.

ボーイング777型貨物機で週5便、該当区間週当たり56万7000kg拡大
フェデラル エクスプレス（フェデックス）は11月10日、アジア太平洋地域～米国間の週当たりの輸送能力を125万ポンド（約56万7000kg）拡大し、太平洋横断ネットワークを増強したと発表した。
ボーイング777型貨物機による週5便の運航により、特にEC、先端製造業、半導体製造、AIインフラなどの分野で年末に高まる物流需要に対応する。
新たなフライトの運航は、中国・広州のフェデックス アジア太平洋地域ハブと、中国・上海、フィリピン・クラークの主要ゲートウェイ、関西国際空港の北太平洋地区ハブを、米アラスカ州アンカレッジ経由でテネシー州メンフィスのフェデックス グローバル・スーパーハブへ結ぶ3つの運航ルートで構成している。
ネットワーク拡充により、ユーザーは輸送ルートの柔軟性、輸送能力、ネットワークの接続性を活用して米国市場へのアクセスを強化し、変化する顧客需要に迅速に対応できるようになるとみている。
さらに、今回のネットワーク強化により、アジア太平洋地域のユーザー向けに、アンカレッジ経由で米国のダラス・フォートワース地域と結ぶ週5便のダイレクトフライトを新設。米国有数のテクノロジーおよびデータセンター市場への新たなアクセス手段が加わるとみている。
アジア太平洋地域の製造拠点とダラス・フォートワース地域に拠点を置く企業間のアクセスを強化し、半導体やサーバーをはじめ、AIインフラの拡大を支える重要部品など、高付加価値で時間厳守が求められる貨物を取り扱う産業を支援する。
（藤原秀行）
