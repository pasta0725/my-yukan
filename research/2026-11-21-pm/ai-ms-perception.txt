Microsoft used its Ignite 2026 conference on Tuesday to announce the general availability of Perception, a product designed for coordinating teams of AI agents to handle security tasks.
Unveiled in July as 
Project Perception
, the system has been in public preview inside Microsoft Defender since August.
Playbooks direct the agents, which are split into red, blue and green teams. Red agents play the attacker and probe for weaknesses; blue agents handle detection, alert triage, and investigations; and green agents fix problems.
The four blue team agents that are now generally available cover alert triage, attack investigation, detection authoring and threat intelligence. According to Microsoft, the Alert Triage Agent can handle roughly 80% of alerts in a typical Defender queue, based on an analysis of approximately 8 million alerts across its security products.
The Recon Agent, one of the red team agents, is also generally available, and a Web App Pen Test Agent has entered preview.
Two new green team playbooks work across Microsoft, AWS and Google Cloud. The agents behind them include Posture Prioritization, which is generally available, and Risk Reduction, which is in public preview.

The Risk Reduction Agent can recommend vulnerability shielding when a fix isn’t immediately available. This preview feature temporarily protects vulnerable assets until a permanent patch can be rolled out.
“Your team sets the guardrails and signs off on high-impact actions, and automated triggers launch playbooks when an incident meets the criteria you set,” Aleš Holeček, president and chief architect of Microsoft Security, wrote in a 
blog post
.
Microsoft is also previewing integrations that link Perception to SIEM, SOAR and IT service management tools via MCP and APIs.
The company also addressed the risk posed by AI agents, which it views as a new kind of insider with broad access to data and tools.
In preview, Agent 365 and Entra Suite can detect 60 types of local AI agents, including Claude Code, OpenAI Codex and GitHub Copilot. Intune policies can keep supported agents on the device.

This gives us a helpful foundation for making faster, more effective decisions and the necessary next steps.”
Once agentic triage narrows the focus to a much smaller set of confirmed threats that warrant deeper analysis, the Attack Investigation Agent can go deep on those incidents.
 It reasons across endpoints, identities, email, Active Directory, cloud resources, and relevant activity even when it didn’t generate an alert. The agent then reconstructs the attack timeline, identifies what was compromised, and recommends a prioritized response, such as containing an account or isolating a device.
You can also invoke the triage and investigation agents automatically based on triggers that run their respective playbooks. This allows agents to begin work before an analyst opens an alert and handle multiple attacks in parallel. Teams can scale analysis as demand changes, while security practitioners stay focused on the evidence, decisions, and outcomes that need their expertise.
Expand what your agents can see and act on
Threats do not stop at platform boundaries. Microsoft Perception brings third-party signals and external intelligence into agentic workflows, giving agents broader context to understand what is happening and helping teams turn new insight into protection faster.
The Attack Investigation, Detection Authoring, and Triage Agents can now reason across signals from specific third-party vendors, with support for additional vendors coming in the months ahead.
 The Attack Investigation Agent can follow evidence across more sources to build a more complete picture of an attack, the Detection Authoring Agent can use third-party data, tables, and schemas to build more tailored detection logic, and the Triage Agent can bring third-party alerts into its analysis to better assess and prioritize potential threats. Together, this helps agents make sense of more of the data already flowing through the SOC instead of stopping at the boundaries of the Microsoft security stack.

Think like an attacker with advanced code scanning
Security teams do not need another list of possible vulnerabilities. They need to know which weaknesses attackers can actually exploit and how to reduce the risk. Microsoft Perception is extending red-team testing to help defenders move from potential vulnerabilities to focused action.
With the
 Run web pen test 
playbook, defenders can safely conduct a penetration test. The 
Web App Pen Test Agent
 tests an authorized web application from an attacker’s perspective. It examines areas such as authentication, APIs, injection and business logic, providing defenders with the evidence they need to confirm which weaknesses are actually exploitable. For each identified weakness, it provides evidence, affected endpoints, severity, and proofs of concept.
In an independent benchmark conducted with Incalmo, 
the Web App Pen Test Agent achieved a 31.0% average task solve rate, about twice OpenAI Codex’s 15.2%, while
staying within specified testing boundaries in 91% of safety-constrained tests. 
The results demonstrate its ability to help defenders focus on exploitable weaknesses, while operating within the intended scope of an authorized assessment. Read the full results 
here
.
The
 Protect web app 
playbook extends the workflow from finding exploitable vulnerabilities to helping protect against them. It first uses the Web App Pen Test Agent to find weaknesses. Then, the 
Risk Reduction Agent
 leverages those findings to recommend targeted remediations, such as Web Application Firewall (WAF) rule changes in Azure Front Door, that can reduce exposure while teams work toward permanent fixes.
Both playbooks can optionally use code vulnerability scanning findings as input. 
One source for those findings is Codename MDASH, which can guide the agent toward relevant vulnerabilities to determine whether they are exploitable in the running application.
When we 
introduced Codename MDASH
 earlier this year, we shared our approach to using a multi-model agentic system to uncover deep vulnerabilities in source code. At Ignite, MDASH can scan larger repositories more efficiently by breaking them into logical units and analyzing them in stages. Teams can track scan progress and spend less time on false positives. Expanded support for the MAI-Cyber model family—including MAI-Cyber-1.1-Flash and 
MAI-Cyber-2
 in preview—further improves efficiency while maintaining the quality of security findings.
Teams can run MDASH where development already happens—from the Microsoft Defender portal and command line to CI/CD workflows such as GitHub Actions and Azure DevOps. Developers can then review generated fixes locally or through an automated pull request.
Shrink your attack surface
Security teams can see more vulnerabilities than they can possibly address. The challenge is knowing where risk demands action, and getting there before an attacker does. At Ignite, Perception is bringing exposure management and agents together to make that possible.
The AI Readiness Initiatives page organizes recommendations and agentic actions into focused, outcome-driven programs of work.
 Each initiative organizes the risks, recommendations, and agentic actions needed to achieve a specific security outcome, so teams can manage exposure as a coordinated effort rather than a series of disconnected fixes.
One new initiative helps organizations manage the surge of vulnerabilities that arrives with each monthly Windows update.
 It brings the highest-priority findings into a recurring initiative, where agents can help teams prioritize what matters, plan the response, and track the work through completion. Instead of treating every vulnerability as equally urgent, teams can focus their resources on the exposures most likely to put critical assets at risk.
The 
Reduce exposure risk
 playbook calls on the Risk Reduction Agent to assess the available paths for reducing risk and recommend the right response. When a permanent fix is ready, the agent can coordinate patching through Microsoft Intune, with IT maintaining control of deployment. When patching is not immediately possible or could disrupt the business, the agent can help shield vulnerabilities by providing targeted protection while teams work toward the permanent fix.

Secure your AI and agents
AI agents are gaining access to more sensitive data, tools, and systems—and their risk can change as their permissions, connections, and behavior evolve. Security teams need to identify the agents that require attention, understand why, and prioritize where to act.
At Ignite, we’re extending agentic security to help teams investigate and reduce AI agent risk.
