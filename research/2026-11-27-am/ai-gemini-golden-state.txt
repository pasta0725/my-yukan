Google has named Gemini the official AI assistant of the Golden State Warriors, the WNBA champion Golden State Valkyries and Chase Center, the San Francisco arena where both teams play. The bigger deal was announced on November 24. It brings Gemini-powered features to the arena's videoboards and to the team's digital channels, including the Golden State mobile app, for fans in the stands and at home.
In its 
announcement on the Google blog
, the company said Gemini now has two titles with the franchise: Official AI Assistant Partner and Official Generative AI & Enterprise LLM Partner. The deal covers the NBA's Warriors, the Valkyries (who won the 2026 WNBA championship) and Chase Center itself.
The news mostly covers what the partnership includes. It doesn't say exactly how the features will work. As of November 25, 2026, neither Google nor the team has given a launch date for the new fan features or described exactly how they'll work.
What Fans Can Expect
The Chase Center main bowl and videoboard above the court showing Golden State Warriors branding. Photo: Américo Toledano, 
Wikimedia Commons
, CC BY-SA 4.0
Google says Gemini will be built in across the whole franchise. For fans, that work happens in two places. Inside Chase Center, Gemini will power interactive features on the arena's videoboards. Fans who follow the teams from home will get interactive features through Golden State's digital channels.
Google's own post about the deal on X added a few details. Fans will be able to use the Gemini app online and in person on game days, with interactive features in the Golden State mobile app and around the Chase Center campus. Photos released with the announcement show Gemini branding on the overhead scoreboard, the ribbon boards, the courtside signs and the screens above an arena entrance.
That last part matters most for Android owners. The Gemini app is already Google's main assistant on Android phones, so fans with Pixel and other Android devices will already have the app the teams plan to use.
Brandon Schneider, president and chief operating officer of the Golden State Warriors, said the organization is “always looking for ways to use emerging technology, including AI tools, to improve the fan experience both at live events and digitally for our fans globally.” He added: “We are excited to expand our partnership with Google Gemini as our teams continue to focus on how we can make every visit to Chase Center even better for basketball fans.”
Gemini Behind the Scenes
The exterior of Chase Center in San Francisco at dusk, with the lit glass facade, the “Chase Center” sign and metallic spheres on the plaza. Image: 
Chase Center
The deal also covers the team's staff. Golden State is using Gemini for business to build custom AI agents that help its staff on both the business side and in basketball operations. Google hasn't said which jobs those agents will handle or how many of them the organization plans to build.
The relationship isn't new. Google has been a founding partner of Chase Center since the arena opened in 2019, and the franchise already uses Google's generative AI tools in its day-to-day operations. According to the team's announcement, the two have worked together over that time on personalized features in the team's mobile app, on player and game data analysis, and on front-office and admin work using Gemini for business.
Marvin Chow, Google's vice president of consumer apps marketing, said: “To truly push the boundaries of AI, you need partners who share the same vision and drive to innovate.” He continued: “As a founding partner of Chase Center since 2019, we've seen Golden State consistently set the standard as early tech adopters. Collaborating in our own backyard gives us a unique opportunity to co-create and show how helpful technology can be in empowering athletes, driving smarter organizational decisions, and creating unforgettable game-day experiences for fans.”
The “own backyard” line is a nod to geography: Google is based in the Bay Area. Chase Center is one of the area's best-known venues, home to an NBA franchise and the league's newest WNBA champion.
The bigger question for fans is what Gemini will actually do on game day. The announcement mentions interactive videoboard segments and app features, but neither side has shown what they'll look like or when they'll appear. Google has also not said whether any of the features will reach Gemini users who aren't at Chase Center or using the Golden State app.
The first fan features should show up at Warriors home games this season, and on the Valkyries' side when their next WNBA season starts. Until Google or the team gives dates, the Golden State app and Google's Gemini channels are the places to watch.

Warriors, Valkyries, and Chase Center Announce Expanded Partnership with Google Gemini
Google Gemini To Drive Enterprise Innovation and Create A More Personalized Game Day Experience
November 24, 2026
8:00 AM PST
The Golden State Warriors, the 2026 WNBA Champion Golden State Valkyries, and Chase Center have announced an expanded partnership with Google, a Founding Partner of Chase Center since the arena opened in 2019. Through the agreement, Google Gemini will serve as the Official AI Assistant Partner and Official Generative AI & Enterprise LLM Partner of the Warriors, Valkyries and Chase Center.
"We are always looking for ways to use emerging technology, including AI tools, to improve the fan experience both at live events and digitally for our fans globally," said Brandon Schneider, President & Chief Operating Officer of Golden State. "We are excited to expand our partnership with Google Gemini as our teams continue to focus on how we can make every visit to Chase Center even better for basketball fans, concertgoers, and Thrive City attendees alike."
Golden State already uses generative AI tools from Google, such as Gemini, to further streamline operations across the organization. Since Google’s partnership with Golden State began in 2019, the organizations have collaborated to deliver hyper-personalized experiences through Golden State’s mobile app, analyze player and game data to improve team performance and player management strategies, and innovate front office workflows and administrative functions by adopting Gemini for Business.
As part of the expanded partnership, the Warriors and Valkyries will use Gemini to create more interactive fan experiences, both inside Chase Center via interactive videoboard features and across digital channels for fans following from home. Additionally, by building custom agents with Gemini for business, Golden State aims to further optimize and enhance business and basketball operations.
“In basketball operations, we used to start with what we think we know, or what we think we should do in terms of decision-making, then we would try to prove it out,” said Kirk Lacob, Executive Vice President, Basketball Operations. “With Google’s expertise and continued partnership across the Golden State Warriors and Golden State Valkyries, we start with data to help us pare down our decision tree and where we should focus our efforts based on what the data says.”
“To truly push the boundaries of AI, you need partners who share the same vision and drive to innovate,” said Marvin Chow, VP, Consumer Apps Marketing, Google. “As a proud Founding Partner of Chase Center since 2019, we’ve seen Golden State consistently set the standard as early tech adopters. Collaborating in our own backyard gives us a unique opportunity to co-create and show how helpful technology can be in empowering athletes, driving smarter organizational decisions, and creating unforgettable game-day experiences for fans.”
