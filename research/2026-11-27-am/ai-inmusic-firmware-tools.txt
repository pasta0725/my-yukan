AI-assisted ‘vibe coding’ is rapidly changing the music technology landscape and established brands are being forced to make quick decisions about how they are going to react.
While the phenomenon of users creating their own DIY firmware, mods or plugins isn’t necessarily anything new, both the volume and speed of these community projects has accelerated rapidly in recent weeks. Now, user communities are often developing ideas and new features for their hardware at a pace the official developers can’t match.
The question for music tech brands is, how do you respond to this? While some hardware developers are so far choosing to ignore or actively block DIY firmware, others, including the likes of 
Behringer
 and 
Polyend
, have made moves to embrace community development.
Latest Videos From
MusicRadar

Akai is another brand that falls into this latter category. While 
teasing its upcoming Sample Key hardware last month
, the company signposted its intention to move towards making elements of its product line open source. In the weeks since, we’ve seen the first glimmers of what this might look like in practice, and now with its latest MPC update, Akai is making good on its promise of opening up the MPC ecosystem.
With the launch of 
software update 3.10.1
, Akai is now letting users create their own custom plugins for the MPC and Force hardware, assisted by the newly released SDK (software development kit). The update also unlocks the MPC platform’s 'enhanced development' features, which lifts limitations on plugin and track counts within the hardware, as well as adding mouse support.
This isn’t exactly the fully all-guns-blazing open source approach the 
Behringer is promising for its – as yet unreleased – MPC-alike Kick
. Rather, Akai’s approach appears to be a more measured middle ground, where the company is opening up specific elements of the hardware to custom development without encouraging users to completely rewrite the entire system. It's more akin to 
Korg’s approach to its ‘Logue’ and Nu:Tekt instruments
.
In a video announcing the update, Akai Pro’s Andy Mac explains the need to balance the creative possibilities offered by AI-assisted coding with the risk of cutting corners. While, as he acknowledges, AI tools allows the company itself to speed up elements of development, that doesn’t mean the company will be churning out updates to keep up with the community.

“Faster development doesn’t mean taking shortcuts,” Mac says, “the same focus on testing, stability, and reliability remains essential before we put any releases into the hands of the community.”
Personally, I think this is the right approach for Akai. 
As I wrote in relation to Behringer’s Kick announcement
, I worry that the ease and abundance of unofficial firmware risks leading to a situation where developers feel they can offload some responsibility for ongoing development to their user communities. By trying to balance focused support for DIYers with a steady stream of properly tested and stable official updates, Akai can hopefully find a happy middle ground.
Akai MPC Sample 
(Image credit: Future)
Alongside the update to the bigger MPCs, the MPC Sample gets an update to version 1.4. This adds a variety of new workflow features, but most significantly it also adds community development support and an official SDK to allow users to create their own effects for the unit.
Again, this is something of a middle ground approach to open source, which follows in the wake of numerous high-profile DIY firmware mods that have appeared for the MPC Sample in recent weeks, such as the popular 
SampQOL
 mod that, among other things, expands the device’s track and effect counts.
Akai isn’t ignoring these completely though, stating in the announcement video that the company is “already working alongside some of these new community developers, offering ongoing support” – which certainly seems to imply that some of those popular mods may become at least semi-official.
These updates aren’t purely about the SDKs, however. Another handy feature added to the MPC and Force range is the ability to mirror the touch interface and audio to a desktop computer, which is likely to be handy for streamers and content creators.
Akai is launching two new official plugins too. The first is an emulation of the classic SP-1200 vintage sampler, while the second is an SSL SL 4000-style channel strip plugin.
You can hear more about all of these updates in the video above, or 
head to the Akai site
 to download the latest firmware.

Akai MPC OS 3.10.1 introduces the new MPC Plugin SDK, a development kit that lets users create custom instrument and FX plugins.
On 
October 28
, Akai announced it is making its MPC Sample hardware sampler open—as is the upcoming 
SampleKey
. This sparked a great deal of discussion. I believe it’s actually the biggest one this year. 
Then, the news broke that Akai plans to unveil 
22 new products at NAMM 2027
, many of them open to experiment with. Today, they take a big step. Akai has released MPC 3.10.1 with the new open SDK for custom instruments and FX plugins.
Akai MPC OS 3.10.1
Things are turning out differently than we previously thought. MPC OS is not becoming open-source right now, but with MPC OS 3.10.1, Akai has done something else exciting.
With this new update, Akai is introducing the MPC Plugin SDK—an open SDK for custom instrument and effect plugins. This means that users can now develop their own plugins for the MPC hardware. 
The “
Community plugins for Akai MPC OS” project
—which now features 227 free plugins—will certainly welcome this development. It makes the initiative official with support from Akai.
This brings a flood of new instruments and effects—including synthesizers never seen on this platform. In their new video, Akai reveals that Loopop has built a granular synthesizer for the MPC OS.
Other Features
Alongside this, the new Akai MPC OS 3.10.1 brings more user-requested additions.
First, it brings USB mouse support – a feature previously available only on the Hakai project- now also available for MPC OS. Connect a USB mouse to control the standalone interface with an on-screen pointer.
It also adds screenshot capture, a new function that saves touchscreen screenshots as PNG files to the internal drive or an attached drive.
MPC OS 3.10.1 also expanded the track and plugin limits. It removes the separate limits on audio and plugin tracks while keeping the overall track limit, and optionally lifts instance limits on selected instruments and effects.
Then, there are new advanced audio settings where you can adjust the buffer size, the latency compensation, and sample rate where supported by audio hardware.
It also introduces a new developer panel with access to system logs, resource monitoring, voice statistics, and external plugin management. 
And lastly, you now have remote screen control, where you can view the hardware screen and stream audio to a computer on the same network, with optional remote control, screenshots, and screen recording. Plus, there are bug fixes.
According to 
Pete Goodliffe, CTO (Chief Technical Officer), they will open the MPC OS later. He confirmed this in a Sonicstate video from the ADC 2026. Not now because they
 want to take a careful approach before doing this. 
First Impression
Many expected Akai to make the MPC open-source. Instead, things are taking a different turn for now: they are introducing an MPC plugin SDK.
I find this a very exciting concept, as it lets users create plenty of new content and adds extra value to the MPC OS. 
The new Akai MPC OS 3.10.1 is now available as a free download.

Akai Professional
 has released an official software development kit (SDK) for its MPC hardware, opening the platform to third-party developers who want to create custom synthesizers, effects and other plugins for compatible standalone instruments. The new 
MPC Plugin SDK
 provides a development framework, build tools and documentation for creating software that runs directly on MPC hardware.
The SDK is published through the GitHub organization of 
inMusic
, Akai Professional’s parent company. It provides developers with a C++ framework, plugin templates, example projects and cross-compilation tools for building instruments and effects compatible with the MPC’s plugin host.
One particularly interesting aspect is the SDK’s support for AI-assisted development. Its documentation includes instructions for working with coding agents such as 
Claude Code
 and 
OpenAI Codex
. Developers can describe an instrument or effect in natural language and use an AI coding agent to generate the underlying C++ code, build the plugin and prepare it for installation.
The SDK includes examples of synthesizers, stereo delay effects and drum instruments, along with tools for designing touchscreen interfaces and assigning controls to the MPC’s hardware Q-Links.
The latest update also introduces advanced Arrange Mode editing tools, AAX support for Pro Tools, deeper MPK controller integration, MPC Beats mode, new 8×8 performance modes for MPC Live III and MPC XL, and workflow enhancements across MPC and Force.
Features:
Official development framework for creating third-party MPC instrument and effect plugins.
C++ plugin framework with support for audio processing, MIDI, transport synchronization, parameters and program states.
Cross-compilation tools for supported MPC hardware architectures.
Example synthesizer, drum machine and stereo delay projects.
Tools for designing custom touchscreen interfaces and Q-Link controls.
AI-assisted development workflows using coding agents including Claude Code and OpenAI Codex.
Plugin validation, testing and packaging utilities.
Host simulation tools for testing plugins before installation on hardware.
The release represents a significant change in Akai’s approach to its hardware ecosystem. While the MPC operating system itself is not open source, the SDK provides an officially supported route for independent developers to expand the platform with their own instruments and effects.

loopop
 video, host

 takes a deep dive into the update, and discusses how he created his own custom plugins:

The 
MPC Plugin SDK
 is available free of charge from 
inMusic’s GitHub repository
 under the MIT license. Compatible MPC hardware requires OS 3.10.1 or later and External Plugin Support to load third-party plugins. Visit the 
Akai Professional MPC release notes
 for firmware information.

MPC Live III

AKAI MPC 3.6 introduces interesting new features, such as the ability to set a project’s time signature and apply it to the entire project. The Drum Articulation Engine allows you to assign flams and other drum effects directly to individual pads, which is intriguing! This feature takes advantage of the drum pads’ capabilities on the MPC Live III. A 32-cell modulation matrix is now available for drum tracks, allowing for greater creativity.
Another notable feature is Layer Play Offset, which involves creating a defined “sample window” determined by start, end, and loop points. This window can then be moved as desired.
For instance, if you set the start, end, and loop points so that the first sixteenth note of a one-bar sample is “framed,” you can use the Layer Play Offset parameter to move this window to different sixteenth note positions within the sample. Cool! Even cooler is the ability to control the movement of this window through modulation, such as with an LFO.

There are also new modulation options. LFO 1 now has additional parameters, and LFO 2 has been added for drum programs. Additionally, there is a note counter for stepwise modulation, as well as two ramps and drift LFOs, which are useful for slight pitch modulation.
The AKAI MPC 3.6 offers more options for crossfades. You can apply these between sample layers using envelopes or LFOs to create sounds similar to wave sequencing or vector synthesis. Layer crossfading is now available for key groups as well!

MPC Plugin SDK
This SDK allows you to build instrument and effect plugins for Akai MPC standalone hardware from a plain-language description using an AI coding agent, and install it directly onto your MPC. It writes C++ that you never have to read if you do not want to.
The plugins can run on MPC X, X SE, Live, Live II, One, One+, Key 61, Key 37, Force (32-bit) and MPC Live III, MPC XL and later (64-bit). Effects also run on the 
MPC Sample
 (64-bit) as Knob FX.
Plugin support requires MPC OS 3.10.1 or later, with the External Plugin Support feature enabled on the device
: Preferences → Enhancements (Beta) → turn on 
Enable Enhancements
 and 
External Plugin Support
.
MPC Sample
: firmware v1.4.0 or later; in 
MIDI Config
 turn on 
Dev Enhancements (Beta)
 and 
Custom Plug-In Support
, then copy plugins to 
VST2 Plugins
 on its microSD card through 
SD Card Access
 mode. It loads effect plugins only, not instruments, and has no plugin screen: each effect exposes 6 parameters on its knobs (3, plus 3 more while holding SHIFT), set in 
plugin.json
. See 
docs/knob-fx.md
.

Example prompts
Paste one of these prompts into your agent with the SDK folder open:
Create an MPC instrument called "Dune Bass": a monophonic bass synth with saw and square oscillators, sub oscillator, a resonant low-pass filter with envelope amount, glide, and a drive stage. Put oscillator controls on page one and filter/envelope on page two. Add 8 presets ranging from clean sub to aggressive acid. When built, install it on my MPC and restart the device so plugins are rescanned.
Build an MPC effect called "Shimmer": a reverb with an octave-up pitch-shifted feedback path, pre-delay, damping, size, mix. Keep CPU low enough for two instances on an MPC Live II.
Make an MPC drum synth with 8 voices on notes 36–43 (kick, snare, rim, clap, closed hat, open hat, tom, perc). Each voice has tune, decay and level. Kick and snare go to the main stereo output, everything else to a second stereo output. One page per pair of drums.
I want a generative melody instrument: while the MPC plays, it picks notes from a selectable scale on every 8th or 16th note (density control), with octave range and rest probability, played through a simple FM pluck with decay and brightness controls.
Port the DSP from this open-source filter I pasted into an MPC effect with cutoff, resonance, drive and mode (LP/BP/HP) controls. Credit the original author and licence in the README.
My plugin "Dune Bass" makes a click when I load a project. Find out why and fix it. When you're done, update the plugin on my MPC.

Requirements
Python 3.9 or newer. Everything else (the Zig cross compiler) is fetched on first build, or use your own GNU cross compilers / Docker (
docs/building.md
).
macOS, Windows or Linux.
An MPC running MPC OS 3.10.1 or later, with External Plugin Support enabled in Preferences → Enhancements (Beta), plus a USB drive or SD card; or an MPC Sample running firmware v1.4.0 or later with Custom Plug-In Support on (effects only), a microSD card and a USB cable.
How it works, briefly
An MPC plugin is a Linux shared library following the VST 2.4 binary interface, in a folder with a 
plugin.json
 manifest and optional JSON "skins" for the touch screen and the 16 hardware knobs. The MPC loads it in-process; there are no plugin windows, so the UI is data. The SDK framework hides the binary interface behind a small C++ class: declare parameters, implement 
process()
, done. 
docs/overview.md
 has the full picture.
Safety
Third-party plugins run inside the MPC application without protection. A bug can freeze or restart the MPC. Test with the host simulator first, save your work often, and remove a misbehaving plugin's folder from the drive to recover. inMusic does not test or support third-party plugins.
