Harbinger Raises $240 Million Series D Funding Round, Led by Tiger Global
Press Release
November 5, 2026
Fidelity Management & Research Company, Capricorn, Greycroft, Ridgeline, and PanaceaVC also participated in the round
The new funds will support Harbinger’s growth as a manufacturing platform powering the AI economy and electrification across mobility, defense and autonomy
GARDEN GROVE, Calif., 
(Nov. 5, 2026) — 
Harbinger
, 
an American industrial technology platform company, 
today announced that they have raised $240 million in Series D funding to help fuel the company’s next phase of growth. The funding round was co-led by 
Tiger Global
, 
Capricorn
’s Technology Impact Fund and 
Ridgeline
. Additional investors in the round include crossover investors Fidelity Management & Research Company; 
Squarepoint Capital
 and 
Xantium
; returning investors 
Maniv
and
Greycroft
, 
Australian Gulf Capital,
PanaceaVC
, 
ArcTern Ventures
, and 
Leitmotif
; and strategic investors in mobility 
FedEx
 and 
THOR Industries
 through 
TechNexus
, as well as 
BuildGroup
 in data centers; among others. 
The new funding will support Harbinger’s further expansion across multiple verticals, spanning commercial vehicles, energy storage systems, power generation, autonomy software and defense applications, as well as the continued ramp up of its medium-duty platform manufacturing capacity. With the new financing, Harbinger has raised over $650 million since its founding in 2021.
“
Harbinger exists to challenge what industry has learned to accept, and reinvent the critical systems the world depends on
,” said 
John Harris
, Co-Founder and CEO of Harbinger. “
By rethinking critical systems from first principles, we aim to unlock better performance, greater reliability, safer operations, and more room to grow. Electrification is at the core of everything we do, and increasingly, electrification is at the core of industry, defense, mobility, and AI. Harbinger’s role is not simply to give customers another option. It is to reset expectations for what critical systems should deliver.
”
The critical industrial systems that serve the United States have long been constrained by outdated technology and accepted compromises. These are the essential systems that are depended upon to move goods, power infrastructure, protect people and enable autonomy. Rather than optimizing existing products, Harbinger engineers systems from first principles, using deep vertical integration to control product development, performance, cost and scale. This approach, first demonstrated through its medium-duty electric vehicle platform, is now being applied across industries, enabling Harbinger to address longstanding challenges and raise the standard for what customers should expect.
“
Harbinger excels at electrification, and electrification has become one of the most pressing bottlenecks of the AI era
,” said Griffin Schroeder, Partner, Tiger Global. “
We led this financing because we see tremendous value in bringing Harbinger’s technical expertise and manufacturing capabilities to new markets.
” 
Harbinger recently announced that FedEx Senior Vice President of Safety and Transportation 
Paul Melander
 has joined Harbinger’s Board of Directors, to support Harbinger’s focus on enhanced driver safety, delivery quality, and enabling outstanding customer execution. FedEx 
recently placed an order
 for 2,000 all-electric Harbinger trucks valued at more than $300 million. 
Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley served as placement agents on the financing round.
About Harbinger
Harbinger is an American industrial technology platform company 
reinventing the critical systems the world depends on across transportation, power, defense, autonomy and components. The company develops advanced electrification technologies, including all-electric and hybrid medium-duty vehicle platforms, battery and drivetrain systems, energy storage and auxiliary power solutions, autonomous driving technology and defense applications.
Harbinger engineers systems from first principles, using deep vertical integration to deliver safer, more reliable, more capable and more efficient solutions at economic parity. Its technologies serve a range of applications, including medium-duty trucks, recreational vehicles (RVs), mobile healthcare units, power infrastructure and defense vehicles. Harbinger's mission is to solve the problems industry stopped questioning and raise the standard for what customers should expect.

Harbinger raised $240 million in Series D funding to push its electric vehicle platform into energy storage, power generation, autonomy, and defense while it ramps production for a 
2,000-truck order from FedEx
. Tiger Global, Capricorn’s Technology Impact Fund, and Ridgeline co-led the round, the Garden Grove, California, company said Thursday.
The FedEx order, valued at more than $300 million, is due by the end of 2027. Harbinger said the new money will fund the continued ramp of its medium-duty manufacturing capacity alongside the newer businesses.
The release describes Harbinger as “an American industrial technology platform company” and lists commercial vehicles as one of five markets the new funding covers.
“Electrification is at the core of everything we do, and increasingly, electrification is at the core of industry, defense, mobility, and AI,” said John Harris, Harbinger’s co-founder and CEO, in the release.
What Harbinger builds today
Harbinger builds Class 4 through Class 6 chassis in all-electric and plug-in hybrid versions and designs its own powertrain, battery system, steering, and brakes. Its 
HC Series Cab, unveiled in March
, adds a gasoline range extender good for up to 500 miles, depending on configuration, and up to 15 kilowatts of exportable power, so crews don’t need a separate generator. The company also acquired autonomy software maker Phantom AI.
Harbinger’s first defense deal came this summer: it is building the platform for an 
uncrewed Army supply vehicle under an American Rheinmetall contract
. It has also started selling its batteries for energy storage, 
Bloomberg News reported
. The release did not name any energy storage or power generation customers.
Round follows $160 million Series C
The Series D follows a 
$160 million Series C last November
, when FedEx placed an initial order for 53 trucks. Harbinger said it has raised more than $650 million since its 2021 founding.
Fidelity Management & Research Company, Squarepoint Capital, and Xantium joined as crossover investors, and Maniv and Greycroft returned. In an interview with Bloomberg News, Harris said the company has focused on bringing in investors that are “generally crossover or public markets focused.” FedEx and THOR Industries came in as strategic investors through TechNexus, the release said. Paul Melander, FedEx’s senior vice president of safety and transportation, recently joined Harbinger’s board. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley served as placement agents.
BuildGroup, named in the release as a strategic investor in data centers, also took part. Tiger Global tied its investment to the AI buildout.
“Harbinger excels at electrification, and electrification has become one of the most pressing bottlenecks of the AI era,” said Griffin Schroeder, a partner at Tiger Global.
Cargo vans drive zero-emission truck growth
Zero-emission trucks reached a record 4.14% of U.S. truck deployments in the second half of 2025, according to 
CALSTART’s June 2026 Zeroing in on Zero-Emission Trucks market update
, as total truck sales fell 35% year over year. Cargo vans supplied 12,158 of the 12,996 new units. Every other segment, including medium-duty step vans and trucks, added 838. Harbinger’s Class 4 through Class 6 trucks fall in that group.
Production pace and undisclosed terms
But challenges remain. Harris told Bloomberg News in September that Harbinger had recently finished its 1,000th vehicle. The FedEx order alone calls for twice that many trucks by the end of 2027.
The release did not disclose Harbinger’s valuation, its current production capacity, or how the new money splits between trucks and the newer businesses. The Army program is in a prototype phase that typically runs three to 10 units, and no contract value has been disclosed.
The company plans to carry the in-house engineering behind its trucks into each new market, the release said.
“Harbinger’s role is not simply to give customers another option,” Harris said. “It is to reset expectations for what critical systems should deliver.”
Why it matters:
 Fleets pricing medium-duty electric delivery trucks now have a builder backed by Fidelity, Tiger Global, and FedEx itself, with $240 million in fresh capital. Its FedEx deliveries through 2027 will show whether that backing turns into trucks at scale.

Harbinger Motors Inc. raised $240 million in its latest funding round to expand manufacturing of its electrification technology in markets including defense, autonomy software and energy storage. 
The Series D round was co-led by Tiger Global, Capricorn’s Technology Impact Fund and Ridgeline, with participation from existing backers including FedEx Corp. In total, the maker of medium-duty electric trucks has now raised over $650 million since 2021, the company said in a statement.
“We’ve been focused for a long time on pretty carefully curating what the investor mix looks like to reflect the stage that the company is at,” said John Harris, co-founder and CEO of Harbinger in an interview with Bloomberg News. “We’ve been focused on bringing investors onto the cap table that are generally crossover or public markets focused.”
In September, Harbinger said it received an order from FedEx for 2,000 electric delivery vans in a deal worth more than 
$300 million
. Harbinger said it plans to deliver the vehicles by the end of 2027.
Throughout the year Harbinger has taken steps toward its ambitions beyond commercial vehicles by announcing new business lines, including selling its EV batteries for energy storage, an acquisition of autonomous driving company Phantom AI, and a partnership with defense 
manufacturer American Rheinmetall to develop 
unmanned support vehicles
 for the U.S. Army.
FedEx ranks No. 2
 on the

 in North America and No. 3 on the 
TT Top 50 list of the largest global freight companies
.

.
