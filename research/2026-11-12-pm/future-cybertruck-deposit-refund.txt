Tesla Inc. will refund Cybertruck deposits for some customers because it has more orders than it can process before ending production of the divisive pickup next year, according to Chief Executive Elon Musk.
“The Cybertruck order rate far exceeds our production rate,” Musk wrote Monday in a post on X, without providing specific details. 
He added that the model was not unprofitable and the decision to cancel was driven by the “opportunity cost” in how the manufacturing capacity is utilized.

“We will try to make as many as possible before shutting down the line in about 8 months, but will have to return deposits for a lot of people,” he said.
Musk announced last week that Tesla would stop making the Cybertruck at its Texas plant around the middle of 2027 to free up capacity for its Cybercab autonomous vehicle.
Cybertruck sales have fallen well short of demand projections and Tesla is making only a fraction of what the facility is capable of producing. The angular vehicle has been polarizing with car buyers and stands in contrast to the curvy designs of its other consumer models.
Advertisement
The automaker has been paring the more expensive and slower-selling models from its lineup — including killing off the Model S and Model X this year — to refocus on business lines including artificial intelligence, robotics and autonomous vehicles. 
The vast majority of Tesla’s sales in the core auto business come from two vehicles: the Model Y SUV and the Model 3 sedan.
The company’s stock was down 20% this year through Friday’s close, compared with a 7.8% gain in the Stardard & Poor’s 500 Index.

“We will try to make as many as possible before shutting down the line in about 8 months, but will have to return deposits for a lot of people,” Musk wrote.
He did not say how many customers would get refunds, or how much money was involved.
The post follows Musk’s 
announcement on Friday
 that Tesla will start winding down Cybertruck production at its Texas factory around the middle of 2027. The move will free up space to build more Cybercabs, Tesla’s robotaxi.
Musk has also said Tesla will add an extra production shift to fill outstanding Cybertruck orders before the line is taken apart, Reuters reported.
The factory near Austin is the only known Tesla plant that builds the pickup. It is not clear whether the truck will be made anywhere else.
The Cybercab is a two-seat car with no steering wheel or pedals, and the company began building it in Texas earlier this year and started offering paid rides in Austin in September.
Tesla unveiled the steel-bodied Cybertruck in 2019 and began deliveries in late 2023, after several delays. Musk once said the truck could sell 250,000 units a year.
Tesla raised Cybertruck prices again in August, putting the cheapest version at $74,990. Last month it also cancelled reservations in Australia and New Zealand, nearly seven years after it began taking them, according to Not a Tesla App.
Sales have fallen far short of that. US sales fell 48% to 20,237 last year, down from 38,965 in 2024, according to Kelley Blue Book. Sales in the first nine months of this year fell a further 28%, the Associated Press reported, citing the same data.
In response to a user on X, Musk called the Cybertruck the “best vehicle Tesla has ever made” and the wind-down the “end of an era”.
The Cybercab plays a central role in Musk’s plan to turn Tesla into an AI and robotics company. Tesla lists an installed capacity of more than 125,000 Cybercabs a year at the Texas plant.
