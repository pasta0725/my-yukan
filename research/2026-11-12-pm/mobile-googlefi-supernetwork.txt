Google Fi is bringing back multiple cell network support for better coverage
Google Fi will swap users between two 5G networks, but only if your phone is a Pixel 9 or newer.﻿

Google Fi is 
launching a new “super network”
 that can automatically switch users between two different 5G networks depending on which one has better connectivity. The feature is rolling out starting today on Pixel 9 and newer phones that have Google Fi active on eSIM and have any non-Fi SIMs disabled.
When Google Fi first launched in 2015, users were 
originally
 able to switch between T-Mobile and Sprint’s networks. But those two networks eventually became one when T-Mobile 
acquired Sprint
 in 2020, and so the network switching stopped there. Jane Harnett, Google Fi Wireless senior product manager, told 
The Verge
 that Fi stopped offering the feature because “the wireless landscape consolidated via mergers and limited our ability to offer multiple networks within the US.” (Networking switching has continued to be 
available internationally
.)
Now the feature is returning to the US, with Fi once again hopping between two networks. Harnett declined to share which networks Fi will be working with to make this happen. 
Google Fi’s FAQs
 say it currently provides coverage through T-Mobile’s network.
According to Harnett, Google Fi’s new approach to network switching is faster than what it offered before, and uses eSIMs’ ability to 
connect to multiple carriers at once
:
“Previously Fi’s switching was single-SIM switching which could only connect to one network at a time, so your phone had to wait until service dropped completely before performing a slow SIM profile swap into another network without knowing if coverage was actually better there. Fi also tried dual-SIM iterations that required both a physical SIM and eSIM which could be slower to transition.
Now we are back with added networks domestically. We have rebuilt the experience from the ground up for the modern era. With our new switching on newer Pixel devices, your phone keeps two eSIM network profiles on standby at the same time (via MEP technology) and uses the Android OS to monitor live connection quality. It also lets your phone transition faster and more fluidly with minimal battery impact—knowing a better connection is available before it switches.”

Google has announced a “
super network
” upgrade for its Fi cell service. For the past few years, Google Fi has operated exclusively on the T-Mobile network, but now it’s adding support for a second 5G network. Oddly, Google refuses to say which one.
Google’s MVNO (mobile virtual network operator) launched in 2015 as Project Fi with support for both T-Mobile and Sprint service. The service initially only worked on Google’s Nexus phones, which would switch between networks based on signal strength. After T-Mobile absorbed Sprint, Fi switched to being a single-network service in the US while continuing to hop between networks for international travel.
With the super network, Google Fi is again a dual-carrier service stateside. Phones connected to Fi will be able to hop between T-Mobile 5G and a different 5G network to provide the best possible connectivity. Google’s blog post announcing the change conspicuously doesn’t mention either network, saying only that it supports “two of America’s best 5G cellular networks.”
We know one of them is T-Mobile, and the other must be either AT&T or Verizon. When reached for comment, a Google spokesperson declined to name the second network, which is odd. Looking at the 
Fi coverage map
, my best guess for the super network partner is Verizon. The shape of unserved regions is a closer match than AT&T, but carriers do tend to fudge the fine details of these maps.
      Google Fi Wireless | Introducing our super network
MVNOs don’t always advertise which network they use, and that information is getting harder to find in FAQs and support pages. 
Google’s Fi FAQ
 no longer even confirms T-Mobile as a network partner. Google used to be very upfront about its partnership with T-Mobile, pointing out that, unlike most MVNOs, it gets top prioritization on T-Mobile’s towers. Network information like this is really something consumers should have when shopping for plans—you probably know which carriers work best where you spend your time.
There are a few caveats in the super network rollout beyond the mysterious new partner. Currently, network switching is only supported on the Pixel 9, Pixel 10, and Pixel 11 series. Other devices will get access in 2027. You also won’t be able to enjoy traditional dual-SIM functionality if you want Fi to switch networks. This will only work if you have Fi configured as an eSIM with all non-Fi SIMs disabled.
If you meet those requirements, super network support is rolling out now at no additional cost on Unlimited Premium and Flexible plans.

Google Fi's new Super Network brings automatic 5G switching, Pixel deals, and more
Bad 5G signal? Your Pixel might have a clever escape plan.

Shimul Sood

TL;DR
Google Fi’s new Super Network lets supported Pixel phones automatically switch between two major US 5G networks for better connectivity.
Network switching initially works only on Pixel 9 and newer phones using eSIM, while Wi-Fi Auto Connect+ is expanding to select Samsung and Motorola devices.
Google is introducing an AI-powered assistant called Ask Fi, along with Pixel 11 offers and discounts on select Google Fi plans.
Most of us 
choose a mobile carrier
 based on its coverage, data speeds, and pricing. But even the best networks have their weak spots, and there’s usually not much you can do when your phone struggles to find a decent signal. What if your phone could connect to another carrier whenever that happens?
That’s exactly what Google Fi is trying to do with its new Google Fi Super Network. The service is bringing back automatic network switching, giving supported phones access to two major US 
5G networks
 instead of relying on just one.
Bad signal? Your phone can switch sides
Pankil Shah / Android Authority
Say you’re traveling across town and notice your mobile data slowing down. It could be because your carrier doesn’t have great coverage in that area, even though another network works perfectly fine. That’s where Google Fi’s new feature comes in. Your phone can automatically switch to a second 5G network if it offers a better connection, without you changing any settings.
The switch only happens when there’s a noticeable difference in network quality, so your phone won’t constantly jump between the two. You’ll also get priority data speeds on both networks, including support for Ultra Wideband. Interestingly, Google isn’t revealing which carriers it’s partnering with.
There are a few catches, though. For now, the feature is limited to 
Pixel 9
 and newer phones using Google Fi on eSIM, with no other SIM active. If you’re using a physical SIM, you’re out of luck. Support for more phones is planned for 2027.
But what happens when you’re somewhere crowded, like an airport or a shopping mall, where even mobile networks can struggle? Google has something for that, too. It’s expanding Wi-Fi Auto Connect+ (W+), a feature that automatically connects your phone to supported Wi-Fi networks without requiring passwords or sign-in pages.
According to Google, these Wi-Fi connections are twice as reliable and have 30% lower latency compared to using cellular alone. They’re also encrypted, and the data you use through W+ won’t eat into your high-speed allowance on unlimited plans. The feature is already available on supported 
Pixel phones
 and will start rolling out to select Samsung Galaxy and Motorola devices next week.
And if you frequently travel abroad, Google Fi also offers international coverage in more than 200 destinations, with access to multiple network partners where available.
An AI assistant and some Pixel perks? Yes, please
Joe Maring / Android Authority
Alongside the network improvements, Google is also making it easier to get help when something goes wrong. An AI assistant called Ask Fi is built into the Google Fi app and can answer questions about your account without making you search through support pages.
For instance, if you’re planning a trip abroad and want to check whether your plan covers international data, you can just ask the assistant. And if you’d rather speak to a real person, human support is still available.
Another addition to the app is Your super network, which gives you a closer look at how your phone stays connected. You can check which network you’re currently using, see when your phone switched networks, and get an idea of your connection quality.
Google is also throwing in a 
few offers
 to make switching to Fi more appealing. New customers can get up to $899 back on a 
Pixel 11 series phone
, with $500 taken off immediately and the remaining $399 returned through monthly credits over two years. The promotion also includes $10 off the Unlimited Premium plan every month for 24 months.
If you already have a Pixel and don’t want to upgrade, there’s something for you, too. Eligible customers who bring their existing phone to Fi can get 50% off Unlimited Standard or Unlimited Essentials for a year. All offers are subject to eligibility requirements.
Personally, I think automatic network switching is the biggest reason to pay attention to this announcement. We often talk about how fast 5G has become, but speed doesn’t help much when your phone struggles to stay connected. Being able to switch to a better network without even thinking about it could make a noticeable difference, especially if you travel or commute regularly.
