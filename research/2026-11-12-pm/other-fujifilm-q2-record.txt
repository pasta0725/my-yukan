TOKYO, November 9, 2026 – FUJIFILM Holdings Corporation announced today its financial results for the first half, which ended September 30, 2026.
                              First Half Results for the Fiscal Year Ending March 31, 2027
Revenue was JPY1,711.5 billion and increased 8.8% year-over-year.
Operating income was JPY141.2 billion and decreased 10.9% year-over-year.
Net income attributable to FUJIFILM Holdings was JPY105.2 billion and decreased 12.5% year-over-year.
                              Outlook for Fiscal Year Ending March 31, 2027
For the fiscal year ending March 2027, the company projects record-high revenue of JPY3.59 trillion, reflecting strong sales of Medical Systems and Semiconductor Materials.
Operating income is expected to reach JPY365.0 billion, in line with the previous forecast, as increased profits in the Electronic Materials segment are expected to be offset by factors including a decline in gross profit resulting from lower revenue in the Business Innovation segment.
Net income attributable to FUJIFILM Holdings is forecasted at JPY280.0 billion.
In the second quarter, the three months ended September 30, 2026, Fujifilm achieved record-high revenue, operating income and net income attributable to FUJIFILM Holdings supported by strong sales of endoscopes in Healthcare segment, semiconductor materials and data tapes in Electronics segment, and instax
™
 products in Imaging segment, while continuing to improve profitability in the Bio CDMO business and strengthening the operational foundation of the Business Innovation segment.
 “We continue to capture robust demand across our growth businesses and deliver solid growth across the Company. As we enter the final six months of our current medium-term management plan, VISION2030, we will remain firmly focused on delivering results, while executing the necessary reforms and establishing a clear path toward our next medium-term management plan,” says Teiichi Goto, president and chief executive officer, representative director, FUJIFILM Holdings Corporation.
                              Second-quarter financial highlights from July to September by business segments
Healthcare
Revenue increased by 9.8% year-over-year to JPY297.3 billion, while operating income declined 17.3% year-over-year to JPY16.0 billion, primarily due to upfront costs associated with the start-up of new large-scale Bio CDMO facilities in the United States, the impact of unplanned shutdowns in response to regulatory inspections, and higher raw material costs due to surging silver prices.
In the Medical Systems business, revenue increased, driven by significant performance in endoscope in the United States, continued strong growth in Europe and Japan, as well as in India, Southeast Asia and other markets, and higher sales of MRI systems and IVD products.
In the Bio CDMO business, revenue increased, driven by the ramp-up of new large-scale facilities.
In the LS Solutions business, revenue increased, driven by expanded use of cell culture media among major pharmaceutical companies.
Electronics
Revenue increased by 23.0% year-over-year to JPY132.0 billion, and operating income rose by 66.6% year-over-year to JPY32.4 billion.
In the Electronic Materials business, revenue increased, driven by strong sales of advanced-node materials, including those used in generative AI applications, such as the world’s leading CMP slurries for copper interconnects, NTI developers and liquid polyimides.
In the Advanced Functional Materials business, revenue increased, supported by higher sales of data tapes to major IT companies, among other factors.
Business Innovation
Revenue decreased by 1.7% year-over-year to JPY293.5 billion, and operating income declined by JPY14.9 billion year-over-year to JPY0.9 billion.
In the Business Solutions business, revenue decreased, primarily due to the absence of large-scale projects for local governments recorded in the previous fiscal year and lower revenue from related services as PC and server projects in Japan were postponed to the second half of the fiscal year due to semiconductor supply constraints.
In the Office Solutions business, revenue declined due to lower exports to Europe and North America.
In the Graphic Communications business, revenue increased, supported by higher sales of production printer Revoria Press series in new markets.
Imaging
Revenue increased by 11.0% year-over-year to JPY162.3 billion, and operating income rose by 29.6% year-over-year to JPY50.1 billion. 
In the Consumer Imaging business, revenue increased due to strong sales of instax
™
 products, supported by robust demand for mid- to high-end models such as the instax mini Evo
™
 and instax WIDE 400
™
, coupled with strong sales of the new entry-level instax mini 13
™
.
In the Professional Imaging business, revenue decreased due to the impact of strong sales of new products in the same period of the previous fiscal year.

In Q2, 
Fujifilm
 is celebrating record revenues – but there could be some unwelcome news ahead for photographers planning their next 
Fujifilm camera
 purchase.
As the new 
financial report
 states, the manufacturer has reached record revenue of ¥885 billion (approximately $5.59 billion / £4.17 billion / AU$8.09 billion) for the second quarter of its 2026 / 2027 financial year, covering July to September – an increase of 7.6% compared with the same period last year.
Operating profit also reached a record of ¥90 billion ($569 million / £424 million / AU$823 million), up 8.2%. But behind the impressive figures, rising production costs are putting increasing pressure on Fujifilm's imaging business – and photographers.

The Instax sales boom
The Instax Mini Evo (left) and the Square SQ40 (right) are both retro hybrid cameras

Fujifilm's Imaging division recorded revenue of ¥162.3 billion ($1.0 billion / £764.3 million / AU$1.5 billion) in Q3, up 11% year-on-year, while operating profit jumped 29.6% to ¥50.1 billion ($317.2 million / £235.8 million / AU$458.2 million).
Much of that growth came from the 
Instax
 line of 
instant cameras
, with consumer imaging revenue climbing 22.5% to ¥104.5 billion ($661.7 million / £491.8 million / AU$955.8 million).
In a world where almost anyone can use their 
camera phone
 for photography, phone fatigue leads people to straightforward models like the hybrid 
Instax Mini Evo
 – which combines digital shooting with instant printing and creative filters.
Also popular are the affordable 
Instax Mini 13
, 
Square SQ40
 and 
Wide 400
 – all of which give people a dedicated shooting experience, along with physical prints.

Fujifilm X and GFX cameras revenue slips 5.1%
Fujifilm X-T30 III is a good-looking entry to Fujifilm’s line of vintage-inspired X-T cameras 
(Image credit: Gareth Bevan / Digital Camera World)
The picture is rather different for Fujifilm's digital camera business, however.
Professional imaging, including the X (APS-C) and GFX (
medium format camera
) systems, recorded ¥57.7 billion ($364.8 million / £271.7 million / AU$528.1) in revenue in Q2, down 5.1% from the same period last year.
Fujifilm puts the decline down largely to strong camera sales during the same period last year, following several new product launches like the
 GFX100RF
, the 
X Half
 and the 
X-E5
.
Fujifilm also mentions that the
 X-T30 III
 and 
X-M5
 cameras continue to sell well.
Could your next Fujifilm camera cost more? 
The latest addition to the Instax pack is the Mini Evo 2. It will be released on November 27 and features various filters to foster creative photography 
(Image credit: Hillary K Grigonis / Future)
In the Q2 report, there is one key figure that photographers should keep an eye on.
Fujifilm expects rising memory and component costs to have a ¥14 billion ($88.51 million / £65.93 million / AU$128.1 million) negative impact on its Imaging division over the full financial year.
The manufacturer plans to offset some of this through pricing measures, which could eventually mean higher prices for cameras and other products.
But there's another interesting development in Fujifilm's latest results: it has more than doubled its Imaging R&D spending in Q2 to ¥5.2 billion ($32.88 million / £24.48 million / AU$45.6 million), an increase of 108% compared with last year.
R&D, or research and development, is the money invested in creating and improving products and technologies. In photography, that can include everything from developing new sensors and image processors to improving AF (autofocus), lens technology and overall image quality.
For the first half of the financial year, Fujifilm's Imaging R&D spending reached ¥9.5 billion ($60.06 million / £44.73 million / AU$86.9), up 72.2%.
The company hasn't revealed how this additional investment is being divided between Instax, X and GFX, so we don't yet know what it could mean for future cameras. But it's certainly a figure worth watching…
