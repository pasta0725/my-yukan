The Team That Shipped Cyanogen, Android and Playtron Unveils KORA: A New Desktop OS for the Age of Intelligence, Open to Every Model and Agent
Built on Linux from the kernel up, KORA gives local models, frontier models and agentic services the system-level integration closed platforms cannot offer, and opens the entire operating system to every model, AI service and agent.
PALO ALTO, Calif., Nov. 10, 2026
KORA Systems Inc. today unveiled KORA, The Operating System for Intelligence: a new desktop operating system, built on Linux, in which models and agents are part of the OS itself. KORA is a pure-play company building a truly native desktop operating system for intelligence, and it comes from a team that has already shipped three operating systems, Cyanogen, Android and GameOS, to more than a billion devices.
Every desktop operating system in use today was finished before intelligence arrived. On macOS and Windows, AI lives in a browser tab or an app sandbox. It cannot see the machine it runs on, schedule the silicon beneath it, or reach the files, memory and applications around it. Both platforms are closed, so that is where the integration ends. Linux is the only operating system that can be completely transformed by intelligence. KORA is that transformation.
KORA is built from the kernel to the shell around one idea: intelligence belongs inside the operating system. KORA's Cortex is the nervous system of the OS, a hybrid inference engine optimized for local models that turns every chip in the machine, CPU, GPU and NPU, and every machine on the network into one computer. Local models run on the user's own silicon by default, and frontier models join when the work calls for them. Neural workspaces and file systems replace the folders-and-apps desktop with one organized around intent, context and memory. Each workspace is a secure sandbox for its applications and agents, with permissions the user manages, and memory and context stay on the user’s own computer. The KORA SDK lets third parties integrate with KORA’s primitives, including projects, memory, context, semantic search and email.
“The desktop operating system is the ultimate harness. Roughly 45 million people, about half a percent of the world, account for 80 percent of the spend on intelligence. They are the AI-native prosumers who run agents and coding tools all day, and almost all of that work happens on a desktop running an operating system built 40 years ago. By 2033 that is more than 150 million of them spending over $400 billion a year. Nobody has built an operating system for them. KORA is the first.”
Kirt McMaster, Chief Executive Officer of KORA Systems Inc
Openness is the point. KORA's goal is an open intelligence ecosystem: an operating system open to every model, every AI service and every agent, where partners integrate at the system level. The capabilities behind KORA’s own applications, including memory, neural workspaces and file systems, will be available to third-party developers under the same user-managed permissions. A local model can become the default mind of the machine. An agentic service can run as part of the OS, with the identity, memory, permissions and compute the OS provides. That has been KORA's thesis from the first day of the project, and it is the one thing no incumbent can copy. Their platforms are closed by design, and their intelligence answers to their business models. KORA answers to the person at the keyboard.
“I joined Android when it had a hundred thousand users and left when it had a billion. It got there because it was open to everyone who wanted to build on it. Intelligence is the next platform shift, and it needs the same thing: an operating system where every model, every service and every agent is a first-class citizen. KORA opens that door on day one. The ecosystem that walks through it will decide the next decade of computing.”
John Lagerling, Chief Business Officer of KORA Systems Inc
KORA grew out of Playtron, the company McMaster founded to break Windows' hold on the gaming PC. Playtron built GameOS, a complete Linux operating system from the kernel to the compositor, and shipped it on devices in record time on a record budget. That work produced the one thing every intelligent operating system needs and no application can supply: a full OS the team controls end to end. KORA has been in development on that foundation since November 2025, built by the same engineers who shipped GameOS. GameOS lives on inside KORA as GameFrame, its play mode: one click turns a KORA desktop into a game console running the PC catalog. Work, Create and Play, on one machine.
“An operating system was once something only a giant could build. That era just ended. We believe 50 superpowered engineers can take on Windows. In January 2023 we started hiring for this, from the kernel up, and the result was GameOS, a full Linux operating system running on shipping hardware. Now the same engineers work side by side with frontier and local models and agents, and each of them ships what a team used to ship. The acceleration is astonishing. Our own product is the evidence: KORA is built inside KORA.”
Franck De Girolami, Chief Technology Officer of KORA Systems Inc
KORA is built first for developers and creators: the people who already pay for intelligence, run models on their own silicon and buy the hardware to do it. KORA’s own applications connect communication, organization and creation in one environment, so people move between ideas, tools and agents without rebuilding context. On KORA, a single workflow can span a local model, a frontier model and a dozen agentic services, scheduled across the whole machine, with the results landing in a workspace that remembers.
KORA is coming to PCs in 2027, including Snapdragon-powered machines through KORA's collaboration with Qualcomm Technologies. HUMAIN OS, the enterprise operating system KORA is co-developing with HUMAIN, was announced separately and is planned for 2027. Updates and the full story are at kora.fun.

KORA Advances Linux-Based Operating Systems for Intelligence on Snapdragon
Close to a year of engineering brings a full Linux desktop, system on a chip inference and large AI models on-device to Snapdragon X2 Elite; HUMAIN OS for the enterprise and KORA, The Operating System for Intelligence for developers and creators, arrive on PCs powered by Snapdragon in 2027
PALO ALTO and SAN DIEGO, Calif., Nov. 10, 2026
KORA Systems Inc. is working with Qualcomm Technologies, Inc. to advance Linux-based operating systems for intelligence on PCs with Snapdragon® X Series platforms. Close to a year of engineering by the two companies has brought a complete Linux desktop operating system to PCs with Snapdragon X2 Elite, with large AI models running entirely on the device and inference scheduled across the platform's CPU, GPU and NPU.
The work is the foundation for two operating systems arriving on PCs powered by Snapdragon in 2027: HUMAIN OS, the enterprise-grade, AI-native operating system KORA is developing with HUMAIN first unveiled at LEAP 2026 in Riyadh; and KORA, The Operating System for Intelligence, a new desktop operating system built for developers and creators.
Operating systems in use today were designed before computers could run intelligence of their own. KORA takes a fundamentally new approach, building on Linux as an open foundation engineered around intelligence from the kernel up, and Snapdragon X2 Elite supplies the silicon: the Qualcomm Oryon™ CPU, Qualcomm® Adreno™ GPU and an 80 TOPS Qualcomm® Hexagon™ NPU on one platform, with the efficiency to run large models on a laptop.
Engineers from Qualcomm Technologies worked alongside KORA throughout, from bringing up the Linux desktop on Snapdragon X2 Elite to tuning inference across the CPU, GPU and NPU. Qualcomm Technologies continues to upstream Snapdragon X2 Elite support into the mainline Linux kernel, work that benefits both operating systems and the wider Linux ecosystem.
What KORA built for PCs with Snapdragon
A complete Linux desktop on Snapdragon X2 Elite.
 KORA's Linux platform runs on Snapdragon X2 Elite from the kernel up, including a new desktop environment designed and built by KORA for intelligent PCs. The first OEM PCs in the world to ship with a Linux desktop operating system on a PC with Snapdragon X2 Elite will run HUMAIN OS in 2027.
Inference across the whole processor.
 KORA's Cortex, the hybrid inference engine at the core of both operating systems, schedules AI workloads across the CPU, GPU and NPU of Snapdragon X2 Elite, so the entire chip works on the task at hand. Cortex extends the same scheduling to other machines on the local network.
Large models entirely on-device.
 The ability to run a 26-billion-parameter language model locally on PCs with Snapdragon X2 Elite, alongside automatic speech recognition and embedding models. Responses stay fast, dependence on the cloud goes down, and sensitive information can stay on the device.
Agents built into the operating system.
 Agents are part of the OS architecture and can work with authorized system capabilities, applications, files and services, so AI can complete real work across the desktop. A neural file system indexes files by meaning, so people and agents can find information by what it contains as well as by name or folder.
Windows software for Snapdragon.
 Compatibility and translation technologies developed by KORA run selected Windows x86-64 applications inside the Linux environment on Snapdragon, so people keep access to the software they rely on as they move to a new platform.
Two operating systems. One foundation.
HUMAIN OS is the enterprise-grade, AI-native operating system KORA is developing in collaboration with HUMAIN. It is organized around intent, intelligence and action: users express what they want to accomplish, and the operating system assembles the relevant context, selects the appropriate local or cloud intelligence, and coordinates agents and applications to complete the work. HUMAIN OS integrates enterprise email, calendars and chat to provide a unified view of workplace information, alongside a suite of AI-native office applications. First demonstrated at LEAP 2026, HUMAIN OS is expected to become commercially available in 2027.
KORA is The Operating System for Intelligence: a new desktop operating system built for developers and creators, and a ground-up reimagining of the desktop for the age of intelligent PCs. KORA shares the foundation developed with Qualcomm Technologies, including Cortex, neural workspaces and file systems, and it is open at the model layer: any model, from any lab, local or cloud, the user's choice. KORA comes to Snapdragon-powered PCs in 2027.
“Every operating system in use today was designed before the computer had a mind. For close to a year, we have been building what comes after it with Qualcomm Technologies, on Snapdragon platforms: a full Linux desktop, large models running on the device, and the CPU, GPU and NPU working on the task as one machine. HUMAIN OS is the first operating system to ship on that foundation. KORA, The Operating System for Intelligence, follows on Snapdragon in 2027, built for the developers and creators who want the whole computer working for them.”
Kirt McMaster, Chief Executive Officer of KORA Systems Inc
“Snapdragon X2 Elite was designed to run advanced AI on the device, across the CPU, GPU and NPU. KORA's work brings a complete Linux desktop operating system built around that capability to Snapdragon, and we look forward to HUMAIN OS and KORA arriving on PCs powered by Snapdragon in 2027”
Kedar Kondap, SVP & GM, Compute and Gaming, Qualcomm Technologies, Inc
“HUMAIN OS is redefining the enterprise computing experience by putting intelligence at the core of how people work. By bringing together enterprise applications, contextual intelligence and AI agents in one unified operating system, we are enabling people to move seamlessly from intent to action. Together with KORA and Qualcomm Technologies, we are bringing this next generation of AI-native computing to market, delivering powerful on-device intelligence, greater productivity and a fundamentally smarter way to work”
Tareq Amin, Chief Executive Officer, HUMAIN
Availability
HUMAIN OS will be commercially available in 2027, debuting on the world's first OEM PCs to combine a Linux-based desktop operating system with the Snapdragon X2 Elite platform. KORA will follow later in 2027, expanding availability to Snapdragon-powered PCs.

Almost a decade after Cyanogen 
shut down its Android operating-system business
 following a years-long, unsuccessful effort to loosen Google’s dominance, its co-founder, Kirt McMaster, is now targeting Microsoft’s Windows with a new operating system designed for people who rely heavily on artificial intelligence in their everyday work.
McMaster’s company, 
Kora Systems
, on Tuesday unveiled a Linux-based desktop operating system that integrates AI models and autonomous agents directly into the computing experience, allowing them to work across applications, access files, and retain context between tasks. The software is expected to launch in 2027 as a free-to-use desktop operating system, with support for existing Intel- and AMD-powered PCs as well as machines running Qualcomm’s Snapdragon chips.
Kora is developing the eponymous operating system with a team of about 50 engineers, relying on AI coding tools, including OpenAI’s Codex and Anthropic’s Claude. McMaster told TechCrunch that these tools have accelerated development by at least fivefold, with most of the gains coming over the past six months.
Microsoft has long dominated the PC operating-system market, making it hard for newer players to establish a foothold. Windows 
held about 77%
 of the worldwide desktop operating-system market in October, compared with nearly 10% for Apple’s OS X, according to Statcounter’s web traffic data.
However, McMaster believes Kora does not need to compete for Windows’ entire user base. Rather, he is targeting people who use AI extensively and are willing to pay for access to powerful models and services.
“Microsoft can keep its billion users,” McMaster said, estimating that 45 million to 50 million people currently account for much of AI usage and revenue. He expects that market to exceed 200 million users by 2033.
McMaster told TechCrunch that his experience at Cyanogen, where he 
spent over five years
, taught him the dangers of relying on the very company he was trying to challenge. Cyanogen remained heavily dependent on Google Mobile Services despite offering features beyond Google’s version of Android. That ultimately restricted its ability to build an independent ecosystem.
“If you are trying to compete with an incumbent but are still reliant on them, you have no chance,” he said.
Kora, McMaster said, builds on an idea he pursued toward the end of his time at Cyanogen, when he began exploring ways to integrate third-party services directly into the operating system. With Kora, he wants AI models and agents to work across applications and access files without being confined to individual apps.
Kora’s Chord chat interface with access to local and frontier models.

Kora Systems
Previously known as 
Playtron
, a 
gaming operating-system company
 led by McMaster, the Palo Alto-based company rebranded as Kora in July 2025 as it shifted its focus to AI. The decision came as early companies, including Perplexity and OpenAI, were moving into AI-powered web browsers, McMaster said. He believed browsers would not give AI agents enough access to a computer to carry out more complex tasks, making an operating system a better starting point.
The shift also allowed Kora to build on nearly three years of work developing Playtron’s Linux-based gaming operating system, called GameOS, instead of starting from scratch.
Kora is designed to let users work with different AI models and agents across applications, while keeping track of conversations, documents, and other information. It also allows AI agents to operate in separate workspaces with restricted access to files and other resources.
While McMaster has drawn on his experience at Cyanogen, none of Kora’s engineers worked there. Instead, the company recruited heavily from the Linux gaming community, he told TechCrunch.
Kora’s software is built on Linux and uses Nix. It replaces the Fedora Silverblue foundation of Playtron’s earlier GameOS.
While Linux and Nix are open source, McMaster said Kora’s own technology, including its Cortex AI engine and desktop interface, remains proprietary. The company, he said, intends to support AI models and services from different providers, although it has not committed to making its own software open source.
Gaming also remains part of Kora’s plans. McMaster said the company intends to introduce AI-powered tools that would allow developers to create, publish, and play games directly within the operating system. He added that support for Android apps is also on its roadmap.
Kora’s GameFrame to bring a Game console-like experience.

McMaster told TechCrunch that Kora’s team has worked closely with Qualcomm’s engineers for nearly a year to optimize the operating system specifically for Snapdragon-powered PCs. The collaboration includes support for Qualcomm’s Snapdragon X2 Elite chips.
Alongside Qualcomm, Kora is working with Saudi Arabia’s AI company Humain on an enterprise version of the operating system, called Humain OS, which is expected to run on Snapdragon-powered laptops in 2027. Kora also plans to announce partnerships with PC manufacturers at a later date, though McMaster did not identify any companies or provide a specific timeline.
Kora plans to generate revenue through subscriptions that bundle access to AI models and agentic services. Moreover, its Cortex engine is designed to run AI tasks locally when possible and use cloud-based models for work requiring more computing power.
Kora’s investors include Samsung Next, Square Enix, Alumni Ventures, Polychain, VanEck, and Circle, all of which previously backed Playtron. The company, McMaster said, has raised additional funding since shifting its focus to AI. However, he declined to disclose the total amount raised or its valuation. Kora has enough funding to operate for several years, he said.

KORA Desktop OS Builds AI Into Linux, Heads to Snapdragon X2 Elite PCs in 2027
November 10, 2026
crmaris
KORA Systems, led by Cyanogen co-founder Kirt McMaster, has unveiled KORA, a Linux-based desktop OS that makes AI models and agents part of the operating system. Its enterprise sibling, HUMAIN OS, ships first on Snapdragon X2 Elite PCs in 2027, and KORA follows later that year.
The embargo is up on the KORA desktop OS, which its maker bills as “The Operating System for Intelligence.” KORA Systems Inc. built it on Linux from the kernel up, and the pitch is easy to follow: on Windows and macOS, AI lives in a browser tab or an app sandbox and sees very little of the machine it runs on. On KORA, the models and agents are part of the OS itself.
The company is small and makes a point of it. “We believe 50 superpowered engineers can take on Windows,” says CTO Franck De Girolami. The team came out of Playtron, the startup McMaster founded to build GameOS, a Linux operating system for gaming PCs, and it counts Cyanogen, Android and GameOS among the operating systems its people have shipped. Chief Business Officer John Lagerling says he joined Android when it had a hundred thousand users and left when it had a billion. KORA has been in development since November 2025, and its backers include Samsung, Square Enix, Polychain, VanEck, Circle and Alumni Ventures.
Image: KORA Systems press kit
Cortex, neural workspaces and local models first
At the center sits Cortex, which KORA calls the nervous system of the OS. It is a hybrid inference engine tuned for local models, and it schedules AI work across the CPU, GPU and NPU, and even across other machines on your local network. Local models run on your own hardware by default, cloud frontier models step in when a job needs them, and the platform is open at the model layer: any model, from any lab, local or cloud.
The folders-and-apps desktop gives way to neural workspaces and a neural file system that indexes files by what they contain, not only by name or folder. Each workspace is a sandbox with permissions the user controls, and memory and context stay on the local machine. Agents can work with the system capabilities, apps, files and services you authorize, and the KORA SDK gives third-party developers access to projects, memory, context, semantic search and email. Gamers get something too: GameOS lives on inside KORA as GameFrame, a play mode that turns the desktop into a console for the PC game catalog.
Built with Qualcomm on Snapdragon X2 Elite
In a joint release, KORA and Qualcomm say close to a year of engineering has produced a complete Linux desktop on Snapdragon X2 Elite, with inference spread across the Oryon CPU, the Adreno GPU and the 80 TOPS Hexagon NPU. KORA says the platform can run a 26-billion-parameter language model entirely on the device, next to speech recognition and embedding models. It has also built a compatibility and translation layer that runs selected Windows x86-64 applications inside the Linux environment on Snapdragon. Qualcomm engineers worked alongside KORA throughout, and Qualcomm continues to upstream Snapdragon X2 Elite support into the mainline Linux kernel, the effort we 
covered in September
.
“Snapdragon X2 Elite was designed to run advanced AI on the device, across the CPU, GPU and NPU,” said Kedar Kondap, SVP and GM of Compute and Gaming at Qualcomm Technologies.
HUMAIN OS goes first
The first operating system on this foundation does not carry the KORA name. HUMAIN OS is an enterprise OS that KORA is co-developing with 
Saudi AI company HUMAIN
, first shown at LEAP 2026 in Riyadh. Users state what they want done, and the OS gathers the context, picks a local or cloud model and coordinates the agents and apps, with enterprise email, calendars, chat and AI-native office apps built in. It is due in 2027 on what the companies call the first OEM PCs to pair a Linux desktop OS with Snapdragon X2 Elite. KORA follows later in 2027, aimed first at developers and creators.
What to watch for
This is an announcement, not a release: there is no download yet, no named KORA laptop, and no pricing or system requirements. The main release says KORA is coming “to PCs in 2027, including Snapdragon-powered machines,” so other platforms look likely, but Snapdragon is the only one named so far. The Windows layer covers “selected” applications, and how far that list reaches will matter to anyone thinking of leaving Windows. We also want to see speed and memory figures for that 26B model on a real laptop. McMaster’s market figures, roughly 45 million AI “prosumers” today and more than 150 million by 2033, are the company’s own estimates.
Even so, an OS that schedules local models across every block of the chip suits the NPU-heavy laptops coming next year, and Linux on Arm PCs has needed a backer this committed. Both releases, the launch film and the images are in the 
KORA press kit
, and updates will appear at 
kora.fun
