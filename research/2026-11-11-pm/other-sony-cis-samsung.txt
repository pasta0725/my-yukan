A note on fiscal-year labels.
 Sony's fiscal year ends March 31. Sony's own materials call the
    year ending March 31, 2027 "FY2026" and this quarter "FY26 Q2." This report follows our house convention of
    labelling a fiscal year by the calendar year in which it ends, so the quarter analysed here is
FY2027 Q2 (July to September 2026)
, the guided year is FY2027, and the year that follows is
    FY2028. Where a figure is quoted from Sony's own disclosure the company's label is retained in the quote.
    Reporting currency is the Japanese yen under IFRS; the NYSE line is an ADR trading one-for-one against the
    ordinary share.
Key Takeaways
Operating income of ¥622.3bn beat consensus by 23%, and this time the beat is operational.
 Pictures added ¥78.7bn year-on-year on Spider-Man: Brand New Day and gaming profit rose 29% excluding last year's charges. Disclosed currency explains only ¥40.7bn of the increase, and the tariff refund that carried the June quarter is largely spent.
It is a content quarter, not a broad one.
 Excluding Pictures and disclosed currency, clean operating income grew 3.4%. Image sensors and electronics both shrank excluding currency, and a further ¥30bn Sony Honda Mobility loss sits outside the company's non-recurring schedule.
Two of the three conditions we set for an upgrade in August have now been met, and the third moved the wrong way.
 The TSMC venture is signed with Sony in control, and the quarter beat on operations. Group capital expenditure guidance rose to ¥780bn, and because the venture will be consolidated, its plant spending will stay inside Sony's reported capex.
The raised ¥1,870bn guide implies only 3.5% clean growth in the second half, and part of the gaming raise is a deferral.
 Some next-generation development cost moves into FY2028 on accrual timing. With Grand Theft Auto VI launching November 19 and the yen weaker than the guide assumes, the guide is conservative for a third consecutive forecast.
Rating: Maintaining Hold; raising the target to $28 from $25.
 The ADR rose 6.1% on a day the S&P 500 fell 4.4%, and it is now 18% above where we downgraded. At 13.6 times enterprise value to guided operating income, the improvement is in the price.

  institutional investors position against, and the ADR earnings-per-share line, which the U.S. listing screens
  on. Both are shown below. Where no reliable published consensus exists for a line, the cell reads "n/a" rather
  than a manufactured number.

The sequential step-up is the better guide to what changed inside the year. The June quarter's ¥476.5bn
  contained roughly ¥56bn of tariff refund; the September quarter's ¥622.3bn contains an undisclosed
  residual of it, at most roughly ¥24bn, and it absorbed a ¥30bn Sony Honda Mobility loss on top. A ¥145.8bn sequential gain built
  on a smaller refund and a larger charge is the first evidence in three quarters that Sony's operating line is
  rising on its own content rather than on items outside the businesses.
Quality of the beat.
 Clean operating income rose ¥135.6bn year-on-year, from ¥486.7bn to
    ¥622.3bn. By segment, the increase is concentrated in two places.
Pictures, +¥78.7bn (58% of the increase).
 Segment profit went from ¥13.9bn to
      ¥92.6bn on theatrical revenue of $1,276M against $114M a year ago, almost all of it Spider-Man: Brand New
      Day. This is real operating profit, and it is also the least repeatable kind: a single film at the top of the
      North American all-time box office chart.
Game & Network Services, +¥49.9bn (37%).
 Against last year's ¥170.2bn
      clean base (reported ¥120.4bn plus ¥49.8bn of Bungie and development-cost charges), the segment earned
      ¥220.1bn, up 29%, or about 24% after ¥8.7bn of disclosed currency. Marvel's Wolverine, third-party
      software and network services drove it.
Everything else, +¥7.0bn combined.
 Imaging & Sensing Solutions added ¥16.4bn
      but received ¥25.9bn of currency. Entertainment, Technology & Services added ¥3.2bn with ¥6.1bn
      of currency and a tariff refund inside it. Music added ¥7.4bn with an undisclosed currency benefit. All
      Other and Corporate lost a further ¥20.0bn, mostly the Sony Honda Mobility charge.
    Excluding Pictures and the ¥40.7bn of disclosed segment currency, clean operating income grew 3.4%, from
    ¥472.8bn to ¥489.0bn. Adding back the Sony Honda Mobility loss lifts that to roughly 10%, and removing
    any second-quarter tariff residual takes it back down. The honest range for the rest of the portfolio is
    low-to-high single digits, which is where our third bear point has always placed it.
Revenue.
 Sales of ¥3,429.4bn grew 10.3%, but Sony's segment currency disclosures
  sum to ¥212.6bn against a ¥321.5bn increase, which leaves growth of roughly 3.5% excluding currency.
  Pictures contributed ¥167.7bn of the increase on its own. By customer location, the United States grew 20.2%
  and Europe 14.5%, the two regions where the film and the game slate sold; China fell 6.0% and Japan was flat,
  which is consistent with the smartphone and camera weakness Sony describes in its hardware segments.
Margins.
 Operating margin reached 18.1% against a clean 15.7% a year ago, and gross margin
  rose from 32.4% to 37.0%. The mix did most of the work: Pictures went from a 4.0% to an 18.0% margin, and
  gaming from a clean 15.3% to 18.2%. Selling, general and administrative expense rose 13.8%, faster than sales,
  which is what a quarter with a global theatrical tentpole and a major first-party launch should look like.
  Music was the one large segment whose margin fell, from 21.3% to 20.5%, for a second consecutive quarter.
  Inside operating income, the equity-method loss widened from ¥1.6bn to ¥22.8bn, which is where most of
  the Sony Honda Mobility charge sits.
Earnings and cash.
 Diluted earnings per share rose 47.9% against a 44.4% increase in
  attributable net income; the gap is a 2.4% lower diluted share count from the buyback. The effective tax rate
  eased from 27.9% to 26.7%. The bigger change is cash. Operating cash flow for the quarter was roughly
  ¥457.9bn, nearly double last year's, and six-month operating cash flow of ¥655.4bn is up 33.1% even
  though income taxes paid rose from ¥89.9bn to ¥252.0bn. The conversion problem we flagged in August
  looks, on this evidence, more like tax timing than like a deterioration in the businesses.

Pictures
The segment that made the quarter. Sales rose 48.5% to ¥513.7bn, or 37% on the U.S. dollar basis Sony
  uses for this business, and operating income rose roughly sevenfold to ¥92.6bn, a second-quarter record. On
  the dollar basis, operating income was $581M against $93M. Theatrical revenue of $1,276M against $114M is the
  whole story: Spider-Man: Brand New Day, released July 31, had taken $952M in North America and $1,548M
  internationally by September 30.
"Spider-Man: Brand New Day, released in July, was a record-breaking hit with worldwide cumulative box
  office revenue exceeding 2.5 billion U.S. dollars. It is the highest-grossing film of all time in North America
  and the third highest-grossing film of all time globally." 
— Lin Tao, Executive Officer and CFO
Underneath the film, the segment is weaker than the headline suggests. Television Productions revenue fell
  31% in dollars to $483M, Media Networks slipped 2% to $896M, and the motion-picture streaming-licence line fell
  to $186M from $237M. Sony raised the full-year Pictures operating income guide by a third, to ¥200bn, but
  that still implies a second half of ¥82.6bn against a clean ¥99.5bn a year ago, with Jumanji: Open World
  in December carrying launch marketing.
Assessment:
 This is what the bull case on Sony's content portfolio looks like when it works,
  and it worked unusually well. It is also the hardest profit in the group to underwrite forward. The FY2028
  slate has two large titles, The Legend of Zelda on April 30 and Spider-Man: Beyond the Spider-Verse on June 18,
  plus post-theatrical windows for this film, so the segment need not collapse. But the incremental ¥78.7bn is
  a hit, not a run-rate, and we model it that way.
Game & Network Services
Sales rose 8.6% to ¥1,208.5bn, of which ¥73.0bn is currency, so sales excluding currency grew about 2%.
  Operating income rose 82.8% to ¥220.1bn, a second-quarter record, and 29% against last year's clean base.
  The composition is the encouraging part. Digital software revenue rose 23.3% and network services 19.5%, while
  hardware revenue fell 4.9% on 2.6 million PlayStation 5 units against 4.0 million. First-party software units
  rose from 6.3 million to 8.2 million on Marvel's Wolverine, which sold through 2.4 million copies in its first
  50 days.
"With regard to PlayStation (PS5) hardware, we have been able to offset the rise in memory prices
  through adjustments to promotions and pricing, as well as additional cost-reduction, and we expect hardware
  profitability for the current fiscal year to remain on par with the previous fiscal year." 
— Lin Tao,
  Executive Officer and CFO
Engagement turned. Monthly active users reached 127 million in September, up 7% and a September record,
  against +2% in the June quarter, and total playtime rose 4% after falling 4% in the June quarter. Sony raised the
  segment's full-year operating income guide ¥40bn to ¥700bn, citing third-party software, network services
  and lower costs, partly offset by moving God of War Laufey from February into the first quarter of FY2028.
Assessment:
 The best-quality profit in the release. It comes from software and subscriptions
  on roughly 98 million PlayStation 5 consoles sold in to date, not from refunds. Two caveats keep us from
  extrapolating it. Hardware sell-in has now fallen 35% or more for three consecutive quarters, and part of the
  guidance raise is next-generation development cost that is moving into next year rather than going away (Key
  Topic 3).
Music
Sales rose 10.3% to ¥598.4bn, of which ¥34.3bn is currency, and operating income rose 6.4% to
  ¥122.8bn, a second-quarter record despite the absence of last year's Demon Slayer theatrical release, which
  shows up as a 14.6% decline in Visual Media & Platform revenue. On the dollar basis that removes translation,
  streaming revenue grew 7% in Recorded Music and 6% in Music Publishing, down from 10% and 8% in the June quarter
  and from the 9% to 14% band we cited at initiation.
"We believe such evergreen catalogs are assets that will continue to generate value over the long
  term, even in the age of AI." 
— Lin Tao, Executive Officer and CFO
The prepared remarks leaned on catalog: songs older than 18 months made up more than half of streams on
  Spotify's Global Top 200 through September, and Michael Jackson's catalog went from roughly 100 million to more
  than 3 billion streams on that chart after the biopic. The balance sheet tells the investment side. The
  Recognition Music Group transaction, closed July 15, brought in ¥542.7bn of music catalogs and ¥308.7bn of
  long-term debt secured by them, for ¥256.4bn of cash consideration and ¥66.3bn of third-party
  non-controlling interest. Music's long-lived assets rose 22% in the quarter to ¥2,513.7bn.
Assessment:
 Still the highest-quality earnings stream in the group, and still the one whose
  returns are drifting the wrong way. Margin fell for a second quarter, dollar-basis streaming growth slowed again,
  and the segment return-on-invested-capital table in the supplement has not been updated since May, even though
  the denominator just grew by more than a fifth. The guide went up ¥25bn to ¥445bn; the return on the
  capital behind it is the number Sony is not showing.
Imaging & Sensing Solutions
A record quarterly operating income of ¥154.6bn on falling sales. Revenue fell 6.0% to ¥577.6bn, and
  12.7% excluding ¥40.9bn of currency; image sensor revenue fell 7.3%. Operating income rose 11.8%, but
  currency contributed ¥25.9bn, so it fell about 7% excluding currency. Margin of 26.8% is the highest in the
  group, helped by mix and by the yen.
"In the smartphone market this quarter, growth declined approximately 20% year-on-year mainly due to
  the impact of conditions in the memory market. Although the market for mid-range and lower phones declined
  significantly, we believe the decline in the high-end market was limited." 
— Lin Tao, Executive Officer and
  CFO
The physical data confirm the softening. Image sensor wafer input fell to 147 thousand 300mm-equivalent
  slices a month from 155 thousand a year ago, against capacity of 160 thousand, so utilisation dropped from about
  99% to about 92%; Sony attributes part of the dip to the July Kumamoto earthquake. More telling, Sony now
  forecasts December-quarter capacity of 155 thousand, the lowest in the period we track. The full-year operating
  income guide rose ¥30bn to ¥450bn on cost reductions and currency, net of an approximately ¥16bn
  earthquake loss now included.
Assessment:
 The margin is excellent and the trajectory is not. Sales excluding currency fell by
  double digits, the implied second-half operating income of ¥173.2bn is 14% below last year's clean
  ¥201.2bn, capacity is being trimmed, and management, while declining to comment on its largest customer's procurement,
  did not contest that the customer will diversify supply from next year (Analyst Q&A). The segment is earning record profit at the top of a cycle
  that its own forecast says is turning.
Entertainment, Technology & Services
Sales slipped 1.4% to ¥567.8bn, about 6% lower excluding currency, and operating income rose 5.1% to
  ¥64.1bn only because of currency and the U.S. tariff refund, which Sony names as the main driver. Excluding
  the ¥6.1bn of disclosed currency, operating income fell about 5% before removing the refund. Imaging revenue
  fell 1.5% on a slowdown in China, Displays rose 1.2% on the new True RGB BRAVIA range, and Sound fell 1.0%. The
  full-year guide was left at ¥150bn of operating income, which implies a second half 21% below last year's.
One balance-sheet line deserves attention. Segment inventory reached ¥441.9bn, up 20% in the quarter and
  18% year-on-year, ahead of a holiday season and the April start of the television joint venture with TCL.
