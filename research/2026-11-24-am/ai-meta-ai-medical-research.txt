LSU System Researchers Secure Meta Funding for AI Projects Transforming Healthcare Training and Patient Care
November 19, 2026
NEW ORLEANS
 – LSU system researchers are helping lead a groundbreaking effort to harness artificial intelligence and virtual reality technologies to improve patient care, train future healthcare professionals, and protect some of the most vulnerable patients.
Three projects involving LSU Health New Orleans faculty are among five selected across four LSU campuses to receive funding through Meta’s invitation-only Open Source AI for Science initiative. All projects were chosen as part of a competitive collaboration. Two of them involve researchers with the LSU College of the Coast & Environment in Baton Rouge, underscoring the power of the LSU System’s multidisciplinary approach to solving complex healthcare challenges for Louisiana and beyond.
The funded projects focus on helping clinicians identify cancer treatment risks earlier, creating advanced virtual reality training environments for medical students, and protecting critically ill infants through AI-powered monitoring technology.
“These projects demonstrate how LSU researchers are not just adapting to emerging technologies but helping define how AI and immersive learning tools can be safely and effectively applied to healthcare,” said Dr. David Guzick, Senior Vice Chancellor of LSU Health New Orleans. “What makes this effort especially meaningful is the collaboration across our campuses. Physicians, scientists, engineers and computer scientists are all working together to develop solutions that can improve outcomes for patients and strengthen healthcare education for the next generation of providers.”
Bringing AI-powered coaching into medical training
Another project is transforming how healthcare professionals prepare for high-stakes clinical situations. Dr. Peter DeBlieux, Assistant Dean for Advanced Learning and Simulation at LSU Health New Orleans, is collaborating with Dr. Felipe Fronchetti, Assistant Professor of Computer Science and Engineering at LSU, and Dr. Kevin Lord, Associate Professor of Pharmacology, Biochemistry and Experimental Therapeutics at LSU Health New Orleans, to develop Communication Optimal pathway Procedural platform Environment Specific coaching (COPES), an advanced training platform that combines virtual reality, haptic simulation, and artificial intelligence coaching.
The system allows medical students and residents to repeatedly practice complex procedures in realistic scenarios while receiving immediate feedback from an AI-powered virtual coach. In one prototype, trainees can insert a chest tube in a simulated combat field hospital while simultaneously communicating with a conscious and anxious patient. The project reflects a growing shift in healthcare education toward immersive technologies that allow clinicians to develop technical skills, decision-making abilities, and communication techniques before encountering rare or high-risk situations in real patient settings.
“In medicine, we used to learn by practicing on patients, but now we can practice before we ever touch one,” said DeBlieux. “An AI coach in virtual reality lets a young doctor rehearse a rare, high-stress procedure again and again, hear honest feedback and learn to speak with patients. This means better care when it matters most.”
Leveraging environmental expertise to further advance health outcomes
One funded project brings together Dr. Sagar Kansara, Assistant Professor of Otolaryngology at LSU Health New Orleans and Dr. Supratik Mukhopadhyay, Professor of Environmental Sciences at the LSU College of the Coast & Environment with a joint appointment at the LSU Center for Computation & Technology, to develop AI tools that identify potentially preventable complications in patients undergoing surgery for head and neck cancer.
The researchers are creating interpretable AI models that move beyond simply predicting risk. Instead, the tools identify factors physicians can address before surgery, such as nutritional deficiencies or other modifiable conditions associated with complications, longer hospital stays, and delayed treatment. By leveraging Meta’s open-source AI technologies, researchers can securely analyze clinical notes while keeping patient records within LSU’s protected environments. The goal is to provide surgeons with practical, actionable information that helps improve outcomes and potentially reduce delays in cancer treatment.
“AI is most valuable when it helps clinicians take action,” said Dr. Mukhopadhyay. “This work focuses on providing insights physicians can use to improve care before complications occur.”
Protecting infants through AI monitoring
Another project aims to improve safety for premature and critically ill newborns who depend on breathing tubes to survive. Dr. Michael Dunham, Professor of Otolaryngology at the LSU Health New Orleans School of Medicine and Director of the Medical Innovation Design Studio at LSU Health New Orleans, is also partnering with LSU’s Dr. Mukhopadhyay to create “Eyes-On,” an AI-powered monitoring system designed to detect potentially dangerous breathing tube displacement in infants as early as possible.
Using a camera-based system positioned beside a hospital crib, clinicians can identify a breathing tube on a touchscreen, allowing the technology to continuously monitor its position. The platform uses advanced AI models to observe movement and alert caregivers if the tube appears to move outside an expected range. Notably, the system processes video locally rather than transmitting data to cloud servers, helping protect patient privacy while maintaining rapid monitoring capabilities. The researchers are working with Manning Family Children’s in New Orleans and Our Lady of the Lake Children’s Hospital in Baton Rouge to advance development of the technology.
“Losing an airway is one of the events we fear most in the neonatal intensive care unit,” said Dr. Dunham. “These infants cannot tell us when something is wrong, and our goal is to recognize the problem as early as possible so caregivers can respond right away.”
Advancing innovation through systemwide collaboration
Together, the projects illustrate how LSU’s academic, research, and healthcare campuses are working collaboratively to address challenges across the continuum of care, from medical education and workforce development to cancer treatment and patient safety.
The funding also highlights LSU’s growing role in the development of responsible AI applications in healthcare. By bringing together expertise in medicine, engineering, data science, and computer science, LSU researchers are helping create technologies that can support clinical decision-making, expand healthcare training opportunities, and improve patient outcomes.
The three LSU Health New Orleans-led projects are among five selected from across the LSU System through Meta’s Open Science Effort, which supports researchers exploring innovative applications of advanced AI models to solve real-world challenges.
About LSU Health New Orleans 
LSU Health New Orleans educates Louisiana’s health care professionals. The state’s health sciences university leader, LSU Health New Orleans includes a School of Medicine with campuses in Baton Rouge and Lafayette, the state’s only School of Dentistry, Louisiana’s only public School of Public Health, and Schools of Allied Health Professions, Nursing, and Graduate Studies. LSU Health New Orleans faculty members care for patients in hospitals and clinics throughout the region. In the vanguard of biosciences research, the LSU Health New Orleans research enterprise generates jobs and enormous annual economic impact. LSU Health New Orleans faculty have made lifesaving discoveries and continue to work to advance treatment and prevent and cure disease.
About LSU College of the Coast & Environment
LSU’s College of the Coast & Environment (CC&E) stands out for its world-class research and academic programs. Our faculty and students work at the epicenter of one of the world’s most dynamic and environmentally significant regions, confronting pressing challenges such as coastal erosion, pollution, and extreme weather events. Research and education at CC&E are closely aligned with the needs of Louisiana and its people, providing our work with a strong sense of purpose that empowers students and scientists to make a real, lasting impact. Here are a few of the projects that are benefitting from our partnership: 
Training Doctors in Virtual Reality
At Louisiana State University, Dr. Peter DeBlieux and his team are using Muse Glimmer, SAM 3, DINOv3, and other models from Meta, along with Meta Quest 3 headsets to develop COPES, an experimental coaching platform that supports healthcare professionals with medical procedural training in virtual reality. The system will aim to provide adaptive guidance, real-time performance assessment, and personalized feedback across technical execution, patient communication, clinical reasoning, and decision-making. Working virtually means medical students can learn complicated procedures in situations where practicing on real patients may be risky, and virtual instruction means more medical professionals can receive high quality technical and communications training — no matter where they are.

Helping Doctors Find the Source of Epilepsy
Dr. Pierre Bourdillon at Rothschild Hospital Foundation is using AI research and models from Meta including 
NeuralSet
, 
Brain2Qwertyv2
, 
Tribev2
, and 
DINOv3
, in research projects aimed at better understanding the source of epilepsy in the brain. Currently, epilepsy can’t be directly visualized in brain images, making it difficult for clinicians to identify its source. Dr. Bourdillon is using AI to develop a tool that can enable simulations of epileptic seizures originating in different regions of the brain. This approach could one day enable doctors to select the simulation that best matches their patient’s case, allowing them to form a strong hypothesis about where the patient’s epilepsy originates. This early-stage research could improve accuracy in a range of epilepsy treatments, including surgical removal of affected brain tissue and device implantation designed to disrupt epileptic activity. The simulations also have the potential to help researchers address speech and motor restoration in patients with brain injuries.

Improving Our Understanding of Rare Diseases
At Columbia University, Dr. Wolfgang Pernice has partnered with Meta to adapt the DINO family of models to improve analysis of microscopy images of cells, helping doctors understand how rare diseases appear in cells. In conventional image analysis methods, researchers have to know what they are looking for in advance, limiting their analysis. But the adapted Cell-DINO, DINO-BoC, and FINO models generate unbiased representations of the inner workings of cells, allowing scientists to decode the complex patterns by which rare diseases manifest in human cells. This can help improve our understanding of rare diseases and accelerate drug discovery.
