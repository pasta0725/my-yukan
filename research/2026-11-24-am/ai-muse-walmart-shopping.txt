Your next Black Friday haul might be picked out by software that never has to fight for a parking spot. 
Walmart
 is now letting 
Meta
’s Muse shop on its website on behalf of users, finding and buying holiday deals without a human clicking through every page.
The move turns Walmart’s Agent Gateway, announced last month, into a working system just as the busiest shopping stretch of the year arrives. It also marks a clear split from 
Amazon
, which has taken a much more guarded approach to the same AI agent.
How the Agent Gateway works
Walmart first unveiled the Agent Gateway and a companion feature called Agent Profiles on October 21, 2026. The live rollout means AI agents, starting with Muse, can search for products, assemble shopping carts, and complete purchases on Walmart.com and Sam’s Club.
Under the hood, the system relies on the Model Context Protocol, or MCP. Think of it as a dedicated service entrance for robots, instead of making them squeeze through the front door built for people.
Without something like MCP, an AI agent typically has to operate a web browser the way a person would. It reads pages, clicks buttons, and hopes the layout doesn’t change mid-checkout. MCP lets the agent talk directly to Walmart’s backend systems, which sidesteps a lot of that fragility.
Walmart says the gateway helps agents surface relevant products without handing over a shopper’s purchase history. According to the company, agents will not get access to extensive customer purchase records.

Agent Profiles put guardrails on the bots
Agent Profiles are Walmart’s answer to the obvious question: what stops an AI from going on a spending spree? The feature gives users a way to manage how their AI agents interact with Walmart.
Shoppers can set spending limits and monitor transactions the agent makes. Access to payment information is also restricted, keeping sensitive details out of the agent’s reach.

Walmart is also stressing that prices will be the same whether a human or an agent is doing the shopping. That pledge addresses a quiet worry in AI commerce: that bots might see different deals than people, or that retailers might use agents to steer buyers toward pricier items.
Muse’s fast rise and the Amazon snub
Meta launched Muse on September 8, 2026. Within weeks, the AI shopping agent had picked up more than 10 million users across the US and Canada.
Amazon chose to restrict Muse’s access after the agent launched, limiting what it could do on Amazon’s platform.
Muse adjusted accordingly. It now defaults to searching retailers such as Walmart and Target, skipping Amazon unless a user specifically asks for it.
Why Black Friday is the real test
Black Friday sales run from November 23 to November 29, 2026. That window gives Walmart a high-pressure environment to see whether agent-driven shopping holds up at scale.
The rollout reflects a broader trend of retailers leaning on AI to engage customers during peak shopping periods. Walmart’s version stands out because it doesn’t just add a chatbot to its site. It opens a structured path for outside AI agents to transact directly.
What this means for retail and shoppers
The bigger story is a philosophical fork in how major retailers treat AI agents. Walmart is building a formal on-ramp with privacy limits, spending controls, and price parity. Amazon, at least for now, is keeping Muse at arm’s length.
With Muse now defaulting to rivals, Amazon risks missing traffic from a user base that already exceeds 10 million people.
For shoppers, the practical upside is convenience with guardrails. Spending caps and transaction monitoring reduce the odds of an AI making an expensive mistake, while limits on purchase history access ease some privacy concerns.

How a Muse Purchase Works
A shopper can ask 
Muse
 for a television deal or for Walmart Black Friday deals available for delivery, then explore products and build a basket in the conversation. The customer signs in through a Walmart handoff and returns to Muse. Adding the first item to the cart triggers Agent Profile setup, including a spending limit chosen by the customer.
Checkout stays inside the Muse conversation and uses a card already saved in the Walmart account. The customer pays the same prices shown on Walmart.com, and Walmart’s return policy continues to apply.
Customer Controls and Checkout Rules
An Agent Profile keeps the agent’s activity in a dedicated space within the Walmart account, separate from the customer’s other purchases. Walmart’s product page says customers can set spending limits, choose which purchases need approval, and review and track what the agent buys.
Under Walmart’s 
Agentic Access Terms
, only the customer may designate the payment method an agent can use. The agent cannot add or change payment methods and never receives raw stored payment-card data. Outside the Gateway, Walmart requires human approval before every agent checkout; within it, autonomous checkout can proceed when the agent stays within customer-configured guardrails, although Walmart may require additional approval.
The practical effect is that Muse can complete an authorized purchase without receiving the customer’s card details or gaining unrestricted authority over the account. The customer can delegate the transaction while retaining the payment and approval boundaries.
Gateway Design and Beta Limits
Walmart introduced Agent Profiles, the Gateway and agent terms in an 
October 21, 2026 announcement
, with profiles expected in the following month and an initial Gateway beta for companies and developers. It said browser-based agents can be sluggish and prone to mistakes on websites designed around human navigation and sign-ins. The Gateway uses an MCP or similar connection protocol instead, allowing an external agent to shop with 
Sparky, Walmart’s own agent
. Walmart also said the connection can help identify a customer’s usual products without disclosing broader personal information or purchase history to the agent.
The current rollout still has explicit limits. Walmart describes the overall Gateway as an early beta with invitation-only access that may be suspended at any time, and says some features may change or remain unavailable to some participants. The live Muse integration operates within those restrictions.
Walmart said support for Muse was the beginning, with shopping experiences for other AI agents and apps on the way. The Muse implementation gives Walmart a working model for those additional integrations, with the retailer controlling the connection and transaction rules and customers retaining control over spending and approval.

Kathmandu—
 Walmart has activated its Agent Gateway platform with live support for Meta's Muse AI assistant, enabling the artificial intelligence agent to search for and purchase Black Friday deals on Walmart's website on behalf of shoppers. The retail giant announced the rollout as a first-of-its-kind implementation that lets an external AI agent operate within a customer's Walmart account through a dedicated Agent Profile, where users can set spending limits, approve individual purchases, and review the agent's transaction history. While competitors such as Amazon and eBay have restricted AI agents from accessing their platforms, Walmart characterized the Muse integration as an initial step with additional AI shopping experiences planned for other agents and applications.
How the Agent Gateway Works
The system operates through Walmart's newly created Agent Profiles, which provide a segregated space for AI agent activity within a customer's existing Walmart account. When a user directs Muse to find a product — such as a television deal or available Black Friday offers for delivery — the AI explores Walmart's catalog and builds a shopping basket. Before the first item is added to the cart, the user is taken to Walmart to sign in and establish an Agent Profile, including a self-defined spending limit. Checkout is completed within the Muse conversation using a payment card already saved in the Walmart account, and purchases carry the same pricing and return policy as standard Walmart.com orders.
Contrast With Competitor Approaches
Walmart's decision to open its platform to an external AI agent marks a departure from the stance taken by other major online retailers. Both Amazon and eBay have blocked AI agents from browsing their sites, citing concerns over automated traffic, scraping, and unauthorized transactions. Walmart, by contrast, has built a controlled framework that requires explicit user authentication, spending caps, and purchase approvals, positioning the Agent Gateway as a secure alternative to unrestricted bot access. The company said support for Meta's Muse is "just the beginning" and that additional shopping experiences for other AI agents and applications are "on the way."
The Agent Gateway is now live for Meta Muse users, with Walmart indicating that broader support for other AI shopping agents will follow in the coming months.

Walmart Makes It Possible for Agents to Shop on Behalf of Customers, While Keeping Customers in Control

When Sam Walton opened the first Walmart more than 60 years ago, he set out to help people save money and live better. He believed new ideas and technology could make that possible – and that spirit still drives us today.
Today, we serve customers through stores and clubs, online shopping, pickup and delivery, Sparky and conversational commerce integrations with Muse, ChatGPT, Gemini and others. We want to be wherever customers choose to shop, bringing Walmart and Sam’s Club’s broad assortment, convenience and everyday low prices into the experiences they already use.
Shopping keeps changing, and Walmart has never been one to stand still. As AI agents become more mainstream, we’re not waiting to see what shopping becomes – we’re helping shape it, guided by our values – respect, integrity, service and excellence – and by listening and responding to our customers.
That’s why we’re introducing Agent Profiles, the Walmart Agent Gateway and terms for AI agents. Together, these tools will let customers shop with Walmart and Sam’s Club through the apps and agents they choose, with clear permissions, controls and protections built in.
Shopping with an agent won’t be for everyone, and it doesn’t have to be. But for customers who choose to use one, our goal is to make our experience the most trusted by building clear controls and protections into the experience from the start.
Agent Profiles: A dedicated profile for agents
Today, when a customer uses an AI agent, it signs in using a customer’s account. This means that all purchases – whether made by the customer or the agent – appear together, without any differentiation.
Agent Profiles, launching in the next month, will offer a new level of transparency for customers. Customers will be able to authorize each AI agent with a dedicated profile within the customer’s account, so the customers will be able to see the agent’s purchases separately from the rest of their purchases.
In addition, the customers will be able to determine how they want to approve purchases. They can require approval for every purchase or provide permission for the agent to make purchases within a spending limit they set – for example, $50, $100 or $200. That will give the agent a way to help while the customer stays in control.
Think about it like adding someone as a cardholder on your credit card. You can give them a way to pay, set a spending limit and keep track of how the card is used. Here, an agent gets its own profile to shop, with permissions and spending limits set by the customer and the ability to turn them off at any time.
And whether customers choose to shop directly or through an agent, our commitment to Every Day Low Price remains the same. We price products, not people, so the prices that an AI agent sees are the same prices that a human would see on Walmart.com, with applicable offers and fees shown before purchase.
Agent Profiles: How it Works
Start in the agent’s app
. The first time you use an agent to shop through the Walmart Agent Gateway, Walmart will automatically create an Agent Profile for that agent.
Connect through Walmart
. You'll authorize and link that Agent Profile to your account by signing into your Walmart account. Only Walmart sees this.
Set your controls
. Choose a spending limit and see purchases from every agent.  Delete an Agent Profile at any time.
The Walmart Agent Gateway: Talking to agents, like agents
Today, agents may shop on Walmart’s site through a web browser, following steps designed for people. This can be slow and error-prone, and agents may get stuck along the way. But the website is built for people to browse, click and sign in themselves. The way agents shop is different, and so we are building the Walmart Agent Gateway which will give agents a faster, more reliable way to shop by using an MCP (model text protocol). We'll begin with a 
beta for companies and developers
.
Another benefit of the Walmart Agent Gateway will be the ability to help agents shop for a customer’s usuals without having to share their personal information or purchase history with the agent. For example, if a customer tends to buy sourdough bread or a particular brand of milk, Walmart will help the agent find those usual items for the customer without sharing the customer’s broader shopping history.
Terms for AI Agents
In addition to profiles and a gateway designed for agent shopping, we are also setting clear expectations for how agents must operate, establishing what agents can and cannot do when shopping on behalf of a Walmart customer:
Customers are in control, and Walmart’s returns process applies – including if an agent doesn’t act as intended.
Customers must approve agent purchases by default, and Walmart may require additional approvals even for purchases within an agent’s spending limits.
Agents must not take actions beyond what the shopper intends.
Agents don’t receive customers’ payment-card information and can’t add or change a customer’s payment methods.
Agents can access only the information needed for the task and allowed by the customer.
The agent’s privacy policy applies to the information it receives. The company that provides the agent handles what you share with its app under its privacy policy. Walmart collects and processes information the agent provides in accordance with Walmart's Privacy Notice.
Agents that do not follow these terms will be blocked from Walmart’s site.
Put simply, Agent Profiles give customers control over what their agents can do, the Walmart Agent Gateway provides a reliable way for agents to shop, and our terms establish the rules those agents must follow.
We’ve always worked to understand what customers need and make shopping easier for them. As customers begin using agents to help them shop, we’re building connections, permissions and options that complement those tools and support how customers want to use them. It’s just another way we’re living our commitment to serve customers and earn the trust they place in us every day.
