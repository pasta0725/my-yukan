Nothing's next earbuds are called the Ear (note), and they're set to launch on December 8, 
Bloomberg reported
 after a preview in which Nothing co-founder 
Carl Pei
 demonstrated a pair that records conversations and turns them into text. Nothing's own November 20 teaser gave only the date and the line "Be present. It's noted." It didn't name the product or say when it goes on sale, and Nothing hasn't set a price yet, per Bloomberg.
The charging case has microphones of its own, and Pei used it to show how you'd record an in-person meeting. The earbuds capture phone calls. Either way you end up with a recording and a written transcript of what was said.
In a meeting, earbuds are usually shut inside their case, so the case does the listening. On a call they're in your ears, so they do it. Nothing put microphones in both places.
It's Nothing's first device built around AI, from a company better known for its phones.
If you spend your week in meetings, that means a transcript without typing notes while someone is talking. If your work happens on the phone, it means a written record of a call you'd otherwise have to remember.
You can already do this with a phone on the table or a separate recorder. The Ear (note) folds it into something that's already in your pocket.
The Ear (note) joins a line that runs from the first Nothing Ear through the Ear 2, the 2024 Nothing Ear and the Ear 3. Every one of those names is a number or nothing at all. This one says what the pair is for.
Nothing also posted a teaser for a product codenamed Kingdra on its community forum on October 23. A support page on Nothing's own website had tied that 
codename
 to an 
Ear 3 Pro
, which would be the first Ear with the Pro name. The teaser had no picture and no date, and Nothing said only that Kingdra is coming soon. The Ear (note) carries a different name, and it's the one with a day on the calendar.
December 8 is about two weeks away. That's close enough to wait for if you were about to buy earbuds for work calls or a voice recorder for meetings.
The price is the number to look for that day. Look too for which phones the Ear (note) works with, how long the case can record, where your recordings are kept, and if the transcripts cost a monthly fee.
If you want Nothing earbuds mainly for music, this launch may not be yours. Kingdra, the pair tied to the Ear 3 Pro name, still has no date.
