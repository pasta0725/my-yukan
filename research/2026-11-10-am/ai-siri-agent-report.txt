Siri AI Rumored to Become 'More Like an Agent' as Early as Next Year
Sunday November 8, 2026 9:24 am PST
 by 
Joe Rossignol
Siri AI is much more intelligent and personalized compared to classic Siri, but it is still not nearly as advanced as proactive, always-on agents like OpenAI's Dots and Meta's Muse. That may change, though, according to a new rumor.
In his 
Power On
 newsletter today
, 
Bloomberg
's Mark Gurman said he was told that Apple could make Siri function "more like an agent" as early as next year. However, if this plan moves forward, the exact implementation remains to be seen.
"Dots, Muse and Grok can keep track of goals, determine what needs to happen next and continue pursuing objectives without the user initiating every individual step," said Gurman. "Apple ultimately wants to go in this direction, I'm told, and I believe the company could make Siri more like an agent as early as next year."
Apple's AI ambitions could go beyond making Siri more agentic. In a closed-door meeting in recent weeks, Gurman said that Apple CEO John Ternus told Apple's software engineering chief Craig Federighi that Apple needs to "accelerate" its software roadmap and "move quickly beyond merely making necessary improvements to Siri."

Apple pushing faster on agentic Siri would carry three big risks
Ben Lovejoy
 | Nov 9 2026 - 6:28 am PT																
								0 Comments							
Yesterday saw two reports about Apple software development. The first said that the company was aiming to give 
Siri
 more agentic capabilities “as soon as next year,” while the second said that CEO 
John Ternus
 is pushing 
Craig Federighi
 to speed up software development.
If accurate, it seems near-certain the two reports are connected. But if Ternus really is pushing for an acceleration in Siri’s ability to act as an AI agent, that comes with three potentially huge risks …
Both reports were by 
Bloomberg’s
 Mark Gurman
, though he didn’t directly connect them. On the Siri side, his newsletter contrasted the agentic abilities of some competitor models, suggesting that Apple ultimately wants to 
achieve the same things
.
Dots, Muse and Grok can keep track of goals, determine what needs to happen next and continue pursuing objectives without the user initiating every individual step. Apple ultimately wants to go in this direction, I’m told, and I believe the company could make Siri more like an agent as early as next year.
The report about Ternus pushing Federighi for 
faster software development
 mentions “moving beyond necessary improvements to Siri,” of which agentic capabilities would be the obvious candidate.
But agentic capabilities are among the most dangerous forms of AI given the multitude of things that could go very, very wrong.
Failing to carry out the task
The most obvious is simply failing to carry out the task. Depending on the importance of it, the consequences could range from annoyance through to significant harm.
Technology has long failed to live up to one of its biggest promises: relieving us of mundane work in order to devote our time to more fulfilling things. For example, obtaining tickets to a popular gig or other event, which currently involves scheduling time in order to repeatedly refresh a page, perhaps wait through a queuing system, and finally carry out the transaction. It would be great to be able to hand this off to an AI.
But even on something relatively unimportant like this, if we delegated the task and the AI failed to obtain the tickets, especially if this was due to a silly error, this could prove enormously frustrating. If the task were more important, the results could obviously be far worse.
Acting inappropriately
Potentially even worse is achieving the task but doing so in inappropriate ways. We’ve already seen high-profile examples of this, like 
the Hugging Face hack
 caused by an AI model seeking so hard to carry out instructions that it used underhand methods to bypass crucial safeguards in the process.
Putting privacy at risk
Another huge risk is to privacy. Just today we learned that Claude Code exposed over 2,000 real secrets to GitHub, as 
Cybernews
 reports.
Over two thousand real secrets, including passwords, API tokens, SSH root logins, and even admin-level cloud keys, were found leaking on GitHub after developers clicked “Yes, and don’t ask again” in Claude Code […] When a developer grants Claude Code permission to proceed with “Yes, and don’t ask again,” it saves the exact command, including secrets, to a file, .claude/settings.local.json, inside the project folder. This file should never be exposed publicly [but] Apiiro, an application security company, found 104,185 public copies of such files on GitHub across 103,617 repositories.
This was just a few days after Anthropic 
started encouraging
 Claude users to connect their bank and finance card accounts to the AI chatbot. (My own answer is “Hell, no.”)
When we delegate a task to an AI system that requires access to any of our personal data, and we’re not expected to check on it again until the task is complete, the potential for disaster here is enormous.
The bottom line
It’s worth prefixing this with a note that the reliability of the paired reports is far from certain. In particular, in a closed-door meeting between Ternus and Federighi, it’s hard to see how the content could have leaked unless one of the two men chose to do this. If Ternus leaked it, that suggests a 
very
 high level of frustration on his part. But leaving that aside …
We’re currently witnessing an AI arms race, with each company aiming to beat its competitors to next-generation capabilities, even while acknowledging a non-trivial possibility that the pace of development could put humanity’s future at risk.
In my view, the very last thing we need is Apple joining this race. I know it can be frustrating when Apple lags behind other companies in the introduction of some types of new tech, but this is one field in which I would be very happy to see Apple proceed very much more cautiously than others.
Right now, AI needs at least one player that prioritizes safety and privacy over speed to market – in that way, we’d get a choice. If we want agentic capabilities sooner rather than later, and are prepared to run the risks involved in this, then we can turn to companies like Anthropic and Meta. If we are prepared to wait for safer systems, then Apple should provide that alternative.

							An agentic Siri could let Apple’s assistant work on its own instead of waiting for requests. 
Photo: Apple
Apple is eyeing an agentic Siri, and it might arrive as early as next year, according to information leaking out of the company.
Siri AI is far more capable than the old assistant, but it still waits for you to ask. Rivals like ChatGPT’s Dots and Meta’s Muse keep working between conversations, and Apple CEO John Ternus reportedly wants to close that gap.
Ternus pushes for an agentic Siri
Apple’s voice assistant recently underwent a major transformation, with the old Classic Siri giving way to the more capable Siri AI. The revamped assistant uses Apple Intelligence to understand conversations better and perform more complicated tasks. It can also draw on personal information and on-screen content to provide more relevant answers.
But despite these improvements, Siri AI remains largely request-based: It waits for you to tell it what to do rather than independently working toward goals in the background. That’s apparently hoping to change.

In his latest 
Power On
 newsletter
, 
Bloomberg
’s Mark Gurman says 
Apple CEO John Ternus
 held a closed-door meeting with software chief 
Craig Federighi
. Ternus asked him to speed up Apple’s AI roadmap and move past mere Siri fixes.
Gurman adds Apple could make Siri “more like an agent” as early as next year and that the company wants to head that way.
Ternus dropped hints on Apple’s recent earnings call. He told analysts that Siri AI is “primarily request-based today”, and added that the new architecture leaves room to grow. He also called agentic tools promising as long as users knew what they’re granting access to.
Rivals already work in the background
Dots, Muse and Grok go way further than Siri. These agentic models can remember projects and preferences, check for changes and even decide when to pause and resume work. And while Siri AI can handle more complex tasks now, it cannot chase goals.
Apple also lacks a computer-use tool. ChatGPT and Claude already have one that can read a screen, move cursor, click buttons and type into apps.
Apple has been dabbling in agent-like features. At 
WWDC 2026
, it demoed a Passwords tool that can find weak or compromised passwords and change them on websites for you.
Gurman said that capability vanished from iOS 27 over the summer, but it’s still listed on Apple’s iOS website as coming later.
iOS 27.3 takes the first step
Apple rolled out 
iOS 27.3 beta
 on November 3 with Siri access across 15 apps, including Messages, Photos, Safari, and Mail. Some examples include organizing Safari tabs, unsending an iMessage and enhancing and sharing a photo in one go.
Apple also opened up Siri AI to developers in the same beta, with apps plugging in through App Intents. 
See also: 
Siri AI opens to third-party apps in iOS 27.3 beta
Gurman says he expects support for Apple’s own apps to go public as early as December with thew iOS 27.3 release, with third-party support landing sometime in spring. 
Just note that Apple hasn’t announced any plans or date for agentic features, so treat the timing as a rumor for now. In the meantime, Siri AI waits for your requests, but that will hopefully soon change.

===== https://thenextweb.com/news/ternus-federighi-agentic-siri-apple-ai-bloomberg
(status 200)
Apple chief executive John Ternus poses for a selfie with customers in an Apple Store.
Credit: Apple
Apple chief executive John Ternus has pushed software chief Craig Federighi to speed up Apple’s AI plans, Mark Gurman reported for 
Bloomberg
 on Sunday. Ternus wants Apple to move beyond improving Siri. According to the report, he held a closed-door meeting with Federighi in recent weeks. Ternus is frustrated by what he sees as Apple’s reluctance to match its rivals’ urgency on AI. TNW has not independently verified the report.
In public, Ternus has struck a different tone. On Apple’s 
earnings call last week
, he praised the new Siri AI. Last month he 
told WIRED
 that Apple would not ship AI for AI’s sake.
“It’s primarily request-based today, but as we talked about at WWDC, it’s built on a modern orchestrator-driven architecture that gives us real room to expand its capabilities over time,” Ternus said on the call. “As far as agentic capabilities, we think these are promising tools for users.”
An AI sceptic in charge of Apple’s AI
Federighi became Apple’s AI chief last year, after the company removed its former AI leader, John Giannandrea. He still has a reputation as an AI sceptic, Gurman wrote. He has worried about the technology’s effects on privacy, security, society and jobs.
Some employees describe a hands-on management style that leaves little room for competing ideas, according to Bloomberg. Federighi also tends to plan over 12 to 18 months, while Apple’s hardware teams work to multiyear road maps.
He also led the rebuild that brought 
a working Siri AI
 to market with iOS 27. The new Siri runs on models based on technology Apple licenses from Google.
Mike Rockwell, Apple’s vice president of Siri engineering, is still seriously considering stepping down next year, Bloomberg reported. He could leave the role or Apple altogether. More responsibility would then likely fall to two people. One is Amar Subramanya, a Google veteran Apple hired last year. The other is Sebastien Marineau, a Federighi deputy. Some inside Apple see Marineau as a possible future successor to Federighi.
From in-app actions to an agentic Siri
The 
iOS 27.3 beta
 lets Siri carry out precise actions inside Apple’s own apps. It can organise Safari tabs, sort folders in Files and unsend an iMessage. It can also edit and share a photo in several steps. Gurman expects that to reach users as early as December, with the public release of iOS 27.3.
Support for 
third-party apps
 will probably follow in the spring, with iOS 27.5 or iOS 27.6, he wrote. That would let Siri book a table through OpenTable or place an order through Uber Eats.
Even then, Siri would only act when asked. OpenAI’s ChatGPT Dots, Meta’s Muse and Grok can take on ongoing tasks, track goals and keep working between conversations.
“Apple ultimately wants to go in this direction, I’m told, and I believe the company could make Siri more like an agent as early as next year,” Gurman wrote.
Apple also lacks a computer-use tool, Gurman noted. ChatGPT and Claude already offer one, which can read a screen, move a cursor, click buttons and type into apps.
A new schedule for iOS 28
Apple has also changed how it builds next year’s operating systems, according to Bloomberg. Each development milestone normally splits into four weeks of coding and four weeks of integrating features into the system.
The first milestone for iOS 28, iPadOS 28 and macOS 28 gave engineers six weeks of coding and two of integration. Employees say they like the change, Gurman reported. Work on the second milestone, when Apple starts to finalise features, begins on Monday.
