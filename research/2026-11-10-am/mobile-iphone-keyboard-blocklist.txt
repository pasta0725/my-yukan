iOS bans certain terms from auto-complete and swipe-to-type
They include references to politics and drugs, researchers report
The list could include more than 100,000 entries, apparently
Try and type out phrases like "sleepy Joe", "dictator Trump", or "Tibetan independence" on your iPhone, and you'll notice that you don't get auto-complete suggestions for them, and you can't swipe-type them — evidence of a 'gray list' of terms that has been discovered hidden in iOS.
The so-called gray list was discovered by researcher Derek Kauffman and reported by 
Reuters
, and verified by an independent cybersecurity engineer. It's thought that there are at least 100,000 words and phrases in the list.
Other entries on the gray list, as reported by 
The Guardian
, include "NSA spies", "question your sexuality", and even "Australia is a very bad place". References to drug use also seem to be unavailable through auto-complete or swipe typing.

There's no issue with typing out any of these terms and including them in any kind of message — but the point is that iOS won't try and auto-complete them like it will with other words and phrases, or let you type them through swiping.

Apple
 is keeping its cards close to its chest: it didn't comment on the list directly in an email sent to Reuters, but did acknowledge that the iPhone keyboard was set up to avoid offering suggestions that might be inappropriate or offensive.
You may remember last year there was 
something of a kerfuffle
 over iPhones changing "Trump" to "racist" during dictation. It wasn't clear exactly what happened, but with any kind of AI-driven automation, these sorts of issues can arise.
The Electronic Frontier Foundation's David Greene told Reuters that while he could see the thinking behind a gray list for the iOS keyboard, "this is something users should be really well aware of and have some ability to customize".

===== https://www.macworld.com/article/3279221/revealed-apples-secret-list-of-slurs-insults-and-political-phrases-that-cant-be-swiped.html
(status 200)
According to a research project by Derik Kauffman, Apple has a built-in list of words and phrases that the keyboard suggested words will never complete, and the swiping keyboard will not produce. This isn’t a censorship list—typing individual characters always works—but it can slow you down if you’re looking to use swiping or suggested words to speed up your iPhone typing.
The project is detailed at 
Swipeblocked.com
, with additional code and data 
hosted on GitHub
. There, Kauffman details his forensics to discover this feature and the blocked phrases.
Here’s how it works: Apple has built into iOS a hash table of “blocked” phrases. It’s not storing plain text of words, but rather matching hashes of word IDs to a set of banned hashes. If there’s a match, the iOS keyboard will not suggest the word. And if you are swiping, it will either input some other word for your swiped word or replace an earlier word in your phrase with a different word that has a similar swipe pattern.
The list of phrases has not been made public by Apple, and the company does not disclose how it chooses what goes on it. Kauffman reverse-engineered it from the hash table within iOS.
The phrases are mostly what you would expect. They include vulgarities, politically and socially charged phrases, rascist terms, various forms of bigotry, and so on. You can literally type out the phrase, “smoking weed,” but if you type “smoking wee…” then the keyboard suggestions to finish off that second word will never include the obvious “weed.” Instead, you’ll get “weekend” or “weekly.” And if you’re using Apple’s QuickPath (Slide to Type), you’ll get “smoking Wed” or some other word, no matter how precise you are about swiping the word “weed” correctly.
The Swipeblocked site has a 
list of all the English-language banned phrases
 in iOS 27. 
Warning: Many of them are not safe for work and highly offensive.
 But some are things that you may very well wish to type or swipe, especially in a particular context (illegal immigrant, NBA surveillance, etc.), and the refusal of the iOS keyboard to complete swiping entry or suggest the natural next word is one reason users are frustrated with it.
In addition to the phrases that are blocked from the suggested word area or swiping, there is a mechanism to prevent the autocomplete and suggested word list from suggesting 
individual words
. The iOS dictionary labels words as either vulgar, offensive, or sensitive. Vulgar words are not suggested until the user manually types them three times. Offensive words are never suggested or completed by swiping (though you can always type them out yourself). Sensitive words are similarly never offered as a prediction or completion, and some of them can’t be swiped.
Why does Apple do all this? It’s likely less about policing your speech than it is about avoiding lawsuits and bad PR. All it takes is one person posting a screenshot of the iOS keyboard suggesting the word “good” after they type “The Nazis were” or “real” after Santa isn’t” (both included in the list) to cause a bad viral moment criticizing Apple’s intentions, even if the keyboard suggestions would only suggest things that the user had typed several times before.
However, it’s still overreaching. A good solution would be to give users a choice. Allow a keyboard setting, enabled by default, to moderate the suggested word list and swipe keyboard to avoid offensive content, but also give users the ability to turn it off. But until then, you’ll need to type a little more slowly if you want to write certain phrases.

===== https://www.digitaltrends.com/phones/apples-iphone-keyboard-has-a-hidden-list-of-words-it-refuses-to-suggest-and-some-entries-are-surprising/

Chinese smartphone brands have often found themselves in hot water over restrictions on what you can do or type on them, thanks to built-in, system-level text filtering and censorship capabilities. Even third-party apps such as LINE have had to follow these censorship rules and block hundreds of words and phrases deemed sensitive or offensive in China, even though they are perfectly normal elsewhere. 
It appears 
Apple
 also maintains such a list of filtered words, and tens of thousands of them are baked into its iPhone keyboard at the system level. However, these words and phrases are not outright blocked. You can still type them, but the autocomplete and swipe-typing systems automatically ignore them, so they never appear among the suggested words above the keyboard.
Michi NekoMichi
What exactly is Apple hiding in its iPhone keyboard?
Independent researcher Derik Kauffman detailed the discovery (via 
Swipe Blocked
). A majority of the words are the usual suspects, related to violence, drugs, and overtly sexual themes. But a few other items are going to raise censorship concerns. For example, phrases like “dictator Trump” and “sleepy Joe,” both widely used in public political commentary, are on the keyboard’s gray list. 
Recommended Videos
In a statement shared with 
Reuters
, Apple did not offer a detailed explanation and only said the iPhone’s built-in keyboard is designed to avoid suggesting words that could be offensive or inappropriate.
But this odd behavior isn’t limited to words or themes related to the US. The predictive typing system in the iOS keyboard also blocks terms like “Tibetan independence” and “United Ireland,” as well as phrases that mock politicians, including Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Leader of the Opposition Rahul Gandhi. Kauffman says that every major language supported by the 
iOS
 keyboard has some version of this invisible gray list, which Apple has never disclosed publicly.
Joe Maring / Digital Trends
How did researchers uncover Apple’s hidden word list?
Kauffman says he first suspected that such a gray list existed when he tried to swipe-type phrases like “bad boy” and “white man.” He then used 
OpenAI’s Codex
 and Anthropic’s Claude Code to dig into iOS, and eventually discovered thousands of words and phrases on the gray list.
Reuters’ own testing found that when you try to swipe-type “Tibetan independence” and “United Ireland,” the keyboard often changes them to “Tibetan independences” and “United Iceland.” Likewise, swipe-typing “white man” yields “white mean,” while “red man” and “blue man” go through without a problem.
The discovery is surprising, although not entirely shocking. Over the past few years, users have reported scattered instances of trying to type a word, only for the predictive system to refuse to comply. David Greene, a lawyer at the digital rights group Electronic Frontier Foundation, told Reuters that part of him found the list “creepy,” even though he could see the case for curbing inflammatory suggestions.

What the keyboard does
Predictive text never offers a phrase on the list, even when it's the obvious next word.
Swipe typing won't produce most of them either. The keyboard picks another word, sometimes an implausible one, and can change a word you already entered.
Typing letter by letter always works. Nothing on the list stops you writing it.
The rules match exact word sequences. A blocked phrase can go through with a slightly different wording.
Some phrases are blocked only in suggestions and can still be swiped. They're marked Swipe works below. Separately, single words Apple labels offensive are never suggested, and vulgar ones only after you've typed them three times; see 
Word labels
