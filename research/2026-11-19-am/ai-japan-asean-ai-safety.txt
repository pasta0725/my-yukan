"In order to address new vulnerabilities associated with rapid development of AI, Japan-ASEAN AI Safety and Security Partnership was just launched," Sanae Takaichi said during her opening speech at the ASEAN-Japan Summit. 
Under its three pillars of ASEAN-Japan Comprehensive Strategic Plane, Sanae Takaichi shared that Japan will promote the JAPAN-ASEAN AI co-creation initiative.

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Tuesday called for stronger economic, security, and people-to-people cooperation with Southeast Asia, citing India’s Act East policy as a key driver of closer ties during the 23rd ASEAN-India Summit in Manila.
Modi said India and ASEAN, which together account for about a quarter of the world’s population, could help promote regional peace and stability amid global uncertainties.
“Today, what the world needs most is peace, stability and predictability,” he said.
Since 2014, India has undertaken more than 100 initiatives to strengthen people-to-people relations, launched over 250 quick-impact projects and provided more than 10,000 skills development scholarships.
Modi also cited the alignment between ASEAN Community Vision 2045 and India’s Viksit Bharat initiative, which aims to make India a developed country by 2047.
The summit was among a series of meetings in Manila where ASEAN and its major dialogue partners pursued closer cooperation in trade, energy, technology and regional security.
Korea FTA
At the 27th ASEAN-Republic of Korea Summit on Monday, South Korean President Lee Jae Myung confirmed that negotiations had begun to upgrade the ASEAN-Korea Free Trade Area, a nearly two decade-old agreement intended to strengthen trade ties and respond to changes in the global economy.

November 18, 2026
MANILA 
– Japan will partner with Association of Southeast Asian Nations (Asean) member states to ensure that the impacts of artificial intelligence (AI) on the region’s economies will be addressed, Japanese Prime Minister Takaichi Sanae said on Tuesday.
At the 29th Asean-Japan summit at the Philippine International Convention Center (PICC), held as part of the 49th Asean Summit and Related Summits hosted by the Philippines, Takaichi said that through the Japan-Asean AI Safety and Security Partnership, Japan and the regional bloc can jointly explore solutions to “new vulnerabilities associated with the rapid development of AI.”
“Japan will continue to support enhancement of the maritime safety and security capabilities of Asean countries, and combat organized fraud,” she said.
Tokyo will also “promote the Japan-Asean AI cocreation initiative and FOIP (free and open Indo-Pacific) digital corridor concept, and enhance the region’s resilience to prevent disasters by supporting the Asean Coordinating Centre for Humanitarian Assistance,” she added.
“While the international order is experiencing a period of great instability,” Takaichi said, “Japan will play an ever more proactive role to maintain and strengthen international order based on freedom, openness, diversity, inclusiveness, and the rule of law under the updated FOIP.”
Regional cooperation
Speaking as this year’s Asean chair, President Marcos said the group is looking forward to working more closely with Japan, especially in the field of AI.
“In 2023, we elevated our ties to a comprehensive strategic partnership and adopted the joint vision statement on Asean-Japan friendship and cooperation. Together, these set out our ambitions for the next generations. As partners for peace and stability, Asean values Japan’s support for Asean Centrality and the [AOIP],” said Marcos.
Takaichi also said the Asean-Japan Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement upgrade would be reviewed, a move that Marcos said would help “deepen our economic integration.”
Oil supply disruption
On the energy front, the Philippines and Japan are pushing for regional cooperation to address oil supply disruptions, including coordinated oil releases, storage arrangements, and information-sharing among Asian countries.
The two leaders raised the proposals during the 4th Asia Zero Emission Community (Azec) Summit on Tuesday.
At the meeting, Takaichi reiterated her proposal for the creation of an Asia Oil Security Task Force to establish rules for emergency oil releases and transfers.
She said the task force, with a hub proposed to be built in Japan, will be Asia’s version of the International Energy Agency.
“I also wish to propose an initiative for cooperation on oil supply in Asia through the use of idle tanks which can coordinate with the Philippines-led joint stockpiling scheme,” Takaichi said in her opening remarks.
Nexus for producers
The Japanese leader said the proposals would form part of efforts to evolve Azec into Azec 2.0, which would help strengthen regional energy resilience while advancing economic growth and decarbonization.
“Japan will serve as a nexus connecting oil-producing countries in Asia,” she added.
Marcos lauded Azec’s progress in working toward the goal of regional oil stockpiling and greater energy cooperation.
“Disruptions along critical supply routes, such as the Strait of Hormuz, never stay local. They reverberate across global supply chains,” he noted, stressing that oil stockpiling must be complemented by closer cooperation between oil-producing and oil-consuming countries, including information-sharing and practical storage arrangements.
“Neither buyers nor sellers benefit from volatility. Energy resilience is a shared global good that demands our collective commitment,” he said.

On November 17, commencing at 11:50 a.m. local time (12:50 p.m. JST) for approximately 1 hour, the 29th ASEAN-Japan Summit was held in Manila, the capital of the Philippines. Ms. TAKAICHI Sanae, Prime Minister of Japan, attended the meeting.
1. ASEAN-Japan cooperation
The meeting began with an opening remark by Ferdinand Romualdez Marcos, Jr., President of the Republic of the Philippines and chairman of the meeting. Prime Minister Takaichi expressed her appreciation for the efforts of President Marcos and Prime Minister Lawrence Wong of Singapore, the Country Coordinator for ASEAN-Japan. She then stated as follows:
While the international order is experiencing a period of great instability, Japan will play a more proactive role to maintain and strengthen the international order based on "freedom," "openness," "diversity," "inclusiveness," and "the rule of law."
Respecting ASEAN Centrality and unity and working hand in hand with ASEAN countries, which are the key to realizing FOIP, Japan aims to enhance the resilience of countries and their freedom to decide for themselves, while promoting synergies with the "ASEAN Outlook on the Indo-Pacific (AOIP)," and to become "more resilient and prosperous together."
It is very pleasing to adopt the joint statement on the realization of regional economic resilience today. Under "Partnership On Wide Energy and Resources Resilience Asia (POWERR Asia)," Japan will promote economic and energy resilience and will also strengthen regional supply chains, including critical minerals, to address challenges such as arbitrary export restrictions.
To address new vulnerabilities associated with the rapid advancement of AI, Japan will work together with ASEAN to explore solutions to pressing challenges under the newly launched "Japan-ASEAN AI Safety and Security Partnership."
Japan is also committed to advancing discussions toward strengthening the ASEAN-Japan Comprehensive Economic Partnership (AJCEP) Agreement.
Prime Minister Takaichi then shared with the ASEAN leaders the progress made in cooperation aligned with the three pillars outlined in the 2023 Joint Vision Statement for the 50th Year of ASEAN-Japan Friendship and Cooperation as follows:
“Partners for Peace and Stability”
- Japan will continue supporting the strengthening of marine law enforcement and maritime security capabilities of ASEAN countries.
- We will continue providing assistance through ASEANAPOL and other channels to counter organized crimes such as online scrams.
“Partners for Co-creation of Economy and Society of the Future”
- We will promote the “Japan-ASEAN Co-creation Initiative for Artificial Intelligence (AI)”.
- We will promote the “FOIP Digital Corridor Initiative.”
- We will strengthen regional disaster resilience through support to the ASEAN Coordinating Centre for Humanitarian Assistance on Disaster Management (AHA Centre).
“Heart-to-Heart Partners across Generations”
- We will continue youth exchanges through programs such as JENESYS and the Ship for Southeast Asian and Japanese Youth Program, co-create new innovations through scientific and technological cooperation under the Networked Exchange, United Strength for Stronger Partnerships between Japan and ASEAN (NEXUS).
The ASEAN Member States successively welcomed her attendance at the ASEAN-Japan Summit.
		They also praised Japan's cooperation in various fields. They highly valued Japan's cooperation, noting the common principles and synergies between the AOIP and the FOIP. They welcomed the adoption of the "Joint Statement of the 29th ASEAN-Japan Summit on Realization of Economic Resilience."
		Furthermore, they expressed high expectations for strengthening economic and economic security partnerships through the ASEAN-Japan Comprehensive Economic Partnership (AJCEP) and others.
		Additionally, they expressed high regard and expectations for Japan's cooperation in cultural and youth exchanges, education, and science and technology. This includes the Japan-ASEAN Co-creation Initiative for AI, FOIP Digital Corridor Initiative, JENESYS, the Ship for Southeast Asian Youth Program, MEXT Scholarship Program, and Sakura Science Program Exchange Program.
2. Regional and International Issues
The ASEAN Member States stated about the South China Sea, the importance of peaceful settlement of disputes in accordance with the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS), the importance of denuclearization of North Korea and the situation in the Middle East.
In response, Prime Minister Takaichi made the following remarks:
General Principles
- Attempts to unilaterally change the status quo by force or coercion must not be tolerated anywhere in the world.
The East China Sea, the South China Sea, Taiwan
- In the areas surrounding Japan, including the East China Sea, activities that infringe upon Japan's sovereignty as well as military activities continue and are intensifying. In the South China Sea, militarization and coercive activities also continue and are intensifying, causing serious concern.
- We strongly expect that the Code of Conduct in the South China Sea (COC) will be in accordance with UNCLOS and respect the legitimate rights and interests of all stakeholders using the South China Sea.
- Peace and stability across the Taiwan Strait are important for the entire international community.
North Korea
- North Korea's nuclear and missile development is unacceptable. Japan wishes to closely cooperate toward the complete denuclearization of North Korea.
- Malicious cyber activities by North Korea cannot be overlooked.
- The abductions issue is an ongoing humanitarian matter. Japan asks for continued understanding and cooperation from the ASEAN countries.
Situation in Myanmar
- To improve the situation in Myanmar, efforts contributing to the cessation of violence, political progress, and improvement of people’s lives are indispensable. Japan will support ASEAN's efforts.
Closing remarks
Japan and ASEAN becoming “more resilient and prosperous together” is of great importance.
Next year marks the milestone of the 60th anniversary of the founding of ASEAN as well as the 50th anniversary of Japan’s three principles on its policy toward Southeast Asia, the so-called “Fukuda Doctrine.”
Japan will continue to build a peaceful and prosperous future together with ASEAN countries as a “trusted partner” with “heart-to-heart” connections.
3. Adoption of Joint Statement of the 29th ASEAN-Japan Summit 
The joint statement on realizing economic resilience was adopted.
