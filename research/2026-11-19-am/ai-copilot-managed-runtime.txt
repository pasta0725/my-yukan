    Ignite 2026: Microsoft's Copilot story for Dynamics 365, Salesforce evolves for partnership, competition
                by 
        Jason
        Gumpert
                Editor,
        MSDynamicsWorld.com
November 17 2026
Microsoft is advancing its Copilot story, with new GA and preview plans announced today at Ignite 2026. 
Copilot Home is “where Chat and Cowork come together”, explained Microsoft’s Jared Spataro, and the experience is now generally available. Copilot Managed Runtime for application hosting and management is also GA, and Copilot Code will reach GA in the coming weeks. 
Copilot Home is “your new starting point in Copilot,” Spataro explained. It’s intended to be the place where you can view recent activity and get suggestions about what to do with Copilot. And Microsoft is introducing a new experience in Copilot called Today, which they describe as “a command center across mail, calendar, Teams threads, meetings, and tasks.” “Today” is in private preview in the Copilot app and Outlook. 
Microsoft also announced that the "Dynamics 365 in Copilot" initiative is adding plugins for D365 Sales, Customer Service, and ERP applications. There are currently 57 CRM skills, 39 finance and operations skills available via the plugins, with more on the way, 
according to Jeff Teper
, Microsoft EVP for Apps and Agents. Initial releases of the Sales, Customer Service, and ERP plugins are 
already GA
. 
Microsoft Contact Center Intelligence, which provides agents for voice and digital channels on top of contact center platforms, has reached GA.
Dynamics 365 Activate
, the AI-based toolset for accelerating a migration to Dynamics 365 and originally known as 
Project Mia
, is already GA for D365 Customer Experience apps. Activate has now reached private preview for D365 ERP, Teper announced, and will be focused first on SAP ECC. Organizations running SAP ECC can work with their Microsoft partner to use Dynamics 365 Activate to analyze processes, configurations, customizations, and dependencies to surface migration gaps and scope their project, according to Microsoft. Early results have shown it can shorten implementation lifecycles, according to partners cited by Microsoft.
On the CRM side, Salesforce migrations are the first target scenario for D365 Activate. 
On the cooperative side of the Salesforce relationship, Microsoft today 
announced Salesforce in Copilot
, a plugin with over 38 prebuilt sales skills. Salesforce will also gain new integrations for Teams and Fabric.

Microsoft makes Copilot Home generally available as Autopilot rolls out
Copilot Home and Office in Copilot are generally available, while Autopilot has begun rolling out to Frontier customers. Microsoft also says Code will become generally available in the coming weeks and Copilot Managed Runtime is now generally available.

Microsoft
 has made Copilot Home and Office in Copilot generally available, bringing chat, delegated tasks and Office editing together in its workplace AI app. Autopilot, an agent designed to continue working without a prompt, also began rolling out to Frontier customers on Nov. 17. Code is expected to become generally available in the coming weeks, while Copilot Managed Runtime is now generally available.
At Microsoft Ignite, Chairman and CEO Satya Nadella delivered the opening keynote. In an edited version of his remarks, he said, “Copilot is the new OS for work.” Microsoft describes Copilot as bringing Chat, Cowork, Code and Autopilot together in one experience, spanning knowledge work, coding and business processes. Jared Spataro, Microsoft's chief marketing officer for Copilot, wrote, “Code will become generally available in the coming weeks, while Copilot Managed Runtime for securely hosting and managing apps is now generally available.”
A shared starting point
Microsoft introduced the features in September, when it said Home and Code would begin rolling out through its Frontier program and Autopilot would expand to private preview. Autopilot was previously called Scout.
Home combines Chat, for questions and conversational work, with Cowork, for tasks a user delegates. Users can review recent activity and pick up work in progress; Office in Copilot lets them create and edit Word documents, Excel workbooks and PowerPoint presentations in the app.
For more complex requests, Auto may recommend moving a task from Chat to Cowork; the user can agree to the transfer. A separate feature called Today, which surfaces items across mail, calendar, Teams threads, meetings and tasks, is in private preview in the Copilot app and Outlook.
Agents and shared work
Microsoft says Autopilot can watch Teams channels, follow up on emails, run recurring work and manage projects without waiting for a prompt. It operates within an organization's tenant and has its own identity, memory and tools.
Each Autopilot has a Microsoft Entra Agent ID and a named manager. IT can limit its access or block it, and its sign-ins and actions are logged under its own name.
Microsoft also made Workspaces available in Frontier. An evolution of Copilot Notebooks, it is intended to let people and agents work toward a shared goal using common context, with files and conversations accessible in the Copilot app.
Building apps and managing costs
Code, built on the same underlying technology as 
GitHub
 Copilot, lets users describe an app, dashboard or other solution in natural language. Copilot Managed Runtime, the infrastructure for hosting and managing those apps in an organization's tenant, is now generally available; Code itself is not yet generally available.
Microsoft's September post said Cowork, Code and Autopilot run on usage-based billing, while a user subscription license covers Copilot Chat and its use across Office apps. Microsoft also described tools for administrators to manage spending policies and for users to view credit usage and balances.
Microsoft also announced updated Dynamics 365 plugins with more than 100 prebuilt skills for work including sales, service and finance. A 
Salesforce
 plugin brings more than 38 prebuilt sales skills into Copilot. The Ignite announcement also noted new plugins and skills for sales, service, finance and legal teams, along with an expanded Agent 365 platform for managing intelligence spending.

Copilot Managed Runtime Blog 
5 MIN READ
Copilot Managed Runtime is now generally available, with additional new preview features
DavidBlyth
Microsoft
Nov 17, 2026
AI is making it easier to turn ideas into working apps in Copilot Cowork, Copilot Code, Copilot Studio and the dev tools you already use. Running those apps for work still requires security, governance and ongoing operations. In September we introduced Microsoft Copilot Managed Runtime in public preview to bring those requirements together.
Today at Microsoft Ignite, we’re announcing that Microsoft Copilot Managed Runtime is generally available and beginning to roll out to tenants. Copilot Managed Runtime brings Microsoft-operated hosting together with identity, governed data access, and app lifecycle management, giving teams the foundation for turning the apps they create into managed apps they can use for enterprise work and IT can trust
.
Build in the tools that fit your team
Makers can start in Copilot Code, Copilot Cowork, Copilot Studio, GitHub Copilot, VS Code or third-party development platforms and tools. Copilot Managed Runtime provides Microsoft-managed hosting and execution for published apps across these paths. That gives teams room to choose how they build, and open collaboration opportunities across products all while bringing their published apps onto a common operating foundation.
“AI agents can now build many of the web apps enterprise teams need, and many organizations want those apps to run in their own environment. With Copilot Managed Runtime, apps built with Manus will run in an organization’s own Microsoft environment and automatically inherit existing security and compliance policies, giving teams the capability and speed of an AI agent with enterprise-grade control.” 
Tao Zhang, Co-founder and Chief Product Officer at Manus
Microsoft operates the platform, hosting, scaling and availability are handled for you, with no cloud services to configure, so teams can focus on the app and the work it needs to do. Entra sign-in and sharing make it easier to put that app in colleagues’ hands. Governed connections give the app access to the organizational data and services it needs within the boundaries set by IT. The governance controls exist in the platform to allow IT to federate and scale development and operations, all guided by IT.
Regardless of the entry point, Copilot or third-party tools, the code stays editable and Git-backed. This allows makers to keep iterating on the app, while the published app stays live, then promote changes when they're ready. Organizations using GitHub Enterprise can connect their own repositories, so apps follow the same branching, review and release practices as the rest of their software
.
Give IT one place to see and govern every app
For apps on Copilot Managed Runtime, Entra identity, sharing and access sit alongside policy enforcement and audit logging. Organizational policies govern connector, data and endpoint access. These capabilities bring the operating requirements of an app into its managed foundation, so makers and IT can work from the same boundaries.
App inventory and monitoring in Microsoft 365 admin center give administrators a central view of the apps teams have published. That visibility helps IT oversee apps created through different tools without organizing its inventory around each authoring experience. Makers retain flexibility in how they create, and administrators have a common place to see and manage those apps
.
Give apps a place for their own business records
Moving onto our preview features, some apps also need to keep data of their own, such as the owners, dates and status updates behind a team’s launch tracker. A new frontier public preview of Microsoft Fabric integration adds an out-of-the-box option for persistent app storage and backend capabilities for those business records; this is in addition to existing connectivity with SharePoint and Dataverse. Teams can choose this data layer when an app needs it, alongside the hosting and execution provided by Copilot Managed Runtime.
Now supporting agents and additional artifacts
Create Copilot Studio agents and workflows locally
Now in public preview, Copilot Managed Runtime dev tooling will allow users to build custom Copilot Studio agents and workflows locally, make modifications with their developer tool of choice and then publish to Copilot Studio using the CLI. This enables makers and developers using Copilot Studio to easily work with other members of their team, some who may be using the browser based UX or those working locally on their machine.
Developers can continue using their developer tools and pushing changes directly from their machine, working together on a single source of truth integrated into their existing version control platforms such as GitHub. For organizations like Qualcomm, this approach brings together familiar developer workflows with new ways to build and manage agents
:
"What excites us about the CLI is bringing code-first development to Copilot Studio. For Qualcomm users and engineers, the combination of the CLI and coding agents offers a fast and natural way to build and test agents, while also supporting more mature ALM practices and complementing the Copilot Studio UI experience. Together, these approaches give teams the flexibility to build and manage agents in the way that works best for them” 
- Paulo Joaquim, IT Architect, Principal, Qualcomm Incorporated
Get started with the CLI here: 
https://aka.ms/msagent-cli
Create custom agents in code
For makers and developers who want to author an agent directly in code, we’re introducing Code Agents in early-access preview (this will require signing up and being accepted). Coming soon, these custom agents are created and hosted on Copilot Managed Runtime, with a choice of model, orchestrator or harness within the preview’s supported scope. This lets teams reuse the AI stack, developer tools and workflows they already have, while Microsoft provides hosting, Microsoft Entra identity, channels, monitoring and lifecycle management. Customers and partners can sign up at 
aka.ms/cmrcodeagents-signup
. 
From idea to the show floor
Consider an event team creating a show-floor operations app in Copilot Cowork. In this illustrative scenario, an interactive 3D map shows booth readiness and open help requests. The team reviews and publishes the app for colleagues with Microsoft Entra identity access, governed connections, IT control and hosting all through Copilot Managed Runtime.
The team chooses to use the new Fabric integration to store the app's records. A developer could clone the code locally with Git and improve it using their preferred tools while colleagues continue using the published app.
For an agent project, the developer could edit Copilot Studio agent definitions locally, keep changes in source control and push the updated project back to Copilot Studio, keeping the agent and associated workflows together.
These paths support collaboration across Copilot Code, Copilot Cowork, Copilot Studio and third-party tools as teams create, publish and maintain solutions
.
Start with the artifact your team needs today
Start with a business process your team wants to improve. Consider who will use the artifact, what information it needs, and how your team will maintain it. Choose the authoring experience that fits the work, then use Copilot Managed Runtime to bring the result to your colleagues, with a foundation for building more connected solutions over time.
Already have an app created and want to use it with your enterprise business? Then use Copilot Managed Runtime and check out the Copilot Managed Runtime CLI to get started: 
https://aka.ms/cmrrepo
General availability makes Copilot Managed Runtime a foundation for the apps teams put to work. The new previews expand the options for local agent development and custom app data. Together, these steps give makers more ways to create useful tools, collaborate more than ever, all while keeping app and agent operations secured and governed for your enterprise.
