Sales reps can pull Clay's prospect data into ChatGPT. Unmentioned: the plugin also works in Claude, and OpenAI's own sales team uses Clay.
OpenAI published a customer story on 24 November 2026 about Clay, a sales data company whose plugins put its prospect research inside Codex and ChatGPT. Clay runs on OpenAI's GPT-6 models, and the story is OpenAI's own marketing, with no figures attached.
For anyone in sales, the point is where the work happens. A rep who already has ChatGPT open can ask about an account and get Clay's data back in the chat, instead of switching to a separate research tool.
Clay builds lists of companies and people, then fills in each record with details such as work emails, phone numbers and recent company news. Its research agent is called Claygent. Sales teams call this enrichment, and it is what Clay's data credits pay for.
In the story, Clay's head of AI, Jeff Barg, describes the ChatGPT plugin as "a big success". He says reps mix data from their own systems with public datasets to work out how to pitch a customer. The Codex plugin also lets the people who build those sales workflows create and run them.
Which OpenAI models Clay uses
Barg says GPT-6 Sol is Clay's main model, with GPT-6 Astra for harder tasks and GPT-6 Luna for cost-sensitive work. He calls Sol "a really great model for go-to-market plays". Clay switches between them through OpenAI's Responses API, one interface for every price point.
The spread is wide. On OpenAI's API pricing page, read on 25 November, GPT-6 Astra costs $50 per million output tokens, GPT-6 Sol $10 and GPT-6 Luna 50 cents. Adding it up, a task sent to Astra costs about 100 times what Luna would charge for the same output.
The page also lists newer versions. GPT-6.1 Sol has the same $10 output price as GPT-6 Sol, and GPT-6.2 Astra is cheaper than GPT-6 Astra at $40 per million output tokens. The story does not say whether Clay has moved to them.
Barg also uses Astra on his own code. He says it now writes a feature, adds tests, runs them and records the result for review, much as 
Greptile described using Astra to test apps like a person would
. Next, Clay is working on an agent that would show a product to a prospect, and Barg sees OpenAI's GPT-Live-1 as a way to use Clay and Codex by voice.
What OpenAI's story leaves out
We checked Clay's own pages, and the plugin is not an OpenAI exclusive. Clay's plugin repository on GitHub offers the same plugin for Claude Code, Codex and Cursor. Its MCP page, the connector that links Clay to chat assistants, invites reps to prompt "ChatGPT, Codex or Claude".
The relationship also runs both ways. Clay's customer page says OpenAI's own sales and revenue teams use Clay, and credits it with raising OpenAI's lead-enrichment coverage from the low 40% range to the high 80s. The figure is on Clay's page, and OpenAI's story does not mention it.
On Clay's pricing page, the rep connector is included on every plan, the free one too. The free plan caps searches through Clay's command-line tool at 100 results every 30 days, against a million or more on paid plans, so a real sales team will be paying.
Our view
This is a vendor story, and it reads like one. It has quotes and no numbers: no count of reps using the ChatGPT plugin, no time saved, no deals won. Treat it as a description of what Clay offers, not evidence it works.
Still, the direction is clear. Sales research is moving into the chat window, as with 
the dots agent that drafted proposals for Intentful
 and 
Merge's CEO having Astra suggest dinner guests and email them
. If your company already pays for Clay, ask your sales operations team whether the connector is switched on, and what it is allowed to write back to the CRM, the customer database.
There is a privacy side too. The example on Clay's GitHub page asks an agent for the work emails of 20 fintech chief executives, and shows all 20 found. Nothing there suggests the people on such lists are asked. Expect more cold emails that know a little too much about you.
The next thing to watch is the prospect-facing agent Barg describes. If Clay ships it, a buyer could end up hearing a product pitch from a voice model that has already researched them.
