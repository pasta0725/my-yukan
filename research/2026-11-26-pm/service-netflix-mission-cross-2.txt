Mission: Cross 2 teaser is out now, and Netflix has locked a Christmas Day premiere for the Korean action sequel. The official teaser landed today, 26 November 2026, on the Netflix K-Content YouTube channel, confirming that the film will stream from 25 December 2026 only on Netflix. For fans who enjoyed the first film’s mix of marriage comedy and high-stakes action, the Mission: Cross 2 teaser is a clear promise that the househusband agent and his detective wife are being pulled back into danger, this time on a mission ordered from the very top.
Image Credit: Netflix K-Content — Official Trailer/Poster via YouTube
Mission: Cross 2 teaser confirms Christmas Day premiere
The biggest news in the Mission: Cross 2 teaser is the date. Netflix’s own announcement published today is headlined as the film returning on December 25, and the teaser description carries the same message, calling it a Netflix film coming December 25 only on Netflix. That makes Mission: Cross 2 a Christmas Day release, one of the most valuable slots in the streaming calendar, and it ends months of shifting windows for the sequel.
The film was first placed in Netflix’s third-quarter 2026 Korean slate earlier this year, then reported as delayed into December, before today’s announcement finally fixed 25 December 2026. The Mission: Cross 2 teaser itself runs under a minute, but it packs in the new premise quickly, showing Kang-mu back in action gear, fights staged around cultural artefacts, and the couple operating as a team rather than hiding secrets from each other. It is a short first look, yet it answers the two questions viewers care about most, when the film arrives and what the new mission involves.

What the new mission is about
This time, the mission centres on a stolen national treasure. According to Netflix, just as life returns to normal for former agent-turned-househusband Kang-mu and his detective wife Mi-seon, a secret mission hits them, recover a lost Joseon royal treasure. The President personally calls on the couple after a shadowy organisation steals a national treasure, described in the new announcement around a bronze Pensive Bodhisattva statue and Joseon royal treasure.
The goal is bigger than recovering one object. The couple must stop Korea’s cultural heritage artefacts from being smuggled abroad and illicitly exported, which gives the sequel a treasure-hunt flavour on top of its action comedy roots. The Mission: Cross 2 teaser leans into that idea, with glimpses of Kang-mu rappelling in an apron and clashes fought amid artefacts with shields and bows, a playful visual contrast between domestic life and the violent world he keeps getting dragged back into. Netflix describes this as their most dangerous mission yet, which sets expectations for larger set pieces than the first film.
Hwang Jung-min and Yum Jung-ah lead the cast
Hwang Jung-min returns as Kang-mu, full name Park Kang-mu, the former special operations agent who traded missions for housework. Yum Jung-ah is back as Mi-seon, the former Asian Games shooting silver medallist who became a Violent Crimes detective. In the sequel, Mi-seon has been demoted to a seaside provincial station, a detail that should give the couple’s dynamic a fresh comic edge even as the stakes rise.
The returning supporting team includes Jung Man-sik as Sang-woong, Mi-seon’s colleague and Violent Crimes Unit leader, along with Cha Rae-hyung as Heon-gi and Lee Ho-cheol as Dong-su, both Violent Crimes detectives. New faces raise the threat level. Yoon Kyung-ho plays Cheon In-hak, head of the shadowy organisation plotting to seize cultural treasures, while Im Seong-jae plays Duchil, a black-market dealer described as a connoisseur of the tomb-raiding world.
Cha In-pyo appears as the President who enlists the couple, and Kim Kuk-hee plays the President’s Chief of Staff. That mix of familiar faces and new antagonists is exactly what a sequel teaser needs to sell, continuity for returning viewers and a bigger villain for the new story.
How the sequel connects to the first film
The first Mission: Cross, released on Netflix in 2024, built its hook on a marriage secret. Kang-mu was a former secret agent living quietly as a househusband, hiding his past from Mi-seon, who worked as a detective in the violent crimes unit. Netflix summed it up as the ultimate test of marriage, an agent-turned-househusband tangled in a perilous mission with his detective wife, who was clueless about his past. The comedy came from the double life, and the action came from the past catching up with him.
Mission: Cross 2 starts from a different place. The secret is out, life has returned to normal, and the couple are now recruited together. That shift matters because it lets the sequel play as a true team mission rather than repeating the hiding-the-truth formula. If you are catching up before December, the first film is still streaming on Netflix, and our recent coverage of other Netflix premieres, including

Director, runtime and production details
Lee Myung-hoon returns as writer-director, having directed and written the first film as well. Netflix credits him as directed and written once again by Lee Myung-hoon, and trade coverage from Variety noted the same return when the sequel was announced. Keeping the same writer-director is a good sign for tone, since the first film’s balance of domestic comedy and action was its main selling point.
The sequel runs 113 minutes, just under two hours, and sits in Netflix’s action space with Korean, crime, comedy and action and adventure labels, plus treasure hunt and adventure descriptors. It is a South Korean production in Korean, rated U/A 16+ on Netflix in India, and produced by Sanai Pictures, Plus M Entertainment and Obra Creative, presented by Netflix.
Filming wrapped earlier this year after production began in late October 2025, which explains how the film could move from a third-quarter plan into a December slot without losing its Christmas positioning. Dub and subtitle language lists for the sequel have not been announced on the title page yet, so viewers should check the Netflix page closer to release for their preferred language options.
