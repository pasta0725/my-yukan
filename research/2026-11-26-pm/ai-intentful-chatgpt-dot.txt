Intentful AI dots in daily work
On 24 November 2026, OpenAI News published a customer story about Intentful AI dots. AI means artificial intelligence. It says Marina Petrova, the company's chief executive and cofounder, uses a dot, an AI assistant named Aria. The dot handles research, proposals and routine messages.
Intentful is a small team that helps brands with interactive marketing. It also advises marketers on AI. Petrova said the dot gave her more time for strategic work. The story reflects Intentful's own account.
Intentful works with destination organisations and local firms such as hotels, restaurants and attractions. A tailored proposal is part of how the company wins work. It shows prospects what is possible and what delivery would require.
From many websites to a proposal
For one tourism client, Petrova needed to price a package for hundreds of local businesses. She first had to identify which websites could be included. OpenAI News says one rule was that a site's sitemap should list no more than 3,000 pages. A sitemap is a list of pages on a website.
Petrova gave Aria a spreadsheet of URLs and selection criteria. Aria then helped review Intentful's sales records through ChatGPT plugins. Plugins are add-ons that connect to other services. These included past deals and notes in HubSpot, a sales platform. The aim was to find earlier prices and context for the next year.
The two built a pricing model. Page counts set the tier, while earlier agreements shaped the price. Aria then produced a short proposal for Petrova to review and edit. OpenAI News says this took hours rather than days.
Messages, tasks and team updates
Petrova also uses Aria with Gmail and Slack plugins inside ChatGPT. The dot helps her review conversations, locate work that is still outstanding, and understand where colleagues need input. It can highlight messages that require her attention.
OpenAI News says Aria once noticed a reply Petrova had been waiting for. The reply included an invitation to a call that morning. Petrova can also ask Aria to discuss ideas, search conversations, check a customer's record in the CRM, or create and assign tasks in Asana. A CRM is software for managing customer records.
The dot works inside tools the team already uses. When Aria writes a draft, Petrova can leave comments in Google Docs. For longer documents, she can ask for suggesting mode, which shows proposed edits. Aria also saw new slides added by a colleague and refreshed the speaker notes on its own. It then told Petrova.
Petrova decides what Aria may see. She also checks email before it goes out. She often briefs Aria by phone while travelling.
Teaching and controlling AI use
Petrova used Aria to prepare an AI basics session about agents. Agents are AI systems that can carry out tasks. OpenAI News says the process showed the lesson she wanted to teach. The steps were to delegate a real task, provide context, challenge the result and keep control of final output.
The dot also helped after the session. It had already shared a training file when a participant asked for access. After a session for 50 marketers, Aria spotted a software fault that stopped people opening a recording. It raised the issue and offered to fix it before the link was shared.
The story shows how AI assistants can support small teams. It also shows the need for review. Petrova keeps the final decision on messages and access. That makes the dot a helper rather than an unsupervised decision maker.
