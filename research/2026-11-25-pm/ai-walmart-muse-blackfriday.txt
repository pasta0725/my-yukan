Walmart will now let Meta’s Muse shop on your behalf, allowing the AI agent to find and purchase Black Friday deals via Walmart’s Sparky AI assistant. The retail giant is rolling out a live implementation of the 
Agent Gateway it announced last month
, which helps AI agents find relevant products for you without sharing your purchase history, according to Walmart.

AI agents are ready to shop. Some retailers are wary
Muse
 can make purchases through Walmart’s “Agent Profiles,” which provide a dedicated space for agent activity within your Walmart customer account. These profiles also allow you to set spending limits, approve purchases, and check the purchase history of everything bought by Muse. While other online retailers like 
Amazon
 and 
eBay
 have blocked AI agents from browsing their platforms, Walmart says that support for Meta’s Muse is “just the beginning,” and that additional Walmart shopping experiences for other AI agents and apps are “on the way.”
Here are Walmart’s instructions for shopping its website via Muse:
1. Tell Muse what you need.
 For example, ask it to find a great TV deal or show you which Walmart Black Friday deals are available for delivery. Muse can help you explore products and build your basket.
2. Set your shopping rules.
 Muse will take you to Walmart to sign in, then bring you back to Muse. The first time you add an item to your cart, you’ll set up your Agent Profile on Walmart within your Walmart account, including a spending limit you’re comfortable with.
3. Check out.
 Complete your purchase within the conversation using a card saved in your Walmart account, with the same Walmart prices you’d find on Walmart.com. And if something isn’t quite right, Walmart’s return policy still applies.
Update, November 23rd:
 Walmart informed The Verge after publication that the Agent Gateway launch has been delayed until November 24th.
Update, November 23rd:
 Walmart contacted The Verge again to say the update is no longer being delayed, and will roll out today on November 23rd.

Walmart Opens Black Friday Shopping to Meta’s AI Agent                    
By
PYMNTS
 |

Walmart
 says its customers can now use 
Meta
’s AI agent, Muse, to find Black Friday deals.
The offering, powered by the Walmart Agent Gateway, is designed to combine product discovery and purchasing into “one experience,” the retail giant said in a Tuesday (Nov. 24) 
news release
.
“Imagine 
asking Muse
 to find a Black Friday TV deal under $500, track down the season’s hottest toys or help you check off everyone on your holiday gift list,” the release said.
“Instead of searching for each item yourself, Muse connects to the Walmart Agent Gateway, where it passes your shopping list to Walmart. Walmart then does the shopping, finding products that match your request.”
According to the release, Muse can find relevant products from Walmart without sharing customers’ larger purchase history with others. The company’s 
Agent Profiles
 function lets shoppers “set spending limits, approve purchases and check your purchase history to see which purchases were made by your agent.”
Walmart notes that it’s also made some enhancements to its in-house artificial intelligence (AI) assistant 
Sparky
, which can now turn a “handwritten list into a shopping cart” and use “images and voice to find what you need.”
PYMNTS wrote earlier this month that while companies like 
Amazon
 and Walmart have 
stretched the concept
 of Black Friday beyond its traditional Thanksgiving weekend spot, consumers will continue to spend heavily during this period.
Adobe’s figures for the 2025 holiday season placed online Black Friday purchases in the United States at $11.8 billion, a 9.1% increase from the prior year. 
Cyber Monday
 sales reached $14.25 billion, holding the title for the year’s biggest online shopping day.
While Walmart teams with one of the world’s biggest tech companies to boost holiday shopping, 
small and medium-sized businesses
 (SMBs) could have a tougher time ahead of them this year heading into the shopping weekend, as PYMNTS wrote Tuesday.
“A national retailer can 
potentially withstand
 a disappointing promotional campaign, negotiate favorable supplier terms and finance inventory relatively cheaply. An independent merchant or small SMB chain may need the proceeds from Black Friday to finance the following week’s operations,” that report said.
“That distinction is turning holiday shopping into a 
working capital
 management exercise as much as a customer acquisition opportunity. For Main Street, the challenge is determining which innovations improve the economics of selling and which simply make it easier to spend money pursuing sales.”

How Muse shops through Walmart
Walmart says customers can use Muse to find Black Friday products, add items to a basket and complete checkout within the agent experience. Customers sign into their Walmart account and pay with a saved card. Before shopping, they can set a spending limit for the agent and review what it has purchased.
The retailer says the prices and return policies remain those of Walmart. Muse does not become an independent retailer simply because it provides the interface. The shopper's established relationship with Walmart is still involved in the transaction.
The distinction is useful for understanding what has actually changed. In October, Walmart described plans for agent-specific controls and a gateway that would give external software a direct route into its commerce infrastructure. The new update is a live shopping use case for that infrastructure, rather than another announcement about the planned architecture.
Walmart adds controls for AI shopping agents
Walmart is creating separate profiles, permissions and a gateway for AI shopping agents, turning customer control into retail infrastructure.

Why the gateway matters to retail marketers
Retail marketing teams typically optimize the path from discovery to a product detail page, a cart and then checkout. An AI agent can collapse several of those interactions into one conversation. That could alter which product attributes are important during a recommendation, and which customer interactions a retailer or its suppliers can observe.
Walmart's announcement describes shopping and purchasing capabilities. It does not disclose a sponsored product format in Muse or promise that marketers can buy placement in its recommendations. Brands should treat the integration as a new shopping interface, not automatically as a new media inventory source.
There are practical implications even without paid placements. Availability, accurate product descriptions, current pricing and fulfillment information may become more valuable inputs when software assembles a basket on a shopper's behalf. Product teams that maintain those inputs have a role in the marketing funnel alongside creative and media teams.
A different approach to agent access
Walmart's strategy differs from Amazon's approach to third-party access. Amazon has challenged Meta's Muse shopping access, while Shopify has brought Shop Pay checkout to Muse. These approaches illustrate that the underlying commercial relationship depends on the permissions and infrastructure each retailer or platform chooses to offer.
The Agent Gateway offers Walmart a way to permit external shopping while retaining its own account, spending controls and order policies. For brand partners, the key question is where the retailer's control ends and the agent's role begins. An integration can provide a route to checkout without necessarily sharing the same visibility into consumer intent as a retailer's own interface.
Walmart has indicated that further agent experiences are on the way. The extent of those integrations, their availability and their commercial terms remain to be established. The Black Friday Muse use case is a concrete implementation, but not evidence that every shopping agent can transact at Walmart.
What brands should measure next
Start by separating discovery and order measurement. A team can ask its retail partner whether agent-originated sessions or orders can be identified, how returned and canceled purchases are treated, and whether existing retail media reporting distinguishes this route from Walmart's own app and website.
Marketers should avoid assuming that a new shopping interface automatically improves conversion or creates incremental sales. Some orders may simply shift from another route the consumer would have used anyway. The useful comparison is the incremental contribution of agent-enabled journeys, not just gross orders attributed to them.
Walmart has not released transaction volume, conversion performance or an attribution methodology for this launch. Until such evidence becomes available, the sensible work is to maintain reliable catalog data, review retailer reporting capabilities and test hypotheses about customer demand without assigning an unproven value to the channel.
The timing is significant because Black Friday offers a real purchasing context for agent-enabled shopping. Whether the new workflow changes marketing budgets will depend on evidence about adoption, incremental sales and how retailers report the resulting transactions.

Black Friday shopping has come a long way from lining up outside stores before sunrise. This year, there’s a new way to shop Walmart’s deals: Put an AI agent to work.
Starting today, customers can shop Walmart’s Black Friday deals directly through 
Muse
, Meta’s personal AI agent, which can help them find items, build a basket and complete their purchase.
Powered by the 
Walmart Agent Gateway
, this new Walmart shopping experience is a big step forward for shopping with AI, bringing product discovery and purchasing in one experience.
Your shopping list. Your Muse. Your rules.
Imagine asking Muse to find a Black Friday TV deal under $500, track down the season’s hottest toys or help you check off everyone on your holiday gift list. Instead of searching for each item yourself, Muse connects to the Walmart Agent Gateway, where it passes your shopping list to Walmart. Walmart then does the shopping, finding products that match your request.
You’ll find Walmart’s Every Day Low Prices and Black Friday deals, along with the membership benefits you’re used to, like free delivery.
The Walmart Agent Gateway lets you decide what your agent can and can’t do.
For more than 60 years, Walmart has worked to earn customers’ trust by helping them save money and live better. The Walmart Agent Gateway brings that same commitment to AI shopping, helping agents find relevant Walmart products without sharing customers’ broader purchase history with others.
And with Agent Profiles, you’re in control. You can set spending limits, approve purchases and check your purchase history to see which purchases were made by your agent.
How to shop Walmart with Muse — including our Black Friday Deals
Tell Muse what you need.

Set your shopping rules.

Check out.

Black Friday is changing. Walmart is ready.
Customers have more ways to shop Black Friday than ever before. Some love browsing the aisles. Others prefer searching Walmart.com, asking Sparky for help or putting an agent like Muse to work. This year, 
Sparky can do even more for shoppers

However customers choose to shop, they can count on the same great prices, assortment, convenience and trusted experience they expect from Walmart.
And Muse is just the beginning. The Walmart Agent Gateway will make it possible to shop Walmart through more apps and agents, with additional experiences on the way.
This Black Friday, you can hunt for the deals in stores and online, as well as with Sparky, Muse and agents. Either way, Walmart is ready to help you save.
