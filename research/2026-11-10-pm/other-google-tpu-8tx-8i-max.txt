We first designed TPUs more than a decade ago motivated by Google’s internal demands, including voice recognition and language translation. Eight generations of TPUs later, the use cases have exploded, covering: i) training and serving our own Gemini models, ii) agentic workloads, iii) delivering services to Google Cloud customers on our cloud infrastructure, and most recently, iv) allowing customers to deploy TPUs directly into their own data centers. TPUs are now a rapidly growing business with nearly 100 customers — including frontier labs, trading firms, retailers, universities, and more.
At Google Cloud Next in April, we 
introduced
 two new specialized TPUs in the same generation — 8t for large-scale training and 8i for real-time reasoning. TPU 8t and 8i offer up to 2.7 times better training performance and 80% better inference price performance, respectively, versus our previous generation of TPUs.
Today, we’re taking that a step further, expanding our eighth-generation TPU portfolio with two additional systems: TPU 8i Max for massive context windows and the highest compute density per rack, and TPU 8tx for secure and confidential inference. This is the first time we have introduced four TPU systems in a single year.
Our expanded eighth-generation TPU portfolio: TPU 8t, TPU 8i, TPU 8tx, and TPU 8i Max.
While we’ve always co-designed our TPUs in partnership with Google DeepMind, we’re now increasingly getting feedback from external organizations on their specific needs. With computing moving from human to agent scale, businesses are asking for accelerators that can reason across AI models’ massive context windows. In addition, some highly regulated customers are asking for specialized encryption capabilities for unique workloads.
The new TPU systems we are announcing today address this new reality. Coupled with 
our recent PyTorch announcements
, our aim is to make TPUs more easily accessible to every developer and Cloud customer. By combining modular custom silicon with an open and easy-to-use software stack, we can deliver new hardware and system configurations at the pace of the AI industry.
Building the computer around the chip
Today, we’re announcing two new TPU systems — and there’s an important difference between a new chip and a new system.
Designing a new chip means laying out billions of microscopic transistors on a silicon wafer in a multi-year process bound by the laws of physics and a complex, long-duration manufacturing lifecycle. A system, on the other hand, is about how we bring together the compute, networking, and storage around the chip. This can include how they are packaged with the chips, to quickly meet emerging customer needs and research use cases.
When we built our very first TPU more than a decade ago, we knew AI software would evolve much faster than silicon manufacturing ever could. So we made a foundational bet on system balance and modularity, architecting our TPUs as building blocks as part of our 
AI Hypercomputer
, so engineers could rapidly adjust packaging, high-speed memory, optical networking, and security around them. That approach allows us to deliver specialized supercomputers in a matter of months rather than years.
Specializing for secure, high-context AI
Each system in our eighth-generation TPU portfolio targets a distinct part of the AI lifecycle.
 TPU 8t and TPU 8i established the baseline for this generation of chips:
TPU 8t was designed to be a training powerhouse for massive foundation models with maximum “goodput” — or the useful intelligence delivered per watt of energy used.
TPU 8i is the reasoning engine, designed to break the memory wall, cutting inter-chip communication delays by up to half.
Now, as enterprises bring autonomous agents into production, we have created two new configurations of these foundational systems to solve two urgent new challenges: hardware-level data security and million-token context scale.
TPU 8tx
TPU 8tx, our third eighth-generation system, is optimized for secure and confidential inference and is also great for small-scale training. It’s built for regulated enterprises and governments running frontier or proprietary models and autonomous agents alongside their most sensitive data.
Normally, data is encrypted when stored or sent over a network, but has to be unlocked in memory while a computer processes it. Built on the same silicon as TPU 8t and paired with 5th Generation AMD EPYC CPU hosts, TPU 8tx builds security directly into the hardware layer, keeping sensitive data and model weights protected and isolated even while in active use. And by adopting the same low-latency Boardfly network we created for real-time reasoning, TPU 8tx keeps multi-step agents moving, delivering up to 2x lower latency compared to TPU 8t with a near-zero performance penalty.
Pairing training-grade compute with a fast inference network also means organizations can simultaneously run and customize their models on a single system. TPU 8tx enables teams to fine-tune models using their private data, distill large models into smaller ones, and train their agents through reinforcement learning, all without needing to migrate sensitive data between separate clusters.
Designed for sensitive enterprise workloads, TPU 8tx provides hardware-level confidential compute for private fine-tuning and secure agent execution.
TPU 8i Max
Our fourth system, TPU 8i Max, is built for a different problem: the memory wall.
When dozens of AI agents work together across an entire software codebase or hours of video at once, memory bandwidth quickly becomes the bottleneck. So we took two low-latency TPU 8i chips and combined them onto a single package.
TPU 8i Max scales compute and memory in lockstep, delivering double the compute, double the SRAM and double the HBM bandwidth of TPU 8i, so processors remain equipped with essential data and so that large workloads do not stall. Further, because TPU 8i Max fits twice the silicon within the existing rack footprint, data centers can deploy significantly more computing power per square foot and per watt.
The dual-chip TPU 8i Max system, engineered for massive context windows and maximum compute density.
While flexible across workloads, together the four systems in our eighth-generation TPUs give customers a purpose-built accelerator for every stage of the agentic lifecycle. TPU 8t for scale-up training, TPU 8i for low-latency reasoning, TPU 8tx for confidential computing, and TPU 8i Max for massive context windows and the highest compute density per rack.
Opening up TPUs to every developer
When we launched the first TPU more than a decade ago, there were only a handful of companies running workloads that needed the processing power of a TPU. The use cases were specialized, so engineers would use optimized frameworks and hand-tuned code.
Over the years, as AI has advanced, more and more companies began using accelerators. Now, AI adoption is at a point where TPUs are excellent for a wide range of companies and use cases, and as a result, we’ve been working systematically to remove barriers to adoption so that TPUs are as easy-to-use as general-purpose hardware.
This investment comes together today across these key areas:
Opening up TPU development to every PyTorch developer; no code rewrites needed:
 Last month, we open-sourced 
TorchTPU
, so PyTorch code and Hugging Face models now run natively on TPUs, alongside out-of-the-box TPU backends for leading open-source inference engines like vLLM and SGLang. And to make hardware optimization effortless, our new open-source 
TPU agent suite
 (now in preview) plugs directly into coding tools like Claude Code, Cursor, and Google Antigravity to automate TPU onboarding in minutes, diagnose bottlenecks, and autotune custom kernels using plain language. For production deployments, our new open-source 
XLA Serving Engine
 lets you train models in Python and serve them directly in a low-latency C++ runtime across TPUs, GPUs, and CPUs.
Industry-leading production performance on InferenceX and AgentX benchmarks:
 In production, what really matters is outcomes on real AI workloads rather than theoretical peak chip speeds. On industry-standard InferenceX and AgentX 
benchmarks
, which test realistic agentic coding and long-context work, our native vLLM-TorchTPU software stack on TPUs achieved state-of-the-art price-performance marks. 
New evaluations
 demonstrate these advantages extend across a wide range of frontier open models and open-source serving stacks while maintaining high model accuracy. Upcoming software optimizations on this same hardware will drive those results even further.
Eliminating idle compute waste for autonomous agents:
 Because AI agents are stateful, they frequently pause for seconds or hours while waiting on a database lookup, an external software tool, or human feedback. To make our agents more resource-efficient during those pauses, our new
 Agent Compute Engine
 software automatically suspends and resumes idle sessions, cutting operational infrastructure costs.
Breaking through the memory wall with the model-state layer (TPU Sync):
 Building the computer around the chip also means unifying memory, storage, and networking in software. As agents reason across million-token contexts and continuous reinforcement learning (RL) loops, memory quickly becomes the bottleneck. We’re open-sourcing 
TPU Sync
, the model-state layer for TPU clusters. TPU Sync turns chip HBM, host DRAM, shared storage, and the data center network into a single, co-designed memory hierarchy — automatically tiering massive KV caches off the chip in milliseconds, streaming context across disaggregated prefill and decode nodes, and synchronizing RL weights to cut KV transfer latency and Time to First Token (TTFT) by more than 90%.
Scaling distributed workloads across TPUs from a single controller:
 Now 
generally available
 on Google Kubernetes Engine (GKE),
 Cloud Pathways
 brings the Google DeepMind runtime behind Gemini to every Google Cloud customer. Using a single-controller architecture and
 pathwaysutils
, you can orchestrate large-scale TPU systems across multiple slices as if they were one local machine for pre-training, reinforcement learning, and multihost inference. Cloud Pathways keeps long-running jobs moving through hardware disruptions with colocated Python sidecars, sub-minute slice hot-swapping, and elastic resharding.
Stitching custom TPU topologies on-demand with GKE dynamic slicing:
 Now generally available,
 GKE dynamic slicing
 decouples physical hardware provisioning from logical slice formation using optical circuit switching (OCS) technology. By dynamically assembling TPU cubes into larger topologies (super-slicing) or partitioning them into smaller, isolated workloads (sub-slicing) on-the-fly, this update completely eliminates rigid, manual nodepool recreation cycles. This topology agility slashes job startup times by up to 5x and accelerates fault recovery by up to 4.5x, maximizing overall fleet utilization and workload goodput across all TPUs.
Co-designing with a decade of experience
Shipping four TPU systems and a unified, open software stack in a single year only happens when silicon architects, networking engineers, compiler teams, and Google DeepMind researchers build side-by-side.
Increasingly, we’re even tapping into AI to ideate and build the next generation of AI infrastructure. From using Google DeepMind’s 
AlphaChip
 reinforcement learning system to using Gemini to deliver chip layouts in hours rather than weeks, we’re using TPU-powered AI services to rapidly accelerate next-generation TPU hardware development. This is true co-design and a unique Google differentiator.

                Google specializes TPUs for agentic, confidential computing
Google customized the TPU 8i Max to increase rack density and the TPU 8tx to ensure enterprise data sovereignty.

Extending its eighth generation of TPUs, Google has added two new variants, the 8i Max and 8tx, which specialize respectively in agentic computing and confidential computing. 
Google created its 
TPU
 (Tensor Processing Unit) line of hardware accelerators to meet the specific demands of its AI work and, increasingly, that of its cloud customers. Each new generation of TPUs has addressed the particular bottlenecks for AI at the time.
The latest designs optimize not just the chips but also the CPUs, memory, networking and shared storage around them for specific AI workloads. 
“[AI] used to be mostly about how much compute you had available, but now it's also about how quickly data can move, how efficiently you can use the memory, how do you minimize latency, and how you manage the cost and security of running all these workloads at scale,” said 
Mark Lohmeyer
, Google Cloud’s vice president and general manager for AI and computing infrastructure, in a press briefing last week.
A TPU for agentic work
Google designed the TPU 8i Max for supporting massive context windows required for agentic work. 
“
With roughly two-thirds of AI compute now going to inference, specializing [in] inference hardware is the right call,” wrote Patrick Moorhead, founder, CEO and chief analyst of 
Moor Insights & Strategy, in an email
.
Google designed the 8i Max to give agents as much memory as possible, mostly by adding a second TPU onto a multi-chip package. A group of AI agents may work together on tasks such as developing software or creating a video. Thanks to a two-chip design, the 8i Max gets 768 MB of on-chip static RAM and 17.2 TB/s of high-bandwidth memory (HBM) bandwidth.
When used in large numbers, these TPUs can also 
maximize the amount of compute
 per square foot of the data center.
Many TPUs can collectively work on bigger problems. New software Google recently open-sourced, called 
TPU Sync
, orchestrates chip HBM, host DRAM, shared storage and the data center network into a single memory hierarchy.
Securing the workload
The TPU 8tx focuses on secure and confidential inference. 
Secure AI computing
 is one emerging class of workloads the company is finding from customers. Google built the TPU 8tx for sensitive government work and enterprises in regulated industries.  
“A bank or government agency can fine-tune, distill and run reinforcement learning on the same confidential system that serves the model, without moving sensitive data between clusters,” Moorhead wrote.
TPU 8tx enables teams to fine-tune models using their private data, distill large models into smaller ones, and train their agents through reinforcement learning, all without migrating sensitive data between separate clusters, according to a 
blog posted Monday
 by Amin Vahdat, senior vice president and chief technologist of AI and infrastructure at Google.
Data and model weights remain encrypted and isolated at the hardware level during processing, with a near-zero performance penalty and half the latency than the TPU 8t, Lohmeyer said during the briefing.  
An increasingly diverse user base
As with previous generations, Google designed the TPUs with the input of the Google DeepMind research lab. But with each generation, the company is building a base of outside users to consult with, both from its cloud service and channel sales. 
More than 100 customers and partners – including frontier labs, trading firms, retailers and universities – are now using TPUs, according to the company. One is Anthropic, which will 
expand its use
 of 
next-generation TPUs beginning in 2027
. The California Institute of Technology has shifted some of its own cancer research 
over to Google Cloud TPUs
 from its own HPC system, Moorhead learned in a conversation with the team in charge of that operation. 
Google did not provide any availability dates and pricing for the TPU 8i Max and TPU 8tx. 
For Moorhead, these new TPUs represent a turning point in the direction of AI computing. No longer is the GPU the only focal point. 
“Agents put the CPU and host platform back at the center,” Moorhead wrote. 
Hardware specialization
Google revealed the first batch of 
eighth generation TPUs
 in April, with two variants, still due to go online by the end of this year. The 8t variant is for large-scale training, and the 8i is for inferencing. This generation of TPUs was the first to bifurcate the AI workload into two separate specialties, each with its own modifications to hardware, Lohmeyer said. 
Key to the TPU architecture has always been a rack-level design, rather than just innovating at the chip level itself. The eighth generation takes that approach further, tailoring the whole system to different AI workloads. The hardware specialization allows the 8t to offer up to 2.7 times better training performance and the 8i to provide 80% better inference price-performance compared to previous TPU generations, according to Vahdat.
"You can't optimize that infrastructure just by focusing on the chip,” Lohmeyer said. 
The new TPUs continue this design practice, addressing the current bottlenecks of AI operations.
Freelance news writer Joab Jackson has been writing about back-end IT technologies for the past three decades. His grandfather programmed mainframes, and his father wrote computer games for hobbyist programming magazines in the 1980s.

Google Cloudは11月9日（米国時間）、第8世代TPUに新しいシステムを2つ追加すると発表しました。機密データを扱う推論向けの「TPU 8tx」と、長いコンテキストを扱うAIエージェント向けの「TPU 8i Max」です。4月に発表した学習向けの「TPU 8t」、推論向けの「TPU 8i」に続くTPUです。なおいずれも提供開始時期や価格などは明らかにされていません。
TPU 8tx：使用中のデータを守る
データは保存中や通信中なら暗号化できますが、計算するときにはメモリ上で復号するため、これまでは計算中のデータは暗号化できていませんでした。今回のTPU 8txは、暗号化の仕組みをソフトウェアではなくチップ側に持たせることでこの問題に対処します。
チップ性能は4月に発表したTPU 8tと同じもので、FP4（4ビット浮動小数点）で12.6PFLOPSの演算性能と216GBの広帯域メモリを備えます。発表によると、1）ホストとTPUを結ぶPCIe接続の暗号化、2）このTPUとホストが正規のハードウェアとソフトウェアで動いているということの証明、3）メモリの中に外から読めない囲いを作る仕組み（エンクレーブ）を、ハードウェアで実現すると説明しました。独自のLLMや社内データを計算中も外から見えない状態で動かせるということです。
TPU 8i Max：エージェントを同時にたくさん動かすためのメモリ
100万トークン級のコンテキストを持つエージェントを並列に動かすと、計算速度よりもメモリの帯域がボトルネックになります。その場合、コンテキストを削るか、同時に動かすエージェントの数を減らすか、遅延を受け入れるかのどれかを選ぶしかありません。
TPU 8i Maxは、TPU 8iのチップ2つを1つのパッケージにまとめた構成です。広帯域メモリは384GBで、TPU 8i（288GB）より33%多くなります。メモリの読み書き速度は17.2TB/sでTPU 8iの2倍、オンチップSRAMは768MBでこちらも同2倍。演算性能もTPU 8iの2倍です。ただし、1つのエージェントがトークンを生成する速さは変わりません。増えた計算力とメモリは、同時に動かせるエージェントの数を増やすことに使われます。Googleはこれを「制限速度は変えずに車線を増やす」とたとえています。今のラックと同じ設置面積に2倍のシリコンを載せられるため、床面積あたり・電力あたりの計算能力も上がります。
ソフトウェアも同時に発表
TPUの分散実行基盤「Cloud Pathways」をコンテナ管理サービスのGKE（Google Kubernetes Engine）で一般提供し、光回線スイッチでTPUの割り当てを組み替える「GKE動的スライシング」も一般提供を始めました。動的スライシングはジョブの起動時間を最大5分の1に縮め、障害からの復旧を最大4.5倍速くするとしています。待機中のエージェントのKVキャッシュをチップの外へ退避させる「TPU Sync」もオープンソースで公開しました。KVキャッシュの転送遅延と、最初のトークンが出るまでの時間（TTFT）を90%以上減らすとしています。

Google has 
expanded
 its eighth-generation Tensor Processing Unit portfolio with TPU 8tx and TPU 8i Max, two systems aimed at confidential AI workloads and memory-intensive agents. The additions bring the generation to four systems, alongside TPU 8t for large-scale training and TPU 8i for low-latency reasoning. Google has not specified pricing, supported regions, or a general availability date for either new system.
The key distinction is that TPU 8tx and TPU 8i Max are new system configurations built around existing silicon. 
Google
 says its modular architecture lets teams change packaging, memory, networking, and security without waiting for a full chip development cycle. TPU 8tx uses TPU 8t silicon, while TPU 8i Max combines two TPU 8i chips in a single package.
Google TPU 8tx/8i Max, image source - Google
TPU 8tx targets regulated enterprises and governments running models and autonomous agents against sensitive information. It pairs TPU 8t silicon with custom AMD CPU hosts and adds hardware-level confidential computing to protect data and model weights during processing. This extends protection into active computation, beyond encryption during storage and network transmission.
The system also adopts the Boardfly network developed for TPU 8i. Google claims the combination delivers up to twice the performance of TPU 8t, with a near-zero performance penalty for its security capabilities. Alongside inference, TPU 8tx supports smaller training workloads, private fine-tuning, model distillation, and reinforcement learning. Organizations can perform these tasks on one system without transferring sensitive data between separate clusters.
TPU 8i Max addresses the memory demands of agents processing large codebases, lengthy videos, and million-token contexts. According to Google, its dual-chip package provides twice the compute, SRAM, and high-bandwidth memory bandwidth of TPU 8i. The company also says it places twice the silicon within the existing rack footprint, increasing compute density.
For comparison, Google’s previously published TPU 8i specifications list 288 GB of high-bandwidth memory, 384 MB of on-chip SRAM, and 8,601 GB/s of memory bandwidth. The new announcement specifies doubled SRAM and bandwidth for TPU 8i Max but does not provide an absolute HBM capacity figure for the new package.
Google is pairing the hardware expansion with software intended to make TPUs accessible through existing development tools. TorchTPU supports native PyTorch execution, with TPU backends for vLLM and SGLang. A TPU agent suite, available in preview, connects to Claude Code, Cursor, and Google Antigravity to assist with onboarding, performance diagnosis, and custom kernel tuning. Google also announced an open-source XLA Serving Engine for serving models through a low-latency C++ runtime across TPUs, GPUs, and CPUs.

Another component, Agent Compute Engine, suspends idle agent sessions and moves their working memory off the accelerator in milliseconds, Google says. It targets workloads that spend extended periods waiting for tools, database responses, or human input, leaving accelerator resources occupied between computation steps.
TPU Sync handles a related problem at the memory layer. Google describes it as a system connecting accelerator HBM, host DRAM, shared storage, and networking into a coordinated hierarchy. It moves KV caches, the stored attention state used during model inference, between memory tiers. It also transfers context between separate prompt-processing and token-generation nodes and synchronizes weights for reinforcement learning. Google claims reductions of more than 90% in KV transfer latency and time to first token, although the supplied announcement does not detail the test conditions.
For cluster orchestration, Google says Cloud Pathways is now generally available on Google Kubernetes Engine. It brings the runtime used behind Gemini to Cloud customers, allowing multiple TPU slices to be managed through a single controller for pre-training, reinforcement learning, and inference. Its recovery features include replacing slices in under a minute and redistributing workloads across available hardware.
GKE dynamic slicing is also generally available, according to the announcement. It uses optical circuit switching to assemble TPU resources into larger configurations or divide them into smaller workloads. Google reports up to five times faster job startup and up to 4.5 times faster fault recovery. The company also claims leading price-performance results on InferenceX and AgentX using its native vLLM-TorchTPU stack, though it did not include numerical results in the supplied text.
Google
 developed its first TPUs more than a decade ago for internal workloads such as voice recognition and translation. The accelerators now support Gemini training and serving, Google Cloud workloads, and customer data center deployments. According to Amin Vahdat, Google’s chief technologist and senior vice president for AI and Infrastructure, the TPU business has nearly 100 customers across frontier AI labs, financial firms, retailers, and universities. The expanded portfolio reflects that broader customer base, with separate configurations for training, reasoning, confidential computation, and large-context processing.
