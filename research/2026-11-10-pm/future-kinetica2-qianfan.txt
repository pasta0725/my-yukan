China’s Kinetica 2 rocket sends 18 satellites into orbit
 By Zhao Lei
A Kinetica 2 rocket lifts off from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center in northwestern China on Nov 3, 2026. (PROVIDED TO CHINA DAILY) 
CAS Space, a major Chinese commercial space company, conducted the second flight of its Kinetica 2 rocket on Tuesday morning, sending 18 satellites into orbit.
The 53-meter-tall rocket lifted off at 8:15 am from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center in northwestern China. After a brief flight, it reached outer space and deployed the 18 satellites, which make up the 21st group of space-based components of the Spacesail Constellation, into their designated orbits, according to the Beijing-headquartered company.
ALSO READ: 
China's CERES-1 rocket launches new satellite from sea
The mission was the 18th orbital launch conducted by CAS Space, which was established by the Chinese Academy of Sciences. It also marked the first time the company's rocket had been used to deploy a large group of satellites for China's internet satellite network.
The Kinetica 2 is a medium-lift, liquid-fueled rocket and the second launch vehicle developed by CAS Space, following the Kinetica 1. It is the company's first liquid-propellant rocket and is designed to support China's large-scale satellite deployment and low-cost cargo transportation to the Chinese space station.
READ MORE: 
China's Zhuque-2E rocket launches 10 satellites into space
The rocket consists of a multistage core booster, which has a diameter of 3.35 meters, and two side boosters, each also 3.35m in diameter. Its liftoff weight is 625 metric tons, and its maximum thrust is 753 tons. The rocket can transport spacecraft with a combined weight of 8 tons to a typical sun-synchronous orbit at an altitude of 500 kilometers, or 12 tons to a low Earth orbit 200 km above Earth.
To date, CAS Space has used its rockets to send 139 satellites and an experimental cargo spacecraft into orbit, with a combined payload weight of more than 27 metric tons, the company said. It claimed to rank first among Chinese commercial rocket contractors in terms of the number of satellites launched and total payload weight.

Kinetica 2、G60星座向け18機打ち上げに成功
2026年11月3日（火）
出典: 
Kinetica 1 / CAS Space
Kinetica 2、2機目の飛行でG60衛星18機の軌道投入に成功
2026年11月3日00時15分（UTC）、CAS SpaceのKinetica 2が酒泉衛星発射センターから打ち上げられ、SpaceSail Polar Group #17ミッションを成功裏に完了した。Kinetica 2としては通算2回目の打ち上げとなる。
Kinetica 2はG60星座の構築を担うミッションとして18機の低軌道通信衛星を搭載。ペイロード投入完了をもって打ち上げ成功が確認された。
18機の通信衛星、Ku・Q・Vバンドを搭載
今回投入された18機はShanghai Spacesail Technologies（上海格思航天）が運用するG60星座向けの衛星群で、Ku帯、Q帯、V帯の通信ペイロードを搭載する。G60星座は上海市政府の資金支援を受けたプロジェクトであり、SpaceSail Polar Group #17はその構築フライトの一環として位置づけられる。
Ku帯は従来の衛星通信帯域として実績があり、Q帯・V帯は高周波数による大容量通信に対応する。3つの帯域を並行して搭載することで、ユーザー向けサービスと地上局間のフィーダリンクを柔軟に構成できる。
2027年までに1296機、長期目標は1万2000機
G60星座の初期コンステレーションは2027年末までに1296機の体制を目指している。最終的には1万2000機規模への拡張が計画されており、SpaceXのStarlinkやAmazon Project Kuiperと同じ超大型LEOメガコンステレーションとして国際的な通信市場に参入する構想だ。
今回のSpaceSail Polar Group #17は極軌道投入フライトであり、地球全体をカバーする軌道面の構築に貢献する。Kinetica 2は複数衛星を一括投入できる中型ロケットとして、CAS Spaceがこのコンステレーション構築の主要な打ち上げ手段として活用していくとみられる。2回目のフライトで本番ミッションを成功させたことは、同ロケットの信頼性確立に向けた重要な実績となった。

CAS Space flies Kinetica 2 rocket for second time with 18 Spacesail satellites
by Clarence Oxford
Jiuquan, China (SPX) Nov 04, 2026
SPX
CAS Space flew its Kinetica 2 rocket for the second time on Tuesday, 3 November 2026, launching 18 satellites for the Spacesail Constellation from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center in northwestern China, the state-run China Daily reported.
The 53-meter rocket lifted off at 8:15 am. According to the company, it deployed all 18 satellites into their designated orbits, and they make up the 21st group of the Spacesail Constellation.
China Daily said it was the 18th orbital launch by CAS Space, a Beijing-based commercial launch company established by the Chinese Academy of Sciences. The newspaper also said it was the first time one of the company's rockets had deployed a large group of satellites for China's internet satellite network.
Kinetica 2 is a medium-lift rocket and the company's second launch vehicle, after the Kinetica 1. It is also the company's first rocket to run on liquid propellant. According to China Daily, it is designed to support China's large-scale satellite deployment and low-cost cargo transport to the Chinese space station.
The rocket is built around a multistage core booster 3.35 meters in diameter, with two side boosters of the same width attached to it. It weighs 625 metric tons at liftoff and produces a maximum thrust of 753 tons.
China Daily gave the rocket's stated capability as 8 tons to a typical sun-synchronous orbit at 500 kilometers, or 12 tons to low Earth orbit 200 km up. Those figures describe what the rocket is designed to lift. This flight did not demonstrate them.
CAS Space says its rockets have sent 139 satellites and an experimental cargo spacecraft into orbit, with a combined payload of more than 27 metric tons. The company claims to rank first among Chinese commercial rocket makers by number of satellites launched and by total payload weight.
