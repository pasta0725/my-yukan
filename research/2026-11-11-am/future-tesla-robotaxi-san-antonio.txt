Tesla launched unsupervised Robotaxi service in San Antonio, Texas, on November 9, 2026, with the first rides beginning at 5 a.m. local time.
The company’s official Robotaxi account on X posted the announcement just after midnight, sharing a map that outlines a large operational zone covering much of the city. The geofence is roughly 274 to 275 square miles, making it 
Tesla’s largest day-one Robotaxi service area to date
.

The zone centers on downtown, covers nearly everything inside Loop 410, and extends north to Loop 1604 between Interstate 10 and U.S. 281, with further reach west past State Highway 151, east beyond I-35, and south toward I-37. This area encompasses about one million residents and hundreds of thousands of jobs.

San Antonio International Airport lies inside the geofence, though Tesla is not currently offering airport pickups or drop-offs. This hopefully will happen soon, as ride-sharing to and from the airport can be expensive.
The launch makes 
San Antonio Tesla’s eighth unsupervised Robotaxi city
 and its fifth in Texas, joining Austin, Houston, Dallas, and Fort Worth. Florida markets in Miami, Orlando, and Tampa are also live.

Service uses modified Model Y vehicles operating without a safety driver in the vehicle, consistent with recent unsupervised expansions. 
Cybercab two-seaters remain limited
 primarily to Austin for now. It’s worth noting that Dept. of Transportation officials in Texas have already ok’d the Cybercab’s operation in Austin, and one official called it “a tangible example of how quickly our transportation system is evolving.”
Advertisement
-
San Antonio was previously listed among near-term targets in Tesla’s October 2026 Robotaxi safety materials, alongside Las Vegas, Phoenix, and Jacksonville. The rapid Texas rollout follows Fort Worth’s unsupervised debut on November 2 and builds on Austin’s original launch in June 2025.
The 274-square-mile debut remains substantially larger than Waymo’s local footprint and marks continued progress in Tesla’s unsupervised autonomy efforts after starting the year with no such rides. The company has stated ambitions for broader 24/7 operations by the end of 2026 as vehicle counts and software reliability improve.

Tesla may one day use lasers instead of wiper blades to keep its robotaxi cameras clear. Lars Moravy, Tesla’s Vice President of Vehicle Engineering, floated the idea in a new episode of Jay Leno’s Garage released Monday, where he and design chief Franz von Holzhausen walked Leno through the Model Y L.
“If you fire them in a focused area, you can clean the sheet of water and create a barrier,” 
Moravy said
. “When you think about autonomy, it might be a good solution to only clean the area where the camera is.” He added that clearing an entire windshield would take too many lasers to be practical.

The comment brings back one of Tesla’s stranger patents. The company filed for “Pulsed Laser Cleaning of Debris Accumulated on Glass Articles in Vehicles and Photovoltaic Assemblies” in May 2019, and the U.S. Patent Office 
granted it in September 2021
. An updated divisional version was granted on March 31, 2026. The system detects debris on the glass, identifies what it is, and pulses the laser at a calibrated rate so the beam never penetrates deeper than the glass itself. The filing names windshields, side windows, camera lenses and solar panels as possible targets.
Lasers were once rumored for the Cybertruck, which appeared at its November 2019 unveiling with no wipers at all. Tesla eventually settled on a single oversized blade, and Elon Musk admitted the wiper was the part of the truck that 
troubled him the most
. That production line is now scheduled to shut down around mid 2027 to make room for more Cybercab output.

What has changed since then is who is in the vehicle. A privately owned car has a driver who can notice a smeared windshield and do something about it. A Cybercab heading to its next pickup has nobody on board to check. Tesla has been chipping away at that problem with conventional hardware. Model Y Robotaxis in Austin were spotted with washers on their side repeater cameras in January, and a lens cleaning patent 
granted in May
 describes a fluid and wiper assembly for vehicle cameras and Optimus’ eyes. A laser would remove the fluid and the blades, both of which need regular replacement across a fleet.
The same episode gave Leno his first ride on Full Self-Driving (Supervised). The Model Y L pulled over on its own when an ambulance approached with its sirens on. “We got it on self-driving mode! I’m not touching anything,” Leno said. Moravy and von Holzhausen also explained that owners remain responsible while using FSD (Supervised), while Tesla takes responsibility for Robotaxi rides because it acts as the operator. Tesla 
launched the Model Y L in the U.S. in July
 at $61,990.
Moravy gave no timeline, and he described the laser setup as something Tesla is considering rather than a confirmed feature. The fleet it would serve keeps getting bigger, though. Tesla 
launched unsupervised Robotaxi service in San Antonio
 on Monday, its eighth city, and expanded its Austin service area to roughly 443 square miles on the same day. Several of those markets, including Houston, Miami and Tampa, see heavy seasonal rain.

Tesla begins groundworks on $10B ‘Project Crystal Sun’ in Texas

on

By
Joey Klender
Tesla has started to perform earthworks engineering on the roughly 3,000-acre site in Fort Bend County, Texas, advancing the 
massive $10 billion Project Crystal Sun
 plan to build a vertically integrated solar manufacturing complex.
Flyover drone footage from S.E. Robinson, Jr. released this morning shows the location under development. Tesla has crews on site performing large clearings, but you can also see equipment staging areas, the use of an existing homestead, and active leveling across a substantial portion of the property.
Tesla has started construction on Project Crystal Sun, its $10B vertically integrated solar factory near Houston, Texas.
The plant will make everything from silicon ingots and wafers to finished cells and panels, with operations targeted for Q1 2029.
@SERobinsonJr with a great flyover of the facility:
— TESLARATI (@Teslarati) 
November 9, 2026
Public filings first submitted in July under Texas’s Jobs, Energy, Technology and Innovation Act describe a plant that would manufacture the full solar chain: everything from silicon ingots, wafers, coated cells, and finished modules.
Tesla projects 9,712 permanent jobs by 2033, ramping from roughly 1,900 positions in the first year of operations, plus a peak of about 1,147 construction jobs. Construction is targeted for 2026–2028, with commercial production beginning in the first quarter of 2029. An accompanying economic analysis estimates the project could add roughly $107 billion to Texas GDP over several decades.
Local incentives have cleared major hurdles. Lamar Consolidated Independent School District 
unanimously approved school-tax limitations in September
, and Fort Bend County commissioners approved a package of abatements and related agreements in late October that could extend equipment tax breaks for as long as 35 years.
Tesla had previously indicated it was evaluating a competing out-of-state location.
The effort fits broader statements by 
Musk that Tesla and SpaceX are each working toward
 100 gigawatts of annual U.S. solar manufacturing capacity. If built as described, Project Crystal Sun would stand as one of the largest domestic attempts to create a fully integrated solar supply chain on American soil.
While Tesla has not issued a formal groundbreaking announcement, the visible site preparation and high-profile social media activity represent a concrete step forward for a project that has moved from confidential filings to active earthworks in a matter of months.

Tesla Launches Robotaxi in San Antonio, Its 9th and Largest Region Yet
Rides started at 5 a.m. today across roughly 274 square miles, making San Antonio Tesla’s eighth unsupervised Robotaxi city and its biggest service area at launch.

November 9, 2026
                                Share
Tesla’s Robotaxi network is now in San Antonio. Tesla’s official Robotaxi account announced the expansion on X just after midnight, with unsupervised rides launched this morning, November 9, at 5 a.m. CT.
It’s the second new Robotaxi city in as many weeks, after 
unsupervised rides launched in Fort Worth
 on November 2.

Tesla’s Biggest Robotaxi Launch Yet
San Antonio’s service area covers roughly 274 square miles, making it Tesla’s largest Robotaxi service area at launch. It’s also slightly bigger than Austin’s 272 square miles, 
which has been growing since rides started
 there in June 2025.

The service area covers all of Downtown San Antonio and nearly everything inside Loop 410, then extends well past it in most directions. To the north, it reaches Loop 1604 between I-10 and US-281, with a small extension up I-10 beyond 1604. It also stretches west past State Highway 151, east beyond I-35, and south below I-410 toward I-37.
With San Antonio added, Tesla now offers unsupervised rides in eight cities, with service areas covering about 4.2 million people combined. For the full breakdown of every market, including the supervised Bay Area zone, check out our 
guide to Tesla’s Robotaxi service areas
.
Advertised sizes don’t tell the whole story, though. A recent analysis found that 
Tesla’s real pickup and drop-off zones in Texas are 22–41% smaller
 than advertised, though they still cover most residents. The Tesla enthusiast behind those maps may test San Antonio next, and if they do, we’ll cover the results.
Right on Schedule
Fort Worth came as a surprise, since it wasn’t on any of the lists Tesla had shared of upcoming Robotaxi cities. San Antonio was. During its 
Q3 earnings call
 last month, Tesla 
named Las Vegas, Phoenix, Jacksonville, and San Antonio
 as its next Robotaxi markets. After Fort Worth, we wondered if more launches were coming soon, and San Antonio arrived just a week later.
Tesla has also been improving the service in existing markets. It recently 
extended Robotaxi hours in Austin
 and added Calm Mode, which hides distracting visuals on the touchscreen for a more relaxing ride.
Cybercab Comes Next
Not a Tesla App
San Antonio launches with Model Y robotaxis, but Tesla’s Cybercab is expanding too. Tesla already has 
more than 300 Cybercabs running in Austin
, and Cybercab rides are set to launch in Dallas this month, its first market outside Austin.
With two new cities in two weeks and Las Vegas, Phoenix, and Jacksonville still on the list, another Robotaxi launch next week wouldn’t be a surprise.
