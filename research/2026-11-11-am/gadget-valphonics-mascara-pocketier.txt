Valphonics、共鳴構造で低域を引き出すイヤホン「Mascara Pure」。小型モデル「Pocketier」も
公開日 2026/11/09 17:48
						編集部：原田郁未
飯田ピアノは、同社が国内総輸入代理店を務める英国・北アイルランドのイヤホンブランドValphonicsvz（ヴァルフォニクス）から、イントラコンカ型イヤホン「Mascara Pure（マスカラピュア）」と「Pocketier（ポケティア）」を12月18日（金）に発売する。11月13日（金）より、同社オンラインストアおよび取扱販売店で予約を受け付ける。
左から、「Mascara Pure」と
 「Pocketier」
価格はMascara Pureが46,200円、Pocketierが29,700円（いずれも税込）。Pocketierは4.4mmプラグ仕様と3.5mmプラグ仕様を用意し、価格は共通となる。
Valphonicsは、デザイナーのJoshua Castles（ジョシュア・キャッスルズ）氏が主宰するブランド。北アイルランド・ニュータウンアーズの自社工房で、設計から組み立てまでを一貫して行う。かつて存在した設計や技術を、現在の造形技術で改めて形にするという思想でものづくりに取り組んでいる。
国内では2026年8月に第1作「LANDER 20Hz」を発売。本体後部にヘルムホルツ共鳴器を内蔵し、耳を完全に塞がないイントラコンカ型ながら豊かな低域再生を狙ったモデルで、今回の2機種はその技術を異なる方向へ発展させたものとなる。
英Valphonicsが日本上陸。ヘルムホルツ共鳴器で低音を強化したイントラコンカ型イヤホン「LANDER 20Hz」
2026/08/07
製品群は、筐体内部の共鳴構造を活用する「Resonance Series」と、携帯性や日常的な聴きやすさを重視した「Pocket Series」で構成。LANDER 20HzとMascara PureはResonance Series、PocketierはPocket Seriesに位置付けられる。
同ブランドでは、音楽を聴く場面に応じて、ドライバー構成や筐体形状、ケーブルまで個別に設計。筐体内部の空間や音響経路そのものを使って低域や音場をコントロールする考え方を採用しており、複雑な内部形状を精密に成形できるSLA方式の3Dプリントが、その設計を支えているという。
PUサスペンション＋LCP振動板を採用する「Mascara Pure」
Mascara Pureは、15.4mm口径の独自ダイナミックドライバー「Free Edge LCP」を搭載するモデル。PU（ポリウレタン）製サスペンションと、軽量で応答性に優れるというLCP（液晶ポリマー）振動板を組み合わせ、小型で装着性にも配慮した筐体に収めた。
Mascara Pure
筐体内部には、小さな球状の空間「バブル・レゾネーター」を搭載。LANDER 20Hzの共鳴器と同様の考え方を、よりコンパクトな構造に落とし込んだもので、歪みを伴わずに低域をわずかに持ち上げ、温かみのある音色を狙ったという。低域の持ち上げ量は、LANDER 20Hzより控えめに設定されている。Castles氏は、「小さく、着け心地よく、聴く楽しさを第一に設計した」と説明している。
筐体内の「バブル・レゾネーター」により高音を持ち上げるという
ケーブルはMMCX端子による着脱式で、標準では3.5mmプラグのケーブルを付属。このほか、フォームカバー5ペアとキャリングケースを同梱する。感度は102dB/mW、インピーダンスは64Ω、再生周波数帯域は20Hz〜20kHzとなる。
LANDER 20Hzの低域設計を小型化した「Pocketier」
Pocketierは、日常での携帯性と聴きやすさを重視したモデル。LANDER 20Hzと同じ15.4mm口径のPET振動板を採用しつつ、「ポーチに入れてどこへでも持ち出せる」サイズにまとめた。
Pocketier
筐体内部にはチューブ状の音響経路を配置し、低域の伸びと広がりのある音場表現を追求。LANDER 20Hzに近い低域表現を継承しながら、より気軽でバランスの取れた聴き心地を目指したという。
ケーブルは本体直付けで、専用に配合を決めた「Custom Blend Cable」を採用。導体には銀メッキ銅線を使用し、音への色付けを抑えながら、長期間使えるよう設計したとしている。
プラグは4.4mmと3.5mmの2種類から注文時に選択可能。専用ポーチとフォームカバー5ペアを付属する。感度は102dB/mW、インピーダンスは64Ω、再生周波数帯域は20Hz〜20kHzとなる。
SLA方式の3Dプリントを活用し、北アイルランドでハンドメイド
両モデルとも、フェイスプレートには射出成形したABS樹脂、ボディには一般的なレジンより硬く、耐久性に優れるというABSライク樹脂を採用する。
ボディはSLA方式の3Dプリントで成形した後、スプレーガンで全面を均一に塗装。さらにラッカーと、肌への安全性に配慮した表面仕上げを施す。ドライバーの組み込みから最終仕上げまで、NewtownardsにあるCastles氏の工房で一台ずつ手作業で組み立てられる。
形式はいずれも、耳甲介に収めるイントラコンカ型（インナーイヤー型／フラットヘッド型）。耳道を密閉しない構造で、内部の音響経路や共鳴構造を積極的に利用して低域表現を追求している。

valphonics.com
British audio brand Valphonics has announced two new additions to its lineup of resonance-tuned intra-concha earphones: the 
Mascara Pure
, priced at 
¥46,200
 (approximately $292 USD; exchange rate as of November 9, 2026 — conversions are approximate), and the 
Pocketier
, priced at 
¥29,700
 (approximately $188 USD). Both go on sale in Japan on 
December 18
, with pre-orders opening 
November 13
 via exclusive Japanese importer Iida Piano. Japanese audio publication 
PhileWeb reported the details
 on November 9. Neither model has confirmed Western availability at the time of publication.
What makes the announcement more than a routine product launch is what it reveals about the design program behind Valphonics. With these two additions, every product in the brand's lineup now uses a different internal resonant geometry to solve the same underlying acoustic problem — and each solves it differently, by design.
The company behind these products consists of one person: 
Joshua Castles
, a self-taught acoustic designer based in Newtownards, Northern Ireland, who hand-assembles and calibrates every unit himself. When he shared his original resonance-enhanced earbud on Reddit, Sennheiser's official account left a comment asking if he liked project management work. He said he would take the job. Instead, he built a brand.
The Problem Every Open Earphone Has
To understand what Valphonics makes, it helps to understand what it is working against. Intra-concha earphones — commonly called earbuds — rest in the outer ear but do not enter the ear canal. That openness is comfortable and preserves awareness of the surrounding environment. It also creates an unavoidable acoustic problem: because the earphone does not seal the canal, low-frequency sound leaks around it and dissipates into the air before reaching the eardrum. The larger the air gap, the higher the frequency at which bass response begins to collapse. This is known as acoustic short-circuiting, and the conventional industry answer to it is to abandon the open design entirely and use a sealed, canal-inserting IEM instead.
Valphonics does not use sealed IEMs. Instead, Castles' approach is to put a tuned resonant cavity inside the earphone shell and use it to amplify specific low frequencies before they can escape — using the physics of Helmholtz resonance and related principles to effectively fight the acoustic short-circuit with controlled air motion rather than physical sealing.
What is now apparent across the full product lineup is that he has applied three distinct versions of this idea, each producing a different acoustic character.
A Brand Built on Resonator Physics
The Helmholtz resonator — described by the German physicist Hermann von Helmholtz in his 1863 work 
On the Sensations of Tone
 — works by exploiting the resonant behavior of air trapped in a cavity with a neck. When sound forces air into the cavity, pressure builds and releases cyclically; the air in the neck acts as a mass on a spring, with the enclosed air providing the spring force. The resonant frequency depends on the cavity's volume, the neck's cross-sectional area, and the neck's effective length. In speakers, this principle is applied as the bass-reflex port. In Sennheiser's flagship IE 900 IEM, 
three Helmholtz resonators drilled
 into the aluminum shell smooth the high-frequency response in the 6–10 kHz range.
Castles' innovation was to point the same principle in the opposite direction: rather than using a Helmholtz resonator to manage high frequencies, he tuned one to generate bass. His first commercial product, the LANDER 20Hz, 
launched in Japan by Iida Piano
 on August 28, 2026, at 
¥39,600
 (approximately $251 USD), places a Helmholtz resonator at the rear of the earphone shell. The model's name references 20 Hz — the lower boundary of audible sub-bass — signaling the depth of extension the design targets. The LANDER 20Hz's driver is a 15.4 mm dynamic unit with a composite PET+LCP diaphragm and a CCAW (copper-clad aluminum wire) voice coil, running at 64 Ω impedance.
The design drew attention in the audiophile community long before it became a commercial product. When Castles posted his prototype to Reddit, the thread drew thousands of upvotes and hundreds of responses. One was from Sennheiser's official account. Independent measurement engineer oratory1990 requested a test unit and raised questions about whether the bass effect would hold without the foam covers' partial seal — a limitation Castles acknowledged. The foam covers, included with every Valphonics product, create the semi-seal that the resonator needs to function. Without them, the effect diminishes.
Three Products, Three Resonance Geometries
The Mascara Pure, Pocketier, and LANDER 20Hz now represent what amounts to a systematic survey of resonant cavity shapes applied to the open-earphone bass problem.
The LANDER 20Hz
 uses a classic Helmholtz geometry: a defined cavity volume connected to the outside through a narrow neck. This produces a relatively focused, high-amplitude resonance at the tuned frequency — yielding the pronounced sub-bass extension the product is named for.
The Mascara Pure
 uses what Valphonics calls a 
"bubble resonator"
 — a small spherical chamber inside the shell. A spherical cavity differs from the neck-and-cavity Helmholtz geometry in a consequential way: the sphere supports a more uniform resonant quality factor (Q), producing a broader, smoother energy distribution at the tuned frequencies rather than a sharp resonance peak. The practical result, as described in 
Iida Piano's product documentation
 on PhileWeb, is that the Mascara Pure delivers a milder, warmer bass lift compared to the LANDER 20Hz. It is not a less sophisticated product — it is calibrated for a different listening character.
The Pocketier
 takes the third path, using a tube-shaped acoustic path inside the shell. This directly references the 1990s "pipe earbud" concept that Castles cited alongside Helmholtz physics as the second source for his original design — a technique employed by Sony and Aiwa in some earbud models of that era. A tube creates a quarter-wave resonance effect whose frequency depends on the tube's length. Beyond bass extension, Iida Piano describes this as also producing a wider perceived soundstage.
The three are not positioned as a good-better-best hierarchy. They are acoustically distinct: Helmholtz for deep sub-bass extension, bubble resonator for warmth, tube path for breadth. All three use the same basic driver size (15.4 mm), all require foam covers to work properly, and all are assembled by hand in Newtownards.
Mascara Pure: Driver and Specifications
The Mascara Pure's driver is a custom 15.4 mm unit that Valphonics calls the 
"Free Edge LCP"
. The diaphragm uses Liquid Crystal Polymer (LCP) film — a material chosen for its high stiffness-to-weight ratio and strong internal damping relative to standard PET film. A stiffer, lighter diaphragm reduces unwanted resonances and distortion, particularly at high frequencies, while better damping suppresses ringing after transients. The "free edge" designation indicates that the diaphragm's outer rim moves without a stiff surround constraining it, enabling wider linear excursion — which translates to more headroom in the low frequencies and reduced distortion under heavy bass loads. The suspension uses polyurethane (PU) rather than the more common rubber or fabric, providing a compliant but controlled motion range.
Connection is via MMCX detachable cable, with a 3.5 mm terminated cable included. Sensitivity is 
102 dB/mW
, impedance 
64 Ω
, with a rated frequency response of 20 Hz to 20 kHz. The shell is an ABS-like SLA 3D-printed resin body with an injection-molded ABS faceplate, spray-painted and lacquer-finished with a skin-safe surface treatment. Five pairs of foam covers and a carrying case are included.
Pocketier: Specifications
The Pocketier shares the 15.4 mm driver diameter with the LANDER 20Hz but uses a 
PET diaphragm
 rather than the LCP used in the Mascara Pure — a less expensive material but adequate for the product's everyday-use positioning. Unlike the Mascara Pure's detachable MMCX cable, the Pocketier uses a 
hard-wired "Custom Blend Cable"
 with silver-plated copper conductors, chosen to minimize signal coloring. Plug options (4.4 mm balanced or 3.5 mm single-ended) are selected at time of order. Sensitivity and impedance match the Mascara Pure: 
102 dB/mW
 and 
64 Ω
, with the same 20 Hz–20 kHz rated range. Accessories include five pairs of foam covers and a carrying pouch.
Handbuilt in Northern Ireland
Valphonics shells are made using 
SLA (stereolithography) 3D printing
 in an ABS-like resin, a manufacturing method Castles chose specifically because it allows the complex internal geometries — spherical resonant chambers, narrow tubes, precisely proportioned Helmholtz cavities — that conventional injection molding cannot form. Faceplates are injection-molded ABS for durability and surface consistency. Bodies are spray-painted and finished with lacquer and a skin-safe coating. Each unit is assembled entirely by hand at Castles' workshop in Newtownards, and driver channel-matching — the process of pairing left and right drivers whose frequency responses are as close as possible — is performed by Castles personally. Repairs also return to the same UK workshop.
The production model is a deliberate statement in a market segment dominated by high-volume manufacturers in East Asia. Valphonics' open-sourcing of its designs after a year — as 
noted by Headphonesty
 — reflects Castles' engagement with the audiophile DIY community that helped his work find an audience before he had a commercial product at all.
Market Context
The Mascara Pure enters a market where resonance-based IEM tuning has gained traction but where the specific application — bass generation rather than bass sculpting — remains uncommon. Kiwi Ears' Singolo, developed with audiophile reviewer Crinacle, 
incorporated a Helmholtz resonator
 via its KARS (Kiwi Acoustic Resonance System) to suppress a 300 Hz resonance, allowing bass to be tuned higher without muddiness — a fundamentally different use of the same principle. Sennheiser's IE 900 uses Helmholtz chambers for high-frequency smoothing. Valphonics is unusual in applying resonators to actively generate, rather than manage, low frequencies.
At ¥46,200 (~$292 USD), the Mascara Pure is priced above its stablemate the LANDER 20Hz (¥39,600 / ~$251 USD), reflecting its custom LCP driver and the development costs of the bubble resonator architecture. The Pocketier at ¥29,700 (~$188 USD) is the brand's entry point.
