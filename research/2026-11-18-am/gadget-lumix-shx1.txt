Panasonic has announced the Lumix SHX1, the first compact cinema camera to bear the Lumix branding. Previously, Panasonic separated its cinema and consumer imaging divisions between the two brands, but the SHX1 is the unification of the two under one roof.

“For decades, Panasonic has supported the video industry from broadcast to cinema, and its Varicam cinema cameras have been used to make films, dramas and documentaries worldwide. This expertise has also been carried into Lumix, and it led to the Lumix S1H, introduced in 2019 as a cinema-quality professional video model,” Panasonic says. 
“In 2024, Panasonic took this further by integrating its professional AV and consumer imaging businesses, which share a commitment to supporting customers’ creativity. The new Lumix SHX1 brings together the expertise of both businesses in a compact cinema camera.”
The combination of the two Panasonic camera divisions and the existence of the SHX1 effectively means the end of the Varicam brand, but should result in a more cohesive overall camera line. 
The SHX1 is, effectively, the S1 II packed into a box-style cinema body, with a couple of exceptions. Firstly, Lumix included an anti-aliasing filter to reduce moire at the cost of some sharpness while the camera also offers the ability to shoot BRAW, albeit with a caveat: it can’t capture it to the CFexpress or SD card slot and must instead use an external SSD.
Beyond that, the camera is capable of capturing basically what the S1 II already does. It features a 6K full-frame partially stacked CMOS image sensor with Dual Native ISO and Dynamic Range Boost, giving the camera effectively 15 stops of dynamic range when in V-log. The SHX1 comes with ARRI LogC3 pre-installed as well, allowing users to choose between V-Log and ARRI LogC3.
In standard recording formats, the camera supports 6Kp30 and 5.1Kp60 in 4:2:0 10-bit, as well as in 3.8Kp48 4:2:2 10-bit, which is a new option compared to the S1 II. It also, of course, supports open gate recording and in-camera anamorphic de-squeeze. 
The sensor is equipped with a Phase Hybrid autofocus system with 779 points and the SHX1 has the same AI technology as found in the S1 II which enables eye and face detection. Also on-board is Panasonic Lumix's excellent five-axis in-body image stabilization system along with support for Dual IS 2 when combined with a lens that supports OIS and the feature.

Panasonic has announced the LUMIX AU-SHX1, a compact box-style cinema camera that brings its LUMIX and professional AV lines together in one body. The Panasonic LUMIX SHX1 is built around a 6K full-frame 24.1MP partially stacked CMOS sensor with an optical low-pass filter, and Panasonic rates it at up to 15 stops of dynamic range in V-Log. This is the first LUMIX camera to record Blackmagic RAW to an external SSD and to ship with ARRI LogC3 pre-installed. The camera also offers internal ProRes RAW, 12G-SDI, timecode, unlimited 4K 120p recording, phase-detection autofocus, in-body stabilization, and a top-mounted monitor.
For complete coverage of the camera announcement, head to our 
review
, our 
interview
 with Panasonic’s head of the imaging division, Tsumura-san, and our camera 
Lab Test
. The camera was also added to our 
camera databases
.
Panasonic 
merged its professional AV and consumer imaging businesses
 in 2024, and the SHX1 is the first camera that combines both. Panasonic describes it as its entry into the cinema camera market, positioned between the LUMIX hybrid cameras on one side and its PTZ and live production systems on the other. Compact cinema cameras were already a topic when we spoke with Panasonic’s Toshiyuki Tsumura-san at 
CP+ 2026
, but now, we have conducted a 
fresh interview
 with him, talking about the new camera and what the future might bring to Panasonic. According to Panasonic, the main target group is small crews of five people or fewer, often with one person operating the camera, who want to move on to larger and higher-budget productions. The company also sees the SHX1 as a compact B-camera on bigger sets.
LUMIX SHX1. Credit: Panasonic
A 6K full-frame sensor with an optical low-pass filter
The SHX1 uses a 6K full-frame partially stacked CMOS sensor with 24.1MP effective for video. Panasonic places high-speed processing circuitry above and below the pixel area, and says this reduces rolling shutter distortion and supports high frame rates and fast autofocus. An optical low-pass filter in front of the sensor should suppress moiré and false color on fine patterns. A new engine with a newly developed image processing pipeline handles the high-bitrate data from the sensor, and according to Panasonic, it also reduces latency on the external video outputs.
Panasonic’s Dual Native ISO switches between a low ISO and a high ISO circuit in each pixel, with Auto, Low, and High settings. The ISO range is 100 to 51,200, expandable to 50 to 204,800. Dynamic Range Boost combines both circuits per pixel, weighting a saturation-priority signal from the low ISO circuit and a low-noise signal from the high ISO circuit by the amount of light each pixel receives. Panasonic specifies 15 stops of V-Log/V-Gamut recording with Dynamic Range Boost on and more than 14 stops with it off. Keep your eyes out for our independent LUMIX SHX1 camera Lab Test. The company also notes that with Dynamic Range Boost active, ISO settings is restricted, and rolling shutter distortion increases.
For noise processing, Panasonic says the SHX1 keeps fine detail and natural texture rather than removing all noise, a philosophy it carries over from the VARICAM and the LUMIX S1H. For direct delivery, the camera offers LUMIX Photo Styles, including the Cinelike A2, Cinelike D2 and Cinelike V2 profiles.
Blackmagic RAW, ProRes RAW and ARRI LogC3 in one body
Panasonic sums up the SHX1’s recording options as “3 cinema cameras in 1 body.” The first is Blackmagic RAW, which the camera records directly to an external SSD over USB-C (10Gbps). These are the Blackmagic RAW SSD modes Panasonic lists:
6K 3:2 open gate (5952 x 3968) at 24.00p and 23.98p, with a slight crop
5.8K 17:9 (5760 x 3040) up to 29.97p
5.3K 3:2 (5376 x 3584) at 29.97p and 25.00p
4.4K 3:2 (4416 x 2944) up to 48.00p
3.8K 3:2 (3840 x 2560) up to 59.94p
C4K 17:9 (4096 x 2160) up to 59.94p
Recording settings include constant bitrate at 3:1, 5:1, 8:1, and 12:1 and constant quality at Q0 and Q5. Panasonic gives an example of the storage savings: 6K 24p Blackmagic RAW at 12:1 runs at 599Mbps and fits about 445 minutes on a 2TB SSD, while 5.8K 29.97p ProRes RAW at 2,799Mbps fits about 95 minutes on the same drive. For monitoring and grading, users can choose between V-Log and Blackmagic Generation 5 Color Science, which should make it easier to mix the SHX1 with Blackmagic cameras on a multi-camera shoot. Over HDMI to a Blackmagic Video Assist, the SHX1 outputs RAW for Blackmagic RAW recording in 6K 3:2 (6000 x 4000) and 5.9K 16:9 up to 29.97p, 5.7K 17:9 up to 59.94p, and C4K up to 119.88p.
ProRes RAW is the second route. The SHX1 records ProRes RAW HQ and ProRes RAW internally to a CFexpress Type B card in 5.8K 17:9 up to 29.97p and in C4K up to 59.94p. Panasonic’s overview also lists ProRes RAW recording to an SSD. RAW output over HDMI for ProRes RAW on an external recorder covers 6K 3:2 and 5.9K 16:9 up to 29.97p, 5.7K 17:9 up to 59.94p, and C4K up to 119.88p. The camera also records ProRes, H.264 and H.265 in MOV, and H.264 and H.265 in MP4, with proxy recording available.
The third is ARRI LogC3 with ARRI Wide Gamut 3, which comes pre-installed on a LUMIX camera for the first time. It is available for 10-bit recording in video mode. Panasonic highlights the use of ARRI Looks and productions that combine the SHX1 with ARRI digital cinema cameras, and points out that the ALEXA 35 uses LogC4 instead. V-Log and V-Gamut remain available, with the same gamma and color characteristics as the rest of the LUMIX S and G series.
Panasonic LUMIX SHX1. Credit: Panasonic
Open gate, high frame rates and vertical anamorphic
Panasonic says the fast readout of the sensor lets the SHX1 record open gate (3:2) in 6K up to 30p and 5.1K up to 60p in 4:2:0 10-bit, plus Blackmagic RAW 6K 24p. A new 3.8K open gate mode records up to 48p in 4:2:2 10-bit. Panasonic positions open gate as a way to deliver several aspect ratios, such as 16:9 and 9:16, from one take.
Beyond open gate, the camera records 6K 2.4:1, 5.9K 16:9 and 5.8K 17:9 up to 60p in 4:2:0 10-bit. C4K, 4K and Cs4K (2.4:1) are available up to 120p in 4:2:0 10-bit and up to 60p in 4:2:2 10-bit, and Full HD goes up to 240p in 4:2:2 10-bit. Some high frame rate modes crop the image: Panasonic lists approximately 1.17x for C4K at 120p and 100p, 1.24x for 4K at 120p and 100p, and 1.21x for Full HD at 240p.
For vertical content shot with anamorphic lenses, the SHX1 adds Vertiscope, a vertical de-squeeze display with 2.0x, 1.6x and 1.33x factors. The de-squeezed image is also available on the HDMI and SDI outputs, so an external monitor shows the correct vertical frame. Panasonic says the box design is made to work in landscape as well as portrait orientation.
Phase-detection AF and in-body stabilization
Autofocus is handled by Phase Hybrid AF with 779 points. Panasonic says AI-based eye and face detection keeps focus even when a face is tilted or partially covered, and Tracking AF should stay on the intended subject when several people cross the frame. Real-time Recognition AF covers people, animals, cars, motorcycles, bicycles, airplanes, and trains.
The SHX1 also has a 5-axis in-body image stabilizer, and Dual I.S. 2 combines it with 2-axis optical stabilization in compatible lenses. For walking shots, Active I.S. adapts the correction for pitch, yaw, and roll, and Advanced E.I.S. reduces perspective distortion. Boost I.S. holds a static handheld composition, Cropless Mode reduces distortion at wide angles without changing the angle of view, and stabilization also works with anamorphic lenses, including roll compensation.
Body, monitor and connections
The SHX1 is an L-Mount camera that measures 110.7 x 112.6 x 120.2mm (4.36 x 4.43 x 4.73in) and weighs about 984g (2.17lb), body only. A metal exterior spreads heat evenly, and a large cooling fan moves it out of the body. According to Panasonic, this allows unlimited recording up to 4K 120p within the guaranteed operating temperature range.
Panasonic calls the monitor the first top-mounted monitor on a LUMIX camera. It tilts up or swings out to the side and stays visible with the top handle attached. The control layout follows LUMIX conventions, with dedicated WB, ISO, and IRIS buttons and an AF ON button. Tally lamps on the front and rear are visible from four directions and can be switched on or off independently.
Power comes from a micro V-mount battery, which can also supply connected accessories. A dedicated USB-C power input accepts a USB PD AC adapter for longer sessions, which requires an adapter and cable rated at 100W (20V/5A). A second USB-C port (10Gbps) connects the SSD, and Panasonic plans to let it charge a smartphone running LUMIX Flow via USB PD with a firmware update in the first half of 2027. Two more terminals are reserved for optional accessories.
For monitoring, the SHX1 has 12G-SDI, which outputs up to 4K 60p over a locking BNC connector, and HDMI. Both outputs work at the same time, for example, to record externally over HDMI while a monitor receives the SDI feed. A TC IN/OUT BNC terminal syncs timecode with a generator or other cameras, and Bluetooth timecode generators are supported as well. On the audio side, the SHX1 is the first LUMIX camera to record 32-bit float audio internally through the 3.5mm microphone input, without an XLR adapter.
Panasonic LUMIX SHX1. Credit: Panasonic
Grip, XLR handle and live production unit
Panasonic has three accessories for the SHX1. The AU-GRP1 control grip has dedicated WB, ISO, and IRIS buttons, a joystick, an Fn button, and a zoom rocker, and attaches to the camera via an ARRI-standard rosette. It controls the camera over a wired connection, so it also works on tripods and shoulder rigs. The zoom rocker is designed for a power zoom lens for the SHX1 that Panasonic is currently developing, and with other LUMIX lenses, it operates the crop zoom.
The AU-XHU1 XLR handle unit adds two XLR inputs with 32-bit float recording and high-resolution audio at 96kHz/24-bit. Combined with the camera’s 3.5mm microphone input, it enables 4-channel audio. The handle has a cold shoe, threaded mounting points, and a REC button, and the XLR unit and the top handle can be separated.
The AU-LPU1 Live Production Unit is scheduled for the first half of 2027. It adds Genlock IN, a PoE++ LAN terminal for power, camera control, and IP video over a single cable, and support for a common IP control protocol used by Panasonic’s PTZ camera systems. Panasonic shows the SHX1 with the AU-LPU1 on a remote pan/tilt head, controlled from its studio and PTZ remote panels and the Media Production Suite software, and as a point-of-view camera for esports, indoor sports and music venues. Its system diagram currently shows this setup with the 
AW-UB50
 box camera.
Panasonic also worked with Tilta. Connected over USB-C, the Tilta Nucleus 2.4G Wireless Control Hub (Panasonic) lets the SHX1 control a Tilta Mirage variable ND with the same interface the camera uses for shutter speed, iris and ISO. With a Tilta controller, focus on L-Mount lenses can be pulled without a lens motor.
LUMIX Flow 1.8.0 and LUMIX Lab 3.2.0
Panasonic’s smartphone apps get SHX1 support on December 22, 2026. 
LUMIX Flow
 1.8.0 adds two functions for the SHX1 only. Smartphone Sync Recording stores a simplified copy of each take on the phone, so clips can be played back and rated without a connection to the camera. Sequential Play plays the takes marked as OK in storyboard order, which gives a quick view of the complete sequence on set without an editing application.
LUMIX Flow 1.8.0 also introduces Frame Preview, which crops the live view to a selected frame when the phone switches between vertical and horizontal orientation. This is intended for open gate shoots that deliver 16:9 and 9:16 at the same time, and the cropped view also works on mirrored and external monitors. LUMIX Lab 3.2.0 adds SHX1 support, and both apps add support for iOS 27 and Android 17.
Price and availability
The Panasonic LUMIX SHX1 costs $4,697.99 / €5,199 for the body only. The AU-SHX1U kit, which adds the AU-GRP1 control grip and the AU-XHU1 XLR handle unit, costs $5,497.99 / €5,999. The AU-XHU1 XLR handle unit costs $597.99, and the AU-GRP1 control grip costs $447. The euro prices include VAT. Panasonic plans to ship the camera from the factory in December 2026, so most customers should receive it in January 2027.
The AU-LPU1 Live Production Unit follows in the first half of 2027, and the power zoom lens is still in development. The LUMIX Flow and LUMIX Lab updates are scheduled for December 22, 2026.
Would Blackmagic RAW to an SSD and pre-installed ARRI LogC3 make the LUMIX SHX1 your A-camera on small-crew jobs, or would it work as a B-camera on your bigger sets? And does a box camera with in-body stabilization and phase-detection AF change the way you rig for handheld work? Don’t hesitate to let us know in the comments below!

最大4K 60pに対応する12G-SDIとHDMI Type A出力を搭載。
LUMIXで初めて、φ3.5 mmマイク端子から入力した音声の32 bitフロート形式での内部記録に対応。
ロック機構付きUSB Type-C端子を2基搭載。
Wi-FiまたはスマートフォンのUSBテザリングによるライブストリーミングに対応
2027年上期予定のファームウェアアップデートにより、コントロールグリップ AU-GRP1やパナソニック製コントローラーからTilta Mirage Variable NDの操作に対応予定。
パナソニックの新しいボックス型シネマカメラ「SHX1」はコントロールグリップを付けると、マミヤやペンタックスなどの中判カメラのような形状ですね。このカメラにはIBISや液晶モニタも搭載されているので、グリップを付ければカメラ単体での手持ち撮影も問題なくできそうです。
センサーが部分積層に変わり、画像処理エンジンも最新のものを搭載しているので、動画の機能に関してはてんこ盛りで、動画クリエイターの多くの要望に対応できそうです。ただ、NDフィルターは内蔵されていないようですね。
