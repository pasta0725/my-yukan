Proton has released Lumo Desktop for Windows, Mac, and Linux with new Connectors, Schedules, and Skills capabilities.
“We’ve committed to giving our community greater transparency into how AI is used at Proton, including sharing the tools we build and use internally so anyone can inspect them,” 
Proton’s Eamonn Maguire writes
. “Today, we’re taking the first step by releasing Lumo Desktop on Windows, macOS, and Linux, and introducing Connectors, Schedules, and Skills. As always, we’re making the source code publicly available so our community can inspect how these tools work.”
Proton Desktop adheres to Proton’s strict privacy and security requirements while giving users the ability to connect the AI to your email and personal files and schedule tasks. It doesn’t use your chats or information from connected apps and files to train AI models, and its zero-access encryption ensures that no one else, including Proton or the United States government, can read your chat history or see your data.
Lumo Desktop uses Connectors to access the file system, execute shell commands, use local tools and connect with Proton Mail and third-party services like Google Drive, Jira, and Confluence. You can also use local and remote Model Context Protocol (MCP)  servers.
The new Schedules feature helps Lumo Desktop execute tasks at specific times and automate them for repeated use. You can set up scheduled tasks in chat using natural language or manually, and in each case configure permissions specific to that task.
And Skills works much like the similar feature in other AI assistants by remembering instructions that can be reused across multiple chats.
“Create a writing Skill so emails sound like you,” Proton explains. “Add your resume and career goals to help tailor job applications. Or save your dietary preferences to guide meal plans. You can add templates and reference files so Lumo has the context it needs.”
Lumo Desktop will apply Skills automatically in chats, and you can create Skills by writing them manually, asking Lumo for help doing so, or by uploading a Skill you created elsewhere.
Proton subscribers can download Lumo Desktop for Windows, Mac, and Linux from the Proton website
. And Proton hints that some of its unique features, like Connectors and Schedules, could come to Lumo mobile in the future.

Proton 
released Lumo Desktop
 for Windows, macOS, and Linux on November 17, 2026, introducing Connectors, Schedules, and Skills for its privacy-focused AI assistant. The update lets Lumo work with local files and connected apps, run tasks at set times, and reuse personalized instructions.
The release moves 
Lumo
 beyond a chat interface and into actions across a user’s computer and online accounts. The tools were built for Proton’s internal teams before being released to users, and their source code is publicly available for inspection. That expanded reach makes control over what the assistant can read and do central to the product’s design.
Connectors Extend Lumo Across Apps and Files
Connectors let Lumo retrieve information and perform actions through local filesystem access, shell execution, and browser content fetching. Proton Mail works through Proton Mail Bridge, while supported third-party connections include Google Drive, Slack, Dropbox, Jira, and Confluence. Users can also add local or remote Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers; Proton’s 
connector documentation
 specifies support for local stdio and remote HTTP servers.
Each connector action has one of three permission settings: Auto-approve, Ask human, or Deny. For example, a user can permit inbox searches, require confirmation before an email is sent, and block message deletion. Actions default to Auto-approve, and connectors are enabled by default in conversations, although users can adjust individual permissions or disable a connector through the Tools menu. Without those changes, connected actions can run without confirmation, making the initial permission review a significant part of setup.
Schedules and Skills Target Repeated Work
Schedules can run once at a specified time or recur, with separate permissions for each task. A user can describe the desired work and timing, review and confirm the task Lumo prepares, and later revise its instructions or schedule, pause it, or delete it.
Skills save reusable instructions, templates, and reference files. Lumo can apply relevant Skills automatically and use several in the same conversation, while existing Custom Lumos will be migrated automatically. Examples include a writing Skill for email drafts and a job-application Skill containing a resume and career goals.
Proton’s examples show how the features can be combined. A monthly spending review can draw bills and receipts from local folders and Google Drive, summarize spending, flag unusual charges, and use a Skill to keep each summary in a preferred format. A travel workflow can collect tickets and reservations from an inbox into one itinerary, check weather updates before departure on a schedule, and retain travel preferences in a Skill.
Privacy Architecture and Plan Access
Proton’s 
security documentation
 describes a system that encrypts messages in transit, temporarily decrypts them on its servers for language-model processing, and encrypts them again before returning responses. Conversation history is sent encrypted to supply context and stored with zero-access encryption, leaving only the user able to decrypt stored chats. Plaintext is not retained on the server beyond what is necessary to generate the immediate response.
Proton also says Lumo keeps no server-side records of when users chatted, where they were, or what they discussed. It does not use conversations or information from connected apps and files to train models or serve personalized ads. Ghost Mode makes chats disappear when closed, and the service runs on Proton-controlled servers in Europe.
The 
Lumo download page
 lists web, iOS, and Android versions alongside the desktop apps. A free Proton account is sufficient to start, while paid plans add features and higher usage limits. Connectors and Schedules are currently desktop-only; the mobile apps are oriented toward on-the-go conversations and dictation.
Alongside the release, Proton said its first NVIDIA B300 cluster built on Open Compute Project standards is going live in Switzerland, describing it as an investment in sovereign Swiss AI infrastructure and greater control over the technology behind its AI services.
The desktop release ties Lumo’s automation capabilities directly to Proton’s privacy proposition: the more files, accounts, and recurring tasks users place within its reach, the more consequential its permission defaults and processing architecture become.

Proton is releasing Lumo Desktop today for Linux, Windows and macOS, bringing its AI assistant tools for working with connected apps, running scheduled 
tasks and remembering reusable instructions
. The features, called Connectors, Schedules and Skills, let users give Lumo access to email and files while setting limits on what it can do with them. Users sign in using their Proton account.
Connectors support Google Drive and local files, while access to Proton Mail requires Proton Mail Bridge. Users can also connect their own Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers, whether hosted remotely or locally, and work services including Jira. Proton says Lumo can retrieve travel bookings from email or locate a document on a computer, reducing the need to move information into a chat manually.
For each Connector, users decide which operations can proceed automatically, which need permission and which are prohibited. A user could permit inbox searches, insist on approving outgoing mail and prevent message deletion. Connected tools start enabled in conversations, so users who want to exclude one must disable it through the conversation’s Tools menu.
Schedules supports both one-time jobs and recurring tasks, such as a spending review each month or an inbox digest each week. Users can describe a task and its timing for Lumo to prepare for confirmation, or enter the setup manually. Permissions apply separately to each task, and users can revise its timing or instructions, suspend it or remove it.
Skills stores reusable guidance, templates and supporting files, allowing preferences to carry between conversations. Lumo selects relevant Skills automatically and can apply multiple Skills together; users can supply an existing Skill, write one themselves or have Lumo help build it. Existing Custom Lumos will migrate automatically to Skills.
The additions follow October’s Creations feature, which gave Lumo users a separate workspace for generated 
documents, web pages and code
. That feature also preserved revisions and allowed users to edit documents directly, while other content changes required requests to Lumo.
Proton says connected information and conversations are excluded from model training and personalized advertising, and that zero-access encryption keeps chat history unreadable to the company. We’ve committed to giving our community greater transparency into how AI is used at Proton, including sharing the tools we build and use internally so anyone can inspect them.
Today, we’re taking the first step by releasing Lumo Desktop on Windows, macOS, and Linux, and introducing Connectors, Schedules, and Skills. As always, we’re making the source code publicly available so our community can inspect how these tools work.
AI has evolved beyond answering questions. It can now work with your files, connect to your apps, and complete tasks on your behalf. But doing so means giving AI access to more sensitive information, often with permission to act on your behalf. That’s why we’ve developed these features for Lumo, so our teams can benefit from AI while meeting 
Proton’s strict privacy
 and security requirements.
Now, you can use the same tools to connect Lumo to your email and personal files, schedule tasks, and personalize how it works, while keeping your information private.
We’re also continuing to invest in the infrastructure behind Lumo. Our first NVIDIA B300 cluster built on Open Compute Project (OCP) standards is now going live in Switzerland. This represents a significant investment in sovereign Swiss AI infrastructure, helping us build and operate AI services with greater control over the technology powering them.
Connect everything, expose nothing
When an AI assistant can access your personal files and apps, it may work with sensitive information like bills, medical documents, and private messages. How it handles that information matters.
Lumo never uses your conversations or information from your connected apps and files to train models or serve you personalized ads. Zero-access encryption means no one but you can read your chat history, not even Proton. Lumo is also protected by Swiss privacy law and hosted on European infrastructure, outside the scope of the US 
CLOUD Act
.
Connectors: Work with your apps and files
Connectors let Lumo find information and take action in the apps and tools you connect. Ask it to find a document on your computer, gather bookings from your email, or search your drive without switching tabs or copying and pasting between windows.
Connectors
 are available in 
Lumo Desktop
 for Windows, macOS, and Linux. You can connect:
Local tools, including filesystem access, shell execution, and browser content fetching
Proton Mail, through Proton Mail Bridge
Third-party services like Google Drive for your personal files, plus work tools such as Jira, and Confluence
Your own local or remote Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers
When you set up a Connector, you choose which actions Lumo can run automatically, which require approval, and which are blocked. For example, you could let Lumo search your inbox, but require approval before sending an email, and block it from deleting messages.
Connected tools are enabled in conversations by default. You can turn individual Connectors off in a conversation’s Tools menu.
Schedules: Put everyday tasks on a schedule
Schedules let Lumo run a task at a specific time or repeat it regularly. Ask for a weekly inbox summary, a monthly spending review, or updates on a topic you’re following. 
There are two ways to set up a 
scheduled task
. Describe what you want Lumo to do and when. Lumo will ask for any missing details and prepare the task for you to review and confirm. You can also create a scheduled task manually by setting up its instructions or timing. 
Each task has its own permissions. You decide which actions Lumo can take automatically and which require approval. You can change the instructions or timing, pause the task, or delete it whenever you need to. 
Skills: Teach Lumo once, no more repeating
Skills save instructions that Lumo can reuse across conversations. Create a writing Skill so emails sound like you. Add your resume and career goals to help tailor job applications. Or save your dietary preferences to guide meal plans. You can add templates and reference files so Lumo has the context it needs.
Lumo automatically applies relevant 
Skills
 and can use several in the same conversation. You can write your own Skill, ask Lumo to help you create one, or upload an existing one. 
If you’re already familiar with Custom Lumos, these are now becoming Skills and will be migrated automatically.
Put Connectors, Schedules, and Skills to work
Connectors give Lumo access to the information it needs. Schedules let it run tasks when you need them. Skills tell it how you want the work done. Together, they connect Lumo to your everyday life so you can get more done. 
Here are some examples of how Lumo can help with your daily life and AI workflows:
Keep track of your spending.
 Ask Lumo to review bills and receipts in your local folders and Google Drive, summarize your spending, and flag unusual charges. Schedule a monthly review, and use a Skill to keep every summary in your preferred format. 
Plan your next trip.
 Ask Lumo to gather tickets and reservations from your inbox and put it together into one itinerary. Set a schedule to check for weather updates before you leave, and save your travel preferences in a Skill for future trips. 
Keep your inbox under control.
 Connect 
Proton Mail
 and schedule a weekly summary of messages that need a reply. When you’re ready to respond, ask Lumo to draft replies using your writing style Skill. If sending requires approval, Lumo will ask before sending an email.

The tools we’ve built to help our own teams work more productively and privately are now available to you.

 for macOS, Windows, or Linux and sign in with your
