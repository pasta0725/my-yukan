===== https://www.ithinkdiff.com/gemini-tasks-free-six-countries/
(status 200)
Google is making Gemini Tasks free in the United States, Japan, South Korea, Canada, Australia, and India starting November 17, removing the subscription requirement for a feature that was previously available only through Google AI Pro and Ultra. The Task toggle in the Gemini app was previously called Gemini Spark.
You switch the toggle on the Gemini home screen from Chat to Task, and Gemini works through jobs like booking appointments, managing bills and ordering groceries using your email, calendar and payment methods. Google announced the change in a Gemini Tasks blog post, and the feature runs on 
Gemini 4 Hydrogen
, the model Google released on November 12.
Gemini Tasks Features Start in the US
Google is rolling out a set of advanced features starting in the United States, though it has not listed them. Texting Gemini works on Android and iPhone, but you need a US phone number with an eligible carrier. You open Settings, tap Channels, tap Set up under Text with Gemini and verify the number.
Forwarding emails to gemini@google.com also works only in the United States. Calling businesses through Gemini is blocked in Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana and Nebraska, and businesses can opt out of receiving AI calls.
Free accounts have been 
limited to Flash-Lite
 in the regular Gemini model list since October 9. Google has not said whether free accounts have a limit on how many tasks they can run.
Gemini Books, Pays and Shops for You
Google’s Josh Woodward shared several examples of how Gemini can handle everyday tasks on a user’s behalf. In one case, Gemini called two dealerships about a check-engine light on his 2019 Honda Odyssey, found an available slot and booked an early Friday appointment in under five minutes. It also accessed his utility portal through Google Password Manager and traced a $389 electricity bill to a heat pump running around 4 p.m., when electricity rates were higher.
Gemini also compared USB-C chargers based on wattage and color preferences, completed a purchase through Google Pay and found a discount code that saved an additional $10. In another example, it added forwarded school emails to his calendar in under a minute and flagged a scheduling conflict with a practice session. A text message and photo were also enough for Gemini to fill an Instacart order.
Apple is reportedly working on a similar approach with an 
agentic Siri
 that can continue working on tasks for users.
Services Gemini Can Use
Google Password Manager gives Gemini access to sites such as a utility portal.
Google Pay and the Agent Payments Protocol handle purchases, with digital tokens that work with Mastercard and Visa, and American Express coming soon.
Shopping Graph compares products, and Google Maps finds nearby dealerships.
Gmail and Google Calendar supply your messages and schedule, and Personal Intelligence adds details such as your vehicle and location.
Instacart handles grocery orders.
Google Wallet stores receipts and tracking info from Gemini purchases, and it expands to iOS on November 17.
The Connected Apps Platform links Gemini to more than 60 apps, and developers can apply to join.
Each Task Runs in Its Own Virtual Machine
Every task runs in a hardware-isolated virtual machine dedicated to your account, built on Google Cloud’s Agent Compute Engine. Google says security red teams tested the sandbox, and prompt-injection filters plus Google Safe Browsing limit which links Gemini can open.
Gemini pauses for your confirmation before critical steps such as clicking Buy, and you can step in at any time. You can set routine actions to Always allow, adjust task settings, disconnect apps or delete chat history, and you can set a hard spending cap.

Google has made 
Tasks in Gemini
 free of charge for what it describes as hundreds of millions of people in the United States, India, Canada, Australia, Japan and South Korea. The rollout, announced on November 17, 2026 in a post by Josh Woodward, Google's vice president for Labs, the Gemini app and AI Studio, and listed the same day in the official Gemini Apps release notes, turns the Gemini app from a chat assistant into an agent that carries out multi-step jobs in the background and reports back when the result is ready.
Tasks runs on 
Gemini 4 Hydrogen
, the model Google launched the previous week. Google's examples range from a daily morning briefing built from email and calendar to trip itineraries, repetitive research, comparing products, reviewing utility bills and reordering groceries. A new switch on the Gemini home screen moves the app from Chat to Task, and the agent then works through the connected apps, websites and accounts it needs to finish the job.
The free rollout covers six countries, but the features that act in the real world start in the United States only. Agentic Calling, which lets Gemini phone local businesses, checkout with Google Pay, starting a task by text message and starting a task by forwarding an email to gemini@google.com are all described as U.S. features, and the release notes limit the payment, text and email options to users aged 18 or older with personal Google accounts, in English. Calling through Gemini is also unavailable in five U.S. states: Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana and Nebraska.
Until now, Google's general-purpose agents in the Gemini app had been reserved for its most expensive consumer plan. Gemini Agent arrived on the web in November 2025 for Google AI Ultra subscribers in the U.S., and Gemini Spark, Google's always-on personal agent, launched in beta in May 2026 for the same Ultra audience. Tasks is the first agent feature of this kind that Google offers at no cost and outside the United States.

TASKS IN GEMINI: WHAT GOOGLE RELEASED ON NOVEMBER 17.
Google presents Tasks as a mode of the Gemini app built for work that takes several steps and some time. The release notes say Tasks in Gemini "work on your behalf in the background to tackle multi-step projects and recurring routines" and notify the user when results are ready. A user opens Gemini, flips the home-screen toggle from Chat to Task and describes what needs to be done, and Gemini then plans the steps, uses the apps the account has connected and returns with an outcome or with a request for approval.
The announcement walks through several concrete jobs. Gemini can add appointments and school dates found in forwarded emails to the user's calendar and flag conflicts with existing events. With Google Password Manager, it can sign in to a utility portal, read the billing statements in the user's inbox and look for the reason behind an unusually high charge. It can compare products with Google's Shopping Graph, apply discount codes and check out with Google Pay. In a grocery example, Gemini added items to an Instacart order, including items recognized from a photo, and then handed the user over to Instacart to complete the purchase.
Recurring work is part of the design. The release notes describe Tasks that watch for restocks and price drops and reorder household staples within a budget the user sets, and a morning briefing that runs every day across email and calendar. Gemini connects with more than 60 popular apps for these jobs, according to Google, and developers can apply to join the Connected Apps Platform to add their own services.
Google is also candid that the agent will not always reach the end of a job. The announcement says some websites stop the agent partway, in which case Gemini hands control back to the user for the final step, and it asks people to mark failed tasks with a thumbs-down so the company can work on them with websites and security providers.

The move also follows a pattern in Google's recent consumer releases, in which features first sold to Ultra subscribers reach the free tier within months. Calling is a clear case. Google's existing help page for having Gemini make calls on a phone still lists a Pixel 10 or Pixel 11 with a U.S. SIM card, a Google AI subscription, the beta of the Phone by Google app and daily call limits as requirements. AGENTIC CALLING IN GEMINI: HOW BUSINESS CALLS WORK AND WHERE THEY ARE BLOCKED.
Agentic Calling lets Gemini phone local businesses on the user's behalf, including several businesses at the same time. Google's examples are checking whether nearby stores have a product in stock, comparing haircut quotes from three salons, joining a restaurant waitlist and setting up a recurring monthly appointment with a dog groomer. The user asks for the call, reviews and confirms the call plan, and chooses whether Gemini calls immediately or at a scheduled time. Afterward, Gemini returns a summary and a full transcript.
Google says Gemini discloses that it is an AI when it calls, and that businesses can opt out of receiving these calls at any time. The announcement adds that Gemini will not give out payment details or private account information during a call unless the user tells it to. The existing calling help page goes further on restrictions: it excludes emergency calls, payments over the phone, sharing sensitive personal data such as Social Security numbers or health information, and telemarketing, and it says Gemini identifies itself as an AI assistant from Google calling on a recorded line.
The five-state exclusion is the one geographic limit Google published for calling. Residents of Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana and Nebraska cannot use calling through Gemini for now, and Google did not explain the reason in its announcement.

GOOGLE PAY, AP2 AND SPENDING CAPS IN GEMINI TASKS.
Purchases are where Google put the most guardrails. According to the release notes, when a Task finds an item within the user's budget, Gemini stages the checkout and sends an itemized summary, and nothing is bought until the user gives an explicit one-tap approval on their device. Payment goes through a single-use, tokenized Google Pay virtual card, so the merchant never receives the real card number. Users can set a hard spending cap in advance, and Google says purchases still earn the usual card rewards.
Technically, Gemini uses the Agent Payments Protocol (AP2), the open protocol Google proposed for payments initiated by AI agents, together with digital tokens from Visa and Mastercard. American Express support is listed as coming soon. Receipts and tracking information are saved to Google Wallet, which Google says is expanding to iOS starting on the day of the announcement. For iPhone owners, the change means the receipt trail of a purchase made through Gemini on iOS can be kept in a Google app rather than only in email.
Google also says it plans to bring the Universal Commerce Protocol into Gemini to make shopping faster, without giving a date. For routine actions, users can set Gemini to "Always allow" so it stops asking for confirmation each time, but the release notes keep the one-tap approval for purchases in place.
··········
TEXT WITH GEMINI AND GEMINI@GOOGLE.COM: STARTING TASKS OUTSIDE THE APP.
Two new entry points let U.S. users start Tasks without opening the Gemini app. Text with Gemini works through Google Messages or iMessage after a one-time phone verification. To set it up, users open Gemini on Android or iOS, go to Settings and tap Set up under Text with Gemini, then verify a U.S. phone number on an eligible mobile carrier. A running task can be steered by replying to the thread, for example to change a filter, without starting over, and Gemini pauses for a one-tap confirmation before major actions such as sending or booking. Google says this channel has no access to the user's other message history.
Email Gemini lets users forward an email or attachment to gemini@google.com to start a task. It works from Gmail, Apple Mail and Outlook, must be enabled in the Gemini app's settings, and replies privately to the sender only, never to everyone on the thread, with a link to review and confirm the results. A forwarded school newsletter that becomes calendar entries, or a bill that becomes a cost analysis, are the kinds of jobs Google shows for this route.

WHAT CHANGES FOR GEMINI USERS ON THE FREE PLAN AND ON GOOGLE AI PRO OR ULTRA.
For users of the free Gemini app in the six countries, Task mode is the visible change, and it should appear as a toggle on the home screen as the gradual rollout reaches each account. Free users in India, Canada, Australia, Japan and South Korea get the background agent and its research, planning and scheduling jobs, while calling, Google Pay checkout, texting and email forwarding remain limited to the United States for now. Paying subscribers on Google AI Plus, Pro or Ultra get no announced extra allowance for Tasks, since Google did not publish any plan-based limits; the current prices and limits of each tier are in 
our guide to Gemini plans and pricing
.
U.S. users who want the full set of features need to take a few steps. They should enable Text with Gemini in the app settings and verify their phone number if they want to run tasks from a message thread, enable Email Gemini if they want to forward emails, connect the apps they want Gemini to use, and set a spending cap before asking Gemini to buy anything. Anyone who prefers to keep the agent on a short leash can leave confirmations on rather than switching routine actions to "Always allow".
Developers are affected as well. The Connected Apps Platform is the route for a service to become one of the apps Gemini can act in, and the agent's reliance on Gemini 4 Hydrogen puts the model covered in

 behind a consumer feature that Google expects hundreds of millions of people to try.

GEMINI TASKS SECURITY: AGENT COMPUTE ENGINE, PROMPT-INJECTION FILTERS AND CONFIRMATIONS.
Google describes the security model in some detail. Each account's tasks run in a dedicated, hardware-isolated virtual machine on Google Cloud's Agent Compute Engine, sandboxed from other users. Prompt-injection filters screen the content Gemini reads on the web, and Google Safe Browsing limits which links the agent can open. Gemini is designed to ask for confirmation or pre-approval before clicking through critical steps such as a Buy button, and users can disconnect apps, change task settings or delete their chat history at any time.
Google also points security researchers to its AI Vulnerability Reward Program for bugs in the agent. These measures address the main risk of a browsing agent with access to email, passwords and payment methods, which is a malicious page or message that tries to redirect the agent. Google has not published independent test results for these defenses, so their effectiveness rests on the company's own description for now.

GEMINI TASKS: CONFIRMED FACTS AND OPEN QUESTIONS.
Google's official sources confirm that Tasks in Gemini is free, that it is rolling out from November 17, 2026 in the United States, India, Canada, Australia, Japan and South Korea, and that it runs on Gemini 4 Hydrogen. They also confirm that Agentic Calling, Google Pay checkout, Text with Gemini and Email Gemini start in the United States, that the payment, text and email features require users aged 18 or older with personal Google accounts, that calling is unavailable in five states, and that purchases need a one-tap approval and use a single-use virtual card.
Several points are still open. Google has not published usage limits for free users, has not said whether Workspace accounts are included, has not given dates for the U.S.-only features in the other five countries, and has not updated the older calling requirements that tie the feature to Pixel phones and a paid subscription. It has also not said what happens to Gemini Agent and Gemini Spark for Ultra subscribers now that a free agent exists. Until those details appear, the confirmed change is the one that affects the most people: an agent that Google previously sold only on its top plan is now part of the free Gemini app in six countries.

How we protect your data
As Gemini becomes more capable, we continually advance our security defenses. When you assign Gemini a task, it uses 
Google Cloud’s Agent Compute Engine
 to work in a hardware-isolated virtual machine that’s dedicated to your account and never shared with other users. Each virtual machine is sandboxed, so Gemini works in a separate, isolated environment that’s regularly tested by our security red teams. This sandbox is just one of multiple layers of defense, including prompt-injection filters that scan search results, webpages, and more alongside Google Safe Browsing to limit which links Gemini can open.
As Gemini works on a task, you can jump in to give it more instructions at any point, and it’s also designed to pause and check with you before critical steps. For example, Gemini is designed to ask for your confirmation or pre-approval before clicking "Buy," and will ask clarifying questions if an instruction is ambiguous. For routine stuff you already trust, like adding those Cheerios to your grocery cart, you can set that to “Always allow” so it doesn’t pester you.
And finally, we give you access to settings and tools that help you safeguard your data and protect your privacy. You can adjust task settings, disconnect Google and third-party apps, or delete chat history anytime.
