Amazon
 and 
Meta
 Platforms are in ongoing discussions over a potential deal to connect Meta’s popular Muse AI agent to the e-commerce giant’s online store and end 
a blockade of the shopping bot
 imposed by Amazon two months ago.

The two tech giants have held talks on multiple occasions, and two major factors being negotiated are how the companies will handle search results and advertising in any deal, according to people familiar with the matter. Discussions are fluid, and it’s unclear if or how soon an agreement will be struck. There are numerous issues, ranging from Amazon’s own AI shopping ambitions to how, exactly, Muse would connect to Amazon. A major threat Muse poses to Amazon is that, in theory, it would bypass advertisements on Amazon’s website, which are a large source of revenue for the retailer.
Amazon is worried about losing advertising revenue and about the quality of the product search results Muse would surface, and executives are also concerned that shopping agents like Muse will weaken the relationship Amazon has with consumers, people familiar with the matter said. Any deal between Amazon and Meta is expected to require sign-off from the CEOs of both companies, they noted.
Meta and Amazon declined to comment. The negotiations are being closely followed because they have major implications for the future of online shopping and represent an important test case for how large corporations respond to the advent of AI agents. As agents like Muse become more widely used, companies like Amazon are scrambling to understand how the bots alter typical shopping patterns as well as the 
valued connections
 that brands and retailers have with consumers.
The talks are ongoing as Amazon prepares for another Black Friday and Cyber Monday, one of its most critical shopping periods of the year. This year, roughly half of holiday shoppers plan to use AI in some way to help make purchases, a National Retail Federation survey this month showed.
AI agents promise to bring major changes to many industries. 
Agentic shopping
 could total $190 billion to $385 billion in U.S. e-commerce spending by 2030, representing 10% to 20% of the total market, according to a 2025 report by 
Morgan Stanley
. Analysts at JPMorgan have praised Muse as a powerful agent, recently writing that tens of millions of businesses could eventually use the tool. 
Amazon blocked Meta’s Muse app from accessing its store in September, saying at the time that Muse’s access would violate its user agreements. Muse, which Meta released in the U.S. that month, is a personalized AI agent that users can name and direct to autonomously carry out tasks on their behalf, such as checking email, managing calendars, and booking reservations. As of October, 
more than 10 million people
 in the U.S. and Canada had set up the app, making it Meta’s biggest consumer AI hit to date. Chief executive Mark Zuckerberg has said shopping is Muse’s top use case.
“We think that billions of Muses in a network at scale will be able to get more done for people and businesses by working together,” 
Zuckerberg said last month
 on a call with analysts. “We’d like to create a large-scale Muse network as quickly as possible.”
One of Amazon’s main concerns is how shopping agents such as Muse will affect its advertising business. Advertising has become a $68.6 billion business for Amazon, representing nearly 10% of its total yearly revenue. In October, Amazon chief executive Andy Jassy, while not naming Muse, tried to quell investor concern 
by saying few customers are buying with outside shopping agents
 because the experience is “still not great, nor more convenient than coming to a retailer directly.”
Still, Jassy said this month at a 
Semafor
 conference that Amazon hadn’t ruled out letting outside shopping agents into its store, but that they would have to provide a “fair exchange of value.”
Aside from Meta, 
OpenAI launched its Dots 
personal AI agents in September, and startup Instinct’s personal assistant has also become a mainstream AI agent. Amazon has its own shopping agent for its website, dubbed “Alexa for shopping,” which Jassy said has been used by more than 400 million customers in the past year.
Meta and Amazon have partnered on e-commerce before. In 2023, the companies struck a deal that allowed Facebook and Instagram users to purchase Amazon products without leaving the apps. Meta makes money from ads it serves users on its apps, including for Amazon products, and Amazon earns revenue through the actual purchases. The partnership was made as TikTok expanded into U.S. e-commerce.
Meta signed on other partners early in its Muse efforts, including Amazon rivals such as Shopify, 
Walmart
, and 
Best Buy
. Thus, retailers have gained another channel for selling products as Meta tries to grow Muse, and according to Zuckerberg, the company eventually plans to profit by taking a “small fee from transactions.”

===== https://thenextweb.com/news/amazon-and-meta-talks-muse-blockade-ads-fortune

Jolly, the mascot of Meta’s Muse AI agent

Amazon and Meta are in talks over a deal that would let Meta’s Muse AI agent shop on Amazon. It would end the block Amazon put on the shopping bot two months ago. Sebastian Herrera reported the talks for 
Fortune
 on Wednesday, citing people familiar with the matter.
The two companies have met several times, the people said. How a deal would handle search results and advertising are two of the main issues. The talks are fluid, and nobody knows yet whether or when the two sides will agree. Any deal would likely need sign-off from both chief executives, the people said. Meta and Amazon declined to comment to Fortune.
Amazon’s worry is its ads
Amazon 
blocked Meta’s Muse
 from its store in September, saying the agent’s access would break its user agreements. In theory, Muse could shop on Amazon without its users ever seeing the ads on Amazon’s website, according to Fortune.
Advertising is now a $68.6bn business for Amazon, nearly 10% of its yearly revenue, Fortune reported. Amazon fears losing that money, the people said. It also doubts the quality of the search results Muse would show. Its executives also fear that shopping agents will weaken Amazon’s relationship with its customers.
In October, Amazon chief executive Andy Jassy said few customers buy through outside agents, because the experience is not yet good enough. He pitched 
Alexa for Shopping
 as Amazon’s own answer. This month, at a Semafor conference, he said Amazon had not ruled out letting outside agents in. They would have to offer a “fair exchange of value”, he said.
The companies have worked together before. A 2023 deal lets Facebook and Instagram users buy Amazon products without leaving Meta’s apps. Meta has also signed up Amazon rivals for Muse, including Shopify, Walmart and Best Buy, Fortune reported.
Muse is already lifting Meta’s ads
Muse carries no ads. But it already helps Meta’s ad business through Instagram, several people told Julia Boorstin of 
CNBC
. Meta now prompts Instagram users to organise their saved posts in Muse, which also arrived in 
Instagram DMs
 this week.
About 20% of daily Instagram users save at least one post a day, a person familiar with the details told CNBC. Many of those saves are shoppable items or ads. A senior Meta executive said Muse cuts friction for shoppers and lets them return to items later.
“It gives advertisers a way to get more ROI for the dollars they’re already spending, and that’s all because Muse is helping people shop,” the executive told CNBC.
On 
Meta’s last earnings
 call, Mark Zuckerberg said 25% of Muse users shop through the agent. The average purchase is over $200. He has said Meta plans to take a small fee on those transactions.
Muse handled 51% of all agent-made orders in the week to 22 November, CNBC reported. The figure comes from Forter Intelligence, an e-commerce analytics company. Grok had 24% and ChatGPT 22%, with Google and the startup Instinct sharing the rest.
People told CNBC that Meta will likely add ads to Muse in time. But it is under no financial pressure to do so, they said.
