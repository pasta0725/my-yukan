Flipper Devices launches $399 ‘Flipper One’ Linux-based multi-tool
                            By 
Bill Toulas
November 17, 2026
11:00 AM
0
Flipper Devices, the company behind the popular Flipper Zero pentesting device, has launched a crowdfunding campaign for Flipper One, a portable Linux computer and networking multi-tool.
Despite global RAM shortages and unpredictable price fluctuations, the company said Flipper One will 
launch at $399
, a price that reportedly covers production costs and leaves no profit margin.
Flipper Devices said the device’s RAM and UFS storage account for half of the total hardware cost, but after years of development, they decided not to hold back now.
“Instead of waiting for perfect market conditions or raising the price, we’re moving forward with the Kickstarter campaign,” reads the press release Flipper Devices shared with BleepingComputer.
“We aim to make Flipper One as accessible as possible without building a profit margin into the hardware's price.”
Flipper One is a portable computer that runs a Debian-based system called 
Flipper OS
, and features built-in Wi-Fi 6E, Gigabit Ethernet, and an eSIM.
Source: Flipper Devices
It can manage up to five network uplinks, act as a hotspot, run VPNs and firewalls, and connect to an external display over USB-C.
It is a separate product from 
Flipper Zero
, focused on IP networking and on-device computing.
In May, Flipper Devices asked the community 
to help build it
, highlighting the project's complexity and listing several ambitious goals.
Development appears to have progressed at a good pace since then, and the company is now preparing to build the first actual units of the Flipper One.
The company says that Flipper One already runs on the mainline Linux kernel, while USB-C DisplayPort patches are under review for inclusion in the mainline Linux.
Work continues on memory power efficiency and removing the remaining closed-source boot components.
The campaign also offers LoRa (support for MeshCore, Meshtastic, Reticulum, and walkie-talkie mode), a 5G modem, and software-defined radio expansion modules (full specifications to be disclosed).
Source: Flipper Devices
The Kickstarter campaign for Flipper One will run between November 17 and December 17, with the goal of funding the first production run.
Device shipment for the first buyers is estimated for July 2027, and the package will include the device and a USB-C cable.
Flipper Devices also relied on Kickstarter to launch its iconic Flipper Zero in 2020, since then selling over 1 million devices.
Flipper Zero reached its funding goal within eight minutes and raised $4.8 million from crowdfunding in 30 days.

I own a Flipper Zero. It's a fun little device for anyone with a hacker mindset, the kind of gadget you poke at NFC tags, IR remotes and Sub-GHz signals with just to see what happens.
So when Flipper Devices first showed off the Flipper One, I expected it to be just a spec bump. It isn't one. 
As we noted when the 
Flipper One was first announced
, this is a pocket-sized Linux computer that also happens to be a serious network tool. In short, it's a lot more capable device than its predecessor.
And now it's official. The Flipper One Kickstarter campaign is live now, priced at 
$399
. That's double what a Flipper Zero costs, and you can blame the steep increase on the chip shortage in this age of AI.
Key Specifications

 $399 (Kickstarter)

 Rockchip RK3576 (8 cores) + Raspberry Pi RP2350 co-processor
Memory/storage:
 8 GB LPDDR5 RAM, 64 GB UFS
Connectivity:
 Wi-Fi 6E, Gigabit Ethernet, built-in eSIM
Expansion:
 M.2 and GPIO modules (5G, LoRa, SDR)
Estimated shipping:
 July 2027
The Flipper One costs $399 on Kickstarter. Folks who bought the $20 pre-order coupon earlier were promised a $379 price.
Flipper Devices is also clear on the fact that this is not a successor to the Flipper Zero. The Zero works offline, interacting directly with physical stuff like NFC, RFID, infrared and Sub-GHz radio. Flipper One moves up the stack into networked computing: high-speed connectivity, data processing and real on-device compute.
Under the hood, it uses a dual-processor setup. The 
Rockchip RK3576
 octa-core chip runs Linux, networking, USB 3 and video output. A low-power 
Raspberry Pi RP2350
 handles the small 
256x144 grayscale display
, buttons and power. That means the screen and buttons keep working even when the main Linux OS is not used.
The networking side is where things get interesting. It can juggle up to five uplinks, act as a hotspot, run VPNs and firewalls, scan wired and wireless networks, and analyze Wi-Fi traffic. The built-in 
eSIM
 comes with a Flipper SIM data plan.
It also doubles as a battery monitor, keeping tabs on its own power and whatever you plug into it. 
Plug it into a monitor over USB-C DisplayPort Alt Mode and it becomes a desktop computer. Or hook it up to a TV and use it as a media box. It is a versatile device.
Flipper OS and the mainline kernel
I like the fact that they have put in effort for a custom OS called Flipper OS, a Debian-based system where you switch between preconfigured profiles for different jobs: network router, radio lab, desktop, TV media box.
Each profile has its own settings, kernel and apps. You pick one from a boot menu before Linux even loads. Your changes live in an overlay layer, so you can wipe a profile back to default if you mess it up. Experiment freely, as they say.
Flipper Devices has also partnered with Collabora to get the RK3576 into the mainline Linux kernel. The device already runs on mainline, and the DisplayPort Alt Mode patches are under review. The goal is an ARM computer with no binary blobs. Anyone who has fought with a vendor BSP on an ARM board will appreciate that.
There's still some work left. The team is working on dynamic memory frequency scaling for better power efficiency, and on removing the few closed-source bits remaining in the boot process. All of this is in public knowledge, available on the 
Flipper Developer Portal
.
$399 price tag with no profit margin!
The global RAM shortage has made memory prices volatile (or should I say hostile). Flipper says RAM and UFS storage now account for more than half of the total hardware cost of the Flipper One.
Instead of waiting or raising the price, they are selling the hardware at cost and betting on the community to fund the first production run. Bold move, or a sign of how bad memory pricing has gotten? Probably both.
The track record helps. Flipper Zero raised $4.8 million on Kickstarter, hit its goal in eight minutes, and the company has sold over a million devices since 2020.
Pre-order is open now
The Flipper One Kickstarter campaign runs from November 17 to December 17, 2026. You can back the campaign on Kickstarter.
Flipper One on Kickstarter
The device costs 
$399
, and the box includes the Flipper One and a USB-C cable. Estimated shipping is 
July 2027
. 
As with any crowdfunding campaign, treat the timeline as an estimate. Back it because you want to support the project, not because you're counting on the ship date.
Three expansion modules are also available through the campaign:
LoRa module:
 works with Meshtastic, MeshCore and Reticulum mesh networks, and doubles as a walkie-talkie.
5G modem:
 an M.2 module that replaces the back cover, with four SMA antenna ports and support for both the built-in eSIM and external SIM cards.
SDR module:
 a software-defined radio module, full specs to be announced later.
The modules cost extra.
As an owner of Flipper Zero, I am excited for Flipper One launch. In normal times, this would have been a sub $200 device but we live in the times where hobbyist gadgets are getting expensive because Big Tech has disrupted the supply chain for their AI obsession.

Its makers are insisting it's not a direct successor to 
the Flipper Zero
, but there's a new pocket-sized, mini-computer on the market that's going to appeal to tinkerers, hackers, and tech enthusiasts: the Flipper One.
A Kickstarter crowdfunding campaign for the device just went live: it raised $13 million on the first day, and currently stands at more than $14.5 million at the time of writing (and continues to go up rapidly).
Given that kind of financial backing and widespread interest, you might be wondering what the Flipper One is, what it can do, and whether it's going to cause as many security headaches as the Flipper Zero. Here's what you need to know.

Flipper One vs Flipper Zero: what is it?
The Flipper Zero, which launched in 2020 
(Image credit: Flipper)
Before we get to the Flipper One, it's probably helpful to do a quick Flipper Zero recap. Launched in 2020, the Flipper Zero is a tiny gadget that's 
still on sale
 and which can interact with a whole host of fundamental, low-level tech protocols: think Bluetooth, RFID, NFC, and the like. It can analyze and control devices like key cards, fobs, TV remotes, and electronic access points.
That's led to numerous 
security scare stories
 down the years, but it's not quite the hacker's dream that it's sometimes portrayed as — it can't really get into devices or systems that are competently protected. It's more for geeks and tech enthusiasts who are interested in customizing their gadgets and experimenting with electronics, which does admittedly include testing security vulnerabilities.
And so to the Flipper One. The two devices are "completely different projects built for different tasks" according to the team at Flipper. The Flipper One is a similar, compact size, but it runs regular software (Linux) and taps into more consumer-friendly technologies — so Wi-Fi and 5G rather than RFID and NFC.
The Flipper One is more powerful and is capable of running more complex software, but it doesn't tap into the raw wireless and wired signals that the Flipper Zero does. It's more interested in Wi-Fi networks, routers, and internet-connected devices — so, for example, the Flipper Zero could mimic a wireless garage door opener but the Flipper One can set up a VPN on a network.

What can you do with the Flipper One?
The Flipper One will analyze your network for you 
(Image credit: Flipper)
So just how capable is this device? What's so appealing that over 27,000 (and counting) people have pledged money to bring it into being? The Flipper One's Kickstarter page lists many different things you can do with the device — and while it's not suitable for complete beginners, it should be accessible enough for anyone who's reasonably tech-savvy.
You can use it to set up a VPN on your network, as already mentioned, and it can act as both a Wi-Fi client and hotspot (with an Ethernet port, it'd work as a travel router in a hotel room). It's able to monitor Wi-Fi networks, so you could check up on the devices connected to your network and check for any security issues, and there's also eSIM capabilities so it can get on cellular networks without any other help.
It's helpful for analyzing traffic between smart home devices, and controlling them using custom configurations too. As it's actually a complete mini computer, you can use it as a portable TV media box, and it'll work as an actual desktop computer with the right peripherals attached — just don't expect too much in terms of performance.
There's also a lot of scope for expansion with the Flipper One, too. There are already add-ons for connecting to radio signals, so you could use it as a radio scanner, and off-grid communication protocols — the device can work as an off-grid walkie-talkie, for example. Of course, once the developer community gets access to the gadget, we can expect much more functionality to become available.
Should we be worried (again)?
With the right add-on, you can use the Flipper One as a walkie-talkie 
(Image credit: Flipper)
We've already touched on some of the hacking concerns that have emerged around the Flipper Zero down the years, and they're not without merit. This is a compact device that's able to directly interface with electronics like access cards and key fobs, and test security protocols at basic levels.
Perhaps the most concerning capability of the Flipper Zero is its ability to intercept and repeat wireless signals — with a 
car key fob
, for example. However, these kind of hacks have been 
largely exaggerated
, and the Flipper Zero is far from being a magic hacking toy. The majority of systems will have enough security in place to keep it out.
The Flipper One can also be used as a hacking device, but in a different sort of way. It's more for prodding and pushing full computer systems and networks: you could test vulnerabilities in your Wi-Fi network or a web server, or even simulate some aspects of cyberattack. On the flip side, it can also be used as a honeypot, a way of trying to lure in and expose other hackers and malware.
As with the Flipper Zero though, there's no need to panic about the Flipper One. Its security testing and hacking prowess is not significantly different to a laptop or a Raspberry Pi system — it doesn't suddenly unlock security vulnerabilities in other systems and devices that weren't there before, and could be exposed in other ways.

FLIPPER ONE
Open Linux Multi-tool based on a powerful ARM processor. Network toolkit with built-in Wi-Fi 6E and Gigabit Ethernet. Expandable via M.2 modules: 5G cellular modem, SDR radio and more.
LIVE ON
Follow progress at the 
Developer Portal >>
OPEN LINUX PLATFORM
Flipper One runs on completely open-source Linux firmware with a mainline kernel. This means ultimate compatibility with bleeding-edge kernel versions, free from vendor-specific BSP limitations and binary blobs. Our goal is to create a fully upstream-supported ARM computer that never becomes outdated, and a well-documented platform giving the community maximum freedom to customize:
Full mainline Linux kernel support
No binary blobs
No vendor-locked BSP
          Making things open together
We have partnered with the Collabora team, who are actively working on bringing the RK3576 SoC into the mainline kernel, to make Flipper One fully upstream-supported and free from binary blobs. 
Read Collabora blog post
CO-PROCESSOR ARCHITECTURE
Two independent processors work together in parallel, each controlling different sub-systems of Flipper One. 
 This architecture allows you to 
control the Linux sub-system directly from the built-in LCD display
, without losing access to the device even when the Linux kernel is not yet ready or turned off.
Why 2 processors?
Low-power MCU (Raspberry Pi RP2350)
 — an energy-efficient microcontroller responsible for core device control. It drives the LCD display, physical buttons, and status LEDs. It manages the power sub-system and controls the Linux OS boot parameters when the Linux sub-system is off. 
More about MCU Firmware >>
High-performance CPU (Rockchip RK3576)
 — a powerful 8-core ARM system-on-chip that runs the Linux operating system. It executes user applications, controls all networking interfaces such as Wi-Fi and Ethernet, and manages high-speed peripherals including USB3 and PCIe. It also integrates an NPU for AI acceleration and a GPU for graphics output via HDMI and DisplayPort. 
More about Linux sub-system >>
ONE CABLE FOR EVERYTHING
Get SSH access and charge the device via a single USB cable! No extra adapters needed for Linux console access or powering the device — Flipper One can simultaneously emulate a virtual network adapter, a serial adapter (COM port), and a mass-storage device.
No drivers required on macOS, Linux, Windows, iOS, or Android.
NETWORK MULTI‑TOOL
Multi-WAN network toolkit — connect up to 5 uplinks. Configure dynamic routing, failover, load balancing, and firewall rules. Use it simultaneously as a Wi‑Fi hotspot and client. Tunnel traffic through a VPN and multiple gateways.
NETWORK SCANNER
Analyze wired and wireless IP networks. Flipper One helps you map network topology, discover active hosts, and scan for open ports and services. It can also act as a passive sensor to detect network attacks and report anomalies.
POWER METER
The power consumption meter works like a wattmeter, showing in real time how much power the entire system uses, including the CPU and peripherals. It helps you understand what is happening under the hood: whether the system is idle or actively working.
M.2 MODULES
The core idea of Flipper One is an expandable hardware platform. Create your own specialized tool with a high-speed M.2 expansion module that installs inside the device, under the back plate. It supports almost any type of module — cellular or satellite modems, SDR modules, AI accelerators, SSDs (NVMe or SATA), and Wi-Fi cards via adapters.

Satellite NTN Modem
          Satellite modem
NTN 
(Non-Terrestrial Networks)
 — is a satellite communication technology, a low-speed connection designed for IoT devices, standardized by 3GPP as part of the 5G and LTE specifications. It uses the standard cellular stack, including SIM/eSIM authentication, roaming, and regular IP traffic.
We want to add NTN satellite connectivity support to Flipper One to help popularize this technology and give engineers and enthusiasts a chance to work with real satellite infrastructure. 
To make this happen, we're looking for a partner company like 
Skylo
 that's ready to work with us to add support for their satellite network to our eSIM module and help us choose a specific 
NTN M.2 module
 that we can officially support. 
 Read more on the

Built-in Advanced Wi-Fi Chip
        Built-in Wi-Fi Chip
We want to build the most versatile integrated Wi-Fi into Flipper One — it needs to support all the features required for Wi-Fi network analysis, including monitor mode and packet injection. For now, we've settled on the popular Mediatek MT7921U chipset. It's modern enough, supports three frequency bands, and ticks just about every box. We're actively testing this chip right now and 
we'd love you to join in
.
Why MT7921AUN?
The same chipset is used in the popular ALFA AWUS036AXML adapter, and there's a sizeable community around the open-source MT7921U driver. The main strengths of this chipset:
Modern Wi-Fi 6E: 802.11ax, 2x2 MIMO
Frequencies: 2.4 / 5 / 6 GHz bands
Open mainline Linux driver: mt7921u
Monitor mode support
Integrated Bluetooth 5.2
BUILT-IN eSIM
Flipper One has a built-in eSIM chip that comes pre‑provisioned with a Flipper SIM data plan.
To use the eSIM data plan, you need to install a 5G modem in the M.2 slot. You can also switch between the built-in eSIM and a physical SIM card.
What is Flipper SIM?
We're working on an experimental connectivity project for network geeks. Flipper SIM is a universal SIM card for routers, phones, and IoT devices, with advanced features such as always-on connectivity, outage-proof, traffic filtering, and network management tools. We'll share more details soon!
