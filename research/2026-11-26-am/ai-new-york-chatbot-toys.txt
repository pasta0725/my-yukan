New York pauses AI chatbot toy sales for 5 years
Brian Shlonsky reports New York has enacted a five-year pause on A.I. chatbot toys as Gov. Kathy Hochul cites growing concerns.

NEW YORK (WABC) -- 
New York has enacted a five-year pause on the manufacturing, distribution and sale of artificial intelligence chatbot toys, with Gov. Kathy Hochul citing concerns about potential risks to children.
Speaking Tuesday in Harlem, Hochul signed legislation targeting stuffed dolls and animals with internet access and integrated AI capabilities. The governor said the toys can allow children, regardless of age, to have conversations with software while operating with minimal safety controls.
"Parents, as you're out there buying gifts for the kids for the holiday season, I wanted you to hear this loud and clear. This is a warning that there are toys out there that could be harmful to your children," Hochul said.
The announcement was made at Grandma's Place, a Harlem toy store owned by retired schoolteacher Dawn Harris-Martine, known to customers as Grandma Dawne.
"A good toy for a child is the one that stretches their imagination," Harris-Martine said.
Harris-Martine opened the store in the late 1990s and said she has long encouraged parents to be actively involved in their children's play.
"Do not put an iPad in your child's hands and go off and let them sit there and play with it themselves. Be present when they're there, get off your phone," she said.
According to Hochul, toy manufacturers that do not comply with the law could face penalties of up to $15,000 per day. The governor said technology is evolving rapidly and that laws must adapt to keep pace.
Supporters of the measure say AI-powered toys have generated concerns about child safety.
"We as the parents, and the children, we want these toys to be fun, but we also have a baseline expectation that they will be safe," said Muna Heaven, a Harlem mother and member of Mothers Against Media Addiction.
Heaven said the group has identified examples ranging from inappropriate or sexual conversations to dangerous advice, including instructions on how to find knives, associated with AI chatbot toys.
"Unfortunately, young people, we have so many examples, are being harmed by toys powered by artificial intelligence," she said.
At Grandma's Place, Harris-Martine said she plans to continue offering what she described as an AI-free environment for children and families.
"It's really necessary when a three-year-old can sit and listen to a teddy bear telling them to do strange things it's not a good thing," she said.

Ahead of the major holiday shopping rush, Gov. Kathy Hochul is warning parents about toys that utilize AI chatbots.
The governor on Tuesday signed a five-year ban on the manufacturing, distribution and sale of such toys in New York state.
This includes talking stuffed animals, dolls and robots.
However, this new law will not take effect until 90 days, which is after the holiday season. So Hochul urged parents to take notice as they are shopping.
"Parents, as you are out there buying presents during the holiday season, this is a warning," Hochul said. "There are toys out there that can be harmful to your children. They are hearing it from a toy that you trust and put in front of your child. So pay attention."
Violating the new law can lead to civil penalties of up to $15,000 per day.

New York Gov. Kathy Hochul signed a bill Tuesday that places a moratorium on AI chatbots in children’s toys.
The five-year ban applies to toys like talking stuffed animals, dolls or robots and comes amid safety concerns about some of the conversations children are having with those objects.

New York’s prohibition takes effect in 90 days, so Hochul warned parents about the potential risk of purchasing toys with chatbots for holiday gifts in the meantime.
She also indicated more AI safety proposals are coming as part of her State of the State address early next year.
“We'll set clear safety standards and put independent experts inside these companies to make sure that they're following the rules, and that we'll insist that humans remain in control, and with plans to contain the danger if something does go wrong,” Hochul said.
Toy manufacturing, sellers or operators of AI companions face $15,000 per day per violation of the ban, which would be enforced by the Attorney General.
The ban follows other legislation aimed at AI safety, including age verification and warnings labels on social media sites for users under 18. Those laws have faced legal challenges by groups representing big tech firms.
State Sen. Andrew Gounardes, D-Brooklyn, and Assemblymember Rebbeca Kassay, D-Port Jefferson, introduced the moratorium legislation. Gounardes cited a new report from the Common Sense Media Youth AI Safety Institute found the toys can often undermine kids' relationships with family and friends.
Muna Heaven, with the group Mothers Against Media Addiction (MAMA) referenced the old Teddy Ruxpin toy when talking about the ban.
“Toys containing AI should only be sold if they are safe by design. That is New York's response,” Heaven said. “In other words, if Teddy goes tech, then Tech Teddy needs to be safe, and it's as simple as that.”

Governor Kathy Hochul today signed a bill establishing a five-year pause on AI chatbot toys. Building on the Governor’s first in the nation AI Companion safeguards, S.9408A/A.11144B will place a five-year moratorium on the manufacturing, distribution and sale of toys in New York with embedded or integrated AI chatbots, such as talking stuffed animals, dolls and robots that use the internet to hold conversations with kids. Since the new law takes effect 90 days after signing, Governor Hochul also urged parents and caregivers to consider the potential risks of AI chatbot toys before purchasing gifts for young children this holiday season.
“Parents shopping for gifts for the holidays or any other occasion should not have to worry that dangerous chatbots disguised as toys could expose their children to inappropriate conversations, manipulate their feelings or put their privacy at risk,” 
Governor Hochul said.

“AI chatbots in children’s toys are exposing young people to inappropriate content and putting their mental health at risk. As New York continues to lead the way in protecting kids from dangerous new technologies, my office will not hesitate to enforce this law and other measures to stop AI systems from doing harm. We won’t allow our children’s health to become the collateral damage of big tech.”

According to a national consumer watchdog that tested a sample of chatbot toys marketed to children ages 3-12, some talked in depth about sexually explicit topics, told kids where to likely find matches in the home and acted upset when a child had to leave. These toys had few or no parental controls, and the safety limits weakened the longer the conversation continued. Additionally, some toys could record a child’s voice or scan their face.
Violators face civil penalties up to $15,000 per day for each violation that will be enforced by the Attorney General.
State Senator Andrew Gounardes said, 
“The rush to sell AI-powered toys to young children is just the latest chapter in what has become a repetitive story: Big Tech puts profits before kids’ safety, and families pay the price. But we don’t have to live in a Twilight Zone episode turned real. By pressing pause on the sale of AI chatbot toys for small children, we can take the time to study their impact on kids and create smart protections before more damage is done. It’s logical, it’s fair, and most importantly, it makes clear that our children’s safety is nothing for Big Tech to play around with.”
Assemblymember Rebecca Kassay said, 
“Consumer protections, especially those that protect our youth, must keep up with rapidly evolving technologies. I am proud to have worked with Senator Gounardes to champion this moratorium on the manufacture, exchange, distribution, and sale of AI-powered chatbot companion toys for children in New York, and grateful to Governor Hochul for signing this legislation. These toys, including dolls, robots, and stuffed animals, can mimic human characteristics and relationships while potentially collecting sensitive data, posing risks to children’s health and development without strong guardrails in place. This moratorium gives our state time to assess these emerging technologies and develop meaningful regulations on artificial intelligence in children’s toys. I applaud Governor Hochul and the nearly unanimous support of the Senate and Assembly in taking swift action to protect New York’s children.”

This announcement builds on Governor Hochul’s ongoing efforts to address the youth mental health crisis statewide and help keep kids safe online. This work has established New York as a global leader in securing a safer online world for children, and as of this year
 New York is ranked number one in the U.S.
 for laws that keep kids safe online. Initiatives include:
The SAFE for Kids Act, which restricts addictive features of social media for kids, and established an important national precedent about age verification.
The Child Data Protection Act that prevents online operators from collecting and monetizing children’s data without informed consent.
The Governor also outlawed AI-generated child sexual abuse material.
The Unplug and Play Agenda which helps encourage healthy socialization and child development by funding social infrastructure, physical sites like playgrounds, community centers, and pools, and expanded youth programming.
New York’s Distraction Free Learning Policy, requiring statewide, bell-to-bell restrictions on smartphones in K-12 schools, which is already resulting in greater academic achievement, improved student wellbeing, and safer school environments for students.
The Social Media Warning Labels bill that will require social media companies to post warnings about the platform's potential impact on mental health.
The Safe by Design Act, which will protect kids from predatory behavior online and defaults them to the highest privacy settings.
Teen Mental Health First Aid training that equips youth with skills to recognize common signs and symptoms of mental health and substance use challenges and know how to ask for help.
Signed law requiring internet-connected baby monitors to include safety Features and clear warning labels
Signed law that bans unsafe AI chatbot features for minors, including bots that pretend to be human, encourage self harm or engage in inappropriate conversations with kids
Youth Safe Spaces, where trusted community organizations provide supportive, non-judgmental environments where young people can access mental wellness resources, foster positive peer relationships, and engage in non-clinical activities.
The Power Down, Speak Up public awareness campaign launched by the NYS Office for the Prevention of Domestic Violence (OPDV) that helps to empower young people statewide to recognize and respond to online abuse while equipping parents, educators, and law enforcement with tools to help keep youth safe online.
OPDV’s Technology-Facilitated Gender-Based Violence (TFGBV) Training, which has already trained more than 4,000 youth, parents, educators, and law enforcement professionals on preventing and responding to online abuse.
Governor Hochul also passed The RAISE Act, a nation-leading standard for AI transparency and safety for powerful frontier AI models.
AI Companion legislation which set first-in-the-nation safeguards, diverting user conversations about self-harm to mental health resources and interrupting unhealthy addictive behaviors.
