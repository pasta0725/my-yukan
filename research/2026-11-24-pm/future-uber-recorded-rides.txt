Making Recording Easier

Knowing a trip is being recorded can give riders and drivers greater peace of mind, encourage respectful interactions, and deter inappropriate behavior. That’s why we continue to invest in recording technology and expand its benefits to more trips.
We started with in-app audio recording and expanded to Record My Ride, which allows drivers to capture both audio and video using their phone’s front-facing camera. These tools help riders and drivers feel safe and more in control, with privacy protections built in. Recordings provide valuable context for our teams when reviewing a safety report and help support fair outcomes.
Now, guided by feedback from riders and drivers, we’re building on that foundation to make recording easier, more accessible, and more useful when it matters most.
Recorded Rides on Demand
Today, we began testing “Recorded Rides,” a new option that lets riders specifically request a trip with a driver who is recording using Uber’s Record my Ride feature. Starting in Baltimore, Raleigh, Indianapolis, and St. Louis, riders can select “Recorded Rides” to be matched with a driver who is recording, if one is available.
This pilot will help give riders who want a recorded ride a way to request one from the start—and provide greater confidence that a recording will be available if something doesn’t go as planned.
Easier Recording When It Matters Most
We’re also exploring new ways to make recording easier and more accessible when people may need it most.
Over half of all active drivers in the US now record their trips with Record My Ride, and we want to make it even easier for more drivers to do so. Soon, we’ll begin testing proactively enabling the feature on certain trips, such as late night and Teen trips. If a driver prefers not to record, they can simply opt out of Record My Ride in their settings.
We’re also exploring turning recording on when trips are not proceeding as expected, like when our RideCheck feature detects a possible crash or a trip going significantly off course. This will help ensure recording is available when it matters most, giving our safety team valuable context if an incident is reported.
In select cities, we’ll also start testing turning on audio recording for riders on nighttime trips, for added peace of mind — and one less thing to remember. Recording is still optional; riders who prefer not to record can simply tap ‘Don’t Record’ on the in-app notification.
Built-In Privacy Protections
As we continue to build, privacy remains central to everything we do with recording technology. We’ll let riders and drivers know when they’re matched with someone using in-app recording. Recordings are encrypted, stored on the user’s device, and automatically deleted unless uploaded as part of a safety report or insurance claim. Uber can only review uploaded recordings.
Our goal is to increase adoption of our recording features with both riders and drivers and gauge this growing technology’s capabilities. We look forward to their feedback as we continue to invest in new ways to help people feel more confident on their trips.

Uber now lets riders request drivers who are recording their trips
The Recorded Rides option is rolling out to Baltimore, Raleigh, Indianapolis and St. Louis first.

For riders looking for another layer of security, Uber is rolling out its new 
Recorded Rides
 feature. The latest feature on the ride-hailing app allows riders to opt for drivers who are using Uber's Record My Ride, an in-app function that uses the driver phone's selfie camera and microphone to record trips. Uber is starting the rollout with availability to Baltimore, Raleigh, Indianapolis and St. Louis users first.
"Knowing a trip is being recorded can give riders and drivers greater peace of mind, encourage respectful interactions, and deter inappropriate behavior," Annie Duvnjak, Uber's global head of safety, said in a press release, adding that Recorded Rides will offer "greater confidence that a recording will be available if something doesn't go as planned."
As for drivers, Uber said it's going to expand its Record My Ride feature, even testing out proactively enabling recording by default during late night trips or when picking up teen riders. Uber added that it's also looking into a function to turn on recording in emergency situations, like in cases that the app's RideCheck feature detects a possible car crash or if a trip is going "significantly off course." Uber will also test out having audio recording turned on by default during nighttime trips.
Drivers and riders still have the choice to opt out of any recordings within the app's settings. Duvnjak explained that "recordings are encrypted, stored on the user's device, and automatically deleted unless uploaded as part of a safety report or insurance claim," adding that Uber can only review recordings. 
As the Recorded Rides feature starts its rollout, it's actually not the first time that Uber showed off in-app recording abilities. Earlier this year, Uber debuted a 
live video tool
 that lets parents or legal guardians monitor their teen's trip through the selfie cam on the driver's phone. Uber then expanded the feature with the 
Uber Kids program
, that offers the same video feed for riders between 10 and 12 years old.

While drivers can opt out of using the feature in their settings, more than half of its active drivers in the US choose to record their trips in the Record My Ride feature.
Starting this week, Uber riders in those four cities will be able to request a recorded ride during the hailing process in the Uber app and, when possible, be automatically matched with a driver using the feature. Choosing a recorded ride doesn’t affect the fare.
Uber’s Record My Ride program
Record My Ride has been available to Uber’s drivers, though not passengers, for a few years now. The feature uses the front-facing camera on the driver’s phone to capture trip audio and video. 
Uber says the recordings are encrypted and stored on the driver’s device to protect the privacy of drivers and riders, and are automatically deleted after a period of time. 
“Uber can only review recordings uploaded as part of a safety report or insurance claim,” a representative told CNET.

Not selecting a Recorded Ride doesn’t guarantee that your trip won’t be recorded in some way. Many Uber drivers choose to install separate dash cams in their vehicles, often with multiple cameras recording into the cabin. Uber offers drivers the option to register their dash cam with the service, but this is separate from the pilot.
“Drivers can use a separate dash cam, provided they comply with local laws,” an Uber representative told CNET in an email, “but Recorded Rides specifically matches riders with drivers recording through Record My Ride.”
Automating recordings for safety

trips involving teenagers

Uber says it’s also looking into testing the 
automatic activation of audio recording
 on riders’ phones during nighttime trips. Passengers would have to consent to this, which would require the app to use their phone’s microphone.
