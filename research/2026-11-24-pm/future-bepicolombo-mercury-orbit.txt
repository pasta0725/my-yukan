Update for 2:50 p.m. ET on Nov. 23: 
On Sunday afternoon (Nov. 22), the BepiColombo team announced that, while the spacecraft did successfully arrive in orbit around Mercury on Saturday (Nov. 21), the engine burn that got it there 
was cut short
 due to a software glitch. On Monday (Nov. 23), we got 
another update
: BepiColombo "is safe and in a stable orbit around Mercury" and is on track for the next big milestone: The separation of its two orbiters on Dec. 9.
For just the second time ever, Mercury has received a robotic visitor from Earth.
The 
BepiColombo
 mission — a joint effort of the 
European Space Agency
 (ESA) and the Japanese Aerospace Exploration Agency (
JAXA
) — arrived in orbit around Mercury on Saturday (Nov. 21), more than eight years after leaving its home planet.
Before BepiColombo, just one spacecraft had ever circled the solar system's innermost planet: NASA's 
MESSENGER
, which did so from March 2011 to April 2015.
BepiColombo consists of two main spacecraft: ESA's Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO) and JAXA's Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter (MMO), also known as Mio.
The duo launched — along with a power and propulsion element called the Mercury Transfer Module (MTM) — aboard an Ariane 5 rocket from French Guiana 
in October 2018
, beginning a long and looping trip through the inner solar system.
Mercury
 is a difficult planet to reach because it's so close to 
the sun
. Spacecraft approaching the planet are accelerated to high velocities by the immense gravitational pull of our star and must therefore shed a great deal of kinetic energy to be captured by Mercury's much weaker tug.
So the BepiColombo team designed a trajectory that featured one flyby of 
Earth
, two of 
Venus
 and six of 
Mercury
 over the course of about seven years. That flight time was 
increased by a year
, however, after the mission suffered an issue with its solar panels, which reduced the power available for the MTM's electric thrusters.
BepiColombo's arrival at Mercury will take a total of about six months. 
(Image credit: European Space Agency)
Saturday's orbital insertion came after a 37-minute-long engine burn, which began at 1:24 p.m. EST (1824 GMT). There was a bit drama involved, however, as confirmation of the maneuver's success didn't come for several hours afterward.
"We've heard back from Bepi — the spacecraft is now in contact with the ground and the teams at ESOC [the European Space Operations Centre] are busy assessing the telemetry. Flight Dynamics already has confirmed it achieved orbital capture around Mercury! Early next week we'll share the next update with the upcoming steps," the mission team 
said via X
 on Saturday evening.

Orbital insertion is the latest milestone in BepiColombo's Mercury arrival phase, which is expected to take about six months in total.
Arrival began on Sept. 3, when the MTM released MPO and Mio. The two orbiters remain connected today; they won't separate until Dec. 9 or thereabouts.
Shortly thereafter, Mio will settle into its final orbit, a highly elliptical path that will take it over Mercury's poles. MPO is also destined for a polar orbit, though a much less elliptical one than Mio's; the ESA probe will conduct a series of engine burns before finally getting there on March 10, 2027.
BepiColombo — which is named after the late Italian scientist and mathematician Giuseppe "Bepi" Colombo — won't start doing science work until April 6, 2027. The two probes will gather complementary data from their different vantage points, shedding a great deal of light on a mysterious world.
"The mission will study all aspects of Mercury, from the structure and dynamics of its magnetosphere and how it interacts with the 
solar wind
, to its internal structure with its large iron core, and the origin of the planet’s magnetic field," ESA officials wrote in a 
BepiColombo mission description
.
"The data will enable scientists to understand more about the origin and evolution of a planet located close to its parent star, and a better understanding of the overall evolution of our solar system," they added.

水星探査機、軌道投入成功＝「みお」など日欧の２機―欧州宇宙機関
NEWS
公開日: 
2026. 11. 22

欧州宇宙機関（ＥＳＡ）は２２日（日本時間）、日欧共同の水星探査計画「ベピコロンボ」の探査機２機の周回軌道投入に成功したと発表した。
ベピコロンボは、太陽系の惑星で最も内側を回る水星の磁場や大気などの環境を詳しく調べる計画。２０１８年１０月、磁場などを観測する宇宙航空研究開発機構（ＪＡＸＡ）の「みお」と、表面の様子を調べるＥＳＡの「ＭＰＯ」が結合した状態で打ち上げられた。当初は２５年１２月の到着予定だったが、推進系のトラブルにより約１年遅れとなった。
探査機の水星周回軌道投入は、１１年３月の米探査機メッセンジャーに続き史上２例目。太陽の熱や重力の影響を強く受けるため、周回探査には高度な技術が必要とされる。
１２月１０日ごろには、ＭＰＯとみおを分離。来年４月ごろからの本格的な科学観測開始を目指す。
