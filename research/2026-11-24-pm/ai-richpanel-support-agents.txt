Richpanel uses AI agents to target 50% support automation in 30 days
OpenAI profiles Richpanel’s use of AI agents and a claimed 50% customer-support automation rate within 30 days.
iHeartGeek News Desk
23 Nov 2026, 23:52 UTC
Share
OpenAI is highlighting Richpanel’s use of its models to automate customer-support operations, with the company claiming it can reach a 50% automation rate within the first 30 days. The case study is an official OpenAI customer story published on November 23, 2026. 
1
Richpanel’s support system is built around more than simply answering questions. The company says its AI agents can use the tools and procedures needed to carry out a solution, rather than handing every task back to a human. CEO Amit RG says support workers may rely on 20 or 30 tools, while the procedures for using them are often undocumented. 
1
How Richpanel is using OpenAI models
Richpanel has built an AI manager that customers call Boss AI. It prepares the tools and procedures used by the company’s frontline support agent, copilot and quality-assurance agent. OpenAI says Richpanel uses GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol for Boss AI, prioritising accuracy while those systems build the operational instructions. 
1
For customer conversations, Richpanel uses GPT-6 Luna and other OpenAI models. The company says cost matters because its customers may already be comparing AI support with lower-cost outsourced operations. Richpanel therefore uses more capable models for longer setup and reasoning tasks, then chooses more economical models for high-volume conversations. 
1
Richpanel says it had completed about 300 AI customer-support deployments in the six months before an October 2026 interview. It also says it guarantees a 50% automation rate within the first 30 days or customers get their money back, and that it had met that commitment across those deployments. Those figures are claims from Richpanel, presented by OpenAI rather than independently audited results. 
1
The approach reflects a broader shift in customer-service automation: the difficult part is not only generating a reply, but giving an agent access to the systems, rules and context needed to complete the work. Richpanel’s model splits that problem into two parts — an AI manager that prepares the workflow, and other agents that operate it at scale. The company says its agents handle hundreds of thousands of conversations, making the cost of each interaction a central part of its model choices. 
1
What this means for businesses
The attraction is straightforward. If a support system can turn an undocumented process into a repeatable procedure, companies may be able to deploy automation without assigning an engineer to configure every customer account manually. Richpanel’s Boss AI is intended to handle that setup work itself, according to the case study. 
1
There is an important caveat: the 50% figure is a vendor claim tied to Richpanel’s own deployments and guarantee. It does not establish that every business using the same OpenAI models will see the same result. The case study also does not publish a methodology for measuring automation or customer satisfaction, so the numbers are best read as a description of Richpanel’s commercial offer rather than a general benchmark. 
1

3
Our opinion
Richpanel’s most interesting idea is not the headline percentage; it is the attempt to automate the setup work around an AI agent. Customer support is full of unwritten routines, awkward software hand-offs and exceptions that do not fit a neat FAQ. If Boss AI can genuinely turn that messy knowledge into reliable procedures, it addresses a more practical problem than simply making chat replies sound more human.
The 50% promise still needs careful reading. It is a company-reported result from a promotional case study, not an independent test, and the quality of the underlying procedures will matter more than the model names. The sensible takeaway is that AI support is moving towards tool use and workflow execution — but businesses should demand evidence about escalation rates, accuracy and customer satisfaction before treating an impressive automation claim as a universal result.

The 50% automation promise
OpenAI has published a business customer story about Richpanel, a maker of AI customer support software. The article, dated 23 November 2026, sets out how the company prepares and runs support agents on OpenAI models.
At the centre of the story is a commercial guarantee. Richpanel promises to automate 50% of a customer's support in the first 30 days, or to give the money back. Amit RG, the company's chief executive, said in an interview in October 2026 that Richpanel had completed about 300 AI support deployments in the previous six months, and that it had hit the 50% mark in every one of them.
Those figures come from Richpanel, not from an outside test. OpenAI published them as a customer story, and the material includes no independent audit of the results.
Boss AI handles the setup
Rolling out a support agent usually requires a forward-deployed engineer. That is a technical member of staff who works inside a customer's company to connect a product to its systems and define how it should behave. Amit said many businesses simply did not have engineers to spare, so he asked what would happen if that engineer was an AI agent instead.
Richpanel built one. The setup agent is called Boss AI, a name that customers started using rather than one chosen by the company, according to the account. Boss AI writes the procedures that the other agents follow and decides how they call the tools they need.
Three agents then work from those instructions: a frontline support agent that deals with customers, a copilot that assists human staff, and a quality-assurance agent. Richpanel uses GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol for the setup work, because accuracy matters most when the instructions are being written.
Tools matter as much as the model
Amit said a human support agent may move between 20 or 30 tools while solving a single customer's problem. Many of the steps those agents follow have never been written down in any manual.
That gap explains why Richpanel puts weight on procedures as well as on conversation. Knowing the right answer is not enough if the agent cannot also carry out the fix. In Amit's words, "The intelligence is no longer the bottleneck. It's everything around it."
Choosing models by cost
The frontline agent is designed for very high volume, running to hundreds of thousands of customer conversations. That makes the price of each individual reply an important part of choosing a model. Richpanel runs those conversations on GPT-6 Luna and other OpenAI models, and says it takes the same approach with its copilot and quality-assurance agents.
The deeper reasoning happens earlier, while Boss AI is preparing the tools and procedures. Amit said what counts is a balance of cost and intelligence. He also credited OpenAI with making models available that can take in long context, meaning a large amount of text in one go, and work through longer tasks while staying affordable.
Why the baseline matters
Richpanel's offer is aimed at companies that already buy low-cost outsourced support. Automating that work only makes financial sense if it costs less than the service it replaces, so the 50% figure has to be read against what a business pays today.
For buyers, the case study is a supplier's own account rather than a benchmark. The transferable detail is the method: one agent that writes the procedures, and a deliberate split between costly reasoning at setup time and cheaper models for live chat. The full account, including Amit's comments, appears in OpenAI's business customer stories.

1. The commitments
Resolution.
 Within 30 calendar days of an eligible customer's go-live date, Richpanel commits that its AI agents will autonomously resolve at least 
fifty percent (50%)
 of the customer's eligible conversations across the channels enabled at go-live. If this threshold is not met, the customer may exercise the remedies set out in §5 below.
This is a calendar-day window measured from the date Richpanel and the customer mutually confirm in writing that the customer's AI agents have been promoted to live, customer-facing operation ("Go-Live Date"). The window does not pause for weekends, holidays, or low-traffic periods.
Cost.
 Separately, and before any customer commits to Richpanel, Richpanel calculates the customer's projected total Richpanel cost (helpdesk seats plus AI usage, at the customer's actual conversation volume and configuration) and compares it against the customer's documented current cost for their existing helpdesk platform plus any AI or automation add-on(s). Richpanel commits to demonstrating a total cost at least 
thirty percent (30%)
 below that combined baseline. This comparison is performed at the evaluation stage, before any purchase, so the customer bears no risk: if Richpanel cannot demonstrate the 30% reduction, the customer is under no obligation to proceed and owes nothing. In atypical situations, such as a specially discounted incumbent contract or a configuration with many seats and very low ticket volume, the reduction may not be achievable, in which case the customer may simply decline to proceed.

An 
"autonomous resolution"
 means a conversation that is closed by an AI agent without escalation to or message-level intervention by a human agent on the customer's team, and where the end customer does not re-open the same conversation within 72 hours.
Resolution rate is calculated as the number of autonomously resolved 
eligible conversations
 divided by the total number of eligible conversations received during the 30-day window. Both numerator and denominator are computed from Richpanel's internal analytics and made available to the customer on request.
The following conversation types count as 
eligible

Inbound conversations on channels that were enabled at Go-Live and remained enabled throughout the window.
Conversations in languages that Richpanel's AI agents were configured to handle.
Conversations covered by the SOPs and tools the customer signed off on during onboarding.

The guarantee is available to customers that meet all of the following criteria:
The customer is on a paid Richpanel subscription and has paid all invoices due before the Go-Live Date.
The customer completed Richpanel's standard onboarding, including providing the business context, SOPs, tool integrations, and historical conversation data that Richpanel requested.
The customer and Richpanel mutually confirmed the Go-Live Date in writing.
The customer did not materially alter the AI agent's instructions, tools, or permissions during the 30-day window without Richpanel's prior written agreement.
The customer is a new Richpanel customer (the guarantee is one-time per organization).
4. Exclusions
The following do not count toward the resolution-rate calculation, and a failure to resolve them does not trigger the guarantee:
Conversations on a channel the customer added after Go-Live without Richpanel's confirmation that the channel was production-ready.
Conversations in languages the AI was not configured to handle.
Conversations that fall outside the SOPs, tools, or permissions agreed to at onboarding (for example, fraud reviews, legal escalations, or regulatory disputes that the customer's policy requires a human to handle).
Conversations affected by a third-party outage (channel provider, payment processor, OMS) for the duration of the outage.
Conversations during a period when the customer's instructions explicitly directed the AI to escalate to a human.
Abuse, spam, fraud-attempt, or non-customer traffic, where Richpanel can show that the conversation was not a bona fide support request.
